# Civil Registry and Franchise Service

## Summary

The Civil Registry and Franchise Service maintains the legal identity of Dominion residents.

It records birth, adoption, clone status, family contracts, household formation, migration, service eligibility, citizenship, franchise status, legal name changes, and death declarations.

In daily life, this is one of the most important Federal agencies because it decides how a person is recognized by the state.

## Mandate

The Service administers:

- resident records;
- citizenship verification;
- service-track eligibility;
- voting-franchise status;
- household registry;
- family benefit qualification;
- identity restoration after disaster;
- clone and hybrid legal recognition;
- legal personhood records for uplifted or engineered populations where applicable.

## Powers

The Service may certify identity, correct records, issue civic credentials, flag suspected fraud, and require review when identity data conflicts across archives.

## Limits

The Service may not revoke personhood, erase dissidents from public records, deny basic rights because of political belief, or treat engineered origin as diminished humanity.

Franchise can be regulated by law. Personhood cannot be treated as a reward.

## Public Face

Citizens know this agency through local registry halls, school enrollment offices, marriage and household filings, migration kiosks, service exams, and benefit renewals.

It is bureaucratic, slow, and emotionally important.

## Story Use

Use this agency when identity becomes concrete: a cloned worker demanding equal recognition, a frontier child trying to prove citizenship, a dissident household protecting its benefits, or a service-track youth learning that citizenship is both right and burden.
