[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]. Main Sequence Stars (Hydrogen Fusion Phase)

Defined by fusion of hydrogen into helium via:

Proton-proton chain (low-mass stars, like the Sun).

CNO cycle (higher-mass stars).

Stable phase where outward thermal pressure balances inward gravity.

Lifespan varies drastically:

Red dwarfs (<0.5 M☉): Burn hydrogen slowly, lifespans up to trillions of years.

Solar-mass stars (~1 M☉): Main sequence lasts ~10 billion years.

3. Post-Main Sequence Evolution

Low/Medium Mass Stars (≤8 M☉):

Expand into red giants, burning helium into carbon and oxygen.

Shed outer layers as planetary nebulae.

Core collapses into a white dwarf (degenerate carbon/oxygen core).

High Mass Stars (>8 M☉):

Expand into red/blue supergiants.

Fuse progressively heavier elements up to iron.

End in catastrophic core-collapse supernovae, leaving behind:

Neutron stars

Black holes

[[Chapter (The Old War)|Chapter (The Old War)]]. Stellar Remnants

White Dwarfs: Degenerate matter supported by electron pressure, eventual fate: black dwarf (theoretical, since universe isn’t old enough).

Neutron Stars: Supported by neutron degeneracy, extreme density.

Black Holes: Gravity overwhelms all quantum pressures; forms an event horizon.

5. Traditional Star Types (Observational Classification)

Red Dwarfs: Small, cool, dim, but longest lived.

Yellow Dwarfs (G-type): Like our Sun, balance of stability and habitability.

Blue Giants/Supergiants: Massive, hot, short-lived.

Red Giants/Supergiants: Expanded late-stage stars.

White Dwarfs: Dense stellar remnants, cooling slowly.

[[Brown Dwarfs]] (Substellar): “Failed stars” not massive enough for hydrogen fusion.

6. Stellar Importance in Cosmology

Planetary [[Systems]]: Protostellar disks form planets, asteroids, and comets.

Cosmic Evolution: Star populations (Pop I, II, III) trace the [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]] of galaxy formation.

Energy Distribution: Stars regulate galactic structure via radiation, winds, and feedback.

7. Traditional Stars in the [[VFF]] / [[MOT]] Context
 Stars – Detailed Summary

Stars are self-gravitating spheres of plasma that sustain themselves through the balance of gravitational collapse and nuclear fusion. They are the engines of structure formation in the universe, seeding galaxies with light, heat, and heavy elements. Their evolution depends primarily on initial mass, which dictates both their lifespan and final fate.

Massive stars (>8 M☉): Lifespans are only millions of years.

When hydrogen is exhausted in the core, equilibrium breaks:

Chemical Enrichment: Stars synthesize elements heavier than helium (stellar nucleosynthesis). Supernovae spread these into the interstellar medium.

Stars are baseline baryonic reactors, the [[Natural]] "default" cosmic power stations.

Their role is fundamental for seeding matter for higher-order [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Synarchate/Economy & Infrastructure/Megastructures|megastructures]], black hole engineering, and exotic [[Physics]].

In the [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Dominion/Dominion]] cosmology, traditional stars are the first-tier cosmic furnaces, contrasted with speculative stars ([[Tachyon]], quantum foam, [[Dimensional Stars]]) which push beyond baryonic fusion.
