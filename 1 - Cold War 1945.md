“The illusion of stability hides the machinery of decay.”
World War II ends with the United States and Soviet Union emerging as opposing ideological titans.
The atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki cement nuclear weapons as existential tools of statecraft.
Nuremberg Trials establish the first attempt at global legal accountability—but the seeds of hypocrisy are already sown in the emerging divide.
The world is split into two blocs. Conflict is no longer overt—it is institutional, economic, and memetic.
there were numerous proxy wars that were supported or sponsored by the respective powers of the east and west. These proxy wars were fought in various parts of the world, including Asia, Africa, and the Middle East, and often involved local conflicts that were manipulated by outside powers. For example, the Soviet Union supported communist guerrilla groups in countries such as Afghanistan, Angola, and Mozambique, while the United States provided support to anti-communist rebels in countries such as Nicaragua and Cambodia. These proxy wars were often marked by violence and political instability, and their outcome often had broader geopolitical implications. The presence of these proxy wars during the Cold War underscores the need for transparency and accountability in international relations. It is essential that we avoid manipulating conflicts for our own political gain and prioritize the safety and well-being of those affected by these conflicts.
