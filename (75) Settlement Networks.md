# (75) Settlement Networks

## Manuscript Source

- Primary source: [[Manuscript]] Draft 2, Part III, Chapters 20-24.
- Manuscript date range: approximately Years 75-105 After Landfall.

## What Happened

The settlements became linked by established mega-trains, crawler routes, mobile camps, pressure corridors, underways, ferry systems, geothermal roads, and data relays. The network did more than move people. It moved gossip, food, teachers, tools, prayer forms, songs, laws, medical packets, and political expectations.

By this stage, New Aurora could no longer act as the only center of gravity. Distance had produced local competence.

## What Resulted

- Regional settlement personalities hardened into reputations.
- Route communities developed shared law around carried supplies, repair duty, shelter priority, and convoy obligations.
- Political autonomy began appearing as a logistical reality before it became ideology.
- The first power blocs emerged around transport access and resource dependency.

## Consequences

Settlement networks created the practical skeleton of future nation states. Whoever controlled routes, schedules, shelters, and train access controlled political leverage.
