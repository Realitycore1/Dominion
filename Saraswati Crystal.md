🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: The Saraswati Crystal
Discovery Location: Rajasthan, [[India]], 2039, in a dried riverbed along the ancient course of the Saraswati River, uncovered during satellite-guided excavations of paleo-channels.
Summary:

Material: Large, polished quartz crystal, ~40 cm tall, clear with faint bluish inclusions. Mounted in a copper-gold lattice that shows Mauryan-era metallurgical techniques.

Dating:

Radiocarbon dating of organic wrapping materials → ~300 BCE.

Metallurgical analysis confirms high-purity copper alloying consistent with Ashokan [[India]].

Inscriptions: Brahmi script etched onto copper lattice, invoking Saraswati Devi as source of knowledge, music, and purity.

Anomalies:

Crystal exhibits unusual birefringence, producing faint musical tones when struck by light at specific angles.

Acoustic resonance tests indicate harmonics [[Close]] to ragas described in early Vedic texts.

Archaeological Significance: Physical proof of Vedic rituals involving crystalline objects; suggests early Indian science understood optical and acoustic resonance as sacred.
