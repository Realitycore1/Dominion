Core Definition

A Stone Basin is a megaversal region in which the host axiom preferentially expresses reality through:
	•	stability
	•	mass
	•	compression
	•	structural persistence
	•	load-bearing geometry
	•	slow transformation under pressure

In simple terms:

A Stone Basin is a reality-space where existence tends to become solid, enduring, stratified, monumental, and difficult to erase.

This does not mean “just rocks.”

Stone can express through:
	•	geology
	•	architecture
	•	crystallized information
	•	compressed time
	•	structural memory
	•	fixed symbolic systems
	•	civilization as layered matter

The key is:

Stone = persistence through form

Not just hardness.

⸻

STONE BASIN — PRIMARY BEHAVIORAL RULES

A Stone Basin tends to produce realities where:

1. Structure outlasts motion

What matters is what remains.

2. Pressure creates form

Compression is often generative, not just destructive.

3. Layering stores history

The past accumulates in visible or buried strata.

4. Change is slow but consequential

Transformation happens through epochs, not moments.

5. Mass and geometry confer authority

Things are real because they are weight-bearing, anchored, and enduring.

That makes Stone Basins ideal for:
	•	empire cosmologies
	•	fossilized civilizations
	•	sacred mountains
	•	archive worlds
	•	tomb verses
	•	ruin ecologies
	•	colossal machine worlds
	•	reality that feels immovable until it suddenly breaks

This is a very strong basin type.

⸻

STONE BASIN — SUBTYPE LOGIC

A Stone subtype should answer:

What kind of structure or persistence dominates the basin?

Not just what it looks like.

That means Stone subtypes should define:
	•	what is being preserved
	•	what pressure does
	•	what is load-bearing
	•	how history accumulates
	•	what kind of “solidity” reality prefers
