# Primus Legion Relic Status

## Consolidated Lock - How Legion Relics Begin

Primus relics did not begin as sacred objects.

Most began as ordinary war necessities:

- a damaged helm that still carried a command recording;
- a field standard used to identify survivors in smoke;
- a weapon repaired too many times to be replaced;
- a medical seal used to certify an evacuation;
- a map fragment that proved a route existed;
- a broken enemy key that opened a prison-world;
- an armor plate signed by the last people rescued from a failing city;
- a machine core that kept functioning after its operator died.

The earliest legionaries did not say, "This is holy."

They said:

"Do not lose this. Someone paid for it."

That is the origin of Dominion relic culture.

Relics pass through five stages:

| Stage | Name | Description |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | Field Object | A useful object survives a defining battle, evacuation, judgment, or command decision. |
| II | Witnessed Object | Survivors attach testimony to it: who held it, who died beside it, what decision it preserved. |
| III | Legion Token | The legion carries it into later campaigns as a reminder of first doctrine. |
| IV | Ceremonial Relic | It becomes too symbolically important for casual deployment and is carried only under oath. |
| V | Continuity Relic | After Severance, it becomes proof that the old Dominion truly existed and that memory survived the fracture. |

No Primus relic is sacred merely because it is old. A relic becomes sacred when it can answer three questions:

1. What happened?
2. Who paid for it?
3. What must not be repeated?

## Relic Types

| Type | Origin | Later symbolic use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Battle relic | Survives a decisive battle or impossible assault. | Proof that the legion's first doctrine worked under real terror. |
| Witness relic | Preserves testimony, legal command, evacuation proof, or refusal record. | Used in oath ceremonies, tribunals, succession rites, and memorial proceedings. |
| Martyr relic | Recovered from a commander, medic, engineer, or civilian who died preserving others. | Carries the cost of Dominion victory into later pageantry. |
| Captured relic | Taken from GalFed or a client power after liberation, collapse, or surrender. | Reframes enemy authority as conquered evidence. |
| Living relic | A machine core, continuity engine, bloodline object, AI witness, or semi-aware system that continues functioning. | Treated with legal caution because it may be artifact, person, witness, and weapon at once. |
| Negative relic | Preserved from an atrocity, failed order, or forbidden weapon. | Displayed only to warn later generations what the Dominion must not become. |

## Symbolic Use After the Fact

After the GalFed-Dominion war, relics become a language of restraint.

They are used in:

- legion musters;
- Verarch succession ceremonies;
- oaths before impossible campaigns;
- memorial courts;
- refusal-right witness ceremonies;
- liberated-world accession debates;
- post-Severance continuity rites;
- trials where old Dominion action must be judged by later Dominion law.

Relics also become dangerous political symbols. A relic can legitimize a legion, a bloodline, a military claim, a pilgrimage, or a rebellion. This is why later Dominion institutions distinguish between relic custody and relic ownership.

A legion may carry a relic.

It does not own what the relic remembers.

## Post-Severance Continuity Classification

Following Severance, survival of Primus relics was no longer determined solely by:

- material durability,

- military preservation,

- or technological redundancy.

Instead,

relic persistence became heavily influenced by:

- continuity exposure,

- metaphysical resonance,

- symbolic significance,

- proximity to Severance,

- and connection to the authority structures of the Celestial Empress.

As a result,

many relics entered unusual post-Severance states:

- partially surviving despite destruction,

- manifesting continuity echoes,

- or persisting as lineage-bound artifacts.

Within the Secundus Era,

Primus relics became:

- sacred continuity anchors,

- proof the old Dominion truly existed,

- and symbols of humanity before the fracture.

# Relic Status Categories

## Confirmed Active

Operational and preserved through stable lineage or institutional continuity.

## Fragmentary Survival

Partially surviving relics, damaged systems, incomplete weapon sets, or degraded operational capability.

## Lost

Known or strongly believed to survive somewhere within Totem, but location unknown.

## Confirmed Destroyed

Physically annihilated beyond standard recovery capability.

## Continuity-Echo Status

Physically destroyed or unstable, yet still manifesting through Severance resonance or anomalous continuity behavior.

Primus relics evolved beyond military artifacts.

- continuity anchors,

- sacred inheritance,

- political legitimacy,

these relics were not merely weapons.

They were:

## surviving fragments of a civilization reality itself attempted to erase.
