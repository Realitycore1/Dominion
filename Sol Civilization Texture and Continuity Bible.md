# Sol Civilization Texture and Continuity Bible

## Purpose

This guide makes historical transition visible through ordinary life. Every chapter must select concrete details from both columns of the texture matrix rather than pausing for a worldbuilding inventory.

The matrix uses:

- **F:** food;
- **C:** clothing;
- **T:** transportation;
- **W:** weather;
- **A:** architecture;
- **Tech:** technology;
- **R:** religion;
- **E:** entertainment;
- **L:** language change;
- **H:** holiday or public observance;
- **M:** memorial tradition.

### Institutional Scene Test

For each institutional scene, identify:

1. the document, tool, queue, room, vehicle, or interface through which the institution acts;
2. the worker who must interpret the rule;
3. the person who cannot wait for appeal;
4. the data the institution records;
5. the later institution that inherits the record;
6. the informal workaround that reveals what official procedure cannot handle.

### Restoration Debate Rules

- No restored landscape returns to an untouched original state.
- A successful intervention must still generate waste, maintenance, ownership, labor, and jurisdiction.
- Uplifted peoples and revived lineages possess ecological politics that do not merely support human restoration.
- De-extinction and synthetic taxa remain distinct from uplift.
- The Terra-era Archosaur Program produced rare synthetic reptile-avian working animals derived from living genomes. They are not resurrected dinosaurs or effortless military cavalry.
- Vulcanite remains destructive containment technology with toxic, evidentiary, ecological, and casualty costs.
- EVO-5 is an inherited regenerative platform in the ancestry of later medicine, not an instant explanation for controlled Immortality.

## Solar Colony Dossiers

Accents are described through rhythm, vocabulary, delay, and borrowed professional language rather than phonetic spelling.

### Luna and the Cynthia Protectorate

- **Why it exists:** automated mining, mass-driver export, orbital construction supply, research, and strategic proximity to Earth.
- **Why people stay:** family graves, schools, low-gravity identity, skilled work, ownership campaigns, and attachment to a landscape outsiders call empty.
- **Why people leave:** radiation exposure, medical limits, corporate debt, Earth family care, and restricted transport.
- **Economy:** metals, regolith processing, vacuum fabrication, mass-driver freight, shipyard support, science, and transit.
- **Politics:** Lunar Colonial Committee from 3008; domicile independent of employment; Cynthia gains domestic independence in 3180 while remaining inside the Colonial Union umbrella.
- **Culture:** pressure-door etiquette, grave-wall citizenship, dust humor, contract anniversaries transformed into family observances.
- **Accent and register:** compressed shift phrasing, exact door and level references, Earth languages mixed through multinational crews.
- **Architecture:** excavated caverns, bermed pressure districts, lava-tube expansion, radiative courtyards beneath shielding, later civic halls.
- **Festivals:** First Birth, Colony Day, Long Night races, Cynthia Charter observance.
- **Local sayings:** "A door remembers who changed the seal." "Temporary is an Earth word."

### Mars and the Kindred of Mars

- **Why it exists:** strategic dispersal, science, water and mineral access, planetary engineering, and the Colonial Union's first new-world mandate.
- **Why people stay:** locally born families, water-district kinship, open civic land, maintenance sovereignty, and the belief that Mars should not remain a mission.
- **Why people leave:** low-gravity health effects, dust exposure, water conflict, political hardening, and family ties elsewhere.
- **Economy:** habitat fabrication, water extraction, geology, life-support engineering, research, transport support, and later specialized agriculture.
- **Politics:** begins as a Union foothold in 3020; Meridian councils become the Kindred of Mars and gain independence in 3230 through association compacts.
- **Culture:** kinship is civic and maintenance-based rather than ancestry-based; water service and corridor labor carry public honor.
- **Accent and register:** measured breath groups, water-district references, *inside/outside* and *up-corridor/down-corridor* distinctions.
- **Architecture:** buried modules, regolith berms, linked meridian corridors, pressure gardens, redundant shelter nodes.
- **Festivals:** First Shelter Seal, Water Return, Meridian Ascent, the first Mars funeral remembrance.
- **Local sayings:** "No corridor maintains itself." "Kin is who answers the leak."

### The O'Neill and Lagrange Habitats

- **Why they exist:** transfer, manufacturing, agriculture, population relief, government, rescue, and orbital construction.
- **Why people stay:** habitat-born identity, controlled climate, dense civic life, specialized work, and freedom from planetary gravity.
- **Why people leave:** spin illness, class segregation, utility politics, accident risk, and resentment of centrally managed ecology.
- **Economy:** food, fabrication, administration, ship support, customs, tourism, education, and interworld services.
- **Politics:** municipal councils within the Union; Gilgamesh houses federal institutions; individual habitats demand distinct representation.
- **Culture:** weather schedules, spinward language, neighborhood ecology, station rivalries, and intense local reactions to shared design.
- **Accent and register:** fast transit shorthand, station-specific tool vocabulary, common references to spin, level, spoke, and shift.
- **Architecture:** O'Neill cylinders, Bernal spheres, paired transfer and construction complexes, layered civic cores.
- **Festivals:** First Rain, Founding Triptych, Docking Week, artificial-season changes.
- **Local sayings:** "Weather is a vote with a maintenance schedule." "Same drawing, different city."

### Venus

- **Why it exists:** atmospheric chemistry, carbon and industrial feedstock recovery, high-atmosphere science, Solar energy, and trade position.
- **Why people stay:** aerostat-born families, shared-load culture, unique weather knowledge, political control of lift, and attachment to cloudscapes.
- **Why people leave:** corrosion illness, lift insecurity, imported water dependence, storm trauma, and employer control of docking.
- **Economy:** atmospheric extraction, chemical processing, solar energy, aerostat fabrication, research, and high-altitude transit.
- **Politics:** Venus Colonial Committee from 3160; public fights over reserve lift, mooring, water, altitude, and employer ownership.
- **Culture:** buoyancy is understood as shared obligation; private hoarding of reserve lift carries moral stigma.
- **Accent and register:** clauses shaped by altitude, drift, lift margin, corrosion, mooring, and shared load.
- **Architecture:** linked floating cities, replaceable lift cells, corrosion skins, suspended farms, layered emergency aerostats.
- **Festivals:** First Mooring, Clear Apex, Shared Lift Day, storm-name remembrances.
- **Local sayings:** "No one floats alone." "Every promise has a lift margin."

### Mercury

- **Why it exists:** high-temperature industry, Solar energy, metal refining, observatories, polar volatiles, and mass-driver access.
- **Why people stay:** technical autonomy, public shade politics, polar community, high-value skilled work, and identity built around managed heat.
- **Why people leave:** thermal injury, surveillance through heat records, limited space, family medical risk, and shade allocation conflict.
- **Economy:** metals, refining, Solar power, vacuum fabrication, observatory work, and mass-driver freight.
- **Politics:** Mercury Colonial Committee from 3180; shade, radiator capacity, cold reserve, polar water, and launch schedules are constitutional utilities.
- **Culture:** wasting cooling is condemned more strongly than wasting power; body access and grip design are public issues.
- **Accent and register:** numerically direct language using heat debt, shade-hours, rejection capacity, cold reserve, and window.
- **Architecture:** polar caverns, moving-shadow settlements, buried thermal buffers, long radiators, distributed solar shades.
- **Festivals:** Long Shade, First Cold Store, Radiator Night, Sunward Workers' Memorial.
- **Local sayings:** "Power is cheap. Cold is not." "Count the heat twice."

### The Asteroid Belt and Mega-Stations

- **Why it exists:** water, metals, volatiles, fuel supply, fabrication, research, and route control.
- **Why people stay:** mobile work, cooperative ownership, station family, distance from central rule, debt, and genuine frontier opportunity.
- **Why people leave:** claim violence, medical isolation, cartel or corporate coercion, route collapse, and inability to secure family services.
- **Economy:** mining, refining, salvage, fuel, rescue, mail, fabrication, smuggling, narcotics, protection, and insurance.
- **Politics:** no single Belt state; cooperative fleets, corporate claims, mafia utilities, cartels, gangs, pirates, Areopagus arbitration, and semi-independent mega-stations overlap.
- **Culture:** rescue duty and custody evidence matter; criminals can provide real services without becoming romantic liberators.
- **Accent and register:** vector, burn, mass, custody, claim, rescue, window, and arrival language; warmth often arrives through recorded messages.
- **Architecture:** spun habitats inside or beside rock, modular docks, family pressure districts, defensible utility cores.
- **Festivals:** Rescue Ledger Day, First Spin, Claim Burn, local station founding days.
- **Local sayings:** "Air first, argument after." "A claim without rescue is graffiti."

### Jupiter and Its Moons

- **Why it exists:** science, fusion-fuel economy, moon resources, deep-system transport, radiation engineering, and political expansion.
- **Why people stay:** moon identity, deep family roots, shielded civic life, local expertise, and distance from Earth.
- **Why people leave:** radiation burden, convoy delay, centralization by system authorities, and limited medical access.
- **Economy:** fuel, moon mining, research, shielding, ship service, and convoy control.
- **Politics:** Jovian institutions become an outer power but do not speak uniformly for every moon; central authority and moon jurisdiction remain contested.
- **Culture:** shielding depth and radiation weather shape class, architecture, school, and family planning.
- **Accent and register:** convoy sequence, shelter depth, exposure, moon jurisdiction, and storm language.
- **Architecture:** deeply shielded moon settlements, subsurface transit, rotating transfer habitats, layered refuge cores.
- **Festivals:** First Deep Shelter, Magnetosphere Night, individual moon charters.
- **Local sayings:** "Every roof has a number." "Jupiter is weather, not a flag."

### Saturn and Its Moons

- **Why it exists:** chemistry, hydrocarbons, moon settlement, research, convoy staging, and outer-system independence.
- **Why people stay:** distributed moon cultures, long-route family obligations, chemical industry, and political distance.
- **Why people leave:** route isolation, system-capital control, industrial exposure, and convoy family separation.
- **Economy:** chemistry, fuels and feedstocks, ship support, research, atmosphere and ring science.
- **Politics:** Saturn becomes a major outer power while its moons preserve separate jurisdictions and challenge claims of system unity.
- **Culture:** obligation is distributed across long intervals; a favor may be repaid months later by a different household.
- **Accent and register:** route interval, atmospheric weather, chemistry, convoy turn, and distributed obligation.
- **Architecture:** shielded moon habitats, atmosphere-processing platforms, convoy cylinders, ring research stations.
- **Festivals:** Ring Shadow, Convoy Return, Compact Convocation, moon-specific weather observances.
- **Local sayings:** "A late promise still needs a dock." "The route is part of the household."

### Uranus

- **Why it exists:** cryogenic engineering, containment research, remote science, and support for antimatter infrastructure.
- **Why people stay:** specialized work, local scientific identity, outer autonomy, and family ties to distributed facilities.
- **Why people leave:** distance, classification, low population, containment risk, and limited civic choice.
- **Economy:** cryogenics, containment materials, energy systems, deep-space service, and research.
- **Politics:** associated with the Outer Systems Compact while resisting reduction to Neptune's support service.
- **Culture:** maintenance exactness and dispersed-station cooperation; secrecy is treated as both safety and domination.
- **Accent and register:** containment margin, thermal state, stored field, and communication interval.
- **Architecture:** isolated shielded facilities, cryogenic yards, small rotating civic habitats.
- **Festivals:** First Stable Bottle, Long Inclination, maintenance oath days.
- **Local sayings:** "Cold keeps no secrets; people do." "Margin is time."

### Neptune and Triton

- **Why it exists:** remote high-energy infrastructure, antimatter manufacture, research, and the eventual interstellar mission.
- **Why people stay:** mission-scale work, outer political leverage, family settlements, and belief in opening the stars.
- **Why people leave:** containment danger, security rule, isolation, and the conversion of civilian work into military target.

- **Accent and register:** precise numerical speech; *fuel* is avoided when it conceals manufacture and risk.
- **Architecture:** remote accelerator arrays, separated containment volumes, Triton settlements, sacrificial uninhabited work zones.
- **Festivals:** First Containment, Outbound Flame, names of maintenance dead recited at every major transfer.
- **Local sayings:** "Antimatter is made, not found." "Distance is one of the walls."

- **Accent and register:** bearing, parallax, uncertainty, mass, delay, and archive language.
- **Architecture:** buried Pluto vaults, Charon construction yards, tethered logistics, heavily insulated civic volumes.
- **Festivals:** Watch Night, Joint Charter, Departure Day.

### Human-Moment Rule

The manuscript may call an event "first" only from a defined institutional or remembered perspective. Someone may always dispute an earlier, unrecorded first. That dispute strengthens the archive theme.

## Word and Scene Budget

The full target remains 110,000-130,000 manuscript words.

For forty narrative units and 240 scenes:

- average narrative unit: 2,750-3,250 words;
- average scene: 460-540 words;
- prologue and epilogue may run 1,800-2,500 words;
- major hinge chapters may run 3,500-4,500 words;
- connective chapters may run 2,200-2,800 words.

The six-scene plan is a rhythm, not a command to make every scene equal. A two-page conflict scene may be followed by a three-paragraph transition.

## Final Chapter Review

Before a chapter is marked complete, confirm:

- history changes inside the chapter;
- someone emotionally experiences that change;
- a recurring character is introduced, corrected, aged, or transformed;
- the chapter reveals a new civilizational practice;
- at least one image, decision, or exchange is memorable without summary;
- the ending changes a condition needed by the next chapter;
- a social winner and loser are visible;
- an ordinary person notices the transition through daily life;
- all six scene functions are present;
- hopeful, fearful, political, family, philosophical, and humorous dialogue appear naturally;
- food, clothing, transport, weather, architecture, technology, religion, entertainment, language, observance, and memorial are selected where relevant rather than listed;
- the primary POV makes a consequential decision;
- no Immortal, institution, species, colony, or faction receives automatic narrative vindication.
