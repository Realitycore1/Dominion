I. Physical Definition

An Anisotropic Star is a stellar-scale object whose energy output, magnetic fields, mass distribution, or spacetime curvature are significantly direction-dependent rather than uniform across its surface.
This asymmetry may arise from rotation, [[Exotic Matter]] distribution, gravitational gradients, or engineered stellar modifications.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Directional Radiation Emission — Luminosity varies greatly across surface [[Regions]], sometimes focused into beams.
	•	Gravitational Field Distortion — Uneven mass distribution causes measurable spacetime curvature differences along different axes.
	•	Magnetic Field Anisotropy — Field strength and orientation vary locally, producing complex plasma flows.
	•	Temperature Gradient Extremes — [[Regions]] of intense heat coexist with ultra-cool stellar zones.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Anisotropic [[Stars]] are classified as Directional Field Anchors, used in both [[Natural]] and engineered configurations to control energy projection and gravitational influence in targeted directions.
