# The Belt: law without one horizon

## The committee’s limited reach

The Belt Colonial Committee is best understood as the Colonial Union’s regulatory layer rather than a successful central government. It issues concessions, registers habitats, supports navigation standards, and recognizes claims. Areopagus courts arbitrate rescue, salvage, and ownership disputes.

Practical authority remains fragmented among mining cooperatives, corporate concessions, family stations, mafia houses, cartels, gangs, pirates, mobile fleets, and station councils. A committee decision may take months to reach a claim whose residents needed air yesterday.

The Belt has no single founding year in the current canon. Its committee regime develops during the Inner-System boom of 3050–3180 as fusion drives, fueling stations, O’Neill cylinders, and resource extraction turn scattered worksites into permanent societies.

## Resource economy

The Belt supplies water, metal, silicates, volatiles, fuel feedstock, repair capacity, and construction mass. Asteroid mining and comet mining become distinct cultures.

Asteroid crews work material stable enough to remain rock. Comet crews work bodies that vent, fracture, and change orbit under extraction. Comets provide water, organics, and volatile fuel unavailable in equal abundance from dry claims.

Mining fleets fight corporate fleets because ownership is inseparable from mobility. A corporation may hold a claim while a family fleet owns the only machines able to reach it. Rescue tugs may save a crew and then present a salvage contract after pressure is restored.

## Patchwork civilization

Belter makeshift suits become the frontier’s iconic clothing. A mining torso may connect to a newer rescue helmet, a military glove, a Pallas adapter, and joint rings machined in a shop that no longer exists. Patch colors record pressure tests, family hands, and accidents.

A factory-new suit has no history. Belters distrust completeness because nothing useful remains complete for long.

Meals follow the same principle. Families maintain named stew cultures fed by algae, mushrooms, imported grain, certified comet organics, and unofficial spices. The contents change at every port; the household continuity does not.

## Factions and shadow authority

No faction controls the Belt, but each controls something a family may need before law arrives:

- the Crystal Syndicate dominates rare-mineral contracts and unpriced material;
- the Jade Association coordinates traders, berth brokers, debt collection, and route information;
- the Blue Monkey Boys move packages through spaces adults cannot enter;
- the Metal Death Tribe works abandoned hulls beyond reliable rescue;
- the Magic Hills Cartel extracts protected or unprofitable claims outside official ledgers;
- the Disciplined Few purchases influence for rule by trained system managers;
- the Luciano, Calabria, and Campania families develop dynastic power through shipping, finance, construction, utilities, and protection.

## Semi-independent stations

Around 3230, Belt mega-stations obtain semi-independent charters. Their councils govern schools, air, rescue, and local courts. Corporations retain claims. Mafia houses retain utilities. Mobile fleets ignore borders that do not move with them.

At Vesta, a charter council takes oxygen billing from a mafia utility and discovers that legal control does not guarantee replacement filters. Semi-independence means the council chooses which dependency enters the public record.

The Belt never becomes one polity. At Harmonium it arrives as station delegations, mobile fleets, an Areopagus observer, and a Jade Association broker who was not invited.

## Wars and departure

The First Solar War includes mining fleets, corporate fleets, revolutionaries, pirates, cartels, and Union forces fighting across shared fuel and rescue infrastructure. The armistice preserves Belt autonomy without recognizing one revolutionary government.

The Second Solar War again turns fueling neutrality into a survival decision. Stations reserve fuel for life support and watch armed factions take it in the name of preventing one another’s use.

By 3600, Belt knowledge is essential to the *Deliverance*: incompatible parts must share air, repairs must outlive their makers, and law must function when no single authority owns the route. Belt colonists carry those practices to the ship—and, after 3608, into Craterheart.
