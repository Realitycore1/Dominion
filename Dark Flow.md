A claimed, coherent bulk motion of galaxy clusters on very large scales (hundreds of Mpc) moving in roughly the same direction relative to the cosmic microwave background (CMB) rest frame—i.e., a flow beyond what standard structure growth predicts.

How it was inferred.
Analyses of the kinematic Sunyaev–Zel’dovich (kSZ) signal in CMB maps (initially WMAP), stacked at the [[Dominion Omniverse/Dominion Multiverse/Eos Verse/Dominion Verse/Verse Structure/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/Economy & Infrastructure/Locations/Locations|Locations]] of large cluster catalogs, reported a dipole pattern consistent with a large-scale flow.

Why it’s controversial.
	•	Systematics-sensitive: kSZ is tiny; results depend on filtering, masks, cluster selection, and foreground treatment.
	•	Direction/scale tension: the reported flow seemed to persist to very large [[Depths]] where ΛCDM expects flows to average out.
	•	Independent checks: later studies (including Planck kSZ and velocity reconstructions) generally do not confirm a significant dark flow and place tight upper limits on any all-sky coherent motion.

Current status (consensus).
	•	Local bulk flows (tens–few hundred km/s) on ~50–100 Mpc scales are expected and observed.
	•	A single, universal “dark flow” extending to ≳300–500 Mpc is not required by current data and is likely absent within present sensitivities.

If it were real, implications.
Would challenge statistical isotropy of ΛCDM and hint at superhorizon gravitational potentials (e.g., pre-inflationary structure, multiverse-like effects).

What would settle it.
Deeper, cleaner kSZ measurements with next-gen CMB surveys, better cluster catalogs and peculiar-velocity maps, and joint CMB–lensing/galaxy cross-analyses to disentangle true flows from systematics.
