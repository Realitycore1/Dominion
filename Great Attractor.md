What it is: A massive gravitational overdensity in the local Universe that contributes to the peculiar motion of the Milky Way and nearby galaxies (part of the larger [[Laniakea]] Supercluster flow).
	•	Where: In the Norma–[[Hydra]]–Centaurus region, largely hidden by the Milky Way’s “Zone of Avoidance.” The core is near the Norma Cluster (Abell 3627).
	•	Distance/scale: Roughly 150–250 million light-years away; mass on the order of 10¹⁵–10¹⁶ M☉ spread across clusters, filaments, and gas (not a single object).
	•	How we infer it:
	•	Peculiar velocities of galaxies (bulk flow toward Centaurus/Norma).
	•	Redshift surveys in radio/IR/[[X-Ray]] that pierce Galactic dust and reveal hidden clusters and filaments.
	•	Modern picture: The local flow is pulled toward this region but continues onward toward the even more massive [[Shapley Supercluster]] farther out; simultaneously, large nearby voids/repellers “push” from behind. So the “Great Attractor” is best seen as a waypoint in a push–pull cosmic flow, not a single dominant object.
	•	Why it matters: It anchors our understanding of local large-scale structure, helps explain the ~600 km/s CMB dipole motion of the Local Group, and illustrates how overdensities and voids jointly shape galaxy flows.
