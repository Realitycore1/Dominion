Formal Species Descriptor

Astrocephalopoda beltica
(asteroid-belt dwelling cephaliforms)

⸻

II. Why the Name “Cutholoid” Exists

“Cutholoid” is a cultural misnomer, coined by early spacers and sensationalist media.
	•	Coined after first human visual encounters
	•	Reinforced by:
	•	Tentacular forms
	•	Non-Euclidean movement
	•	Silent observation behavior
	•	Dominion xenologists hate the name
	•	It persists anyway

“The public calls them Cutholoids. We call them a mistake in nomenclature.”
— Dominion Xenology Archive, Vol. 44

⸻

III. Habitat & Ecology

Native Environment
	•	Dense asteroid belts
	•	Trojan clusters
	•	Debris disks
	•	Post-collision planetary rings

They thrive where:
	•	Gravity is weak
	•	Cover is abundant
	•	Motion is chaotic

They do not live on planets.
They do not build cities.

They inhabit structure itself.

⸻

IV. Biology & Physiology

Cutholoids are vacuum-adapted macrofauna.

Key traits:
	•	Semi-gelatinous body with mineralized nodules
	•	Internal pressure maintained via exotic biopolymers
	•	Radiotrophic and electrosensory feeding
	•	Can anchor to asteroids for decades without moving

Tentacles are:
	•	Manipulative
	•	Sensory
	•	Structural
—not weapons by default

⸻

V. Intelligence (This Is the Important Part)

Cutholoids are situationally intelligent.
	•	Comparable to:
	•	Octopuses
	•	Corvids
	•	Tool-using primates
	•	Intelligence spikes when:
	•	Observing artificial objects
	•	Exposed to EM emissions
	•	Near active traffic corridors

They do not have:
	•	Language as humans define it
	•	Civilization
	•	Long-term cultural memory

They do have:
	•	Curiosity
	•	Pattern recognition
	•	Individual variance

This makes them unsettling—but not hostile.

⸻

VI. Behavior Toward Humanity

Observed behaviors:
	•	Shadowing ships at a distance
	•	Manipulating debris near stations
	•	Mimicking ship silhouettes using scrap
	•	Abandoning regions after heavy militarization

No confirmed attacks.
Many unexplained disappearances.

Dominion position:

“Correlation does not imply predation.”

Crews disagree.
