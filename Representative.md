Official Representative

High Convenor of the Pacific Compact

Function:
	•	Primary diplomatic voice to external blocs (UAR, Europolis, CRINK)
	•	Signs communiqués and ceasefire frameworks
	•	Delivers unified statements during crises
	•	Has no unilateral authority over Compact forces

Term:
	•	2-year rotation
	•	Cannot be held by the same nation consecutively
	•	Automatically removed during national-level crisis in their home country

This prevents capture, cults of personality, and assassination leverage.

⸻

Current (2041) High Convenor

Prime Minister Keiko Tanaka (Japan) (fictional but realistic)

Why Japan holds it now:
	•	Japan provides the strategic backbone of the Compact
	•	Viewed as disciplined, restrained, and predictable
	•	Least likely to escalate recklessly
	•	Trusted by Southeast Asian members more than Australia or South Korea in 2041

⸻

Profile: Keiko Tanaka

Position:
	•	Prime Minister of Japan
	•	High Convenor of the Pacific Compact

Background:
	•	Former Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI)
	•	Led post-supply-chain-collapse reforms
	•	Architect of Japan’s “defensive industrialization” policy
	•	Fluent in English, Mandarin, and Bahasa Indonesia

Psych Profile:
	•	Low affect, high clarity
	•	Avoids rhetoric, favors phrasing that limits interpretation
	•	Treats diplomacy as risk containment, not persuasion
	•	Believes alliances fail when they pretend to be friendships

Signature Line (often quoted):

“We do not threaten. We inform.”

⸻

How Power Actually Works Behind Her

Tanaka speaks—but she does not decide alone.

Behind her is a four-node consensus engine:
	1.	Japan – Strategic doctrine, naval posture
	2.	Australia – Logistics, basing, long-range ISR
	3.	South Korea – Cyber, missile defense, AI command systems
	4.	Indonesia – Political legitimacy, geographic continuity, Global South bridge

If any one of these withdraws confidence, the Convenor’s authority collapses immediately.

That fragility is intentional.

⸻

External Perception
