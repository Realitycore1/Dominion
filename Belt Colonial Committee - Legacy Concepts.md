I. The Committee Years (2350–2400)

The Belt Colonial Committee (BCC) was created by the [[Colonial Union]] to regulate asteroid mining concessions and habitat construction in the Main Belt ([[Ceres]], Vesta, Pallas, Hygiea, and beyond).

In practice, the BCC was little more than a charter office for megacorporations. Colonists — miners, technicians, and families — were treated as a transient labor force, moved between rocks as quotas demanded.
	•	[[Ceres]] Base: administrative capital, controlled by the BCC.
	•	Vesta Yards: primary metal and isotope smelters.
	•	Pallas [[Relays]]: communications and navigation hubs.
	•	Scattered habs: small family or guild-run outposts, many barely acknowledged by the BCC.

Resentment grew quickly: unlike Mars or Venus, there was no planet to cling to, only endless void, and no Committee presence could reach everywhere.

II. The Age of Habitats (2400–2450)
	•	O’Neill Cylinders & Bernal Spheres were constructed in stable orbits around major asteroids.
	•	Families, guilds, and mining crews claimed autonomy inside their stations. Inside each hab, the Committee’s rules mattered less than the will of its [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Dominion/Government/Royal Branch/Leaders/Leaders|Leaders]].
	•	[[culture]] fractured:
	•	Some habs developed strict communal codes (share oxygen, share food).
	•	Others became mercantile hubs, trading metals, volatiles, and ice.
	•	Still others descended into lawless company towns, ruled by foremen and armed crews.

The Belt became less a frontier of [[II - Companion Narratives/Syl'Cothan Dominion/3 - Colonial Union/Colonies|Colonies]] and more a patchwork archipelago of drifting worlds.

⸻

III. The [[Ceres]] [[Schism]] (2450–2480)

Tension peaked when the BCC attempted to raise oxygen tariffs on independent habs, forcing them to buy Committee-certified O₂ rather than producing their own.

This triggered the [[Ceres]] [[Schism]]: a revolt of habitats across the Belt.
	•	[[Ceres]] Base was blockaded by guild fleets armed with converted mining lasers.
	•	Independent habs refused BCC inspectors.
	•	The Free Guilds of the Belt declared they would no longer submit quotas or taxes.

The BCC collapsed within decades, its reach too thin to enforce authority across millions of kilometers.

⸻

IV. The Belt Kindreds (2480–2520)

From the ashes of the Committee arose the Kindreds of the Belt — loosely allied communities bound by trade and mutual defense pacts.
	•	Guild [[culture]]: Work is life; oxygen, water, and spin-gravity are sacred commodities.
	•	[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Identity|Identity]]: Each hab or guild keeps its own customs, but all share the belief: “No one rules [[The Void]].”
	•	Symbols: Rope knots, [[Chains]], and ring motifs symbolize connection between drifting worlds.
	•	Language: A creole of [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] tongues blended with engineering jargon and hand-sign codes for zero-G.

Where Martians became the Red Clan and Venusians the Cloudborn, Belters became the Chainfolk — people who lived in scattered links, tied together only by necessity.

⸻

V. Toward the Belt Confederacy (2520+)

By the mid-26th century, the Belt was no longer a frontier but a civilization of its own:
	•	O’Neill Cylinders housed millions.
	•	[[Ceres]], Vesta, and Pallas became great cities, but none ruled the whole.
	•	The Belt Confederacy emerged as a rotating congress of guilds and stations, its authority symbolic rather than absolute.

The Chainfolk knew unity could never be forced — but they also knew survival depended on mutual recognition. The Confederacy embodied that paradox: divided in form, bound in survival.
