# (3120) Belt Colonies

Permanent Belt colonies receive Colonial Union recognition in 3120.

The marker does not create one asteroid colony or unified Belt government. It recognizes a dispersed frontier of spun habitats, excavated pressure compartments, mobile processing trains, family stations, tug crews, prospecting swarms, and extraction claims.

## Resource Economy

Belt extraction focuses on:

- water ice for drinking, agriculture, shielding, oxygen, hydrogen, and reaction mass;
- ammonia and carbon compounds for agriculture and industry;
- nickel-iron, cobalt, and platinum-group metals;
- silicates for glass, ceramics, shielding, and construction mass.

Water is usually more politically important than precious metal.

## Engineering

The colonies rely on:

- spun habitats attached to or excavated inside small bodies;
- sintered-regolith and water radiation shielding;
- autonomous prospectors and remotely supervised mining systems;
- solar-electric and fusion-electric cargo tugs;
- low-velocity mass drivers sending standardized packets toward collection orbits;
- ice vaults serving as treasury, propellant reserve, and public life-support stock.

## Frontier Government

Each charter habitat maintains its own elected council. Areopagus Station provides the common claim ledger, rescue registry, arbitration court, and inspection exchange.

The Belt is a frontier because distance and mobility weaken timely enforcement, not because law is absent. Rescue customs, salvage rules, postal evidence, labor contracts, and witnessed cargo transfers often govern months before a Union ruling arrives.

The Belt supplies volatiles and mineral feedstock to Venus, Mercury, Mars, Luna, and the orbital stations. In return it depends on complex machinery, biological stocks, medicine, manufactured electronics, and access to distant courts and markets.

## Asymmetric Development

The Belt does not develop uniformly.

It contains:

- wealthy mega-stations and impoverished claim camps;
- cooperative mining fleets;
- corporate concessions tying housing and medicine to employment debt;
- family syndicates and mafia houses controlling docks, arbitration, smuggling, and protection;
- drug cartels producing legitimate medicine, counterfeit drugs, shift stimulants, and dependency-forming neurochemistry;
- station gangs controlling individual spin sections, maintenance corridors, or ration markets;
- pirate crews raiding depots, spoofing rescue calls, and selling captured cargo through false registries.

Some criminal groups provide services absent from delayed Union government while coercing the same residents who depend on them.

Mining fleets arm in response to corporate claim-enforcement fleets. Tug defenses, mining lasers, cargo drones, and armored refinery sections become the foundation of Belt combat forces in the First Solar War.
