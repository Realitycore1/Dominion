🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: Knife of the Final Meal
Discovery Location: Jerusalem, Mount Zion excavation, 2038, beneath foundations of the Cenacle (traditional site of the Upper Room).
Summary:

Material: Iron blade, ~17 cm length, bronze handle with faint inlay. Corrosion consistent with 1st-century Judean metallurgy.

Tests:

Radiocarbon of wooden sheath fragments → ~30 CE ± 15 years.

Residue analysis: Traces of wine tannins, olive oil, fish protein.

Microanalysis reveals blood residue, mitochondrial DNA fragment consistent with Levantine population.

Inscriptions: Faint Greek scratching on bronze handle — “εὐχαριστία” (eucharistia, “thanksgiving”).

Archaeological Significance: No definitive proof it was at the Last Supper, but strong contextual evidence places it in the correct [[Time]], location, and cultural setting.

🛡 Intelligence / Security Brief

Relic Code: RELIC-44 (Knife of the Final Meal)
Classification: HIGH SYMBOLIC IMPACT
Custody: Vatican seized artifact immediately after discovery; public narrative controlled, limited to clerical circles until authentication confirmed.
Strategic Assessment:

Vatican: Frames the Knife as an intimate relic of Christ’s table, reinforcing sacramental theology. Links to Eucharist deepen its power — “the hand that broke bread held this knife.”

[[Europolis]]: Pushes for joint custody, citing pan-European Christian heritage. Vatican resists.

Caliphate: Propaganda ridicules it as “a servant’s knife,” mocking Christian obsession with trivial utensils. But simultaneously worried — fears relic mania spreading.
