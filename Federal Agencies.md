# Federal Agencies

## Summary

Federal agencies are the working machinery of the Dominion's present-facing government.

They are not royal ministries. They do not exist to preserve Amber's authority, conduct sacred rites, or make final continuity judgments. They exist to keep ordinary civilization functioning: schools, courts, health systems, transit, public works, resource distribution, scientific standards, civil defense, and federal administration.

The Federal Branch asks: What do citizens need today, this year, and this generation?

The Royal Branch asks: What must survive centuries, catastrophes, and existential war?

Federal agencies are legitimate only when they remain reviewable, budgeted, appealable, and accountable to law.

## Constitutional Position

Federal agencies derive power from:

- federal statute;
- legislative appropriations;
- court-recognized mandates;
- provincial and state compacts;
- public-service charters;
- emergency authorizations with sunset clauses.

They may coordinate with Royal ministries during continuity emergencies, but coordination is not absorption. A Federal agency cannot become a Royal ministry by invoking survival language.

## Oversight Stack

Federal agencies answer to a layered review system:

- authorizing statute;
- public budget record;
- legislative committee oversight;
- inspector-general audits;
- court review;
- citizen complaint process;
- provincial compact review;
- archival preservation of controversial orders;
- emergency-power sunset review.

The [[Federal Agency Oversight Board]] coordinates cross-agency audits when multiple agencies claim overlapping authority.

## Relationship to Royal Government

Federal agencies may receive Royal continuity directives only when the matter exceeds ordinary civic scope.

Examples:

- mass evacuation;
- existential military mobilization;
- VFF or MOT contamination;
- species-level food failure;
- hard-contact alien threat;
- civilizational archive breach;
- forbidden-technology cascade.

Even then, Federal agencies keep their records. The Royal order may be urgent, but it is not allowed to erase the paper trail.

## Citizen Experience

Most citizens encounter the Dominion through Federal agencies long before they encounter the Throne.

A citizen may know the Dominion as:

- the school that tests their service aptitude;
- the clinic that keeps their body functional across centuries;
- the registry office that records family structure and franchise status;
- the transit authority that moves them between arcology districts;
- the labor board that verifies their work contract;
- the emergency team that arrives after a habitat breach;
- the environmental office that forbids settlement in a poisoned zone;
- the courts office that lets them challenge a decision.

This is why Federal agencies matter narratively. They make the Dominion feel inhabited.

## Failure Modes

Federal agencies become dangerous when they:

- use continuity language to avoid oversight;
- hide ordinary policy behind classified security claims;
- treat poor citizens as logistics problems;
- let service status become caste;
- allow automated systems to make unappealable decisions;
- confuse cultural integration with cultural erasure;
- defer every hard moral question to the Royal Government.

## Story Use

Use Federal agencies to show how the Dominion actually works at street level.

They are where citizens wait in lines, file appeals, argue with clerks, receive benefits, lose licenses, get medical waivers, earn service status, challenge policy, and discover that a civilization can be both protective and exhausting.

Federal agencies should create bureaucracy, relief, friction, compromise, procedural justice, corruption risks, and quiet competence.

They should not all feel sinister. A civilization this large survives because many ordinary offices do their jobs.
