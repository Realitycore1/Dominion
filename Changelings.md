Classification

Impostor Sentients / Adaptive Mimics / Identity Parasites

Core Idea

Changelings are beings that survive by replacement. They do not merely disguise themselves—they occupy roles. A changeling doesn’t steal a face for convenience; it steals a place in the social fabric.

They are not illusionists.
They are substitutions.

⸻

Origin

Changelings arise where:
	•	families fracture
	•	communities lose members quietly
	•	records are incomplete
	•	grief is normalized

They are born from gaps—when someone disappears and the world chooses not to look too closely.

Some are:
	•	fae-descended
	•	curse-born
	•	artificially induced (ritual or biotech mimicry)

All share the same instinct: fit in or vanish.

⸻

Physical Manifestation

In true form, Changelings are unsettlingly unfinished.

Common traits:
	•	Grey or pallid skin with wax-like texture
	•	Limbs proportioned almost correctly
	•	Eyes too reflective or unfocused
	•	Facial features that look right individually, wrong together
	•	Veins or markings that shift subtly under the skin

In mimicked form, they are nearly perfect—except under stress.

Tells include:
	•	delayed emotional responses
	•	inconsistent memories
	•	wrong habits at the margins
	•	reflections that lag or distort

⸻

Behavior & Temperament

Changelings are adaptive, cautious, and quietly predatory.
	•	Prefer domestic or communal environments
	•	Avoid direct conflict
	•	Maintain cover at all costs
	•	Will abandon identities if threatened

They do not usually kill replacements.
They outlast them.

⸻

Intelligence

High social intelligence.
	•	Exceptional at pattern recognition
	•	Learn speech, gestures, routines rapidly
	•	Weak at improvisation outside learned context
	•	Fear exposure more than death

Older Changelings may juggle multiple identities across years.

⸻

Metaphysical Traits
	•	Identity absorption: prolonged proximity allows deeper mimicry
	•	Grace-unstable: angelic presence causes identity slippage
	•	Malice-reactive: corruption can lock them into monstrous forms
	•	Memory echo: victims’ habits linger after replacement

True names and mirrors are disruptive.

⸻

Ecology
	•	Feed on stability, routine, and emotional continuity
	•	Reproduce rarely, often through ritual exchange or splitting
	•	Die if isolated too long
	•	Thrive in bureaucratic or urban systems

Removing a Changeling can collapse fragile social structures.

⸻

Threat Assessment

Moderate individually / High socially

One Changeling is survivable.
A network can replace a city quietly.

⸻

Narrative Role

Changelings represent:
	•	fear of replacement
	•	erosion of identity
	•	communities lying to themselves
	•	survival through conformity

Where Boggarts ruin homes,
Changelings become them.
