Core Idea

Loa are middle powers—not distant gods, not bound spirits. They are active presences who walk between worlds, answering calls, making bargains, enforcing balance through personality rather than law.

They don’t demand blind worship.
They demand relationship.

⸻

Origin

Loa arise from convergence:
	•	ancestral memory
	•	repeated ritual
	•	cultural continuity
	•	liminal spaces (crossroads, cemeteries, thresholds)

They are born where communities persist long enough for identity, death, humor, fear, and survival to intertwine.

Unlike Kami (place-first), Loa are people-first.

⸻

Manifestation

Loa rarely appear fully physical unless invited.

Common manifestations:
	•	shadowed humanoid forms
	•	skeletal or mask-faced aspects
	•	symbolic attire (top hats, veils, beads, canes, cigars)
	•	mirrored eyes or eyes glowing with sigils
	•	flickering outlines, like reality is breathing around them

They often ride mortals via possession, speaking through them with altered voice and posture.

⸻

Behavior & Temperament

Loa are intensely individual.

Some are:
	•	playful
	•	cruel
	•	compassionate
	•	transactional
	•	protective
	•	utterly merciless

All are consistent to themselves.

They reward respect.
They punish disrespect.
They loathe mockery.

⸻

Intelligence

Extremely high social intelligence.
	•	Understand human weakness intimately
	•	Speak in riddles, jokes, threats, bargains
	•	Remember debts for generations
	•	Capable of long-term manipulation or guardianship

They are not abstract thinkers—they are streetwise metaphysicians.

⸻

Metaphysical Traits
	•	Faith-responsive, but not faith-dependent
	•	Grace-adjacent, but not angel-aligned
	•	Malice-compatible, but hostile to domination
	•	Can heal, curse, protect, or ruin lives
	•	Power scales with recognition, ritual accuracy, and sincerity

They weaken when forgotten—but become dangerous when insulted.

⸻

Possession (“Riding”)

One of the Loa’s defining traits.

During possession:
	•	Host gains Loa’s voice, mannerisms, and authority
	•	Physical limits may be temporarily overridden
	•	Host often survives—but with lasting psychological or metaphysical scars

Possession is not violence.
It is consensual surrender… or punishment.

⸻

Loa vs Other Powers
	•	Angels: distrust Loa’s informality and emotional methods
	•	Demons: attempt to enslave Loa—rarely succeed
	•	Kami: parallel role, different focus (people vs place)
	•	Tulpa: artificial and fragile by comparison
	•	Rakshasa: ideological enemies—lies vs bargains

Loa sit exactly where order breaks down.

⸻

Threat Assessment

Variable / Contextual

A single Loa can be:
	•	a protector of a neighborhood
	•	the collapse of a bloodline
	•	the unseen ruler of a black market district

They scale horizontally, not vertically.

⸻

Narrative Role

Loa embody:
	•	survival through community
	•	power that knows your name
	•	gods who laugh, smoke, dance, and kill
	•	divinity that never forgets a debt

They are proof that the sacred does not need to be distant
