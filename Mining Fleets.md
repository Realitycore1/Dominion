Industrial nomads of the belt

Mining fleets exist to turn dead rock into movement.

They are not colonies.
They are not communities.
They are machines with crews.

⸻

1. What a Mining Fleet Is

A mining fleet is a mobile industrial system, usually composed of:
	•	1–3 primary extraction vessels
	•	multiple autonomous mining drones
	•	mobile refineries or crushers
	•	bulk cargo haulers
	•	habitat barges for crew rotation
	•	escort or security craft (minimal, not military)

They move constantly, chasing:
	•	resource density
	•	contract demand
	•	price fluctuations
	•	political stability

If a belt body stops paying, the fleet leaves.

⸻

2. Organizational Structure

Mining fleets are hierarchical but practical:
	•	Fleet Director (contract and routing authority)
	•	Operations Leads (extraction, processing, logistics)
	•	Engineering Cadre
	•	Drone Controllers
	•	Temporary Labor Crews

Loyalty is contractual, not cultural.

Most personnel rotate in and out over years, not decades.

⸻

3. Life Aboard a Mining Fleet

Life is:
	•	repetitive
	•	dangerous
	•	highly regulated
	•	psychologically flat

Crew culture emphasizes:
	•	routine
	•	redundancy
	•	safety protocols
	•	emotional containment

Mining fleets produce excellent technicians and terrible philosophers.

They are the working class of space.

⸻

4. Vulnerability and Power

Mining fleets are vulnerable because:
	•	they are predictable
	•	they carry bulk material
	•	they broadcast activity
	•	they operate far from major stations

They are powerful because:
	•	they supply everything
	•	disruption costs fortunes
	•	killing them destabilizes markets

This makes them prime targets for:
	•	cartel taxation
	•	syndicate contracts
	•	mafia labor capture
	•	pirate ransom (crew, not cargo)

⸻

5. Relationship to Belt Crime

Mining fleets:
	•	quietly pay cartels
	•	occasionally hire syndicates
	•	tolerate mafia intermediaries
	•	avoid zealot territory
	•	disappear when routes get hot

They are not naïve.

They know the belt is not neutral.
