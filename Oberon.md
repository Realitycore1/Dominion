Oberon orbits farther out and feels significantly more remote—even within Uranian space.

If Titania is the anchor, Oberon is the edge of the map.

⸻

Why Oberon Exists at All

Oberon is chosen not for comfort, but for distance.

Its value lies in:
	•	isolation
	•	darkness
	•	low traffic
	•	minimal oversight

This makes Oberon perfect for:
	•	deep-system observatories
	•	physics experiments
	•	long-baseline sensing
	•	signal interception
	•	black-site research
	•	cryogenic archives

Oberon is where you put things that must not be disturbed.

⸻

Oberon Facilities

There is no “city” on Oberon.

There are installations.

Typical Oberon Site Features
	•	small, deeply buried habitats
	•	autonomous operation
	•	skeleton crews
	•	high redundancy
	•	extreme environmental hardening

Some Oberon facilities operate with:
	•	crews rotating every decade
	•	no civilian presence
	•	restricted comm windows

If Titania feels quiet, Oberon feels forgotten on purpose.

⸻

Cultural Profile

Oberon personnel are:
	•	psychologically screened for isolation tolerance
	•	deeply specialized
	•	often semi-anonymous
	•	long-term planners

Many never return to inner-system life.

Oberon creates people who think in:
	•	centuries
	•	datasets
	•	probabilities
	•	failure envelopes

They don’t gossip.
They model outcomes.

⸻

Cold-Environment Suits: Uranian Standard

Both moons require extreme cold survival systems, more severe than Titan but less complex than Europa pressure suits.

Uranian Cold Suit Traits
	•	multi-layer aerogel insulation
	•	active heat recycling
	•	rigid outer shells for micrometeoroid protection
	•	limited mobility (work is slow, deliberate)
	•	tethered operation standard

Exposure times are short by design.

Movement is planned.
Mistakes are unforgiving.
