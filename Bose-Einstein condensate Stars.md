I. Physical Definition

A Bose–Einstein Condensate Star is a stellar-scale object whose core (or in extreme cases, the entire star) exists in a Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) state, where constituent bosonic [[Particles]] occupy a single quantum ground state.
This allows the star to behave as a single macroscopic quantum entity, with coherence spanning the full stellar structure.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Near-Zero Internal Temperature — Achieved through extreme radiative cooling or engineered heat extraction, even at stellar masses.
	•	Perfect Quantum Coherence — The star’s matter behaves as one unified quantum wavefunction.
	•	Exotic Transparency — May transmit certain wavelengths of light without scattering due to quantum uniformity.
	•	Ultra-Stable Structure — Minimal internal turbulence or chaotic motion; density and shape remain constant over vast timescales.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), BEC [[Stars]] are categorized as Quantum-State Anchors, serving as ultra-stable, high-coherence points in the [[Verseon]] lattice.
