## Summary

GalFed art and architecture transform trauma into order. Public space is designed to feel like recovery: calm light, open archives, ring motifs, soft acoustics, and visible systems of care.

## Details

Architectural traits:

- Circular plazas evoking containment of the Singularity wound.
- Seven-part symmetry honoring the founding Accord.
- White archive stone, blue transit glass, gold civic inlays, and black memorial voids.
- Hospitals and courts built with similar layouts to imply law as medicine.
- Transit halls shaped like sanctuaries.
- Public memory gardens containing approved fragments of lost civilizations.
- Quiet chambers for citizens overwhelmed by noetic density.

Art forms:

- Harmonic sculptures that change tone as crowds move through them.
- Light-record murals preserving species histories.
- Silence operas performed with long pauses and subsonic field pulses.
- Restoration masks worn by recovered persons during Name Return ceremonies.
- Archive quilts woven from translated scripts of extinct cultures.
- Public grief engines that recite names of unrecovered worlds.

GalFed art is often beautiful and sincere. It also teaches citizens what to feel: gratitude, caution, reverence for memory, and fear of unmanaged freedom.
