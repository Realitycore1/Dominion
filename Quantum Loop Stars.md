I. Physical Definition

A Quantum Loop Star is a stellar-scale object whose spacetime curvature is so extreme and precisely configured that it forms one or more closed timelike curves (CTCs) within or around the star.
Quantum effects stabilize these loops, preventing paradox collapse and allowing matter, energy, and information to circulate through [[Time]] as part of the star’s normal operation.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Closed Timelike Structure — [[Particles]] and photons can loop through [[Time]] and re-enter earlier states.
	•	Quantum Consistency Enforcement — Loop stability maintained via quantum decoherence control, preventing paradoxes.
	•	Self-Fueling Temporal Feedback — Past and future versions of the star exchange matter and energy.
	•	Causality Gradient Zones — Different [[Regions]] experience [[Time]] at different rates or directions.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Quantum Loop [[Stars]] are categorized as Temporal Anchors, serving as points where [[Time]] can be bent, recycled, or redirected without universal destabilization.
