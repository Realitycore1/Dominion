🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: The Iron Crown of Lombardy
Discovery Location: Cathedral of Monza, Italy — long known, but subjected to renewed study and reauthentication in 2037 during Crusade fervor.
Summary:

Structure: Gold circlet with enamel and gemstones, ~24 cm in diameter.

Central Feature: Thin iron band inset within the crown’s core, traditionally claimed to be forged from a crucifixion nail of Christ.

Tests Conducted:

Metallurgical analysis confirms iron is Roman-era, 1st–3rd century CE, containing trace impurities consistent with nails found in Judean excavation sites.

Radiocarbon on organic residue yields inconclusive results (contaminated by centuries of handling).

Enamel and gemstones confirmed to be early medieval additions (~6th–9th century).

Archaeological Significance: While absolute proof is elusive, evidence strongly supports the iron band’s antiquity and plausibility as a relic linked to the crucifixion.

Strategic Assessment:

Vatican: Promotes the Iron Crown as a divine sign that Christendom is once more united under the Cross. Used in ceremonies blessing Crusader brigades; echoes coronation rituals of emperors past.

[[Europolis]]: Competes for symbolic ownership, claiming the Crown is “heritage of Europe,” not just Rome. Nationalist parties demand it symbolize a pan-European empire reborn.

Caliphate: Frames it as idolatrous — “a rusted nail wrapped in gold.” Uses propaganda to depict [[Crusaders]] as worshipers of blood and empire.
