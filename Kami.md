Core Idea

Kami are immanent gods—not creators of reality, but expressions of it. A mountain that remembers every footstep. A river that judges pollution. A battlefield that learned rage. Kami don’t rule the world; they inhabit it.

They are not angels. They are not demons.
They are presence with awareness.

⸻

Origin

Kami emerge where meaning accumulates:
	•	ancient forests
	•	rivers used for centuries
	•	ancestral shrines
	•	old roads and crossroads
	•	sites of tragedy or devotion

They are born from:
	•	long-term reverence
	•	ritual repetition
	•	ecological stability
	•	emotional imprint

A place becomes important → the importance becomes aware.

⸻

Physical Manifestation

Kami are selectively physical.

Common forms:
	•	humanoid figures formed of mist, bark, stone, or light
	•	animal aspects (wolves, serpents, cranes, foxes)
	•	abstract silhouettes outlined by wind, rain, leaves, or embers
	•	partially visible shapes seen only at certain angles or times

They are never fully solid unless they choose to be.

Details sharpen when:
	•	rituals are observed
	•	offerings are made
	•	the land is respected

They fade when ignored.

⸻

Behavior & Temperament

Kami are not moral in a human sense, but they are consistent.
	•	Benevolent if respected
	•	Indifferent if ignored
	•	Wrathful if violated

They do not forgive quickly.
They do not forget ever.

Kami care about:
	•	balance
	•	continuity
	•	ritual correctness
	•	respect for boundaries

They rarely leave their domain.

⸻

Intelligence

High, but contextual.

A forest kami understands:
	•	seasons
	•	hunting balance
	•	disease spread
	•	intrusion patterns

A shrine kami understands:
	•	lineage
	•	oaths
	•	sincerity vs performance
	•	truth of intent

They are not philosophers.
They are situationally omniscient within their domain.

⸻

Metaphysical Traits
	•	Faith-reactive but not faith-dependent
	•	Grace-compatible, but not subordinate to angelic law
	•	Malice-intolerant—corruption poisons their domain directly
	•	Can sanctify land or curse it
	•	Can empower mortals temporarily through pact or rite

When a kami dies, its domain collapses or becomes hostile.

⸻

Kami vs Other Beings
	•	Angels: see kami as inefficient but necessary stabilizers
	•	Demons: hunt or corrupt kami to poison regions
	•	Tulpa: unstable, artificial echoes by comparison
	•	Okami: often considered high kami or war-kami
	•	Beast Rot: deeply offensive to kami; triggers violent response

Kami are the immune system of the world.

⸻

Threat Assessment

Low individually / High regionally

A single kami is manageable.
Angering one can doom an entire valley, city quarter, or bloodline.

⸻

Narrative Role

Kami represent:
	•	the soul of places
	•	continuity without central authority
	•	tradition as a living force
	•	nature remembering everything humanity forgets

Where gods demand worship,
kami demand respect
