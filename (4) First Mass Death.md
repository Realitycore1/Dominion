# (4) First Mass Death

## Manuscript Source

- Primary source: [[Manuscript]] Draft 2, Part III, Chapter 13.
- Manuscript date: Year 0 After Landfall.

## What Happened

The first major death crisis began when surveyors moved beyond safe perimeter assumptions and one of them, Elian, was killed by Harmina's environment. The first funeral revealed that even burial could not follow old customs because the soil was chemically active and microbially hostile.

Soon after, storm pressure, wall failure, floodwater, contaminated mud, and structural uncertainty turned New Aurora's first year into a test of whether the settlement could survive a planet that did not attack but also did not accommodate.

## What Resulted

- New Aurora learned that grief required new procedures.
- The first memorial practices combined Ark salvage, cloth strips, perimeter markers, and names tied to work sites.
- Amber's leadership shifted away from symbolic reassurance and toward practical adaptation.
- The settlement began moving critical functions underground.

## Consequences

The deaths taught New Aurora that Harmina's hazards were not moral or hostile. They were environmental. That difference shaped later Harminan culture: risk became something to study, mark, and teach, not merely fear.
