🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: The Picatrix (Ghāyat al-Ḥakīm) — Expanded Manuscript
Discovery Location: Fez, Morocco, 2037, concealed in the foundation vault of a Marinid-era madrasa, uncovered during urban restoration.
Summary:

Material: Parchment codex bound in tooled leather, ~280 folios.

Dating: Radiocarbon analysis of parchment places [[Origin]] ~11th century CE. Ink chemical analysis matches Andalusian/Maghrebi [[Production]] techniques.

Content:

Complete Arabic text far more extensive than Latin translations.

Systematic tables linking planetary movements to [[Ritual]] formulas.

Alchemical recipes involving mercury, sulfur, and exotic resins.

Diagrams resembling proto-astrolabes, with mathematical ratios suggesting practical use in navigation and early optics.

Anomalies:

Certain diagrams encode values resembling modern orbital mechanics constants — fueling speculation of “hidden science.”

Several folios treated with plant extracts that preserve ink unusually well.

Archaeological Significance: Confirms Islamic Spain and North Africa as centers of scientific-occult [[Synthesis]]. Demonstrates [[Continuity]] of astrology, proto-chemistry, and cosmology beyond mere superstition.

🛡 Intelligence / Security Brief

Relic Code: RELIC-54 (Picatrix)
Classification: HIGH STRATEGIC AND IDEOLOGICAL IMPACT
Custody: Initially seized by Moroccan authorities; soon appropriated by the [[Yamama Caliphate]] via covert operatives. Currently digitized and restricted to elite Caliphate scholars.
Strategic Assessment:

Caliphate: Positions the Picatrix as proof that Islamic heritage contains the true science of the cosmos. Frames it as divine gift suppressed by [[Crusaders]] and heretics. Uses fragments in propaganda to claim Caliphate can fuse science and prophecy.

Vatican: Denounces it as sorcery; some scholars secretly fear its astronomical tables undermine Christian relic primacy by showing Islamic civilization possessed “forbidden knowledge.”

[[Europolis]]: Fascinated; secularists frame it as evidence Islamic Spain seeded the European Renaissance.

Persia: Claims [[Continuity]], arguing Hermetic and Zoroastrian elements in Picatrix show Persia’s influence on Islamic cosmology.

Broader Impact:

Rise of neo-occultist sects across North Africa and Europe, treating the Picatrix as a manual of spiritual resistance.

CRINK scientists quietly analyze orbital constants encoded in diagrams, sparking rumors of proto-aerospace insights.

Extremist Caliphate sub-factions demand rituals from the Picatrix be reinstituted in state ceremonies.

Risk Factors: Text is inherently dual-use: framed as prophecy, but read as technical knowledge. Could fracture Caliphate into “purist Qur’anic” vs “occult-scientific” factions.
