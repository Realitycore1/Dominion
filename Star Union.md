I. Founding (3400 – 3500 CE)

The Star Union was born out of the [[Colonial Union]]’s effort to create model collectivist economies beyond Sol after the Closure Era.
The initial colonists—[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/Biology & Lifecycle/Engineers|Engineers]], miners, agronomists, and trade-union cadres from post-Tharsis reconstruction zones—settled on Star Prime, a cold, mineral-rich super-[[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] with dense nitrogen–ammonia atmosphere.
They founded [[The First People]]’s Congress (3415 CE) to coordinate [[Production]] quotas, labor housing, and [[Infrastructure]] funding.

From the outset, Star Union combined idealistic socialist imagery with pragmatic industrial goals: collective ownership of output, centralized planning, private initiative tolerated under oversight.

By 3500 CE:

Population: ≈ 18 million.

[[Infrastructure]]: first rail–to–mass-driver corridor linking surface mines to orbital foundries.

Exports: ammonia, raw steel, and prefab hab modules.

II. The Age of Planning (3500 – 3750 CE)

The colony adopted Five-Year Industrial Plans, each coordinated by the Central Planning Secretariat but executed through autonomous People’s Councils.
Every plan ended with a Congress of Review where delegates voted to expand or amend quotas.

Economic pillars emerged:

Bulk Shipping: state-owned convoy fleets moving heavy materials between frontier [[Systems]].

Ammonia Cracking: large-scale chemical plants converting local volatiles into hydrogen fuel and fertilizer stock.

Standardized Habitat Kits: modular hab units exported to younger [[II - Companion Narratives/Syl'Cothan Dominion/3 - Colonial Union/Colonies|Colonies]].

Ideologically socialist, the Union nonetheless allowed external trade—any deal that advanced industrial growth was “revolutionary commerce.”
By 3750 CE the Party’s technocrats routinely negotiated contracts with Kuomin and Kuroda interests.

III. Industrial Expansion (3750 – 3920 CE)

Star Union industrialized at astonishing speed.
[[Worker]] Cities spread across frozen plains, connected by magnetic rail grids feeding into State Shipyards up to 1.5 km long.
A growing [[Orbital Ring]]-Station network handled smelting, storage, and convoy staging.

The Union’s Five-Year Plan 9 (3835 CE) targeted full export self-sufficiency; [[Production]] doubled within twenty years.
Political officers were placed aboard every freighter and escort to ensure ideological cohesion—an echo of old [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] naval commissars.
The fleet’s [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Interstice City/Doctrine|Doctrine]] favored swarm tactics: massed light craft overwhelming superior ships through coordination and sacrifice.

By 3900 CE the Union controlled a merchant-[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Military|Military]] flotilla second only to Kuroda’s in tonnage.

IV. The Estela Affair (3920 – 4000 CE)

Industrial success bred internal strain.
When dockworker unions on the ring stations demanded wage parity with planetary miners, the Party sought external “consultants” from [[Estela Integralism]] to break the strikes.
The move backfired spectacularly: Estelan “security advisers” imposed brutal discipline and flooded [[Worker]] media with Integralist propaganda.
The Winter Riots of 3982 CE paralyzed shipping for six months and nearly toppled the Presidium.

The crisis ended only after the Congress expelled the Estelan mission and reinstated collective wage control.
Yet the episode left scars—mistrust of both foreign [[Corporations]] and the Party’s technocrats.
In response, the Union decentralized certain industries, allowing regional committees more autonomy under continued Five-Year guidance.

By 4000 CE:

Population ≈ 180 million.

Legacy

The Star Union stands as the enduring symbol of organized pragmatism: socialist in speech, industrial in spirit, and unashamedly practical in survival.
