I. The Committee Years (2340–2390)

Mercury was colonized late, not for habitation but for industry. The Mercurian Colonial Committee (MCC) was formed under the [[Colonial Union]] to administer mining outposts, solar harvest stations, and the great railways that crisscrossed the terminator line.

Life was harsh:
	•	Outposts had to be buried under meters of regolith to survive radiation.
	•	Colonists lived in shifts, following the terminator to avoid both searing heat and killing cold.
	•	Entire habitats were mounted on massive solar rails — mobile cities creeping endlessly to stay in the “twilight zone.”

The MCC ruled with corporate precision, more an extraction board than a civic council. Colonists were [[Workers]], not citizens.

⸻

II. The Age of Rails and Mirrors (2390–2450)
	•	Solar Rails: vast maglev tracks built around Mercury’s equator, carrying mobile cities that crawled with the dawn and dusk, always in balance.
	•	[[Sunshields]] & Orbital Mirrors: [[Giant]] reflective structures cooled surface mines and redirected energy to orbital stations.
	•	Mining Yards: rich seams of iron, nickel, and rare heavy metals made Mercury indispensable to the Second Space Race.

Mercurians began to see themselves not as prisoners of heat, but as keepers of the sun. Their [[culture]] celebrated precision, endurance, and the rhythm of constant motion.

⸻

III. The [[Schism]] of the Rails (2450–2480)

As Mercury’s population grew — from a few thousand miners to nearly 100,000 rail-dwellers — resentment toward the Committee grew.

The MCC was seen as an arm of CU exploitation. Colonists worked, bled, and died to feed the orbital habitats and Martian domes, yet had no voice in governance.

The turning point came when the City of Dawn, one of the great rail-habitats, refused to yield its metal tithe. CU enforcers tried to seize its solar rig; [[Workers]] sabotaged the maglev line, derailing entire shipments.

This became the [[Schism]] of the Rails.

⸻

IV. The Sunborn [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Identity|Identity]] (2480–2500)

In the [[Aftermath]] of the [[Schism]], the colonists began to call themselves Sunborn. They embraced their unique [[(2030) Existence]]:
	•	Always in motion, never fixed.
	•	Children of the terminator line, living between extremes.
	•	Masters of solar energy, builders of rails and shields.

Culturally:
	•	Rituals centered on the rising and setting sun, which for them was a cycle of survival rather than worship.
	•	Songs and poems celebrated endurance and movement: “We walk with the dawn, we rest with the dusk.”
	•	Tattoos of mirrored suns became common — a mark of balance between [[Fire]] and shadow.

Politically, the MCC collapsed, replaced by the Council of Rails — a rotating assembly drawn from each moving city.

⸻

V. Toward the City of the Sun (2500+)

By the 26th century, Mercury had become the Solar [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Yauturn Sovereignty/System|System]]’s forge.
	•	Its orbital mirrors powered not just Mercury but fleets across the [[Inner System]].
	•	Its rare metals fueled Martian domes and Venusian aerostats.
	•	Its rail cities became monuments to [[Human]] adaptability, crawling endlessly in step with the Sun.

The Sunborn knew they could never tame their world. But they had mastered living with it, and that mastery became their pride.
