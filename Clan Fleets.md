Clan fleets are not industrial systems.

They are mobile societies.

They exist because some populations decided:

“We will never trust stations again.”

⸻

1. Origins of Clan Fleets

Clan fleets emerge from:
	•	abandoned mining operations
	•	corporate collapses
	•	station failures
	•	civil conflicts
	•	refugee convoys
	•	blacklisted crews

What turns them into clans is permanence.

They stop trying to go back.

⸻

2. What a Clan Fleet Is

A clan fleet typically includes:
	•	multi-generation habitat ships
	•	converted mining vessels
	•	schools and medical bays
	•	repair docks
	•	hydroponics or algae farms
	•	cultural and ritual spaces

They do not dock often.
They do not trust fixed authority.

They move slowly, deliberately, and together.

⸻

3. Social Structure

Clan fleets are:
	•	family-based
	•	lineage-oriented
	•	reputation-driven
	•	consensus-heavy internally
	•	deeply suspicious externally

Leadership may be:
	•	elder councils
	•	elected captains
	•	matriarchal or patriarchal lines
	•	merit-based war leaders

Law is internal.
Justice is personal.

⸻

4. Economy of a Clan Fleet

Clan fleets survive by:
	•	salvage
	•	small-scale mining
	•	repair work
	•	escort services
	•	information trading
	•	occasionally piracy (selective, restrained)

They prefer long-term relationships over profit spikes.

Many mafias began as clan fleets that stopped moving.

⸻

5. Relationship to Power Groups

Clan fleets are:
	•	tolerated by mafias (shared values)
	•	preyed upon by cartels (resources)
	•	avoided by zealots (unpredictable)
	•	occasionally hired by syndicates
	•	sometimes absorbed by stations

Some clan fleets become:
	•	cartel auxiliaries
	•	militia forces
	•	station defense contractors

Others vanish quietly.

⸻

6. Cultural Psychology

Clan fleet members:
	•	think in generations
	•	distrust abstract law
	•	value honor and memory
	•	carry oral histories
	•	mark territory symbolically

They are the oldest human culture in space, because they never stopped being nomads.
