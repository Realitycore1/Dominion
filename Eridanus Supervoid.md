A very large underdense region (a “supervoid”) along the line of sight to the CMB Cold Spot in the constellation Eridanus. It’s not a single empty bubble but a chain of connected voids that together form a long, low-density corridor.

Where/size (typical estimates).
	•	Redshift: centered roughly around z ≈ 0.1–0.[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]].
	•	Extent: transverse radius of order 100–200 Mpc/h, with evidence of strong line-of-sight elongation (hundreds of Mpc/h) rather than a round void.
	•	Underdensity: mean matter contrast roughly δ ≈ −0.[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]] to −0.[[Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)|Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)]] (varies by survey and modeling).

How we see it.
Detected as a deficit of galaxies in [[Wide]] surveys (e.g., 2MASS/WISE, Pan-STARRS, DES), plus weak-lensing signals consistent with lower convergence along that direction.

Why it matters.
A supervoid can imprint a temperature decrement on the CMB via the Integrated Sachs–Wolfe / Rees–Sciama effect. For the Eridanus sightline, modeling generally finds it can explain some of the Cold Spot’s coolness—but not all of it. The residual suggests either:
	•	the Cold Spot is largely a statistical fluctuation within ΛCDM, or
	•	multiple effects (void + chance alignment + subtler systematics) combine.

Status/caveats.
	•	Exact shape and depth remain survey- and method-dependent (photometric-redshift scatter, masking, void-finder choices).
	•	Even in “maximal” models, the predicted CMB dip is smaller than the observed Cold Spot’s central amplitude; thus the supervoid is a contributor, not a full explanation.
