The wall stood in [[Silence]].

Helmets faceless, rifles raised, Federal Branch [[Soldiers]] braced against the endless xeno tide. They did not call each other by name. They were not taught to. Names were for lives left behind. The wall did not break, because it was not made of men but of inevitability.

The battle raged three days. By the fourth, the ground was a mire of ichor and blood. [[Soldiers]] fell by the hundreds, but the line did not waver. For every body broken, another stepped forward.

Then — the air split.

The Legion of Fenrir descended, their [[Verarch]] at the fore. His axe cleaved a path of [[Fire]] through the swarm, wolf-banners howling with radiant flame. The [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Galactic Federation/Military/Legions|Legions]] struck like gods descending, and for the first [[Time]] in days, the wall could breathe.

As the enemy faltered, the [[Verarch]] turned. His helm retracted, his golden eyes sweeping across the Federal line. [[Soldiers]] froze, their rifles still smoking. For a heartbeat, they felt the weight of his gaze.

And then he spoke.

Not to all. To each.

“You, Sergeant Kaelen Dross of Ranham. You fought with your father’s courage.”
“You, Lira Venn, once of [[Harmina]]’s outer rings. Your brother waits for you in the upload sanctum.”
“You, Orien Tal, son of fishers. You will see the sea again, even if in memory’s glass.”

Every faceless helm heard its true name, spoken with perfect clarity. Every [[Soldier]] felt seen, remembered, engraved into eternity.

The [[Verarch]]’s voice grew low. “You are not faceless. You are not nameless. You are the stones of the wall. And I will not let even one stone be [[Forgotten]].”

The [[Soldiers]] did not answer. They could not. But something in their stance shifted. They were still the wall — faceless, unyielding — but now, they knew their sacrifice had been counted.

When the tide rose again, they advanced. Some would fall, their memories fractured, their bodies reborn in clone-vats or uploaded into digital refuge to breathe before the next war. Others would fight on, until even memory itself gave way.

But none of them would ever be nameless again.
