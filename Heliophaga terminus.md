Heliophaga stellarum

II. Dominion / Operational Term

SD-Class Phenomena

(Stellar Devourers)

Subcategories:
	•	SD-Latent – attached but inactive
	•	SD-Active – mass-energy siphoning
	•	SD-Terminal – inducing stellar collapse or conversion

Dominion doctrine classifies them as astrobiological disasters, not entities.

⸻

III. What Stellar Devourers Actually Are

Stellar Devourers are macro-scale energy–matter organisms that:
	•	Form in rare, unstable stellar environments
	•	Condense from:
	•	Exotic plasma life precursors
	•	Gravitational shear zones
	•	Failed stellar containment events
	•	Anchor themselves into stellar photospheres and cores

They do not “eat” stars like animals.

They re-route stellar output through themselves.

⸻

IV. Morphology & Presence

Appearance
	•	Radiant, semi-fractal bodies
	•	Limb-like plasma structures anchored into stellar layers
	•	Core nucleus stabilized by:
	•	Magnetic confinement
	•	Self-sustaining fusion siphons
	•	Often mistaken for:
	•	Stellar anomalies
	•	Protuberances
	•	False coronas

To observers:

It looks like the star has grown a parasite made of fire.

⸻

V. Behavior
	•	Drift rarely; migration takes millennia
	•	Attach to stars with:
	•	Unstable fusion cycles
	•	Artificial interference
	•	Heavy stellar engineering history
	•	Feed until:
	•	Star dims
	•	Star destabilizes
	•	Or star collapses into a remnant state

They do not move on quickly.
They finish their work.

⸻

VI. Intelligence (Hard Boundary)

Stellar Devourers are non-sapient.
	•	No communication
	•	No awareness of observers
	•	No aggression
	•	No malice

They respond only to:
	•	Energy gradients
	•	Magnetic field shifts
	•	Stellar mass changes

If they “react” to ships, it is because the ship disturbed the star.

That distinction matters—and is often ignored.

⸻

VII. Cultural & Mythic Names

Human cultures will absolutely mythologize them.

Common names:
	•	Stellar Devourers
	•	Star-Eaters
	•	The Burning Kings
	•	The Sunleeches
	•	Crown of Ash
	•	The Last Light

Religious movements claim:

They are punishments for hubris.

Dominion response:

“Incorrect, but consistent with prior superstition.”
