[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Galactic Federation/Culture & Society/Daily LIfe|Daily LIfe]] in the Federation, 12,200 HC

The Environment
	•	Cities: No glittering skyscraper skylines. Instead, compact green-cities grown around highland rivers, terraces, and reforested zones. Buildings mix stone, reclaimed steel, and bio-composites — often wrapped in vines or moss to help air-filtering.
	•	Energy: Hydropower, wind, and small modular reactors. Solar arrays cover rooftops and hillsides. The scars of the 21st century remind everyone never to rely on brittle, centralized grids.
	•	Food: Terraces, [[Hydroponics]] towers, and community seed-gardens everywhere. Reintroduced crops (like acid-resistant rice, hardy wheat, kelp noodles) are staples. Meat is rare, often cultured rather than animal-farmed.
	•	Transport: Canal-barges, rail lines, glider-airships, and foot/bike paths dominate. Fossil fuels are outlawed under the Gaian Charter.

⸻

Civic Rhythm
	•	Morning: People gather at Community Halls, where work-assignments are issued — reforestation crews, reef-barge missions, floodwall maintenance, teaching, or crafts. Service to the land is expected of all, like ancient [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Empyrean Continuum/The Empyrearchy/Military|Military]] service.
	•	Work: Restoration is not a niche — it’s the central civic duty. A mason may spend one day repairing irrigation canals, the next day teaching geometry.
	•	Education: Children learn literacy, math, and ecology side by side. Instead of catechisms, they memorize [[I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]] lists, watersheds, and soil cycles. Their myths are forests reborn, whales reintroduced, rivers healed.

⸻

Spiritual & Cultural Life
	•	Festivals: Solstices and equinoxes are sacred times. Entire cities gather to plant trees, seed rivers, or release fish into newly restored waters. These acts are the [[Ritual]].
	•	Music & Story: Songs celebrate “The Healing,” often retelling the return of once-extinct animals or the founding of restored biomes. Storytellers travel between cities like medieval bards, carrying ecological lore.
	•	Symbols: The Holocene Calendar is carved into public stones. Each new year begins with a ceremonial act — planting a grove, raising a reef-structure, or declaring a new watershed “returned to life.”

⸻

Governance in Practice
	•	Local: Neighborhood councils run day-to-day matters, elected by households. Each has a quota of ecological service.
	•	Regional: Republic assemblies coordinate agriculture, river projects, and defense. Delegates are chosen not only for popularity but also for proven record of stewardship.
	•	Federation: The Federal Assembly meets in rotating capitals (to avoid the “old world” curse of one eternal seat of power). When the Assembly convenes, entire caravans of barges, airships, and rail-cars pour into the host city — turning it into a world forum.

⸻

The Atmosphere of the Age
	•	People live more modestly than in 21st-century consumer society, but more securely than the famine-ridden Ash Decades.
	•	Their worldview is communal, ecological, and pragmatic. The measure of a person isn’t wealth or faith, but what living [[Systems]] they helped restore.
	•	The scars remain: drowned ruins on coastlines, desert belts across continents, radioactive circles where nothing grows. These are not [[Forgotten]] — they are visited as cautionary “pilgrimage zones.”

⸻

Example Snapshot: A Spring Day, 12,200 HC

A family in the East African Highlands wakes under red dawn skies. The father heads to a dam crew stabilizing a mountain reservoir. The mother tends a seed-bank greenhouse, documenting crop mutations for the local guild. Their [[Child]] recites a lesson at school: “The river is life; the river is [[Time]].” At sunset, the community gathers to plant rows of oak saplings, singing songs about the “Whale’s Return” in the distant Pacific.
