during the Cold War era, the Soviet Union was heavily focused on subversion tactics and infiltration as a means of achieving its political goals. The country was actively involved in espionage and covert operations aimed at undermining Western countries and their allies. The first step in this process was the infiltration of the common workplace. By recruiting people from different walks of life and encouraging them to spy on their colleagues, the Soviet Union aimed to gain access to sensitive information and undermine Western institutions. The next step was to infiltrate education, with the goal of indoctrinating the younger generation with communist ideologies. By targeting universities and other educational institutions, the Soviet Union aimed to groom future leaders who were loyal to the communist cause. Through these subversion tactics, the Soviet Union was able to gain a significant degree of influence over certain countries and individuals. While some of these tactics were effective, others were met with resistance and ultimately led to the collapse of the Soviet Union.
