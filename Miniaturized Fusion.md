Miniaturized [[Fusion Reactors]] are a type of power plant that uses the process of fusion to generate electricity. Unlike traditional nuclear reactors, which require the fission of heavy elements such as uranium to release energy, [[Fusion Reactors]] use hydrogen to create energy. The process of fusion involves taking two light atoms and forcing them together to create a single heavier atom, releasing vast amounts of energy in the process. The challenge in miniaturizing [[Fusion Reactors]] lies in controlling and harnessing this energy output. The main benefit of miniaturized [[Fusion Reactors]] is their ability to produce clean, efficient, and abundant energy. [[Fusion Reactors]] do not emit any radioactive waste or greenhouse gases, making them a more environmentally-friendly alternative to traditional power sources like coal, gas, or nuclear fission. In recent years, several companies and research institutions have made significant progress towards miniaturizing [[Fusion Reactors]]. Some notable examples include General Fusion, ITER, and the Los Alamos National Laboratory. While there are still significant technical and engineering challenges to be overcome, miniaturized [[Fusion Reactors]] have the potential to transform the global energy landscape and lead to a more sustainable future.
