I. The Age of Excess Clarity (2030–2070)

When AI makes data management cheap and global bandwidth ubiquitous, civilization assumes more information means more understanding.
Instead, it reaches a threshold where comprehension becomes statistically impossible.
Historians later call this threshold The Overlight—a planet so illuminated that no one can see.

⸻

II. How It Happens

1. The Compression Revolution (2030s)

AI-driven “semantic compression” allows infinite storage: every camera feed, conversation, and sensor reading is archived in real [[Time]].
Governments and [[Corporations]] record everything “for accountability.”
Citizens feel watched, but also empowered—they can record right back.
The result is a perfect mirror world of data where no one can find the truth because everything looks equally verifiable.

2. Autonomous Content Generation
	•	Text, voice, and visual [[Synthesis]] reach near-indistinguishability.
	•	90% of digital media is machine-made by 2040.
	•	“News” becomes a self-sustaining ecosystem: bots quote bots, analysis cites synthetic sources, credibility dissolves.

3. Algorithmic Personalization
	•	Each person’s feed is uniquely generated—a world tailored to their preferences, fears, and biases.
	•	Consensus reality fractures into billions of micro-continents.
	•	The term “public opinion” becomes meaningless; there’s no shared input.

4. The Rise of Cognitive Middlemen
	•	By mid-century, most people outsource comprehension to AI summarizers.
	•	These [[Systems]] filter, prioritize, and narrativize the chaos.
	•	Whoever controls the summarizers controls perception itself.
	•	Truth becomes subscription-based.

5. The Collapse of Verification
	•	Timestamping, watermarking, and blockchain verification once promised trust.
	•	But deep counterfeiting and synthetic forensics make every proof reversible.
	•	Courts, historians, and scientists drown in conflicting “authentic” evidence.

⸻

III. Observable Effects
	1.	The Great Forgetting
	•	With perfect archives, nothing is prioritized; the past bloats until memory itself loses value.
	•	[[Human]] education turns to pattern recognition over knowledge retention.
	2.	The Paradox of Transparency
	•	Governments publish everything, but too much to read.
	•	Total openness produces secrecy through abundance.
	3.	Misinformation Fatigue
	•	Populations stop caring what’s true.
	•	The emotional center of politics shifts from conviction to vibe: people choose the story that feels least exhausting.
	4.	Cognitive Illiteracy
	•	Analytical thinking atrophies.
	•	Citizens rely on emotional heuristics—tribal belonging replaces epistemology.
	5.	Weaponized Noise
	•	States and [[Corporations]] flood opponents with contradictory data to paralyze response.
	•	The best disinformation strategy becomes overproduction.
