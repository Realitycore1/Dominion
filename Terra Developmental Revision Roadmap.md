# Terra Developmental Revision Roadmap

## Editorial Objective

Expand *Terra* through reader investment rather than word accumulation.

Every added scene must perform at least two functions:

- threaten or advance a character's personal goal;
- deepen a relationship;
- make ordinary life specific;
- develop a recurring location or object;
- dramatize existing lore through action;
- create aftermath for an established event;
- carry a decision into a later generation.

No scene should exist only to explain history already summarized elsewhere.

## Current Baseline

- Active manuscript: [[Lithic Manuscript 1 - Terra - Rough Draft v2.0]]
- Current length: approximately 104,200 words
- Structure: prologue, four parts, forty-seven chapters
- Canonical span: 1889-2079
- Strongest current section: Wetwire through first winter
- Weakest current element: consistent voice differentiation during dense institutional dialogue

The draft is now novel length. A later expansion may reach 110,000-120,000 words, but that range is an outcome, not a quota.

The implemented split sequence and remaining pacing risks are maintained in [[Terra Chapter Decompression Audit]]. The definitive chapter index is [[Terra Chapter Story Engine]].

## Revision Gates

### Gate I - Approximately 60,000 Words: Story Engine

Required result:

- every major viewpoint has a personal goal;
- every major viewpoint has a beginning, middle, and ending worldview;
- every chapter has a page-turn question;
- every chapter threatens identity, family, reputation, purpose, memory, friendship, or livelihood;
- every important victory carries a visible cost;
- failures alter later decisions.

Primary controls:

- [[Terra Character Arc Bible]]
- [[Terra Chapter Story Engine]]

**Status:** Reached. Personal goals, chapter questions, major costs, and the first correction pass are integrated.

### Gate II - Approximately 80,000 Words: Lived World

Required result:

- each part contains meals, work, travel, domestic disagreement, humor, and rest;
- recurring locations change over time;
- infrastructure is experienced by workers and users;
- ordinary professions appear throughout the manuscript;
- culture changes through food, clothing, language, music, architecture, and children's behavior;
- major locations have distinct sensory identities.

Priority scene categories:

- breakfast and evening meals;
- schools and childcare;
- markets and ration lines;
- worship and secular ritual;
- trains, roads, ports, and checkpoints;
- weddings, funerals, festivals, and anniversaries;
- repair shifts, cleaning shifts, and medical handoffs.

**Status:** Reached. Daily life, recurring locations, national culture, faith practice, infrastructure labor, corridor culture, ecological continuity, secondary-state sovereignty, and first-year survival appear across all four parts.

### Gate III - Approximately 100,000 Words: Emotional Continuity

Required result:

- major turning points receive full scenes;
- every major catastrophe receives aftermath;
- summary transitions are converted where they conceal emotional change;
- recurring objects gain new meanings across generations;
- family inheritance is legible;
- children introduced in one era return as adults where canon permits;
- decisions made before 1900 visibly shape choices in 2078.

Primary control:

- [[Terra Narrative Continuity Ledger]]

**Status:** Reached. Major catastrophes now carry administrative, domestic, medical, and material aftermath. The atlas, blue pencil, permits, tokens, maintenance records, food, and family documents change meaning across generations.

### Gate IV - Approximately 110,000-140,000 Words: Novel Finish

Required result:

- dialogue voices are distinct by education, profession, age, region, and temperament;
- philosophical lines are spaced so scenes carry their own meaning;
- humor and affection prevent the manuscript from becoming emotionally uniform;
- repeated themes evolve rather than restate themselves;
- chapter endings create forward pressure;
- prose is line-edited only after structure is stable.

**Status:** Structural finish reached; line-level finish remains open. The next pass should focus on voice differentiation, repeated institutional phrasing, paragraph rhythm, and local transitions rather than new history.

## Phase Sequence

## Phase 1 - Strengthen the Story Engine

### Task 1: Personal Goals

For every recurring viewpoint, identify:

- what the character wants personally;
- what institution or relationship obstructs that desire;
- what the character will sacrifice;
- what the character refuses to sacrifice;
- how the goal changes.

Completion test:

The viewpoint can be described without using "records history," "witnesses events," or "represents a faction."

### Task 2: Emotional Arcs

Record:

- beginning worldview;
- middle worldview;
- ending worldview;
- greatest fear;
- greatest mistake;
- greatest success.

Completion test:

The ending worldview must be caused by choices and losses shown in the manuscript.

### Task 3: Chapter Questions

Every chapter receives:

- a reader question;
- a threatened value;
- a decision;
- a cost;
- a reason to continue.

Completion test:

The chapter question cannot be answered by reading a timeline entry.

## Phase 2 - Expand Human Experience

### Task 4: Everyday Life

Add ordinary scenes only where they reveal pressure. A meal should expose scarcity, family structure, faith, work, or political division. A train ride should change access, relationship, or information.

### Task 5: Sensory Identity

For every major location, track:

- smell;
- sound;
- clothing;
- food;
- dominant color and material;
- weather;
- common technology.

### Task 6: Recurring Locations

Priority locations:

- Selene's classroom;
- the Lotharingian customs station;
- the Constantinople atlas congress and print room;
- Daniel and Ruth's dining tables;
- Miriam's cognition laboratory;
- Leila's hospitals, containment laundries, tunnels, and restored wetland;
- fortress apartment blocks, scan gates, and protected freight corridors;
- Nadiya's Order archive;
- Fariha's clinic;
- the Keene rail yards;
- the Ben-David clinic and Jerusalem shelter;
- Akira's rice field;
- the cold-fusion facility.

Each return should show physical and cultural change.

## Phase 3 - Slow Important Moments

### Task 7: Summary Audit

Search for:

- years later;
- eventually;
- over time;
- by then;
- ministries;
- governments;
- during the following;
- the next year;
- for years.

Convert a summary only when it contains:

- a changed relationship;
- a decision with consequences;
- an irreversible loss;
- a child becoming an adult;
- a recurring object's meaning changing.

### Task 8: Turning Points

Full-scene priorities:

- unofficial atlas publication;
- Stability Index rejection;
- Wetwire Day Zero;
- the Nesting Year;
- the first fortress closure;
- the first protected freight convoy;
- the Moon landing and overview response;
- the Gulf cleanup and its medical aftermath;
- the 2048 legacy-containment failure;
- dual-maternity recognition after the civil war;
- Accerisma's first ecological consequence;
- Miriam's death;
- capital bombing;
- UAR restoration;
- first relic authentication;
- holy-war declarations;
- Jerusalem siege;
- tactical nuclear threshold;
- ISS destruction;
- cold-fusion activation;
- Exchange;
- first-winter burial and school.

### Task 9: Aftermath

For each turning point, show at least one:

- cleanup;
- mourning;
- paperwork;
- medical recovery;
- family argument;
- public reinterpretation;
- reconstruction task.

## Phase 4 - Long-Term Continuity

### Task 10: Recurring Objects

Track the atlas, blue pencil, Celestialist notebook, Stability Index cards, letters, maps, passports, relic tokens, medical licenses, and badges.

Each appearance must change or confirm meaning.

### Task 11: Genealogy

Track biological, chosen, professional, and intellectual inheritance. A mentor relationship can carry history as strongly as blood.

### Task 12: Children Becoming Adults

Priority returns:

- Emil;
- Miriam Varga;
- Leila;
- Nadiya;
- Eve;
- Mara;
- Sade;
- Kavya;
- Elise;
- Abdulrahman;
- Mei;
- Haruto;
- Anja.

## Phase 5 - Historical Immersion

### Task 13: Ordinary Professions

Maintain representation from:

- bakers;
- mechanics;
- nurses;
- cleaners;
- teachers;
- railway workers;
- printers;
- conservators;
- farmers;
- drivers;
- kitchen workers;
- librarians;
- technicians.

### Task 14: Infrastructure

Every political system should be encountered through something it must operate:

- food;
- water;
- medicine;
- transport;
- electricity;
- waste;
- housing;
- records;
- communications.

### Task 15: Cultural Change

Track:

- songs and broadcast language;
- school answers and children's games;
- uniforms and work clothing;
- ration food and ritual food;
- architecture shaped by containment;
- relic replicas and household tokens;
- changing meanings of citizenship and home.

## Phase 6 - Dialogue

### Task 16: Philosophical Density

Keep the strongest thematic line in an exchange. Let behavior carry the remaining argument.

### Task 17: Voice Differentiation

- Selene: patient, pedagogical, precise.
- Daniel: skeptical, compressed, darkly practical.
- Ruth: mathematical, direct, intolerant of false reassurance.
- Miriam Varga: clinical, layered, increasingly guarded.
- Leila: concrete, medical, morally impatient.
- Nadiya: interrogative, archival, resistant to clean answers.
- Mara: labor-centered, procedural, dry.
- Sade: ecological, communal, autonomy-focused.
- Kavya: systems language, exact definitions, controlled defiance.
- Elise: logistical, visual, duty-centered.
- Abdulrahman: formal, evidentiary, wary of ownership.
- Fariha: immediate, practical, unimpressed by rank.
- Luca: pastoral, restrained, morally self-questioning.
- Elena: linguistic, analytical, alert to propaganda.
- Yasin: sardonic, defensive, concrete.
- Akira: sparse, agricultural, resistant to abstraction.

### Task 18: Humor

Use:

- workplace teasing;
- family irritation;
- bureaucratic absurdity;
- children interpreting adult language literally;
- professional rivalry;
- affection expressed through complaint.

Do not use humor to invalidate grief.

## Phase 7 - Reader Investment

### Task 19: Threat

Threaten more than physical survival:

- Emil's future;
- Selene's teaching;
- Daniel and Ruth's marriage;
- Miriam's family;
- Leila's trust in medicine;
- Nadiya's ability to preserve context;
- Mara's ownership of her body;
- Sade's right to choose service;
- Kavya's independent judgment;
- Elise's loyalty to family and system;
- Abdulrahman's moral agency;
- Luca's usefulness as a doubter;
- Miriam Ben-David's family;
- Akira's son and farm.

### Task 20: Costly Victories

Every institutional success should transfer a cost:

- atlas standardization enables conscription;
- containment saves cities and damages ecology;
- medical innovation creates ownership;
- UAR restoration preserves emergency machinery;
- relic protection creates armies;
- cold fusion arrives first in shelters;
- archive survival requires choosing what burns.

### Task 21: Failure

Failures must remain in the narrative record:

- Selene cannot protect Emil completely;
- Daniel cannot make institutions act on the Stability Index;
- Miriam cannot preserve both secrecy and marriage;
- Leila cannot save Tomas or Miriam;
- Nadiya cannot prevent emergency systems from returning;
- Luca cannot restrain the mobilization he blesses;
- Abdulrahman cannot fully control the state built for him.

## Phase 8 - Worldbuilding Through Action

### Task 22: Lore in Use

No institution should be introduced without a person using, resisting, repairing, paying for, or being excluded by it.

### Task 23: Consequence Chains

For every invention, track effects on:

- economics;
- family;
- work;
- religion;
- law;
- military power.

Deferred-science rule:

- reproducibility confirms an effect, not a product;
- a bounded mechanism does not establish a safe engineering path;
- no human-scale prototype exists unless a later canonical event explicitly introduces one;
- religious, political, commercial, and military claims must remain visibly larger than the measured result.

### Task 24: Earlier Decisions

Required long causality:

- the layered atlas informs later archive ethics;
- the blue pencil becomes a method for recording disagreement;
- Celestialist questions shape scientific review;
- classified cognition creates private-thought law;
- Wetwire containment creates walls and crisis government;
- Accerisma creates ecological and respiratory damage;
- emergency governance creates the systems used during the Exchange.

## Phase 9 - Polish

### Task 25: Theme Tracking

Primary themes:

- maps;
- uncertainty;
- continuity;
- institutional simplification;
- memory;
- truth;
- survival;
- ownership;
- faith;
- consent.

### Task 26: Repetition

Repeated ideas must evolve:

- maps begin as political claims and end as survival tools;
- uncertainty begins as an honest limit and becomes both ethical discipline and institutional excuse;
- continuity begins as preservation and becomes control;
- archives begin by protecting evidence and end by choosing people over records.

### Task 27: Chapter Endings

Each ending should provide one:

- unresolved choice;
- altered relationship;
- revealed cost;
- inherited object;
- approaching consequence;
- question whose answer lies in the next era.

## Completion Standard

The revision is complete when the manuscript reads as a novel about people carrying history, not a history that occasionally names people.
