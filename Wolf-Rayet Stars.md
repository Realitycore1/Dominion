I. Physical Definition

In standard astrophysics, Wolf–Rayet (WR) [[Stars]] are massive [[Stars]] (typically >20 M☉) in a late evolutionary stage, having shed their outer hydrogen [[Layers]] through intense stellar winds and exposing their hotter helium-burning cores.
They are characterized by:
	•	Surface Temperatures: 30,000–200,000 K
	•	Luminosity: Hundreds of thousands to millions of L☉
	•	Winds: 1,000–[[Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)|Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)]],000 km/s, ejecting up to 10⁻⁵ M☉ per year

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Wolf–Rayet [[Stars]] are hyperluminous overburn engines — stellar bodies at peak radiative output before catastrophic end stages like hypernovae, long gamma-ray bursts (GRBs), or collapses into black holes.
