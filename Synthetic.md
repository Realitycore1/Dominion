Core Definition

A Synthetic Basin is a megaversal region in which the host axiom is either:
	•	artificially constructed
	•	intentionally rewritten
	•	externally imposed
	•	heavily stabilized through engineered systems
	•	or converted from a naturally emergent basin into a designed continuity environment

In simple terms:

A Synthetic Basin is a reality-space where existence tends to behave as though it has been built, coded, calibrated, or maintained rather than simply grown or formed.

This does not mean “digital” by default.

Synthetic can express through:
	•	machine infrastructure
	•	designed ecologies
	•	codified causality
	•	simulation lattices
	•	ritual-engineered continuity
	•	hypercivilization architecture
	•	artificial basin shells

The key is:

Synthetic = continuity by intentional construction

Not just “technology.”

⸻

SYNTHETIC BASIN — PRIMARY BEHAVIORAL RULES

A Synthetic Basin tends to produce realities where:

1. Structure is intentional

Patterns are often designed rather than emergent.

2. Maintenance matters

Without upkeep, degradation is often catastrophic.

3. Continuity is engineered

The basin persists because systems are holding it together.

4. Optimization often competes with resilience

What is efficient is not always what survives long-term.

5. Artificiality can become naturalized

Built systems can persist long enough to become their own ecology.

That makes Synthetic Basins ideal for:
	•	machine empires
	•	hypercivilization shells
	•	artificial universes
	•	code-based cosmologies
	•	controlled experimental realities
	•	“built heaven / built hell” settings
	•	Dominion-grade infrastructure

This is an incredibly rich basin class.

⸻

SYNTHETIC BASIN — SUBTYPE LOGIC

A Synthetic subtype should answer:

What kind of artificiality dominates the basin?

Not just what kind of “tech” it has.

That means Synthetic subtypes should define:
	•	what was built
	•	why it was built
	•	what is being maintained
	•	what artificial logic governs continuity
	•	what happens if the system stops being maintained
