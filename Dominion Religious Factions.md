# Dominion Religious-Philosophical Factions (v1.0)

This document catalogs the religious and philosophical factions within [[The Dominion]], and explains how they interact with a [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Interstice City/Government|Government]] and civilization that permits worship but denies faith any political franchise.

---

## I. The Dominion Principle: Faith Without Franchise

- Faith in the Dominion is vibrant, culturally omnipresent, and deeply integrated into [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Galactic Federation/Culture & Society/Daily LIfe|daily life]].  
- Shrines hum, choirs shape provinces, Collapse Histories are reenacted.  
- **But faith has no franchise** — religious institutions cannot legislate, vote, or sit in councils of power.  
- This principle arose from the Collapse Histories: every record shows that when ideology or religion gained political power, civilizations fractured and died.  
- By severing faith from franchise, the Dominion preserves both: religion as culture, politics as meritocracy.

To citizens, this is survival wisdom. To outsiders, it can appear as suppression. To heretics, it is a weakness to exploit. To readers, it is a philosophical challenge: *is this stability, or is it control?*

---

## II. Major Religious-Philosophical Factions

### 1. Harmonists (Fundamental Resonance School)
- **Belief:** Pure resonance is the highest expression of the Holy Will. Disharmony leads to Collapse.  
- **Practice:** Daily chanting, harmonic rites, tuning shrines.  
- **Government Relation:** Loyalists; see the meritocracy as resonance embodied.  
- **Tensions:** Criticized as establishment stooges.

### 2. Collapse Theologians (Catastrophic School)
- **Belief:** Collapse is not only history but prophecy; all will fall unless Collapse is mastered.  
- **Practice:** Study Collapse Atlases, perform ritual collapses.  
- **Government Relation:** No franchise but cultural sway; consulted by Infernariate.  
- **Tensions:** Accused of fatalism; radicals welcome Collapse.

### 3. Aetherians (Esoteric School)
- **Belief:** Aether is the true substrate; Dominion law is reflection only.  
- **Practice:** Aether meditation, oracle fusion, technomagic rites.  
- **Government Relation:** Tolerated but distrusted, under surveillance.  
- **Tensions:** Risk of heresy; accused of harboring false chords.

### 4. Pragmatists (Meritocratic Rationalists)
- **Belief:** Dominion thrives because faith has no franchise. Ideology is corruption.  
- **Practice:** Civic service, Collapse study as civic religion.  
- **Government Relation:** Align directly; civil religion of state.  
- **Tensions:** Dismissive of mystics and esoterics.

### 5. Silentists (Abyssal School)
- **Belief:** Silence is the truest chord; resonance is distraction.  
- **Practice:** Silent shrines, meditation, mutism.  
- **Government Relation:** Permitted but monitored; echoes Domain heresy.  
- **Tensions:** Seen as subversives, tolerated until crisis.

### 6. Pilgrimist Orders
- **Belief:** The Empress is the eternal pilgrim; journey is worship.  
- **Practice:** Pilgrimages along Collapse routes, shrine maintenance.  
- **Government Relation:** Neutral, but glue provinces together.  
- **Tensions:** Criticized as passive, yet bind cultural unity.

---

## III. Interaction With Government & Society

- **Civic Life:** Citizens are free to worship; provinces host shrines and choirs.  
- **Councils:** No faith leaders sit in Dominion councils; decisions are merit-based.  
- **Infernariate:** Acts as referee, cutting down sects that drift toward heresy.  
- **Cultural Power:** Factions shape art, education, aesthetics, and moral identity.  
- **Franchise Divide:** Faith influences culture, but never law.

---

## IV. Narrative Consequences

- **Outsiders:** See suppression or hypocrisy; a red flag that Dominion may not truly honor freedom.  
- **Citizens:** Accept it as survival wisdom, but some struggle with the tension.  
- **Heretics:** Exploit it as propaganda — “they let you sing, but never listen.”  
- **Readers:** Confronted with a philosophical question: is faith safer without power, or is it neutered by exclusion?
