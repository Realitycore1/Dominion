The term "wide spread corruption" is often used to describe situations in which corruption has become deeply entrenched in an institution or society, and where corruption is widespread and pervasive. This can have significant negative impacts on a country's economy, politics, and public services. If we were to consider the context of the EU, we could see this type of corruption in several ways. For example, there have been ongoing allegations of corruption and influence-peddling in the EU's legislative and regulatory processes. Corruption in the EU's decision-making process can occur at various levels, including within individual member states, at the EU level, or within individual EU institutions. For example, in recent years, there have been several scandals involving alleged bribery and influence-peddling by lobbyists seeking to influence EU policies and regulations. Moreover, there have been numerous reports of conflicts of interest among EU officials and members of the European Parliament (MEPs), with some MEPs accused of taking bribes or accepting payments from interest groups in exchange for their votes on legislation. In summary, if the context of the EU were to be described as "wide spread corruption," we would see examples of this corruption at multiple levels, including within individual member states, within the EU itself, and among EU officials and MEPs. This corruption can result in the undermining of public trust in the EU's institutions and processes, as well as harming the EU's economic and political interests.
