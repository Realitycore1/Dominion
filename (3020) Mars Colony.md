# (3020) Mars Colony

The first permanent Martian colony is recognized in 3020 after a five-year foothold mission authorized by the newly reformed Colonial Union.

The marker does not record humanity's first arrival on Mars. It records the point at which the foothold possesses the infrastructure and law required for people to remain across mission rotations and raise families under an elected local government.

## Mars Foothold Mission

The mission proceeds in phases:

1. uncrewed vehicles deliver power systems, communications, pressure shelters, and buried emergency stores;
2. Aster-3 and other artificial astronauts prepare landing systems, water extraction, surface mobility, and return infrastructure;
3. a temporary human foothold validates surface operations through a complete abort cycle;
4. rotating crews expand medical capacity, food production, radiation shelter, maintenance, and rescue reserves;
5. family-rated habitats, civil records, schooling, inheritance, burial law, and local elections make permanent residence possible.

## Colonial Recognition

The Union recognizes the colony only after independent safety review confirms:

- redundant pressure and power;
- locally maintained water and oxygen reserves;
- medical stabilization capacity;
- an operable evacuation plan;
- resident civil records;
- elected local representation;
- a legal path from mission command to civil government.

Mars begins with a right to eventual self-government written into its mission charter. It also begins materially dependent on Gilgamesh for traffic windows, rescue policy, appropriations, and recognition.

The colony therefore inherits Luna's central political contradiction: local law can define a home, but local law cannot move the ships or guarantee the replacement systems that keep the home alive.
