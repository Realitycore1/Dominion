# Manuscript 2 - Draft 1 Skeleton

> [!warning] Superseded
> This six-part, thirty-six-chapter version has been replaced by [[Amber Book II - The Final Line - Definitive Skeleton]]. GalFed open war, Operation Prescience, Aether, the Primus Legions, and the Great Crusade now continue in [[Amber Book III - Operation Prescience - Development Spine]].

## Working Position

Manuscript 2 is the New World War novel and the first GalFed recontact novel.

It begins with Harmina already transformed from colony into civilization. It opens at the moment that civilization breaks: the bombing of New Aurora during the Founder Clause debates.

The visible conflict is a Harminan civil war. Beneath it, older institutions are preparing for GalFed's return with evidence too incomplete to reveal cleanly and too dangerous to ignore. Inside both conflicts, biological and synthetic people are being redesigned, corrupted, repaired, and legally reclassified by total war.

The manuscript follows the first strike through late-war unification, Amber's reluctant acceptance of continuity authority, and GalFed's formal arrival. The Covenant of Syl'an creates the structure that will later harden into the Dominion. GalFed then tests that structure through aid, observers, legal harmonization, development guidance, and demilitarization proposals rather than immediate invasion.

This is not a clean military campaign novel. It is a civilization-under-pressure novel in which preparation can save a people and deform them at the same time.

## Draft 1 Target

Target length: 110,000-140,000 words.

Draft structure:

- Prologue;
- 6 major parts;
- 36 core chapters based on Book III and Book IV, with one new formal-contact chapter;
- Epilogue.

Average chapter length:

- 2,800-4,000 words for standard chapters;
- 4,500-6,000 words for major turning points;
- shorter interstitial chapters allowed for documents, casualty records, broadcasts, field reports, and testimony.

## Core Promise

The reader should feel that the New World War was not inevitable because people were stupid.

It became inevitable because every side had a real fear, a real grievance, a real survival doctrine, and enough weapons to turn those truths into catastrophe.

On reread, the reader should also recognize that Harmina's apparently excessive defenses, strange procurement priorities, family warnings, and classified programs were fragments of one long preparation. The elders did not know everything. They knew enough.

## Central Narrative Conflict

Harmina's nation-states must decide whether founder legitimacy, local sovereignty, external-defense preparation, bodily autonomy, synthetic personhood, and survival law can coexist after the bombing of New Aurora.

The visible war asks who may govern Harmina.

The hidden war asks who may know that GalFed is returning and what may be built in secret to resist it.

The internal war asks whether a civilization may alter bodies, memories, and identities to keep those people free.

The war answers badly.

The covenant answers imperfectly.

GalFed arrives with administrative answers of its own.

The Dominion begins as the attempt to resist external management without becoming a management system imposed on its own people.

## Primary Emotional Conflict

People who were raised to believe Harmina was home must watch that home become a battlefield.

Amber's conflict is not whether she wants power. She does not. Her conflict is that every faction wants to turn her existence into an answer.

Emilia's conflict is that she understands why people need Amber and also sees what that need does to the person she loves.

Kieran's conflict is generational. Harmina is home, Ferox is mythology, and GalFed is curriculum. He recognizes how old warnings preserve authority before he understands what those warnings preserve Harmina from.

Sera's conflict is local. Every planetary plan reaches her as rationing, transit priority, shelter law, medical allocation, or somebody else's soldiers.

Dr. Adams's conflict is biological. Adaptation may let humanity belong to Harmina, but war turns every therapy into a state capability.

The side cast carries the rest of the emotional field: soldiers, clerks, engineers, teachers, route families, archive workers, clone laborers, enhanced citizens, synthetic citizens, Project Ra technicians, and children who inherit adult decisions as geography.

## Primary Thematic Conflict

Survival versus freedom is too simple.

The manuscript should push harder:

- survival without memory becomes repetition;
- memory without mercy becomes grievance;
- sovereignty without restraint becomes war;
- unity without consent becomes captivity;
- preparedness without ethics becomes the thing it feared.

## Canonical Knowledge Rule

The original *Ardent Mercy* generation knew GalFed existed. The ship was detected, pursued, and driven toward a world GalFed had classified as too dangerous to approach.

Two and a half centuries later, that knowledge is stratified:

- Amber, Emilia, surviving command witnesses, and select continuity custodians remember direct fear;
- military families, intelligence offices, Project Ra personnel, and sealed archives inherit operational fragments;
- public institutions teach a simplified historical danger through Skyview Day and defense curriculum;
- younger Harminans know GalFed as an old word, distant polity, political justification, or founder story.

The mystery is not whether GalFed has ever existed. It is what contemporary evidence means, when GalFed will return, what it intends, and how much of Harmina's preparation was built for that return.

## Three-War Structure

### Visible War

States, cities, armies, orbital platforms, tunnels, rail corridors, deep routes, and industrial systems fight over Harmina.

### Hidden War

Project Ra, orbital defense, the space elevator, proto-Panoplies, gene therapies, AI combatants, cyborg doctrine, and anti-biological and anti-synthetic programs contain classified requirements for resistance to GalFed.

No faction possesses the whole design. Some leaders know, some suspect, some inherit unexplained procedure, and some exploit the fear.

### Internal War

Mortals, Immortals, enhanced humans, clone laborers, cyborgs, autonomous systems, and artificial intelligences become battlefields. The novel treats memory corruption, gene locks, regeneration sabotage, forced reboot, body-lock attacks, and treatment dependency as violations experienced by people rather than as technical spectacle.

## Warning and Reveal Rule

The warnings must be obvious in hindsight and deniable beforehand.

Early clues should appear as:

- family sayings;
- old survivor stories;
- overbuilt asteroid defenses;
- missing probes;
- strange transmission fragments;
- sealed navigation exclusions;
- language matrices with no recognized speaker;
- procurement designed for capabilities no Harminan rival possesses;
- legal forms for an external authority with no embassy.

The recurring warning is:

> The old warnings said the sky would come wearing laws instead of fire.

Do not explain it fully before GalFed arrives.

## Pacing Rule

Every major war escalation must be followed by a human consequence chapter.

Pattern:

1. Event.
2. System response.
3. Civilian consequence.
4. Moral or political fracture.
5. External-warning echo.

Avoid stacking battle after battle without showing food, transit, clinics, schools, mourning, propaganda, dissent, repair, and records.

Never explain a classified program before showing the person who operates, depends on, repairs, or is harmed by it.

## Program Double Meanings

| Program | Public wartime purpose | Classified external-defense purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Proto-Panoplies | Heavy infantry for cities, tunnels, and hostile terrain | Space infantry against a technologically superior external force |
| Project Ra | Asteroid detection and interception | Anti-orbital coercion, deterrence, and GalFed approach warning |
| Gene therapies | Survival medicine and battlefield recovery | Proto-legionary endurance against unknown environments and weapons |
| AI combatants | Command efficiency and casualty reduction | Resistance to alien electronic dominance and administrative capture |
| Cyborg troops | Casualty recovery and continued service | Adaptable soldiers for unfamiliar systems and interfaces |
| Space elevator | Economic orbital access | Controlled planetary mobilization and defense logistics |
| Anti-synthetic doctrine | Enemy AI suppression | Defense against GalFed machine governance and identity capture |
| Anti-biological doctrine | Rival-faction deterrence | Defense against alien biocontrol and species-targeted attack |

## Proto-Legionary Rule

The early treatments may provide radiation resistance, accelerated clotting, stronger muscle and bone, conditioned reflexes, trauma suppression, oxygen efficiency, anti-toxin metabolism, synthetic-organ compatibility, and armor-interface adaptation.

They also impose chronic pain, immune instability, emotional flattening, fertility consequences, shortened unsupported lifespan, identity dissociation, medical dependency, rejection episodes, and difficulty returning to civilian life.

The later Dominion Legionary must feel inherited from this brutal medical lineage rather than invented fully formed.

Emotional movement: skepticism into shock, then emergency legitimacy.

Purpose:

- establish that children know the word GalFed but not the founders' fear;
- show Project Ra publicly as asteroid defense and privately as a watch for controlled approach;
- let a child repeat the old warning about the sky wearing laws as a joke;
- show orbital defense as civic infrastructure before it becomes civil-war and external-defense infrastructure;
- create quiet dread before the bombing.

Ending turn:

The technician logs an ordinary anomaly, notices that it satisfies a threat criterion missing from the public manual, and returns to the children's questions because Harmina has too many warnings to fear all of them.

## Draft 1 Priorities

### Must Have

- New Aurora bombing as the inciting wound.
- Amber resisting being converted into state machinery.
- Emilia as witness, conscience, and emotional counterweight.
- At least one humanizing POV from each major opposing bloc.
- Civilian systems: food, trains, clinics, schools, shelters, archives, courts.
- Kieran, Sera, and Dr. Adams carrying generational, local, and biological perspectives.
- The Body Question under war pressure through anti-biological, anti-Immortal, and anti-synthetic attacks.
- Proto-legionaries whose capabilities and permanent costs both appear in scene.
- Public and classified purposes for Project Ra, proto-Panoplies, orbital infrastructure, gene therapies, and synthetic doctrine.
- GalFed evidence returning in Chapter 18 without a formal delegation arriving yet.
- Formal GalFed contact after Syl'Cothan unity, delivered through aid and administrative agreements.
- Artemis and Craterheart as late-war strategic axes.
- Covenant of Syl'an as a morally compromised victory.

### Avoid

- battle summaries without lived consequence;
- villains who know they are villains;
- Amber solving every institutional problem by appearing;
- treating unity as obviously correct from the start;
- using GalFed contact as a sudden twist with no thematic preparation;
- making the old warnings so precise that younger skepticism becomes implausible;
- treating biological or synthetic violation as a catalogue of technology rather than a person's experience;
- presenting GalFed first as an openly hostile invasion fleet;
- letting cool weapons replace grief, logistics, and political cost.

## Draft 1 Success Test

The manuscript succeeds if the reader can understand why the Dominion was created without feeling that the Dominion was clean, inevitable, or morally simple.

The war should feel avoidable in every individual scene and inevitable only in hindsight.

The external reveal succeeds if readers finish with one judgment:

> They knew. Not completely. But enough.
