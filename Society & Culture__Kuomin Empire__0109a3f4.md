Life Under [[The Mandate]]

The [[Kuomin Empire]]’s society is a carefully choreographed blend of imperial heritage and party discipline. Citizens live at the crossroads of myth and modernity, raised to believe that they are both subjects of an eternal empire and members of a revolutionary movement.
	•	The Citizen-Subject
	•	People are called Guomin (“people of the nation”), but propaganda also calls them Children of Heaven.
	•	[[Dominion Omniverse/Dominion Multiverse/Eos Verse/Dominion Verse/Verse Structure/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/Post-WW3/Daily life|Daily life]] is saturated with party slogans and imperial symbols—portraits of the Emperor-President hang beside ancestral tablets in homes.
	•	Citizens are expected to bow both to the family altar and to the party banner each morning.
	•	Family as Foundation
	•	Families are the basic political unit. Parents are accountable for the ideological purity of their household.
	•	Ancestral veneration is encouraged, but always tied to the state: ancestors are framed as “links in the unbroken chain leading to [[The Mandate]].”
	•	Intermarriage across provincial and ethnic lines is promoted to dissolve factionalism and reinforce unity.
	•	Education & Indoctrination
	•	Children attend Party-Mandarin Schools, where they study Confucian classics alongside party [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Interstice City/Doctrine|Doctrine]] and modern sciences.
	•	Moral lessons are drawn from Analects, but always reinterpreted through Integral Party texts.
	•	Teachers are treated as semi-sacred, regarded as “Voices of [[The Mandate]].”
	•	[[Ritual]] & Ceremony
	•	Mandate Festivals: held annually, reenacting the Emperor-President’s enthronement with processions, banners, and choreographed pageantry.
	•	Ancestor-Parades: families carry tablets through the streets, followed by [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Empyrean Continuum/The Empyrearchy/Military|Military]] marches, tying kinship to national destiny.
	•	The Thousand Lanterns: night festivals where paper lanterns (and holographic equivalents) are released into orbit, symbolizing the souls of citizens rising to Heaven.
	•	[[Cultural Expression]]
	•	Art & Aesthetics: calligraphy, ink painting, and imperial motifs coexist with party [[Iconography]]. Red and gold dominate, accented with black party banners.
	•	Music: combines classical Chinese instruments (guqin, erhu, drums) with [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Empyrean Continuum/The Empyrearchy/Military|Military]] brass. Marches often begin as ancient hymns before swelling into party anthems.
	•	Architecture: starports and palaces mirror imperial palaces—tiered roofs, sweeping arches, [[Dragon]] motifs—but built on planetary scales, fused with orbital [[Infrastructure]].
	•	Social Discipline
	•	Citizens are required to demonstrate loyalty daily: reciting Mandate Oaths in schools and workplaces.
	•	Surveillance is omnipresent, though framed as “Heaven’s Watchful Eye.”
	•	Public shaming rituals replace some punishments: dissenters are paraded through squares and forced to confess their failure before the Emperor-President’s image.
