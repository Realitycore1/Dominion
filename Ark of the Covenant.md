The Ark of the Covenant is described in various religious sources as a small wooden chest, usually about 30 inches long and 20 inches [[Wide]], covered with gold leaf and adorned with two cherubim figures on the lid. According to Exodus 25:10-22, the Ark was made from acacia wood and was lined with pure gold. It was decorated with two cherubim made of gold, with their wings spread out over the lid, covering it completely. Inside the Ark, there were three items: 1. The stone tablets containing the Ten Commandments, given by God to Moses on Mount Sinai. [[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]. The "mane of Aaron," which was the blood of a goat that was placed on Aaron's head when he performed his duties as a priest. [[Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)|Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)]]. A jar of manna, which is said to have been given to the Israelites as food during their [[Time]] in the desert. The Ark was accompanied by two poles, which were used to carry it during its journey through the desert. According to Exodus 25:17, these poles were overlaid with gold and had rings on them for carrying purposes. Despite being a small object, the Ark was held in great reverence and respect by the Israelites. It was considered a sacred relic and was kept in the Tabernacle, a portable temple that accompanied them during their travels. Many miracles are attributed to the Ark, including the splitting of the Red Sea and the downfall of the walls of Jericho. However, it was ultimately lost in ancient times, and its whereabouts are unknown today.
