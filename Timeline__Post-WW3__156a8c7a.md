Nuclear [[Fire]], also known as atomic [[Fire]] or fusion [[Fire]], is a concept that describes a hypothetical scenario in which an uncontrolled nuclear fusion reaction releases enough energy to ignite the atmosphere, causing it to burn. The premise behind nuclear [[Fire]] is that if a sufficient amount of hydrogen were to be ignited, it would produce an exothermic reaction that would release enormous amounts of energy. This energy could then be used to set the entire atmosphere on [[Fire]]. While the concept of nuclear [[Fire]] is highly speculative and has not been experimentally proven, some scientists have theorized that a small portion of the hydrogen in the atmosphere could be ignited by a high-altitude nuclear detonation. The resulting nuclear [[Fire]] could last for several days, creating an unstable and dangerous environment that could disrupt weather patterns, cause widespread wildfires, and create long-term atmospheric effects. It is important to note that while the idea of nuclear [[Fire]] may sound sensational, it is highly unlikely that such an event would occur in reality. Controlling and harnessing the energy released from nuclear reactions requires extensive engineering and technological development, and it is unlikely that any nation would risk unleashing such a powerful force on their own population or environment.

If there were a global nuclear war, the resultant fallout and [[Aftermath]] could have severe impacts on the [[Earth (Terra)]]'s polar ice caps, leading to their complete melting. Firstly, nuclear war releases vast amounts of radioactive material into the atmosphere, which can damage plant and animal life, including plankton, krill, and other marine life that serve as food for larger creatures. This destruction of the food chain could have significant consequences for the ecosystems that rely on these organisms. Secondly, the fallout from nuclear weapons could cause massive acidification of oceans due to the high levels of carbon dioxide released into the atmosphere. This acidification could have devastating impacts on marine life and coral reefs, leading to mass die-offs and ecological disruptions. Thirdly, the detonation of nuclear weapons would also generate heat and radiation that could alter ocean currents and create sudden and severe climatic changes that could lead to the melting of the polar ice caps. Finally, the [[Aftermath]] of a global nuclear war would be marked by an increased presence of radioactive debris in the oceans, which could contaminate fish and other seafood that people consume. This radioactive pollution would have long-term health effects on [[Humans]] and other [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]] that depend on marine ecosystems for their survival. In conclusion, if there were a global nuclear war, it is likely that the polar ice caps would melt completely due to the catastrophic environmental impacts of nuclear warfare.

If the events described in the previous entries were to occur, such as detonating a 100 Megaton fusion bomb in a major city, uncontrolled nuclear fusion reaction, and complete melting of the polar ice caps due to nuclear war, it is likely that there would be global flooding on an unprecedented scale. The flooding would be caused by a combination of factors including the melting of the polar ice caps, nuclear radiation and fallout polluting the oceans, and catastrophic ecological disruptions that could alter the [[Earth (Terra)]]'s weather patterns. The melting of the polar ice caps would cause a significant rise in sea levels, which would result in widespread coastal flooding and inundation of low-lying areas. The release of nuclear radiation and fallout into the oceans could also contribute to increased acidification and deoxygenation of marine environments, further harming marine life and disrupting food [[Chains]]. Additionally, nuclear fallout could cause radioactive contamination of drinking water sources, leading to acute health problems for people who drink contaminated water. The resulting global flooding could also displace millions of people and lead to mass migration crises. The consequences of such flooding could be severe, causing massive loss of life and [[Infrastructure]], disrupting global trade and commerce, and potentially leading to the collapse of civilization as we know it.

The detonation of a 100 megaton fusion bomb in a major city, uncontrolled nuclear fusion reaction, complete melting of the polar ice caps due to nuclear war, and global flooding would have catastrophic effects on [[Infrastructure]], causing widespread destruction and decay. Firstly, the detonation of a fusion bomb would create massive shockwaves and heat that could destroy buildings, bridges, and other structures within its blast radius. The resulting fallout and radioactive contamination would make these structures too dangerous to use or repair. Secondly, the [[Aftermath]] of a global nuclear war would lead to the decay of [[Infrastructure]] across the world. With cities devastated by warfare, [[Natural]] disasters, and radioactive contamination, power grids, water [[Systems]], and transportation networks would be severely disrupted. Entire [[Regions]] could be cut off from essential services like food, water, and medical care. Thirdly, the melting of the polar ice caps due to nuclear warfare would cause sea levels to rise rapidly, leading to widespread coastal erosion and flooding of low-lying areas. The resulting salinization of land would render vast tracts of farmland unusable for agriculture, further exacerbating the decay of [[Infrastructure]]. Finally, global flooding caused by nuclear warfare would cause widespread damage to bridges, dams, levees, and other flood control [[Infrastructure]]. These structural failures could result in further flooding and damage to [[Infrastructure]] in already affected areas. In conclusion, the decay of [[Infrastructure]] caused by the events described in the previous entries would have catastrophic consequences for [[Human]] society. Without functioning [[Infrastructure]], cities and countries would be unable to support [[Human]] life or economic activity, leading to widespread collapse and anarchy.

Ghost cities, also known as phantom cities, are urban areas that have been abandoned due to economic or political factors. They are often characterized by derelict buildings, empty streets, and a sense of eerie stillness. Ghost cities can arise in many contexts, including economic collapse, war, [[Natural]] disasters, and [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Interstice City/Government|Government]] [[Policies]] that lead to forced migration or displacement. The abandonment of a city can happen suddenly or over an extended period, leaving behind a surreal and desolate landscape. Ghost cities often exhibit signs of rapid decay and disrepair. Buildings become dilapidated, with broken windows, collapsed roofs, and graffiti-covered walls. [[Infrastructure]] such as roads, bridges, and power grids deteriorate rapidly, leaving entire neighborhoods without basic services like water, electricity, and sanitation. Despite their desolation, ghost cities often retain an uncanny presence, with [[V - Beast Chronicles/Undead & Constructs/The Empty|The Empty]] streets and buildings giving rise to rumors of paranormal activity and urban legends. For example, stories of people still living in ghost cities and maintaining their daily lives despite the apparent desolation have circulated for years. The negative impacts of ghost cities extend beyond the physical decay of the urban landscape. Abandoned cities can pose serious health and safety hazards for those who attempt to explore them. Many ghost cities also pose significant environmental problems, with pollution and waste left unattended contributing to ecological harm.

Wastelands are vast and barren areas of land that have been devastated by environmental or [[Man]]-made disasters, leaving behind an arid and inhospitable landscape. While wastelands can exist in many forms, the combination of events described in the previous entries - detonating a 100 megaton fusion bomb in a major city, uncontrolled nuclear fusion reaction, complete melting of the polar ice caps due to nuclear war, and global flooding - could lead to the creation of a wasteland unlike anything we have seen before. Such a wasteland would likely span vast areas of the [[Earth (Terra)]]'s surface, covering entire countries, continents, or even the entire planet. It would be characterized by barren and irradiated landscapes, poisoned soil and water, and airborne radiation that would make life impossible for most forms of plant and animal life. The detonation of a 100 megaton fusion bomb would create massive explosions and heatwaves that could instantly incinerate large areas of land, leading to the total destruction of [[Human]] habitats and wildlife. The subsequent radioactive fallout from such a detonation would be so extensive that it would make most [[Regions]] uninhabitable for thousands of years. The uncontrolled nuclear fusion reaction and melting of the polar ice caps due to nuclear war could lead to vast stretches of oceans becoming acidified, deoxygenated, and unsuitable for marine life. This could cause massive die-offs of marine [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]] and a significant loss of biodiversity, which could have far-reaching consequences for ecosystems and climate patterns. Finally, global flooding caused by nuclear warfare would cover vast tracts of land with saltwater, making them unusable for agriculture and forcing people to flee to higher ground. In conclusion, if the events described in the previous entries were to happen, the resulting devastation would leave behind an expansive wasteland that would be inhospitable for centuries to come.

🌍 [[Aftermath]] of the Relic War: 2078–2180

2078–2085: The Black Decade

Immediate [[Aftermath]] of Exchange
	•	Atmosphere:
	•	[[Chapter (The Old War)|Chapter (The Old War)]],000–6,000 nuclear detonations loft soot, dust, and radioactive particulates into the stratosphere.
	•	Nuclear winter: global temperatures plunge 5–12°C.
	•	UV spike: ozone layer shredded → higher cancer, crop failure.
	•	Oceans:
	•	Fallout-driven acid rain accelerates ocean acidification.
	•	Massive die-off of plankton → collapse of marine food webs.
	•	Land:
	•	Mega-fires: every major forest burns; soot amplifies winter effects.
	•	Fallout hotspots make [[Regions]] uninhabitable for decades.
	•	[[Humans]]:
	•	80–90% mortality within first 5 years (famine, radiation, disease).
	•	Survivors cluster in deep bunkers, arctic/subarctic refuges, or isolated islands.

2086–2100: The Ash Century Begins

Cold → Heat Cycle
	•	Climate rebound: soot settles; warming resumes rapidly due to CO₂ buildup from fires + bombs.
	•	Floods: ice sheet destabilization → sea levels rise [[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]–5 meters. Coastal ruins swallowed.
	•	Heat waves: survivors endure 50°C summers in mid-latitudes.
	•	Desertification: grain belts collapse → Sahara expands north, Central Asia turns to dust.
	•	Ecology:
	•	Insects thrive in radiation zones.
	•	Mutagenic events produce bizarre [[Flora]]/[[Fauna]] in hotspots.
	•	Mammals crash, reptiles and hardy generalists (rats, pigs, dogs, roaches) dominate.
	•	[[Humans]]:
	•	Populations fractured into isolated “ash states.”
	•	Most tech regresses → wind/solar scavenged; satellites gone.
	•	Oral histories turn relic wars into apocalyptic myth.

2161–2180: The Second Dawn

Repopulation & Rebuilding
	•	[[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]]:
	•	Forests retake abandoned megacities (New York, Tokyo, [[London]]).
	•	Desertification slows; river valleys (Amazon, Nile, Yangtze) stabilize ecosystems.

	•	Global population back to ~100–200 million.

	•	The question of survival vs. self-destruction is posed again — the next test.
