Sequoyah replaces Oklahoma

The original Five Civilized Tribes proposal succeeds.
The federal government—eager to stabilize the frontier—allows statehood.

Population makeup:
	•	Majority: Cherokee, Creek, Choctaw, Chickasaw, Seminole
	•	Minorities: African-American freedmen descendants, whites in border regions, some western tribes relocating voluntarily

Immediate effects:
	•	Land allotment is halted early → no Dust Bowl-scale agricultural collapse on tribal lands
	•	Oil rights remain majority tribally controlled
	•	Sequoyah becomes resource-rich and stubbornly independent

This state enters the Union angry, wealthy, and deeply suspicious of Washington.

⸻

2. WWII–Cold War Era (1940s–1970s)

Sequoyah becomes a strange mixture of:

✔ Strategic asset

Military bases, testing ranges, and espionage programs run through state territory. Tribal sovereignty gives the Pentagon unusual leverage.

✔ Internal friction point

A state where federal power is heavily restricted.
Sequoyah challenges federal jurisdiction constantly.
Supreme Court cases pile up.

✔ Civil Rights crosscurrents

Native sovereignty + African-American political movements =
A powerful multiracial coalition unusual for the South.

Sequoyah becomes a magnet for dissidents, activists, and separatists.

Washington always keeps a nervous eye on it.

⸻

3. The Energy Era (1970s–2000s)

This is where Sequoyah becomes a major geopolitical player inside the U.S.

✔ Oil, natural gas, and rare minerals

Most remain tribally owned.
Sequoyah becomes wealthy, like a smaller Texas with sovereignty layered on top.

✔ Native-controlled petro-banking sector

Wealth generates tribal investment funds, parallel courts, and diplomatic missions to foreign powers.

✔ Huge military recruitment

Native veterans return with elite training → Sequoyah forms the most capable National Guard force in the country.

This sets the stage for what happens during the American collapse.

⸻

4. Pre–2nd American Civil War (2030s–2050s)

Your timeline has:
	•	deep-state manipulation
	•	biowarfare lockdowns
	•	collapse of major cities
	•	militarized borders
	•	ideologically fractured states
	•	PMCs as power brokers
	•	nationalist uprisings
	•	the rise of the Constitutionalist forces
	•	Capitol bombing
	•	the rebirth of the UAR

Sequoyah behaves differently from every other state.

⸻

5. Sequoyah During the American Collapse

A. Initially Neutral — but heavily armed

Sequoyah refuses to enforce federal lockdown orders during engineered pandemic waves.
They seal internal tribal borders and operate independently.

B. PMCs attempt infiltration

Private military companies—now standard in pre-WW3 proxy warfare—try to secure Sequoyah’s oil fields.
They fail.
The Sequoyah National Guard repels them brutally.

Sequoyah becomes a “safe-zone state,” like early Texas but more unified.

C. Constitutionalists quietly court Sequoyah

The nationalist-constitutionalist proto-army looks for allies.
They see Sequoyah as:
	•	anti-elite
	•	anti-corruption
	•	anti-globalist ideologue
	•	fiercely constitutional
	•	militarily competent
	•	culturally intact

Sequoyah’s leaders remember centuries of federal betrayal—
but the Constitutionalists promise restoration of tribal sovereignty and constitutional originalism.

It works.

Sequoyah becomes a logistical hub and recruitment bastion for the uprising.

Its special forces fight in several early battles.

⸻

6. Sequoyah During the 2nd American Civil War

When the Capitol bombing wipes out Congress,
and the Constitutionalists storm D.C. to overthrow the globalist regime,
Sequoyah does not secede.

Instead it takes a stance similar to:

“We are part of America.
But not part of your America.”

Sequoyah:
	•	protects its borders
	•	provides covert military support to Constitutionalists
	•	shuts out federal agents
	•	becomes a crucial supply artery in the collapse of the old regime
	•	prevents foreign PMCs from penetrating the interior

By the time the regime falls, Sequoyah is seen as a savior state by the nationalist faction.

⸻

7. Formation of the United American Republic (UAR)

When the U.S. is reborn as the UAR, Sequoyah’s position is unprecedented.

✔ Full state

✔ Full sovereignty

✔ Tribal legal systems integrated into UAR constitution

✔ Permanent seat on the new Senate security council

✔ Autonomous military command structure

✔ Significant influence over reconstruction and refugee policy

Sequoyah becomes:
	•	the UAR’s “cultural anchor”
	•	the moral symbol of restored constitutional values
	•	a living reminder of federal overreach
	•	a stabilizer in the Midwest-South corridor
	•	a recruitment center for the new Federal Guard
