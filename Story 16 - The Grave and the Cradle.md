# Story 16 - The Grave and the Cradle

## Viewpoint

Tomas Revik and a young midwife, alternating through a single night shift.

## Setting

Years 41-93 AL, New Aurora memorial wall and adjacent birthing clinic.

## Story Shape

A birth alarm and a memorial alarm sound within minutes of each other. Tomas registers a death while the midwife assists a Harminan-born child entering the world during acid rain.

The two records are filed in neighboring systems: one preserving a name from before Harmina, one assigning a child a place in the world that killed and sheltered their ancestors.

## Harmina Element

Harmina becomes both grave and cradle in civic procedure, not only metaphor.

## Character Movement

Tomas stops thinking of memorial work as backward-looking. The midwife stops thinking birth means freedom from the past.

## Future Payoff

This story anchors the collection's central image: civilization is built by keeping records for both the dead and the newborn.
