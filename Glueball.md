I. Physical Definition

In real-world particle [[Physics]], a glueball is a composite particle consisting solely of gluons — the gauge bosons responsible for the strong force. Since gluons themselves carry color charge, they can interact with each other, allowing the formation of bound states independent of quark content. Glueballs are predicted by Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD) but remain experimentally elusive due to mixing with mesonic states.

Within the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), glueballs are regarded as stable quark-independent QCD resonance nodes — localized, self-contained packets of strong-force lattice energy. They function as structural keystones in the QCD [[Verse]] Mesh, capable of persisting even in [[Exotic Matter]] environments or in quark-deconfined conditions.
	•	Spin: Integer (commonly 0 or [[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]] for lowest-energy states)
	•	Mass: Variable — typically in the 1–[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]] GeV range for lowest Modes, but can reach TeV scale in high-energy [[VFF]]-modified [[Domains]].
	•	Charge: 0 (color neutral overall)
