# Dominion Overview - Canon Spine

## Core Definition

The Dominion is a dual-government survival civilization built to preserve mankind across collapse, distance, war, metaphysical instability, and long-horizon existential threats.

It is not only an empire, not only a republic, and not only a sacred monarchy. It is a civilization that separates powers by time horizon.

The Federal Government governs the living present.

The Royal Government guards the endangered future.

The Dominion's central conflict is not simply freedom versus tyranny. It is the harder question of how a civilization protects ordinary life today without sacrificing the future, and how it protects the future without consuming ordinary life.

## Origin

The Dominion grows from a chain of ruptures:

1. Earth breaks and rebuilds.
2. The Colonial Union expands order across distance.
3. Equilon refuses absorption or subordination.
4. Revolution, Ark displacement, Harmina, and later wars teach mankind that survival can fail through poor memory, poor provision, poor transit, and poor preparation.
5. Harmina's proto-nations turn survival habits into distinct political cultures.
6. The New World War proves that local identity, strategic fear, and long-horizon weapons can tear a civilization apart.
7. Continuity hardens into doctrine.
8. Doctrine becomes institution.
9. Institution becomes ritual.
10. Ritual becomes beauty, and beauty risks hiding the wound that made it necessary.

The Dominion's grandeur should always point back to this wound.

## Government Type

The Dominion is a dual government.

### Federal Government

The Federal Government is a service-franchised constitutional republic responsible for ordinary civil life.

It governs:

- rights;
- courts;
- budgets;
- taxation;
- public services;
- provincial administration;
- state and local law;
- public health;
- education;
- ordinary infrastructure;
- civil security;
- federal agencies;
- routine military oversight;
- citizenship and franchise.

It is best at immediacy, representation, accountability, and local reality.

It is weak at centuries-long planning, existential preparation, reality-stability threats, and decisions whose consequences arrive after current political actors are gone.

### Royal Government

The Royal Government is the continuity government responsible for mankind's long future.

It governs:

- continuity doctrine;
- existential threat planning;
- civilizational memory;
- succession integrity;
- sacred-civic rites;
- VFF and MOT risk;
- relic and forbidden-technology containment;
- long-horizon defense;
- restoration after collapse;
- species-level evacuation and preservation.

It is best at memory, strategic patience, continuity, and crisis beyond normal politics.

It is weak at daily life, local consent, and the ordinary scale of human happiness when its plans become too abstract.

## Amber's Authority

Amber Zohair is the sovereign center of the Royal Government and the living holder of continuity authority.

Her power is absolute in law, ritual, emergency, and myth, but it is not meant to govern every daily matter. Amber's authority exists for the long horizon, final appeal, existential crisis, continuity legitimacy, and moral burden of decisions no ordinary institution can safely own alone.

Amber willingly accepts forms of restraint because unshaped absolute power can destroy the continuity it exists to defend.

Her greatest political danger is not personal ambition. It is that ministries, rituals, security organs, or admirers may invoke her image until the person disappears inside the office.

## Federal Resistance

Federal resistance is lawful.

The Federal Government may resist the Royal Government without being treated as an enemy when it is defending:

- civil rights;
- due process;
- ordinary jurisdiction;
- public record;
- proportionality;
- witness requirements;
- review of emergency power;
- local life from excessive abstraction.

Federal resistance becomes dangerous when it uses procedure to deny genuine existential risk.

Royal override becomes dangerous when it uses continuity to bypass ordinary accountability.

The Dominion survives by making this conflict lawful rather than forcing all opposition into rebellion.

## Citizenship and Franchise

All sapient persons under Dominion law possess civil rights and legal personhood.

The franchise is earned through public service. Public service may be military, civil, medical, educational, infrastructural, judicial, environmental, research-based, frontier, or emergency-response oriented.

The distinction is:

- Civilian: full civil rights, no voting or office eligibility.
- Citizen: full civil rights plus franchise earned through service.

This system is justified by the Dominion as stewardship: those who direct public power must first carry public duty.

Its danger is status stratification. Service can become a moral gate, a class filter, or a quiet way of treating non-franchised persons as lesser despite formal rights.

## Religion and Civil Religion

The Dominion protects private belief and worship while denying clerical institutions formal political franchise.

Faith may shape culture, art, household ritual, mourning, festivals, music, and moral imagination. It may not sit as a church inside state power.

This principle is called Faith Without Franchise.

The Royal Government uses sacred-civic ritual, but the state is not meant to become a theocracy. The distinction is important:

- religion may console and bind communities;
- ritual may preserve continuity memory;
- but no clerical hierarchy may govern by divine claim.

The danger is hypocrisy. A state that denies religion political power while sacralizing its sovereign must constantly prove that ritual is not just theocracy by another path.

## Economy

The mature Dominion is broadly post-scarcity for basic needs, but not post-conflict.

Food, shelter, healthcare, education, common tools, ordinary transit, and basic information access are guaranteed by provision systems.

Scarcity moves into:

- trust;
- status;
- prestige;
- access;
- command;
- specialized service;
- rare materials;
- attention;
- influence;
- existential-risk infrastructure;
- identity and memory integrity.

The economy is best understood through the Three Great Circulations:

- Provision: food, water, medicine, shelter, seedstock, and relief.
- Energy: grids, rings, transit power, fusion, stellar capture, and reserves.
- Memory: archives, identity, treaties, records, names, and testimony.

The Dominion becomes unjust when access to purpose, status, or command replaces money as a new form of class control.

## Military

The Dominion military exists to preserve continuity, not merely to conquer.

At its best, it protects transit, evacuation, archives, frontier settlements, reality-stability zones, and species survival. At its worst, it can make every threat look existential and every military action look sacred.

Military doctrine should remain tied to cost:

- What does this force preserve?
- What wound made it necessary?
- What does it cost to maintain?

The military is dangerous when symbolism outruns accountability.

## Daily Life

The Dominion is not only throne rooms, legions, sanctuaries, and megastructures.

A Dominion citizen's life should include:

- service records;
- family ledgers;
- public transit;
- rationless but reputation-sensitive economies;
- civic festivals;
- school collapse histories;
- ordinary jobs chosen for purpose rather than survival wages;
- rights audits;
- household water rites;
- medical maintenance;
- local arguments with ministries;
- religious practice without formal political franchise;
- anxiety about surveillance, status, and service expectations.

Daily life should show the Dominion's promise and pressure at the same time.

## Inquisition / Integrity Corps

The Integrity Corps is not ordinary police and must not become secret police in current canon.

It exists to investigate continuity threats: corruption, constitutional subversion, ontological instability, hostile memetics, forbidden technologies, and existential sabotage.

It is constrained by:

- warrants;
- courts;
- Inspector-General review;
- legislative oversight;
- public metrics;
- whistleblower channels;
- strict threat thresholds;
- prohibition on political policing;
- prohibition on indefinite detention;
- prohibition on memory erasure as punishment.

If it violates these limits, the story should treat that as institutional corruption, not normal function.

## Core Tension

The Dominion is strongest when the Federal Government and Royal Government are both strong enough to resist each other.

If the Federal Government dominates, the Dominion becomes short-sighted and vulnerable to slow catastrophe.

If the Royal Government dominates, the Dominion becomes ceremonial autocracy and sacrifices the living present for the imagined future.

The Dominion is stable only when now and future remain in conflict without becoming enemies.

## Page Test

Every major Dominion note should answer:

1. What does this preserve?
2. What wound made it necessary?
3. What does it cost to maintain?

If a page answers only the first question, it is probably decoration rather than story material.
