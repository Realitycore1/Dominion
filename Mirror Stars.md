I. Physical Definition

A Mirror Star is a stellar-scale object whose surface or outer plasma envelope exhibits extremely high reflectivity (near 100% albedo), functioning more like a colossal optical mirror than a conventional radiating sphere.
The star may still generate energy internally, but its visible light output is predominantly reflected and redirected rather than emitted directly.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Reflective Plasma Shell — Composed of exotic ions, metallic hydrogen, or engineered nanostructures aligned to act as a coherent reflective surface.
	•	Directional Luminosity Control — Can focus or redirect light to specific [[Regions]], potentially across interstellar distances.
	•	Thermal Balance Regulation — Manages internal heat via selective infrared transparency or exotic cooling mechanisms.
	•	Optical Camouflage or Amplification — Can appear invisible against space or act as an intense directional beacon.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Mirror [[Stars]] are categorized as Photonic Control Anchors, capable of manipulating light for signaling, energy delivery, or defensive concealment.
