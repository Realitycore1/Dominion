I. Physical Definition

In speculative [[Physics]], a Black Star is a hypothetical ultra-compact object whose density is so extreme that it nearly forms an event horizon, but does not fully cross the threshold into becoming a black hole.
Its radius is marginally larger than the Schwarzschild radius for its mass, producing extreme gravitational redshift and making it appear nearly invisible except for faint emissions escaping from just above the not-quite-horizon.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), black [[Stars]] are recognized as stable [[Singulon]]-anchored gravitational nodes that skirt the collapse [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Oneiroverse Multiverse/Chorus of Archetypes/Boundary]].
They can be naturally occurring in certain axiom-roots or deliberately engineered for horizonless singularity-mimicking applications, where one wants black hole–like effects without the one-way causality of an event horizon.
	•	Core State: Ultra-compressed baryonic or [[Exotic Matter]] at near-horizon density.
	•	Radiation Profile: Extremely redshifted emission; often mistaken for true black holes.
	•	Stability Mechanism: Balance between gravitational collapse and exotic field pressure (e.g., [[Phaseon]] or [[Nulliton]] [[Layers]]).
