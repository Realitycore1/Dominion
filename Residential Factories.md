Residential factories are a type of living arrangement that combines housing with a workplace. In this model, [[Workers]] live on-site in the factory and work there as well, often in shifts. There are several advantages to residential factories: Convenience: Residential factories save [[Time]] and money for [[Workers]], as they do not have to commute to work or find separate housing. This can be particularly useful for [[Workers]] who live far from their jobs or who are otherwise unable to afford their own housing. Efficiency: By having [[Workers]] live on-site, companies can more easily manage their workforce and increase productivity. This can be especially useful for industries that require 24/7 [[Production]] or where shifts are needed. Community building: Residential factories can also provide a sense of community and camaraderie among [[Workers]], who may otherwise feel isolated and disconnected from each other. This can be especially important for younger or immigrant [[Workers]] who may be new to the area or [[culture]]. However, residential factories also have some drawbacks: Isolation: Living and working in the same space can make it difficult for [[Workers]] to maintain a clear separation between their personal and professional lives, which can lead to feelings of burnout or exhaustion. Health and safety risks: Residential factories can also present health and safety risks, such as overcrowding, lack of ventilation, and exposure to toxic chemicals or hazardous materials. Labor issues: The living and working conditions in residential factories can sometimes be exploitative or oppressive, with [[Workers]] being required to work long hours in substandard conditions with little pay or benefits.
