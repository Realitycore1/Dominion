# (20) New Settlements

## Manuscript Source

- Primary source: [[Manuscript]] Draft 2, Part III, Chapters 15-19.
- Manuscript date range: Year 3 through Year 40 After Landfall.

## What Happened

The third-year survey charter began the outward push beyond New Aurora. Survey teams, ecological stations, weather towers, crawler routes, mapping expeditions, and temporary field camps tested whether human life could exist outside the first city's shelter systems.

The expansion was not yet nation-building. It was curiosity mixed with need: food, routes, safer geothermal corridors, underground chambers, coastal pressure zones, and places where children might grow up without every day feeling like an emergency.

## What Resulted

- Low Haven, Atlovaian pressure settlements, Commonwealth mobile communities, Ormova heatline precursors, Estelian archive routes, and other local identities began as practical outgrowths of terrain.
- Mega-train routes and crawler paths became political arteries.
- Settlement differences began as survival adaptations, then hardened into culture.
- New Aurora's map stopped being a diagram of one city and became a planetary argument.

## Consequences

The new settlements made later nationhood possible. They did not begin by rejecting New Aurora. They became different because Harmina required different solutions in different places.
