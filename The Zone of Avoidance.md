A band along the Milky Way’s plane where dust extinction and dense star fields obscure background galaxies. In optical surveys it creates a literal “gap” in maps of the extragalactic sky.

How [[Wide]]?
	•	Optical: typically |b| ≲ 5–10° (can swell to ~20° in the dustiest longitudes).
	•	Shrinks at longer λ: near-IR, mid-IR, radio (21-cm H I), and [[X-Ray]] penetrate dust and crowding better, reducing the effective ZoA.

Why it happens (observational [[Pain]]):
	•	Dust extinction (A_V): dims/erases galaxies.
	•	Confusion: high stellar density + nebular emission hide extended sources.
	•	Galactic lines/backgrounds: e.g., strong Milky Way H I complicates low-velocity 21-cm searches.

Workarounds & tools:
	•	Near-IR (2MASS), mid-IR (WISE): recover starlight-dominated galaxies behind dust.
	•	21-cm H I surveys: find gas-rich galaxies through the plane.
	•	[[X-Ray]]/SZ: detect clusters via hot intracluster gas.
	•	Velocity-field reconstructions: infer hidden mass from peculiar motions across the masked region.

Why it matters:
	•	The ZoA biases large-scale structure maps and flow models unless filled in.
	•	Filling it revealed [[Key]] structures (e.g., parts of Perseus–Pisces, the [[Great Attractor]]/Norma region) and improves our accounting of local cosmic flows (push–pull from voids and superclusters).
