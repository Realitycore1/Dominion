The assassination of President John F. Kennedy occurred on November 22, 1963, in Dallas, Texas. Kennedy was shot and killed while riding in an open-top convertible with his wife Jacqueline, alongside Vice President Lyndon B. Johnson. The assassination was the subject of intense investigation and conspiracy theories, with multiple suspects and theories surrounding the event. The most prominent suspect was Lee Harvey Oswald, who was arrested for the shooting and subsequently killed by Jack Ruby while in police custody. Despite extensive investigations, the assassination remains a matter of ongoing controversy and speculation. Many people believe that it was the result of a conspiracy involving multiple parties, including members of the US government and the CIA. The assassination had a profound impact on American society and politics, with many Americans feeling a sense of loss and grief over the death of their young and charismatic president. It also marked a turning point in US politics, with the Kennedy administration replaced by the Johnson administration and a new focus on civil rights and the Vietnam War. Today, the JFK assassination is remembered as a tragic event that still holds many unanswered questions about the nature of power, conspiracies, and democracy in America.
