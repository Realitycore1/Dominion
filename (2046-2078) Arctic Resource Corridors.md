# Arctic Resource Corridors

## Canonical Function

The melting Arctic creates shorter trade routes, longer shipping seasons, and access to rare earths, nickel, gas, uranium, and seabed minerals.

It does not create empty territory.

The opening routes cross:

- Sapmi seasonal lands and sensor networks;
- northern fishing grounds and settlements;
- competing continental-shelf claims;
- CRINK, Europolis, UAR, and corporate extraction zones;
- commercial weather systems that also serve military warning networks.

## Environmental Conditions

- Retreating ice exposes vessels to stronger open-water storms.
- Permafrost collapse bends roads, rails, pipelines, and foundations.
- Thaw releases buried contamination and destabilizes older waste sites.
- New ports become permanent settlements before housing and local consent systems catch up.
- Rescue infrastructure remains local even when extraction ownership is foreign.

## 2046 Northern Annex

At the Constantinople corridor conference, Sapmi representative Aila Risten presents two maps: the lived network of seasonal routes and stations, and a consortium map calling the same region an Emerging Access Zone.

The resulting northern annex:

- recognizes local weather, rescue, and contamination certificates;
- requires notice before military use of commercial sensors;
- prevents extraction permits from automatically superseding seasonal routes without local hearing.

The great powers do not sign the annex, but their companies cite the wider corridor protocols when demanding passage.

## 2058 Arctic Navigation Incident

The CRINK extraction ship *Collective Dawn*, a Europolis security escort, an American ore carrier, and the Sapmi tug *Rievssat* enter overlapping routes.

The ore carrier loses steering. Sapmi rescue control orders all vessels to hold. The tug intervenes to prevent collision with a fuel barge.

A CRINK defensive system fires during a Europolis drone approach. Pilot Nils Sarri is killed aboard the tug. Competing governments publish partial recordings. Sapmi releases the full station log, whose timing standard every bloc disputes.

The incident produces common distress signals, drone-separation rules, weapons limits, and a compensation fund. It does not settle resource ownership.

Traffic doubles the next summer.

## Strategic Consequence

Arctic communications, weather, navigation, and resource systems become inseparable from military early warning. By 2078, damage or silence in the Arctic can be interpreted simultaneously as commercial failure, sabotage, and preparation for attack.
