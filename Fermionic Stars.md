I. Physical Definition

A Fermionic Star is a stellar-scale object whose structural stability is maintained primarily by fermion degeneracy pressure — the quantum mechanical effect arising from the Pauli exclusion principle, which prevents identical fermions from occupying the same quantum state.
While white dwarfs (electron-degenerate) and neutron [[Stars]] (neutron-degenerate) are familiar [[Natural]] examples, the Fermionic Star category extends into hypothetical and engineered configurations using any fermion [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species]], including exotic quarks, leptons, or composite fermions.

	•	Degeneracy-Dominated Stability — Pressure resisting collapse is almost entirely quantum mechanical rather than thermal.
	•	High-Density Matter States — Mass densities can reach 10¹⁴–10¹⁸ kg/m³ depending on fermion type.
	•	Minimal Thermal Expansion — Degeneracy pressure is largely temperature-independent.
