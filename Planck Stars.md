I. Physical Definition

In speculative [[Physics]], particularly loop quantum gravity (LQG), a Planck Star is theorized to form when gravitational collapse reaches Planck-scale densities, at which point quantum-gravity effects generate a repulsive pressure that halts the collapse before a true singularity can form.
This halting produces an ultra-dense core that, over enormous [[Time]] scales (externally), rebounds and releases its trapped matter and energy in a massive outflow.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Planck [[Stars]] are classified as quantum-curvature bounce nodes — stellar-scale objects in which [[Singulon]] collapse is arrested by [[Verseon]] lattice quantum compression limits, mediated by [[Chronon]]-phase [[Time]] dilation.
From an external frame of reference, their “rebound” may take billions or trillions of years, while internally it could occur almost instantly.
	•	Core State: Matter at or near Planck density (~5×10⁹⁶ kg/m³).
	•	Stability Mechanism: Loop-quantized spacetime prevents further collapse.
	•	Appearance: From outside, similar to a black hole but with eventual explosive outflow.
