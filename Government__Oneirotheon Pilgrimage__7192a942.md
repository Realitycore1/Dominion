# Oneirotheon Fleet Governance  
**A Myth-Distributed Nomadic Polity**

The Oneirotheon do not govern through territory, law, or centralized command.  
They govern through **interpretive authority over meaning**.

Power is not the ability to issue orders.  
Power is the ability to determine **what the Pilgrimage currently signifies**.

Their government is deliberately non-centralized, tension-driven, and resistant to consolidation.  
It is designed to survive schism, contradiction, and loss without collapse.

At its core, governance is divided into three interlocking strata:

1. **Interpretation** — Somnarch Hierophants  
2. **Embodiment** — The Eternal Cast  
3. **Trajectory** — The Council of the Spark  

No stratum can rule alone without breaking the system.

---

## I. Somnarch Hierophants  
**Role:** Interpretive Authority  
**Function:** Dream interpretation, Consensus Slumber stability, local guidance

Somnarch Hierophants are the operational dream-priests of the Oneirotheon fleets.  
They do not issue doctrine. They interpret *states of meaning*.

### Responsibilities
- Interpret outputs of Consensus Slumber
- Identify emergent archetypal pressures
- Resolve dream-conflicts before they escalate into fleet schisms
- Guide ritual cadence and generational transitions within Living Fleets

Somnarchs are embedded within fleets, not elevated above them.

### Authority Model
- Influence flows upward through coherence, not downward through command  
- Multiple Somnarchs may hold contradictory interpretations simultaneously  
- Conflicting interpretations result in fleet slowing, divergence, or separation  

These outcomes are considered healthy rather than failures.

### Structural Limitations
Somnarch Hierophants cannot:
- Declare archetypes
- Assign Eternal Cast roles
- Set long-term pilgrimage destinations

They interpret meaning, but they cannot lock it into permanence.

---

## II. The Eternal Cast  
**Role:** Embodied Myth  
**Function:** Narrative stabilization through archetypal continuity

The Eternal Cast are fixed archetypal roles embodied across generations through magenta Aether fusion.

Common archetypes include:
- Hero  
- Tyrant  
- Betrayer  
- Martyr  
- Monster  
- Oracle  

Individuals change.  
Bodies die.  
The archetypes persist.

### Function in Governance
- Provide mythic continuity across generational drift
- Absorb extreme narrative pressure that would otherwise fracture populations
- Constrain behavior by forcing conflict into known symbolic forms

Crises are often reframed as archetypal activations:
- “The Betrayer has manifested”
- “The Martyr cycle is active”
- “The Tyrant archetype is rising”

This prevents abstract ideological drift by anchoring conflict in recognizable mythic structures.

### Political Paradox
The Eternal Cast:
- Cannot issue orders
- Cannot command fleets
- Cannot interpret Consensus Slumber

Yet no major decision is legitimate unless it aligns with an existing archetypal narrative.

They do not rule.  
They **limit what rulership can become**.

### Failure Mode
If an archetype becomes too dominant or revered, it risks:
- Ossification into doctrine
- De facto divinity
- External intervention by the Dominion or Infernariate

To prevent this, Cast roles are intentionally:
- Mortal
- Replaceable
- Often burdensome rather than exalted

---

## III. Council of the Spark  
**Role:** Strategic Trajectory  
**Function:** Long-cycle direction, shrine-world oversight, fleet-scale decisions

The Council of the Spark is the only governing body that operates on century-scale horizons.

It is composed of high-order dream-seers drawn from multiple fleets, factions, and interpretations.

The Council does not determine truth.  
It determines **which paths are legitimate to attempt**.

### Responsibilities
- Establish pilgrimage vectors
- Designate, maintain, or abandon shrine-worlds
- Authorize fleet convergence or dispersal
- Sanction high-risk mythic experiments (e.g., Singularity Doctrine trials)

### Decision Logic
- Consensus is not required
- Decisions are evaluated by mythic plausibility, not majority vote
- Minority dissent is preserved, recorded, and carried forward

A Council declaration means:
> “This path is now permissible.”

Not mandatory. Not final.

### Structural Limitations
The Council cannot:
- Force fleets to comply
- Override Consensus Slumber outcomes
- Declare final doctrine

A fleet may reject a Council decision and depart without sanction.

---

## IV. Actual Power Flow

Authority within the Oneirotheon follows this sequence:

**Dream State → Interpretation → Archetypal Framing → Directional Choice**

Not:
Command → Obedience → Enforcement

This makes decapitation strategies ineffective and renders external domination nearly impossible.

---

## V. The Primus Hierophant  
**Position:** Structural Mediator  
**Function:** Preventing doctrinal collapse

The Primus Hierophant exists outside the tri-stratum system, not above it.

He:
- Does not vote on the Council of the Spark
- Does not assign Eternal Cast roles
- Does not override Somnarch interpretations

Instead, he:
- Arbitrates when interpretations become mutually annihilating
- Declares when myths must be reframed rather than resolved
- Ensures no single structure becomes final or absolute

His greatest authority is restraint.

If the Primus Hierophant ever endorses a single interpretation as ultimate, the system collapses into dogma.

---

## VI. Suitability for Nomadic Civilization

This governance model ensures:
- No capital to conquer
- No law-code to overthrow
- No leader whose death ends the polity
- No doctrine rigid enough to fossilize

Schism is not failure.  
It is **mobility**.

A fractured fleet becomes a new pilgrimage thread.

---

## VII. External Assessment (Dominion Perspective)

From the Dominion’s viewpoint, the Oneirotheon are:
- Not rebels
- Not subjects
- Not a rival empire

They are a **mobile epistemic hazard**.

They are tolerated because:
- They self-limit
- They fracture instead of expand
- They generate valuable relics and mythic cartography

They are monitored because:
- Occasionally, they approach dangerous truths

---

## Core Principle

The Oneirotheon government is built on a single unspoken rule:

> **No one is allowed to be correct forever.**

This is how a civilization built on myth survives contact with reality.
