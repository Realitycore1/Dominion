🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: The Emerald Tablet of Hermes
Discovery Location: Al-Fayyum Oasis, Egypt, 2038, in a collapsed Greco-Roman catacomb during desertification mitigation works.
Summary:

Material: Polished green crystalline slab, 60 × 40 cm, veined with copper inclusions. Analysis suggests a form of ancient synthetic glass mixed with beryl, not true emerald.

Inscriptions: Greek and Demotic Egyptian script, with cryptic Hermetic aphorisms including variations of the famous line: “That which is above is like to that which is below.”

Anomalies:

Microscopic traces of mercury and antimony embedded in grooves, consistent with early alchemical practices.

UV scanning reveals hidden [[Layers]] of text beneath the surface — mathematical ratios resembling stellar alignments.

Archaeological Significance: If authenticated, bridges Egyptian priestly tradition with later Hellenistic Hermeticism and alchemy. Demonstrates early proto-scientific cosmology.
