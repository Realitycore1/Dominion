# Ritual Calendar of Continuity

## Summary

Continuity is not only imperial doctrine. It is practiced through public rites, household customs, military vows, archive funerals, and post-Severance safety rituals.

Because continuity means preservation of mankind at any cost, Dominion rites are not only beautiful. Many are forms of civic conditioning, emergency memory, and survival discipline.

## Major Public Rites

### Continuity Balcony Rite

Amber appears on the Continuity Balcony during a major alignment of sanctuary, city, orbit, and sky.

The crowd does not celebrate conquest. It recites restored worlds, rescued peoples, repaired habitats, and names preserved from extinction.

### Vigil of the Unreturned

A memorial night for vanished expeditions, lost fleets, failed colonies, disappeared habitats, unresolved VFF events, and missing persons.

Lights are left in windows, corridors, ship chapels, and archive halls. Families may speak names not yet confirmed dead.

### Feast of First Landing

Commemorates the first survival settlement after catastrophe, displacement, or restoration.

On core worlds it is historical and grand. On frontier worlds it is practical: shared food, repairs, oath renewals, and public accounting of the next year's needs.

### Day of Silent Archives

All public ceremonies pause for a fixed hour while archives, schools, courts, and households observe silence.

The silence says that continuity belongs to the dead and the forgotten as much as to the living state.

### Renewal of Broken Worlds

Held when a poisoned, abandoned, war-damaged, or ecologically collapsed world is formally restored to habitation.

The rite includes soil, water, witness records, and a public promise that restoration does not erase what happened there.

## Domestic Rites

### Hearth Ledger

Families keep household records of births, migrations, repairs, vows, meals shared in hardship, and names carried forward.

### First Cup

At certain meals, the first cup of water is poured but not drunk until someone names where the water came from.

### Child's Continuity Lesson

Children learn a simple formula:

We keep the living.
We name the dead.
We repair the broken.
We do not spend the future carelessly.

## Post-Severance VFF Safety Observance

Before the Severance, ordinary citizens generally did not know basic VFF safety rituals. After the Severance, basic VFF safety becomes ordinary civic knowledge.

Possible public instructions:

- shelter when field alarms sound;
- keep household lights stable unless ordered dark;
- report shared dreams, impossible reflections, repeated time-loss, or false memories;
- do not enter transit corridors during false-clearance warnings;
- remain silent during designated calibration intervals;
- follow sanctuary or civil authority instructions during reality-weather events.

## Military Rites

### Oath of Return

Before long deployments, military units swear not only to win but to return records, names, and testimony.

Victory without witness is incomplete.

### Shielding of Transit

Transit guardians certify routes before convoy departure. In secular regions this may be a safety inspection. In sacral regions it may be a full rite.

## Court Rites

### Mantle Procession

Amber's mantle is carried, displayed, or ritually prepared as a symbol of the burden of all timelines and bloodlines under Dominion protection.

The rite should involve real names, losses, and obligations. Otherwise it becomes costume.

### Witness of the Inner Circle

Emilia and Vel may stand as formal witnesses that Amber is both sovereign and person.

This rite is especially important when emergency powers are invoked.

## Disputes and Heresies

- Icon Absolutists believe Amber's public image matters more than Amber's person.
- Provision Purists reject court grandeur while frontier shortages exist.
- Memory Severance movements argue that some horrors must be forgotten.
- Expansion Liturgists reinterpret continuity as endless conquest.
- Anti-Sanctuary republicans accept continuity but reject sacral monarchy.
