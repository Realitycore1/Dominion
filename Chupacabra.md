Classification: Cryptids & Aberrant Fauna
Subtype: Hemovorous Predator / Livestock Parasite
Status: Active, Regionally Endemic

⸻

Core Description

Chupacabras are lean, low-profile predators adapted for stealth, drainage feeding, and rapid withdrawal. Their appearance varies by region, but common traits include:
	•	elongated, sinewy bodies,
	•	hairless or patchy hide (gray, brown, or sickly green),
	•	spined or ridged backs,
	•	oversized eyes adapted for nocturnal hunting,
	•	narrow snouts or proboscis-like feeding apparatus.

They are unsettling not because of size—but because they leave bloodless corpses behind with surgical precision.

The Chupacabra does not maul.
It extracts.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Chupacabras are biological predators altered by sustained exposure to Malice-tainted environments, but they are not undead and not true vampires.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Material Continuum
	•	Secondary Influence: Malice-adjacent resonance (low-grade)
	•	Realm Interface: Shadow Realms (Umbrae) bleed-zones, rural thresholds

They may originate from:
	•	failed bio-experiments,
	•	escaped cryptid species,
	•	degenerate offshoots of vampiric ecology,
	•	or convergent evolution around blood-as-nutrient niches.

They are not cursed humans.

⸻

Intelligence & Will

Chupacabras possess animal-level intelligence enhanced by predatory learning.

They understand:
	•	livestock behavior,
	•	enclosure weaknesses,
	•	patrol routines,
	•	how long to feed without killing prematurely.

They do not understand:
	•	symbolism,
	•	morality,
	•	ritual boundaries.

They flee rather than fight when threatened.

⸻

Behavior & Ecology

Chupacabras target:
	•	goats,
	•	sheep,
	•	poultry,
	•	occasionally cattle or pets.

They feed by:
	•	puncturing major arteries,
	•	draining blood rapidly,
	•	leaving the body intact but desiccated.

They prefer:
	•	rural settlements,
	•	borderlands,
	•	abandoned farmland,
	•	areas with weak spiritual wards.

Outbreaks often coincide with:
	•	economic decline,
	•	drought,
	•	social instability,
	•	increased rumor density.

⸻

Transmission & Spread

Chupacabras do not reproduce rapidly.

Population spikes occur when:
	•	environmental stress increases prey density,
	•	Malice saturation rises,
	•	or larger predators are removed.

They are often blamed for killings caused by:
	•	other predators,
	•	ritual slaughter,
	•	early-stage vampiric entities.

This misattribution is part of their survival strategy.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Extremely vulnerable to fire and direct confrontation
	•	Fragile skeletal structure
	•	Disrupted by strong Grace fields
	•	Sensitive to light and noise

Chupacabras survive through avoidance, not dominance.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Vampires: Competition; vampires often cull them
	•	Rugaru: Avoid; Rugaru hunt Chupacabras opportunistically
	•	Domovoi: Actively repel them from protected homes
	•	Unseelie: Sometimes tolerate them as ecological pressure
	•	Humans: Primary prey-adjacents, rarely direct targets

They are not servants.
They are scavengers of fear.

⸻

Variants
	•	Canid Chupacabra: Wolf-like, furred, faster
	•	Reptilian Chupacabra: Hairless, spined, more infamous
	•	Urban Chupacabra: Smaller, bolder, drains pets
	•	Ashland Chupacabra: Post-collapse, highly aggressive

Urban variants are the most likely to be misclassified as something else.
