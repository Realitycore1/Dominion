Directional structure in the large-scale matter distribution and motions: galaxies, gas, and dark matter arrange into nodes (clusters), filaments, sheets, and voids with preferred orientations and flows rather than isotropic “foam.”

Why it happens (origins).
	•	Tidal shear from the initial density field: the Zel’dovich picture—matter collapses first into sheets, then filaments, then nodes along the eigenvectors of the tidal tensor → intrinsic geometric anisotropy.
	•	Nonlinear growth & torques: halos/galaxies acquire shapes and spins aligned with nearby filaments/sheets.
	•	Kinematics: coherent infall along filaments toward nodes; outflows from voids—directional velocity fields.
	•	Observation geometry: redshift-space distortions (RSD) imprint line-of-sight anisotropy (not a property of real space but of how we measure it).

How it shows up (phenomenology).
	•	Clustering anisotropy: two-point statistics depend on μ ≡ cos(angle to line of sight).
	•	Large scales: Kaiser squashing (structures look flattened toward us).
	•	Small scales: Fingers-of-God (random virial motions elongate clusters).
	•	Filament/sheet alignments: halo shapes and galaxy spins are preferentially parallel or perpendicular to filaments (mass- and scale-dependent).
	•	Void anisotropy: voids appear squashed/elongated along the line of sight; useful for geometry tests.
	•	Baryonic anisotropy: cold gas streams feed galaxies along filaments; metals/temperature trace these lanes; the Ly-α forest shows correlated absorption along/ across sightlines.
	•	Weak-lensing anisotropy: shear maps reveal filament mass bridges; galaxy intrinsic alignments add a systematic, themselves anisotropic.

How we measure it (toolkit).
	•	Multipoles of ξ(s,μ) and Pℓ(k): monopole/quadrupole/hexadecapole of the correlation function and power spectrum capture RSD + geometry.
	•	Alcock–Paczynski (AP) & BAO anisotropy: compare transverse vs radial BAO scale to get DA(z) and H(z) (expansion [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]]).
	•	Filament finders / morphology classifiers: DisPerSE, T-web/V-web, NEXUS; Minkowski functionals and “skeleton” length/orientation.
	•	Spin/shape–environment stats: inertia tensors vs local filament axis.
	•	Kinematic reconstructions: peculiar-velocity fields from RSD or velocity surveys.

What we learn (cosmology & astro).
	•	Growth of structure (fσ8): from RSD anisotropy.
	•	Expansion geometry: AP/BAO anisotropy gives DA, H constraints.
	•	Tests of gravity: compare anisotropic clustering with lensing (e.g., E_G metrics).
	•	Neutrino mass & feedback: scale-dependent suppression/anisotropy patterns.
	•	Galaxy evolution: anisotropic gas accretion, quenching along filaments, assembly bias.

Scales & evolution.
	•	Web morphology: ~1–100 Mpc; BAO anisotropy at ~150 Mpc/h.
	•	Alignments strengthen at late times and for massive halos; thinner, weaker web at high redshift.

Caveats.
	•	Survey masks and selection functions create fake anisotropies; must forward-model.
	•	Photometric-redshift errors smear line-of-sight signals.
	•	Intrinsic alignments contaminate lensing unless modeled.
