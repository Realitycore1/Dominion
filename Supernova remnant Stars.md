I. Physical Definition

A Supernova Remnant Star is the surviving compact stellar object left after a supernova explosion, still retaining partial structural integrity and energy output.
These remnants may range from dense neutron-rich spheres to partially intact stellar cores embedded within expanding debris fields.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Partial Stellar Integrity — Unlike isolated neutron [[Stars]] or white dwarfs, remnants may retain irregular outer [[Layers]] or plasma halos.
	•	Residual Fusion Pockets — Localized zones of ongoing fusion within surviving fragments.
	•	Expanding Ejecta Shell — Surrounding gas and dust interacting with interstellar medium, often forming luminous nebulae.
	•	Dynamic Energy Output — Light from residual heat, shockwave interactions, or fallback accretion events.
