Classification: Territorial Undead & Deathbound Wardens
Subtype: Domain-Bound Revenants / Sovereign Dead
Status: Uncommon, Regionally Dangerous

⸻

Core Description

A Wight is a corporeal undead entity bound to a specific territory, structure, or domain—most often a tomb, barrow, fortress, battlefield, or sacred site. Its body is preserved or desiccated, armored by grave-cloths, rusted mail, or burial regalia, with eyes burning cold and will made rigid by ownership.

A Wight does not roam.
A Wight rules what it died claiming.

Crossing its boundary is a declaration of hostility.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Wights are domain-stabilized undead, animated by a fusion of:
	•	unresolved authority,
	•	possessive identity,
	•	death-saturated land resonance.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Death (territorial enforcement)
	•	Secondary Influence: Shadow Realms (anchoring)
	•	Realm Interface: Material ↔ Shadow overlap zones

They are not accidents.
They are death refusing to relinquish jurisdiction.

⸻

Formation Conditions

Wights form when:
	•	a powerful individual dies while asserting ownership,
	•	burial rites bind identity to land,
	•	oaths or kingship remain unresolved,
	•	cursed or sacred grounds amplify attachment.

Common origins include:
	•	kings,
	•	warlords,
	•	guardians,
	•	priests,
	•	champions sworn to a site.

Wights are rarely created deliberately—but rituals can encourage their rise.

⸻

Intelligence & Will

Wights retain high sapience within their domain.

They:
	•	remember rulership,
	•	recognize hierarchy,
	•	strategize defensively,
	•	issue commands to lesser undead.

Outside their domain, they weaken rapidly.

A Wight’s intelligence is localized.

⸻

Powers & Abilities

Within their territory, Wights can:
	•	command undead servants,
	•	drain vitality through proximity,
	•	curse intruders,
	•	animate corpses,
	•	reshape terrain subtly (fog, cold, collapse).

Some Wights can:
	•	speak law,
	•	enforce oaths,
	•	bind trespassers.

Their strength scales with domain integrity.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Severely weakened outside domain
	•	Vulnerable to domain purification
	•	Susceptible to Grace-aligned reclamation
	•	Bound by original burial anchors

Destroying a Wight’s body is insufficient unless the domain claim is broken.

⸻

Behavior & Ecology

Wights:
	•	patrol boundaries,
	•	test intruders,
	•	punish theft or desecration,
	•	rarely pursue fleeing enemies beyond borders.

They prefer:
	•	intimidation over slaughter,
	•	ritualized challenge over ambush.

Some Wights permit passage—for a price.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Skeletons: Common servants
	•	Zombies: Crude labor
	•	Ghouls: Occasionally tolerated scavengers
	•	Revenants: Mutual hostility
	•	Liches: Philosophical rivals
	•	Reapers: Watch cautiously
	•	Domovoi: Actively oppose Wights in homes

Wights are respected even by Hell-adjacent forces.

⸻

Variants
	•	Barrow-Wights: Tomb-bound lords
	•	Gate-Wights: Threshold guardians
	•	Battlefield Wights: War-dead commanders
	•	Sanctum Wights: Shrine or relic protectors
	•	Crowned Wights: Former monarchs, extremely dangerous

Crowned Wights can enforce ancient law that still functions.

⸻

Distinction from Similar Entities
	•	vs Revenants: Wights are static
	•	vs Liches: Wights are bound
	•	vs Specters: Wights are corporeal
	•	vs Ghosts: Wights have authority
	•	vs Demons: Wights enforce old order, not chaos

This preserves clean undead stratification.
