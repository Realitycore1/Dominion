## Summary

A worldhous
Definition![[Pasted image 202508301
81040.png]]

Think of it as a planetary-scale greenhouse dome.

Instead of changing the whole atmosphere globally, you just put a roof over the world and create a controlled biosphere beneath it.

🔹 Structure

Canopy/Dome

A transparent or semi-transparent shell, usually made of strong composite or nanomaterial films.

Could be supported by orbital tethers, stratospheric towers, or inflated directly with gas pressure.

May incorporate solar filters, micrometeorite shielding, and radiation protection.

Internal Atmosphere

Pressure-regulated air beneath the dome.

Can be [[Earth (Terra)]]-like even if the outside planet has no atmosphere or a toxic one.

Anchor Points

Towers, mountain peaks, or orbital tether structures pin the canopy down.

On small bodies (like asteroids), the “dome” might wrap fully around, making it an enclosed mini-world.

🔹 Function

Habitability Shortcut

Instead of centuries of terraforming, you get a livable biosphere in decades.

Perfect for colonizing marginal planets (Mars, [[Titan]], [[Ganymede]]).

Controlled Climate

Weather can be regulated.

Dust storms, freezing nights, or toxic gases outside don’t matter.

The inner “worldhouse climate” could be perpetual spring, tuned for agriculture.

Radiation Shielding

Dome materials can filter UV or cosmic rays.

Energy Capture

In some proposals, mirrors in orbit redirect sunlight into the dome for warmth and power.

🔹 Advantages Over Terraforming

Speed: Terraforming takes thousands of years; a worldhouse can be built in decades.

Reversibility: If it fails, you don’t ruin the whole planet — you just lose the enclosed biosphere.

Scalability: You can start small and expand — enclosing a valley, then a continent, then the planet.

Customizable Worlds: Different domes can host different climates (tropical, temperate, desert farming zones).

🔹 Disadvantages

Maintenance: The structure must be constantly monitored for micrometeorite damage, pressure leaks, and structural stress.

Resource Cost: Enclosing an entire planet requires staggering amounts of material (but much less than altering a whole atmosphere).

Vulnerability: A single breach could devastate a local region. Large-scale war or sabotage could doom the biosphere.

Not Permanent: Eventually, full terraforming may still be desired.

🔹 Realistic Candidates

Mars: Dust storms, thin atmosphere → worldhouses offer rapid settlement.

Venus Highlands: Dome habitats at mountaintop levels (above the thickest atmosphere).

[[Titan]]: Already has thick atmosphere, but toxic. Dome = Earthlike climate.

Asteroids & Moons: Smaller “wrap-around” domes to make mini-Earths.

🔹 Variants

Partial Worldhouses → cover only [[Regions]] (cities, farmlands, valleys).

Full-Planet Worldhouses → enclose the whole surface.

Orbital Worldhouses → [[Giant]] thin shells suspended by orbital rings or tethers, encasing the planet like a bubble.
