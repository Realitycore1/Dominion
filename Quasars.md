A quasar (short for quasi-stellar radio source) is an ultra-luminous, active galactic nucleus powered by a supermassive black hole feeding on infalling matter.
Luminosity: Brighter than the combined light of all [[Stars]] in its host galaxy.
Fuel Source: Gas, dust, and sometimes entire [[Stars]] spiraling into the accretion disk around the central black hole.
Radiation: Emits across the full electromagnetic spectrum—particularly radio, [[X-Ray]], ultraviolet, and gamma rays.
Relativistic Jets: Quasars often shoot jets of plasma at near-light speed from their poles, forming collimated beams that stretch for thousands of light-years.
In essence: a quasar is the beacon of a feeding supermassive black hole, surrounded by a radiant, turbulent maelstrom of energy and destruction.
