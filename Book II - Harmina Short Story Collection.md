# Book II - Harmina Short Story Collection

## Canon Function

Book II is no longer the main Harmina plot.

It is a mosaic collection about ordinary people becoming Harminan while history moves around them.

The stories follow colonists, children, weather technicians, cooks, grave workers, convoy families, ecologists, teachers, route crews, religious singers, archive clerks, and settlement laborers. The main cast may be mentioned as public figures, rumors, orders, or distant silhouettes, but they are not the point of view.

## Collection Thesis

Harmina becomes a civilization through ordinary days.

Not only through revolution, state formation, or war.

Through meals, repair shifts, school exercises, failed crops, shelter maps, funerals, route songs, storm warnings, first harvests, local arguments, and children learning that the impossible world is normal.

## Structure

### Cycle I - The Struggle

Focus: Year 0 AL through early survival doctrine.

These stories show landfall, hunger, first deaths, wall duty, food fabrication, burial failure, storm weeks, and the first emotional rules of settlement life.

### Cycle II - The Relearning

Focus: Years 3-40 AL.

These stories show ecology, microbial adaptation, first Harminan-born children, field studies, campfire mythology, and the transition from hostility to curiosity.

### Cycle III - The Rebirth

Focus: Years 41-190s AL.

These stories show cultural drift, route identity, convoys, settlement blocs, hymnal divergence, Skyview Day, the Body Question, and the social seeds of the later Dominion.

## Main Cast Boundary

Amber, Emilia, and the central founders should not solve these stories.

Permitted uses:

- a distant speech heard over a failing speaker;
- a law posted on a notice board;
- a child's misunderstanding of the founder;
- a ration order signed by a government office;
- a rumor passed between workers;
- a public holiday whose origin is half-remembered.

Forbidden uses:

- Amber or Emilia as point-of-view characters;
- main-cast emotional arcs taking over the story;
- major political revelations;
- scenes that require the reader to know court politics before caring about the ordinary person.

## Tone

The collection should feel grounded, intimate, atmospheric, and cumulative.

Individual stories can be quiet. The book gains force because each ordinary life adds another layer to Harmina.

The reader should finish the collection believing that Harmina is not a backdrop. It is a homeland being invented by people who did not expect to survive long enough to have customs.
