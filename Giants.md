Classification: Sentient Peoples & Greater Humanoids
Subtype: Macro-Anthropoid Civilizations
Status: Active, Rare, Culturally Distinct

⸻

Core Description

Humanoid Giants are sapient, fully civilized beings whose physiology mirrors humanity in structure but exceeds it in scale, durability, and lifespan. They possess proportional bodies, expressive faces, articulate speech, and complex social systems.

They are not aberrant.
They are not cursed.
They are a valid humanoid solution at larger scale.

Their size ranges from:
	•	Lesser Giants: 3–5 meters
	•	Greater Giants: 6–10 meters
	•	Colossal Giants: 10–15 meters (exceptional elders or leaders)

⸻

Ontological Nature

Giants arise from stable macro-humanoid evolutionary paths, often in high-gravity, high-energy, or low-population environments.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Material Continuum
	•	Secondary Influence: Vital Expanse (biomass efficiency)
	•	Historical Influence: Primordial residue or ancient planetary conditions

They are not divine descendants by default, though myths often claim so.

⸻

Intelligence & Psychology

Giants possess human-equivalent intelligence, but their cognition reflects:
	•	long time horizons,
	•	slow cultural change,
	•	strong memory traditions,
	•	reduced impulsivity.

They value:
	•	permanence,
	•	craftsmanship,
	•	ancestry,
	•	land stewardship.

A Giant does not rush.
The world moves around them.

⸻

Physiology & Capabilities
	•	Dense musculature and bone structure
	•	Exceptional endurance rather than raw speed
	•	Resistant to disease and environmental extremes
	•	Long lifespans (200–800 years, lineage-dependent)

Their strength is immense, but controlled—a Giant swinging wildly is considered uncivilized.

⸻

Societies & Cultures

Giant civilizations tend toward:
	•	clan-holds,
	•	mountain-cities,
	•	plateau realms,
	•	subterranean megahalls.

Common cultural traits:
	•	monumental architecture,
	•	oral-stone histories,
	•	craft traditions measured in centuries,
	•	strict codes of hospitality and territory.

They rarely build empires—not from inability, but from scale mismatch with smaller peoples.

⸻

Technology & Magic

Giants favor:
	•	oversized but elegant tools,
	•	gravity-assisted engineering,
	•	geomantic or structural magic,
	•	runic systems scaled to mass.

They do not innovate rapidly, but their designs outlast civilizations.

⸻

Relationship to Other Beings
	•	Humans: Viewed as fleeting but clever
	•	Beastfolk: Often respected as kin-divergent
	•	Titans: Feared or mythologized; Titans are not Giants
	•	Gods: Interacted with cautiously
	•	Monstrous Giants: Considered corrupted kin or evolutionary dead ends

Giants draw a hard line between people and monsters.

⸻

Distinction from Similar Types
	•	vs Cyclopes: Giants are fully sapient and socially complex
	•	vs Ettins: No cognitive fragmentation
	•	vs Fomorians: Not corrupted or curse-warped
	•	vs Titans: Titans are principles made flesh; Giants are people
	•	vs Colossi: Giants are biological, not elemental or structural

This distinction should be explicit in the Chronicles.

⸻

Variants (Non-Monstrous)
	•	Stone Giants: Mountain and tectonic specialists
	•	Hill Giants (Civilized): Agrarian, clan-based
	•	Frost Giants (Non-Mythic): Cold-adapted cultures, not raiders
	•	Cloud Giants: High-altitude dwellers with aerial engineering
	•	Deep Giants: Subterranean megastructure builders

“Giant” is a scale descriptor, not a moral one.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Low population density
	•	High resource requirements
	•	Vulnerable to targeted warfare and magic
	•	Cultural inertia can be exploited

Giants fall not to monsters—but to numbers and impatience.
