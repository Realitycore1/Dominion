# Uplift Charter and Legal Timeline

## Canonical Revision

This section supersedes the archived legacy draft below.

### I. Emergence and Responsibility (2250-2320)

**2250:** Multiple animal-uplift programs begin during the planetary reconstruction period.

Primary peoples include:

- elephants;
- chimpanzees;
- bonobos;
- gorillas;
- orangutans;
- ravens and other corvids;
- octopuses;
- squids;
- additional successful lines developed under later review.

The programs use genetic engineering, neural repair, developmental support, language systems, and species-specific education. Results are uneven. Some projects fail. Some create people before their institutions are willing to admit what they have done.

Early law attempts to classify uplifted beings as research subjects, protected dependents, or institutional wards. Uplifted advocates and human allies reject these categories because they preserve creator ownership.

**Core principle:** Creating or altering a mind does not create a property right over that mind.

### II. Independent Cultures (2320-2450)

Uplifted communities form cultures that are not extensions of human laboratories.

- Elephant Memory Houses organize around matrilineal history, migration, water, and responsibility to the dead.
- Chimpanzee, bonobo, gorilla, and orangutan communities establish separate Canopy Compacts rather than one generic primate state.
- Raven Moots create distributed courier, archive, salvage, and political networks.
- Cephalopod Littoral Assemblies develop aquatic settlements with distinct concepts of continuity, privacy, territory, and consensus.

Some communities cooperate with human settlements. Some create mixed settlements. Others refuse sustained human contact or prohibit human residence.

Separation chosen by uplifted peoples is treated as political self-determination, not failed integration.

### III. Personhood Cases (2450-2500)

Federal precursor courts reject species membership as the basis of personhood.

Legal disputes focus on:

- laboratory custody;
- control of reproduction;
- compulsory education;
- territorial rights;
- labor contracts;
- family recognition;
- cognition tests designed around human behavior;
- the right to refuse medical or cultural assimilation.

Noa Bell-Reyes and other constitutional advocates help dismantle ownership law. Uplifted litigants criticize even sympathetic human lawyers for forcing nonhuman minds to prove themselves through human standards.

### IV. Terran Constitutional Recognition (2500-2601)

The Pre-Federation Constitution establishes that legal personhood cannot be denied because of:

- species;
- engineered origin;
- natural or artificial gestation;
- body form;
- expected lifespan;
- communication method;
- creator intent.

The Terran Federation Constitution of 2601 grants full status to recognized uplifted persons.

Full status includes:

- bodily autonomy;
- freedom of movement;
- family and community recognition;
- property and contract rights;
- voting;
- public office;
- labor protection;
- education;
- cultural and territorial self-government;
- protection from ownership by laboratories, families, corporations, or states.

Uplifted citizens vote in ordinary districts. Autonomous uplift settlements may form federal districts and elect representatives to the People's Assembly. Federal restoration, habitat, or biological law affecting an autonomous uplift people requires its elected consent.

The Council of Representatives remains organized around the twelve Great City blocs. This creates an unresolved imbalance: uplifted peoples possess full personhood and voting rights, but newer uplift-majority territories do not possess the same entrenched bloc power as the Great Cities.

### V. Solar Expansion and Enforcement Conflicts (2601-3450)

Personhood is settled in Terran constitutional law. Enforcement is not.

As humanity and uplifted peoples enter orbital, lunar, Martian, Belt, and outer-system settlements, disputes emerge over:

- whether colonial charters inherit Terran protections automatically;
- inaccessible habitats and life-support systems;
- species-specific labor exploitation;
- local attempts to restore guardianship language;
- representation across communication delay;
- military and emergency exceptions;
- autonomous uplift settlements that reject Colonial Union authority.

Later Custodial Acts are attempted local and colonial restrictions, not the first recognition of uplifted intelligence. They are challenged as violations of the Terran constitutional settlement.

### VI. Solar-War Pressure (3450-3900)

Solar conflict tests whether constitutional personhood survives emergency.

Uplifted citizens serve as:

- elected officials;
- engineers;
- navigators;
- archivists;
- medical workers;
- soldiers;
- strategists;
- negotiators;
- conscientious objectors;
- separatists.

They are not treated as a unified faction.

The later Recognition Wars concern equal enforcement, colonial sovereignty, military exploitation, and the status of uplift-majority polities. They do not establish that uplifts became people only after the Solar wars.

### VII. Cultural Legacy

By the end of the Lithic Era, humanity is no longer the only constitutional people to originate from Earth.

The interstellar departure is therefore not solely a human expansion. It is a multi-species civilizational movement shaped by cooperation, separation, unresolved grievance, and shared infrastructure.

## Archived Legacy Draft - Superseded

The material below is retained for provenance but is not current canon where it conflicts with the constitutional timeline above.

I. Early Experiments (3100s–3200s)

Context: Post-WW3 recovery and early [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]]-based bioengineering revolutions.

Focus [[I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]]: Ravens (corvids), great apes (chimpanzees, gorillas, bonobos), elephants, cephalopods (octopus, squid).

Method: Gene editing, neuro-prosthetic augmentation, training protocols.

Status: Treated as property under experimental [[law]]. Early debates on ethics suppressed by scarcity-driven pragmatism.

II. The Custodial Era (3200–3350)

[[Key]] Events:

3220s: Custodial Acts passed on [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] and Luna, defining uplifted [[I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]] as wards of [[Humanity]].

3240s: First uplifted Raven “Moots” recognized for intelligence parity with [[Human]] children.

3280s: Martian domes introduce uplifted primates into colony maintenance roles.

Legal Status: Wards — not citizens, not property. Guardianship provided by states, corps, or families.

III. Personhood Debates (3350–3450)

The Case of N’Kala the Elephant (3362): uplifted elephant successfully petitions for freedom from corporate indenture on Luna.

Europa Diver Revolts (3410s): cephalopod uplifts fight alongside [[Human]] miners against CU quotas, triggering outrage in the Belt.

First Uplift Accords (3438): [[Colonial Union]] recognizes uplifted beings as non-[[Human]] persons.

IV. Citizenship Era (3450–3650)

Milestones:

3470s: Belt Chainfolk syndicates grant Ravens full voting [[Rights]] in guild assemblies.

3520s: Martian “Red Clan” ratifies primate citizenhood in dome charters.

3590s: Saturnian Ringclans create mixed councils including uplifted representatives.

Legal Status: Full citizens in many outer [[II - Companion Narratives/Syl'Cothan Dominion/3 - Colonial Union/Colonies|Colonies]], partial [[Rights]] in CU-dominated inner worlds.

Cultural Impact:

Ravens as couriers, informants, and archivists.

Elephants as memory-keepers and strategists.

Primates as [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/Biology & Lifecycle/Engineers|Engineers]], guilders, and negotiators.

Cephalopods as consensus-builders in aquatic research domes.

V. Recognition Wars (3650–3750)

Context: [[First Solar War]] (proxy conflicts).

Issue: Inners exploit uplifts as auxiliaries, while outers push them as equals.

[[Key]] Events:

Io Fireborn refuse to ship sulfur until uplifted “Crew-Folk” gain combat pay parity.

[[Callisto]] Bastions draft uplifted units as full militia partners.

CU propaganda frames uplifts as “biological machines,” sparking riots in [[NEWREA~1/DOMINI~1/EOSMUL~1/THEDAR~1/TOTUMV~1/Totum/VERSES~1/SUPERC~1/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/Colonial Union/Intrastellar/Jovial Colonial Committee[[NEWREA~1/DOMINI~1/EOSMUL~1/THEDAR~1/TOTUMV~1/Totum/VERSES~1/SUPERC~1/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/Post-WW3/Cultures]]nymede]] cities.

VI. Post-War Consolidation (3750–3900)

[[Aftermath]] of First & Second Solar Wars:

Treaty of Acheron (3878) codifies uplifted [[Rights]] across the [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Yauturn Sovereignty/System|System]].

Outclans push for equal representation of uplifts on Kuiper [[Watchtowers]].

Role in Expansion:

Ravens serve as reconnaissance and sabotage experts.

Cephalopods dominate Enceladus and Europa oceanic [[Cultures]].

Elephants become long-term memory archives in Uranus/Neptune deep communities.

Primates integrate seamlessly into Martian and Jovian populations, shaping hybrid “Crew-Families.”

VII. Cultural Legacy

By 3900: Uplifted [[I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]] are not “others” but parallel branches of civilization.

They retain [[Human]]-like [[culture]] — politics, morality, ambition — but with forms shaped by their unique bodies and environments.

Their inclusion ensures [[Humanity]]’s leap to interstellar travel is a multi-[[I - Pleroma Archive/Structure/Science/Species/Species|Species]] endeavor.
