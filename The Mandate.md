Beijing, 2039
[[Dr. Wei Liang]] adjusted his glasses as the spectrometer chimed. On the lab bench, the Jade Seal glimmered faintly, green and inscrutable.

“Composition matches [[Qin]]-era nephrite,” his assistant murmured. “But these inclusions—”

Wei leaned closer. Metallic filaments ran like veins through the stone, forming geometric patterns under magnification. [[Natural]]? [[Artificial]]? The data blurred the line.

He straightened, heart pounding. “It’s authentic,” he said, though he knew authenticity was more a verdict than a fact.

By evening, the state news declared it proof of the Mandate restored. Parades erupted across Beijing. Wei stood in his office window, listening to chants of “Heaven returns to [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]].”

He told himself he was proud.

⸻

Shanghai, 2048
The propaganda machine was tireless. Children marched with Seal replicas, chanting songs he had helped draft as “educational.” Wei sat in committee meetings where generals barked relic slogans. He still spoke of geology, linguistics, measurable truth — but his words became slogans before he left the room.

At night, he opened his notes and stared at the Seal’s data. The geometric filaments looked like code, like a message. But no one wanted analysis. They wanted certainty.

His daughter Mei, twelve, sketched the Seal in her notebook, surrounding it with [[Stars]]. “When I grow up,” she told him, “I’ll build ships that carry the Mandate into space.”

He smiled, but his heart clenched.

⸻

Xichang Launch Facility, 2065
The launch thunder shook the valley. Relic fleets rose into orbit, each shuttle blessed with Seal insignia. Wei watched his daughter, now an engineer, beam with pride as her designs climbed skyward.

“This is the future, Baba,” she said. “The Mandate of Heaven among the [[Stars]].”

He wanted to believe her. He wanted to ignore the [[Reports]] of famine, of riots, of relic wars gutting half the world. He wanted to think the Seal meant progress.

But in the [[Silence]] of his hotel room that night, he whispered: “It is not Heaven’s mandate. It is ours.”

⸻

Orbital Command Center, 2075
The ISS broadcast flickered: astronauts shouting in multiple languages, alarms screaming. Then [[Fire]], then [[Silence]]. Extremists had rammed it, calling it a temple of false science.

Wei’s daughter stared at the debris storm on her monitors. Her hands shook. “They killed the only place where nations still worked together.”

Around them, generals barked: “This is proof the Mandate must control orbit! Heaven rejects idols!”

Wei could not speak. His daughter wept beside him. He knew, in that moment, the Mandate was no longer a philosophy. It was a weapon, pointed downward.

⸻

Beijing, 2078
The first strikes came without warning. Wei sat in a bunker, clutching a jade replica, listening to the [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] tremble.

He thought of the Seal’s filaments — not as prophecy, but as patterns waiting for meaning. He thought of his daughter’s face, lit by orbital [[Fire]]. He thought of the Mandate, stolen, twisted, consumed.

When the bunker shook and the lights went white, he whispered the words he should have said long ago:

“We gave ourselves this Mandate, and we could not bear it.”
