I. Physical Definition

In speculative astrophysics, a Blitzar is a neutron star that is too massive to remain stable without rapid rotation.
Its intense spin creates sufficient centrifugal force to counteract gravitational collapse, temporarily preventing it from forming a black hole.
When the neutron star loses angular momentum — through magnetic braking, gravitational wave emission, or other processes — it collapses catastrophically into a black hole, releasing a massive burst of energy, possibly producing fast radio bursts (FRBs) or other high-energy emissions.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Blitzars are considered rotationally-stabilized singularity precursors.
They exist in a delicate balance between [[Singulon]]-dominated collapse forces and [[Keptron]]-regulated angular momentum fields.
This makes them predictable high-energy collapse triggers, useful both as a [[Natural]] event type and an engineered energy-release mechanism.
	•	Mass: Slightly above the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff (TOV) limit for a neutron star.
	•	Rotation: Millisecond periods common; necessary for stability.
	•	Collapse Trigger: Loss of rotational support.
