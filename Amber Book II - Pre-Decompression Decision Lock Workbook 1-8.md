# Amber Book II - Pre-Decompression Decision Lock Workbook 1-8

## Purpose

This workbook locks the eight foundations required before the next decompression pass:

1. relative war years and visible aging;
2. recurring ordinary characters and families;
3. Ormova and Toldax representatives;
4. Book II outcomes for Holt, Malrec, Orren, Irena, Lysa, and Eidra;
5. the Skyview child's continuity;
6. reader-facing terminology;
7. GalFed cultures and factions;
8. final word-count architecture.

The workbook distinguishes recommended defaults from decisions that still require author confirmation.

## Status Key

- **LOCKED** — already established by the manuscript or canon notes.
- **RECOMMENDED LOCK** — strongest option supported by current material.
- **OPEN** — a choice remains.
- **IMPLEMENTATION** — where the decision enters the manuscript.
- **DO NOT MERGE** — two similar figures or concepts should remain distinct.

# 1 - Relative War Years and Visible Aging

## Current Problem

The manuscript establishes:

- the Founder Session in Year 252 AL;
- the second week of siege;
- a twelfth year of war;
- a late-war settlement;
- formal GalFed contact after the Covenant.

It does not yet give readers enough intermediate time markers.

The result is a war that is chronologically long and experientially short.

## Recommended Chronology

The following structure fits the existing New World War timeline while preserving flexibility inside each era.

| Manuscript section | Recommended dating | Historical function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue | Year 252 AL, days before the Founder Session | Routine warning and hidden GalFed preparation |
| Chapters 1–3 | War Day 0–2 | Assembly Strike and independent FreeStack follow-on strike |
| Chapters 4–6 | War Month 1 through Month 8 | succession collapse, mandate, uprising |
| Chapters 7–8 | War Year 1 | Siege of New Aurora and industrial mobilization |
| Chapters 9–10 | War Years 2–4 | mobile fronts and ArcCell destruction |
| Chapters 11–12 | War Years 5–8 | corridor cooperation and failed accord |
| Chapter 13 | War Years 9–10 | Harmonic Synthesis and system integration |
| Chapters 14–17 | War Years 10–11 | mature body war, synthetic attacks, Immortal continuity |
| Chapter 18 | War Year 12 | breakpoint wars; already stated in the draft |
| Chapter 19 | War Year 13 | Project Ra disclosure |
| Chapter 20 | War Year 14 | Warlight |
| Chapter 21 | War Year 15 | final accord fracture |
| Chapters 22–24 | Late War Year 15 | unity pressure, regency, wartime coronation |
| Chapters 25–30 | Unification Year 16 | silent dawn, debt, council, rebellion, O.R.A.C.L.E. |
| Chapters 31–36 | Unification Years 17–18 | Artemis, Craterheart, planetary siege, last enclave |
| Chapter 37 | End of Unification Year 18 | Covenant of Syl'Cothan |
| Chapters 38–44 | Contact Year 0, beginning within months of the Covenant | GalFed arrival, aid, Standardization, refusal, Final Line |
| Epilogue | Contact Year 1 or the end of Contact Year 0 | atomic-compliance evidence and decision before fire |

### Why this structure works

- It preserves the explicit twelfth year.
- It gives the civil war fifteen years.
- It gives late-war unification three additional years.
- It allows a rebuilt but visibly damaged society to receive GalFed.
- It makes the original Skyview child a young adult by the epilogue.
- It leaves Book III's War Year 0 available as the first GalFed war year if desired.

## Alternative Dating

If the Covenant must occur in War Year 15:

- compress Chapters 22–37 into the final year;
- treat “Unification Year” as a political label inside War Year 15;
- begin GalFed Contact Year 0 immediately after ratification.

This is faster but weakens:

- reconstruction;
- debt administration;
- rebellion development;
- the transformation of enemies into a functioning council.

**Recommendation:** Use the eighteen-year model.

## Part-Opening Time Markers

Add one unobtrusive marker near each part opening.

Examples of acceptable methods:

- a repaired object with a known age;
- a child from an earlier scene now doing adult work;
- a new casualty-anniversary number;
- Thea replacing the Assembly bell handle again;
- Kieran comparing an old map with the current city;
- Tomas's maintenance history;
- a convoy route renamed after someone who died years earlier;
- an explicit line such as “In the twelfth year of war.”

Avoid date-card overload.

The reader needs orientation, not a textbook heading before every chapter.

# 3 - Ormova and Toldax Representatives

## Ormova Replika

### Canon identity

Ormova is:

- a heatline industrial republic;
- a geothermal and deep-mining power;
- a producer of alloys, reactors, armor, artillery systems, and repair capacity;
- governed through inspectable competence;
- suspicious of ceremonial rank;
- morally divided over supplying every side.

Its defining praise:

> That will hold.

Its defining danger:

technical correctness treated as exemption from political responsibility.

**RECOMMENDED LOCK:** Promote the unnamed heatline engineer from Chapter 9 into the recurring Ormovan representative.

### Recommended lock

Holt survives Book II.

He remains necessary during GalFed contact and becomes the strongest internal advocate for immediate preemption after atomic-compliance evidence.

The Covenant:

- strips his emergency implementation office of implied permanence;
- subjects continuity logistics to council witness and expiration;

### Book II ending condition

- alive;
- politically diminished but operationally necessary;
- publicly bound by the Covenant;
- privately convinced the new safeguards may kill people through delay;
- positioned as an internal hawk for Book III, not a traitor.

- one correction or censure he accepts;
- one limit he believes is wrong but follows;
- one epilogue argument for preemption.

### Lock

- [ ] Accept recommended outcome.
- [ ] Alternative: resignation.

## Hierarch Malrec Vaun

### Locked direction

Malrec survives the Last Enclave and surrenders military authority without conceding Amber's legitimacy.

### Recommended lock

- enters witnessed legal custody;
- orders no further armed action under his authority;
- retains protected ideological refusal;
- faces trial for acts, not belief;
- survives into Book III as a politically dangerous prisoner, witness, or defendant.

Death would let him remain a pure martyr.

Survival forces:

- the new state to tolerate refusal;
- Malrec to watch Amber's feared government become real;
- victims to seek justice without extermination;
- Book III to test whether external war erases internal due process.

### Lock

- [ ] Survives into Book III.
- [ ] Trial begins in Book II.
- [ ] Trial is deferred because GalFed arrives.
- [ ] Public statement remains available.

# 7 - GalFed Cultures and Factions

## Principle

GalFed is a ten-thousand-year survivor civilization.

Its people should not feel like three variations of polite bureaucracy.

They share institutional language because GalFed standardizes language.

They differ in:

- why they trust it;
- what their bodies require;
- what their ancestors survived;
- which institution saved them;
- what kind of risk they fear most;
- what limit they believe stewardship should have.
