# Dominion

## Summary

The Dominion is a dual-government survival civilization built to preserve mankind across collapse, war, interstellar distance, metaphysical instability, and threats that exceed ordinary political time.

Its Federal Government governs the living present.

Its Royal Government guards the endangered future.

The Dominion is therefore both a constitutional, service-franchised civic order and a sacred continuity monarchy. These are not competing drafts of canon. They are two halves of the same system.

## Core Identity

The Dominion's public promise is continuity: mankind must continue.

Its central wound is rupture: collapse, exile, broken memory, failed provision, severed transit, and the fear that civilization can die by forgetting what it owes the living and the dead.

The Dominion is beautiful because it is afraid of loss.

It is ritualized because memory needs form.

It is powerful because survival at its scale requires infrastructure, force, law, and sacrifice.

It is dangerous because every institution built to preserve mankind can be tempted to treat living people as material for the future.

## Canon Formula

Federal Government: governs the living present.

Royal Government: guards the endangered future.

Continuity is the mandate.

Provision, energy, and memory are the circulations.

Lawful resistance is not treason.

The Dominion is healthy only when its citizens can depend on it, question it, and survive its protection.

## Consolidated Lock - Neotic Xenopolitical Order

Source: [[Amber Book III - Operation Prescience Workbook Consolidated Locks v1.0]]

During the Neotic Era, humanity becomes increasingly xenophobic as a result of the GalFed War. This xenophobia is not merely biological hatred. It is a civilizational trauma response to GalFed's use of aid, uplift, education, medicine, law, and transit as mechanisms of dependency and control.

The Dominion therefore develops a human-prioritizing political order.

Non-human sapients under Dominion authority are recognized as legal persons with protected civil rights appropriate to their cognition type. They may possess:

- local representation;
- treaty protections;
- protected homeworld status;
- cultural continuity rights;
- access to courts;
- security guarantees;
- cognitive-rights review;
- anti-abuse protections.

They do not automatically receive overarching Dominion franchise. Franchise remains tied to Dominion stewardship, service, loyalty, and the human continuity mandate. Representation gives voice; franchise gives command stake.

This creates a legally protected but politically hierarchical order. Integrated alien species are not second-class persons under Dominion law, but they are not equal to humanity in the Dominion's hierarchy of historical importance or civilizational priority.

Key line:

> The Dominion gave aliens rights. It did not give them the wheel.

## Consolidated Lock - Witnessed Refusal Clause

The first Dominion safeguard against becoming GalFed is the Witnessed Refusal Clause:

```markdown
Any liberated world, protectorate, or client polity must retain a recorded right to refuse full Dominion accession, witnessed by civilian archivists, medical authorities, and neutral legal officers.
```

The clause prevents liberation from automatically becoming annexation, gives protected worlds legal voice, preserves refusal as a civic principle, creates a paper trail future courts can use, and distinguishes Dominion protection from GalFed stewardship.

War pressure later makes this clause expensive, inconvenient, and politically contested.

Key tension:

> A right to refuse means little if refusal makes defense impossible.
