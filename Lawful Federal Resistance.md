# Lawful Federal Resistance

## Summary

The Federal Branch can resist Amber without being treated as an enemy.

This is not rebellion. It is the recognized right of the federal order and one of the chief mechanisms by which Amber's absolute power is willingly constrained in practice.

## Doctrine

Amber's power is absolute in law, ritual, emergency, and myth. The Federal Branch does not possess authority because Amber is weak. It possesses authority because Amber permits law to stand as a living restraint on power that could otherwise become total.

The Federal Branch therefore serves continuity by resisting domination before domination can disguise itself as survival.

## Lawful Powers

The Federal Branch may:

- challenge procedure;
- demand witness;
- force continuity review;
- preserve dissenting record;
- slow non-emergency orders;
- summon institutional testimony;
- require archive preservation;
- distinguish emergency necessity from political convenience.

## Limits

Federal resistance is weakest when continuity faces immediate existential threat.

If mankind's survival is at direct risk, Amber may override ordinary procedure. That override must still be witnessed, archived, named, and later reviewed.

## Narrative Use

Lawful resistance lets the Dominion avoid a simple binary between obedience and rebellion.

It creates a state where loyal institutions may oppose the Empress in order to preserve the very continuity she embodies.

Useful conflicts:

- the Federal Branch delays a decree during a VFF evacuation;
- a court demands the source record behind an imperial order;
- Amber invokes emergency authority and later submits the act to review;
- a faction accuses the Federal Branch of endangering mankind by hesitating;
- Emilia carries Amber's private reasoning into a hostile federal chamber;
- Vel must decide whether the witness-chain has been compromised.

## Open Design Points

- Which chamber or court exercises the strongest resistance right?
- Can the Federal Branch overrule ministries but not Amber?
- Can it delay Amber, or only force review after the fact?
- What is the highest lawful penalty for misusing resistance during existential crisis?
