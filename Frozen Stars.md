I. Physical Definition

A Frozen Star is a stellar-scale object that has exhausted or bypassed fusion processes and cooled to temperatures approaching cosmic microwave background levels — or even lower, in engineered cases.
Despite its lack of radiance, it retains stellar mass and gravitational influence, making it a powerful but stealthy presence in astrophysical [[Systems]].

[[Key]] features:
	•	Thermal Quenching — Core and surface temperatures low enough to suppress significant radiation output.
	•	Residual Gravity — Mass remains unchanged, allowing it to act as a gravitational anchor or trap.
	•	Invisible in EM Spectrum — Detectable only via gravitational lensing or interaction with nearby matter.
	•	[[Exotic Matter]] Residue — Core may contain degenerate matter, crystallin[[Infrastructure]]es, or frozen plasma.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Frozen [[Stars]] are Stealth-Mass Anchors, hidden but potent nodes in the [[Verseon]] lattice capable of influencing [[Systems]] without radiative signatures.
