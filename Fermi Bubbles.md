What they are: Two gigantic, bilobed gamma-ray structures extending above and below the Milky Way’s center, discovered with Fermi-LAT (2010).
	•	Size & morphology: Each lobe spans ~50–55° on the sky (≈ 10–12 kpc tall), with sharp edges and fairly uniform surface brightness; a narrower “cocoon” is seen in the southern bubble.
	•	Spectrum: Hard γ-ray spectrum (~power [[law]] near E⁻² up to tens–hundreds of GeV) with modest spatial variation.
	•	Multiwavelength counterparts:
	•	Microwave “haze” (WMAP/Planck) co-spatial with the bubbles.
	•	[[X-Ray]] shells/lobes (eROSITA) outlining even larger, hot outflows.
	•	Radio polarization features along the rims; UV absorption toward background [[Quasars]] tracing outflowing gas.
	•	Leading [[Origin]] scenarios:
	1.	Past Sgr A* outburst (AGN-like episode) a few Myr ago driving bipolar jets/winds.
	[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]].	Nuclear starburst: sustained supernova/wind activity in the Galactic center funneling CRs into lobes.
Energetics are large—order 10⁵⁵–10⁵⁶ erg—with ages of a few million years.
	•	Emission mechanism (debated):
	•	Leptonic: inverse-Compton from high-energy electrons on CMB/starlight.
	•	Hadronic: π⁰-decay γ-rays from CR protons colliding with low-density gas.
Transport likely involves fast advection and magnetic draping to keep edges sharp.
	•	Why they matter: They are a nearby laboratory for galactic feedback, cosmic-ray acceleration/transport, and Galactic-center [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]]; they also set a structured γ-ray background for dark-matter and high-energy studies.
