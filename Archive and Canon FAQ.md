# Archive and Canon FAQ

This page answers questions about metadata, canon status, drafts, duplicate concepts, and archive maintenance.

## What does CANON mean?

Answer:
CANON means the note is currently treated as the authoritative version of that subject. It can still be improved for readability, but its core content should not be casually overwritten.

Read next:
- [[Pleroma Archive — Chief Archivist Protocol v1.0]]

## What does DRAFT mean?

Answer:
DRAFT means the note is actively under development or not yet ready to be treated as final. A draft can contain useful lore, but readers should expect incomplete explanations or future revision.

Read next:
- [[Pleroma Archive — Chief Archivist Protocol v1.0]]

## What does LEGACY mean?

Answer:
LEGACY means the note is preserved for historical reference. It may show an older version of an idea and should not automatically override newer canon.

Read next:
- [[Pleroma Archive — Chief Archivist Protocol v1.0]]

## What does SUPERSEDED mean?

Answer:
SUPERSEDED means newer material has replaced the note. The page may remain useful for tracing how an idea changed, but it should not be treated as the current version.

Read next:
- [[Pleroma Archive — Chief Archivist Protocol v1.0]]

## Are recovery stubs canon lore?

Answer:
No. Recovery stubs are navigation repairs for missing imported links. They preserve the reader path and identify what needs to be restored, but they should not be treated as new lore.

Read next:
- [[Vault Cleanup Report]]

## Why are there duplicate or overlapping concepts?

Answer:
The vault was transferred from other platforms and contains older structures, draft branches, and parallel definitions. The archive should identify overlaps before merging anything.

Read next:
- Duplicate and consistency review

## What should a reader trust first?

Answer:
Trust CANON pages first, then use portals and indexes to understand placement. If two pages conflict, prefer the one linked from the current portal or archive index until a canon review resolves the conflict.

Read next:
- [[Archive Index]]

## Why are some Q&A answers blank?

Answer:
A blank answer means the question is likely important, but answering it would create or decide canon that is not clearly established in the current archive.

- [[VI - Q&A/VI - Q&A|VI - Q&A]]

## Should open lore questions appear in public Q&A?

Answer:
Only if they are clearly marked. Public Q&A should reduce reader confusion. Archivist open questions should be separated from reader-facing answers.

Read next:
- [[VI - Q&A/VI - Q&A|VI - Q&A]]

## How should new answers be added?

Answer:
Answer from existing canon first. Link the answer to its source portal or note. If the answer requires new lore, leave it blank until canon is decided.
