# (15) Environmental Doctrine

## Manuscript Source

- Primary source: [[Manuscript]] Draft 2, Part III, Chapters 13-15.
- Manuscript date range: Year 0 through Year 3 After Landfall.

## What Happened

New Aurora's first standards failed because they treated Harmina as a problem to contain. Wall failures, hostile soil chemistry, biological contamination, fungal blooms, storm pressure, hollow-crust instability, and titan-scale life forced the colony to rethink what "safe" meant.

Environmental doctrine emerged from field practice rather than theory. Mark routes. Respect unknown calls. Listen before touching. Treat moving ground as possible. Assume every map is provisional.

## What Resulted

- New Aurora developed flexible safety standards instead of rigid Ferox-style compliance.
- Survey teams, weather technicians, ecologists, and maintenance crews became cultural authorities.
- The colony began training children in local hazards as basic civic knowledge.
- Harmina's environment became a participant in governance.

## Consequences

Environmental doctrine later became one of the roots of proto-Dominion culture: disciplined, adaptive, locally literate, and suspicious of abstract law that ignores terrain.
