I. Physical Definition

In real-world cosmology, Population III Stars were the first Stars to form after the Big Bang, composed almost entirely of hydrogen and helium, with trace lithium, and no heavier elements (“metals” in astronomical terms).
They formed from pristine primordial gas clouds, were generally massive (often >100 M☉), hot, and short-lived, burning through their fuel in just a few million years before exploding as pair-instability supernovae or collapsing into black holes.

In the VFF (Verse Field Framework), Population III Stars are classified as Primordial Fusion Initiators — foundational stellar bodies that seeded the Verse Field lattice with the first heavy elements, enabling later star and planet formation.
Their absence of metals makes their fusion spectra extremely pure, serving as baseline calibration points for Omniversal cosmology.
	•	Mass Range: ~10 M☉ to >300 M☉
	•	Surface Temperature: ~30,000–100,000 K (ultraviolet-dominated spectra)
	•	Lifespan: ~Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)–10 million years (standard axiom-root conditions)
