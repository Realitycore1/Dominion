I. Physical Definition

A Self-Replicating Star is a stellar-mass object capable of creating new stellar bodies of its own type without external astrophysical processes such as molecular cloud collapse.
The replication process may be [[Natural]] (in rare, exotic-[[Physics]] [[Regions]]) or engineered (as a product of Omniversal megastructural bio-astroengineering).

Replication can occur through several pathways:
	•	Mass Budding: The star ejects a concentrated plasma and [[Exotic Matter]] fragment that condenses into a new star.
	•	Core Fragmentation: Under controlled instability, a star’s exotic-matter core splits, with one fragment retaining the parent envelope and the other forming a new stellar envelope nearby.
	•	Conversion Seeding: The star irradiates and gravitationally compresses nearby matter into a second active stellar body.
