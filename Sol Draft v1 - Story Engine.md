# Sol Draft v1 - Story Engine

## Draft Position

Version 1 begins immediately after the Exchange in 2079. The main Solar arc ends with the first interstellar departure in 3600, and the Craterheart crash-founding closes the manuscript in 3608.

The manuscript preserves the dated events in the Lithic Era timeline. New characters, households, workplaces, disputes, and consequences are narrative carriers rather than additions to the historical chronology.

## Structural Decisions

- Begin in 2079, without a retrospective frame.
- Carry four lines from *Terra*: the Sayegh archive, Mara Keene's rail practice, Sade Adebayo's public agricultural doctrine, and Iris Bell-Reyes's civil records.
- Keep the blue pencil and Varga atlas as recurring objects. Their meaning changes from correction, to route marking, to constitutional dissent, to navigational inheritance.
- Use the surviving twelve Immortals as the long-horizon ensemble from 2204 onward, but never isolate them from mortal relatives, beneficiaries, opponents, and uplifted peoples.
- Use the thirty-eight-person recurring POV ensemble and the primary-POV assignments in [[Sol Recurring POV Cast and Family Lines]].
- Build the prologue, thirty-eight chapters, and epilogue through the 240 scene functions in [[Sol Chapter Development and 240-Scene Plan]].
- Draw ordinary life, institutional change, restoration, colony culture, historical anchors, Terra inheritance, and required human moments from [[Sol Civilization Texture and Continuity Bible]].
- Treat 2204-2210 as a staggered stabilization range: Elian Voss appears first, twelve others follow, and Voss's later death leaves the twelve enduring cast members.
- Pair each surviving Immortal with one Great City without granting that Immortal authority over the city.
- Preserve Yesterday and Tomorrow as symbolic civic poles rather than additional Great City blocs: Yesterday carries memory from ruined Nairobi, while Tomorrow tests multispecies civic life along the Colorado Front Range.
- Let mortal families become informal dynasties around each Immortal. Their reverence, access, institutions, and name recognition create real privilege without constitutional titles.
- Keep the Quiet Council advisory. The twelve can warn, investigate, testify, and dissent; they cannot govern, command armed forces, veto legislation, or inherit public office through their families.
- Treat the Terran Federation as the midpoint achievement, not the ending.
- Distinguish Lunar industry from Lunar colonization: automated mines and rotating crews exist by 2650, while the first permanent self-governing Lunar colony begins only after Gilgamesh opens in 3007.
- Use the Phaeton Program to connect AI astronauts, mass drivers, skinsuits, skyhooks, the Federation Space Force, orbital habitats, Gilgamesh, and the 3008 Lunar colony as one causal sequence.
- Establish the Lunar Colonial Committee with the colony in 3008. Its first constitutional act makes Lunar domicile independent of employment, but its control stops at the systems Luna still depends on the Federation to provide.
- Treat the 3015 Colonial Union as a constitutional reformation of the Terran Federation. Personnel, debts, courts, and treaty obligations continue, but Earth becomes one member of a multi-world polity rather than the polity's unquestioned center.
- Make the Mars Foothold Mission the first expansion program authorized by the Colonial Union and carry it through advance cargo, temporary surface occupation, family-rated construction, and the permanent colony recognized in 3020.
- Treat the asteroid belt as the 3120-3180 frontier: not lawless and not one colony, but dispersed enough that rescue custom, postal evidence, claim registries, and delayed arbitration matter more than a distant statute.
- Establish the Venus and Mercury Colonial Committees with their permanent colonies in 3160 and 3180. Venus governs lift, altitude, water, and drifting aerostat cities; Mercury governs shade, heat rejection, polar reserves, and mass-driver schedules.
- Keep Venusian floating cities and Mercurian managed-shadow settlements as artificial-life systems, not early terraforming.
- Make Belt development asymmetric: cooperative mining fleets, corporate concessions, mega-stations, ordinary family habitats, mafia houses, drug cartels, station gangs, and pirates coexist without becoming one culture or one political faction.
- Establish Cynthia in 3180 as domestically independent inside the Colonial Union umbrella; establish the Kindred of Mars in 3230 as independent outside direct Union jurisdiction but bound by association compacts.
- Treat the 3250 Great Solar War as the First Solar War. It is principally the Colonial Union and aligned authorities against overlapping revolutionary, pirate, cartel, militia, and rogue private forces rather than a clean Earth-versus-Mars war.
- Make O'Neill cylinders, fueling stations, the Postal Service, Earth's orbital rings, mass drivers, Venusian lift, and Mercurian shade civilian infrastructure before they become military terrain.
- Mature Jupiter and Saturn into distinct outer-system powers, then use their shared experience of requisition, delay, and security control to form the 3425 Outer Systems Compact.
- Place mission-grade antimatter production primarily in the Neptunian system, with Uranian containment and cryogenic engineering; antimatter is manufactured infrastructure, not mined fuel.
- Make the 3450 Second Solar War a clearer inner-versus-outer conflict centered on independence, antimatter, convoy control, and Watchtower command without making every moon or settlement obey its system capital.
- Use Pluto-Charon as the neutral postwar staging ground for the 3600 *C.U.C. Deliverance* mission and make the 3608 Craterheart crash the founding event of the Proxima Commonwealth.
- Show both Solar wars directly through civilian infrastructure and aftermath. Fleet action remains secondary to habitat, freight, rescue, and governance consequences.
- End the main Solar arc at departure, then use the 3608 Craterheart crash to show a mission becoming the Proxima Commonwealth through emergency civil government.

## Inherited Threads

### The Sayegh Archive

Beginning function: shelter, evidence store, improvised government.

Evolution: treaty repository, inter-city records office, constitutional archive, Solar navigation collection.

Failure risk: its custodians assume preserved evidence guarantees honest interpretation. It does not.

### The Blue Pencil

Beginning function: a child marks a map after old borders fail.

Evolution: caravan correction tool, dissent mark in treaty drafts, survey symbol, departure manifest annotation.

Failure risk: later owners may treat correction as neutrality when every correction also chooses what matters.

### Keene Rail Practice

Beginning function: Mara Keene's surviving relays and operational schedules.

Evolution: caravan corridors, continental reconstruction lines, orbital freight doctrine, interplanetary transfer law.

Failure risk: engineers repeatedly confuse keeping a route open with deciding who deserves access.

### Adebayo Public Science

Beginning function: agricultural knowledge released before institutions can control it.

Evolution: recovery commons, reforestation seed libraries, Terradynamics licensing conflict, colonial life-support standards.

Failure risk: open knowledge does not prevent unequal access to machines, land, or launch mass.

### Bell-Reyes Records

Beginning function: civil identity with room for missing people and nonstandard families.

Evolution: refugee registry, city citizenship, enhanced-child status, uplift personhood, off-world birth law.

Failure risk: records that protect identity can also become instruments of sorting and exclusion.

## Planetary Recovery Spine

### Atmospheric Repair

Track the change from soot-dark winter to filtered outdoor life, then to planetary climate engineering. Every atmospheric milestone must include the workers, monitored communities, failed models, and local costs beneath it.

### Ocean Deacidification

Follow mineral supply, treatment platforms, wetlands, marine food recovery, port economies, and cephalopod sovereignty. Repairing the ocean must never mean that humanity automatically owns the repaired result.

### Reforestation

Show seed collection, soil rebuilding, watershed relocation, forest succession, wildfire, labor, and uplifted claims to restored habitat. Immortals can watch forests mature; their mortal crews usually cannot.

### Arctic Reicing

Show the return of seasonal and persistent ice, the contraction of extraction routes, altered ocean circulation, displaced industries, and conflict with Arctic residents subjected to distant planetary planning.

### Destruction and Rebirth

Return to locations from Part I. Towers, corridors, shelters, dead districts, bridges, and old quarantine lines should become forests, civic centers, ruins, harbors, or contested memorials rather than disappearing from the story.

## Uplift Civilization Spine

Uplifted elephants, chimpanzees, bonobos, gorillas, orangutans, ravens, octopuses, squids, and other successful lines are full constitutional persons.

Their cultures should not function as decorative human minorities:

- Elephant Memory Houses protect migration, water, and intergenerational testimony.
- Chimpanzee, bonobo, gorilla, and orangutan settlements develop separate canopy compacts.
- Raven Moots form distributed courier, archive, salvage, and political networks.
- Littoral Assemblies create cephalopod communities with nonhuman property, privacy, and continuity norms.
- Some communities cooperate with human cities.
- Some establish mixed settlements.
- Some deliberately ignore or exclude humanity.

Noa Bell-Reyes helps secure personhood law but remains open to criticism for using human cognition tests. Uplifted characters must speak and act for themselves rather than existing only as beneficiaries of human ethics.

## Second Space Race Spine

The detailed chronology and station functions are maintained in [[Sol Second Space Race and Phaeton Program]].

### Required Sequence

1. automated and rotational Lunar industry;
2. Phaeton Program authorization;
3. AI astronauts;
4. mechanical counterpressure skinsuits;
5. Lunar and terrestrial mass drivers;
6. momentum-exchange skyhooks;
7. Federation Space Force;
8. Gateway and Welkin stations;
9. Stanford tori Orpheus and Eurydice;
10. Bernal spheres Chulainn, Fionn, and Oisin;
11. Gilgamesh as the Federation's orbital home;
12. first permanent self-governing Lunar colony;
13. Lunar Colonial Committee and resident domicile law;
14. Terran Federation reformed into the Colonial Union;
15. Mars Foothold Mission;
16. permanent Martian colony.

### Political Rule

Gilgamesh opens before the Lunar colony so civil government, courts, rescue authority, representation, and failure archives exist before permanent off-world settlement.

This does not make Gilgamesh neutral. Moving government into orbit creates a new center of power.

The Lunar Colonial Committee must precede the Colonial Union. The new Union is therefore a concession to an existing off-world electorate, not a grant of identity from Earth.

The Mars mission must follow the Union's reformation. Its charter promises local government before launch, allowing the narrative to test whether Luna's political victory becomes a transferable right or another standard imposed on a different world.

### Technology Rule

Show each technology through labor and limitation:

- AI astronauts face ownership and identity disputes.
- Skinsuits require intimate fitting, maintenance, and medical surveillance.
- Mass drivers make cargo cheap and kinetic violence possible.
- Skyhooks reduce launch cost while turning access windows into choke points.
- Space Force rescue capacity grows into military reach.
- Rotating habitats create new bodies, childhoods, work rhythms, and politics.

## Dialogue Architecture

The full voice profiles, Yesterday and Tomorrow city practice, and insert-ready scene cores are maintained in [[Sol Immortal POV and Dialogue Architecture]].

### Earth/Terra Reference Rule

Use the Earth/Terra method at scene scale: put the dispute inside a physical or domestic task, remove facts informed speakers already know, let politics change what each person wants, and end on an action, withheld answer, interruption, consequence, or partial repair. Every Immortal scene requires a local objective, a professional error, and a relationship or allocation changed by the decision.

### Baseline Rule

Biology affects sensorium, movement, domestic space, and communication medium. It does not determine intelligence, morality, or one universal species voice. Profession, generation, settlement, class, and personality must remain audible within every population.

### Mortals

- speak from immediate deadlines, aging bodies, career risk, childcare, inheritance, and the knowledge that institutional delay can consume a life;
- use local and professional shorthand before historical analogy;
- interrupt, contract words, joke under pressure, and leave thoughts unfinished when work resumes.

Lev at a housing hearing:

"Her contract ended at eighteen hundred. Her child did not become homeless at eighteen-oh-one."

### Immortals

- reach for dates, precedents, and consequences witnessed across several mortal generations;
- qualify claims because they remember earlier certainty failing;
- can sound patient when patience is precisely what a mortal character cannot afford;
- retain individual profession and temperament rather than sharing one solemn register.

Anika Mirek at the same hearing:

"We protected the address in 2281 and left the heat valve under company control. The right survived. The resident froze. Do not repeat our precision."

### Uplifted Peoples

- differ from one another as strongly as human cultures do;
- communicate through body plans and cultural practices the room may not accommodate;
- notice when supposedly neutral law assumes hands, lungs, vocal speech, nuclear families, or human privacy;
- may use interpreters or interfaces without surrendering authority over their own words.

Iri-of-Third-Bell:

"Correction. The company ended her door access at seventeen fifty-eight. I heard the lock refuse her twice. Your record made her homeless before your clock did."

Pelagos Nine during Mars design review:

"You call two pumps redundant. They share one wall, one heat loop, one command channel. That is not two survivals. That is one failure wearing two labels."

### Settlement Registers

Belt characters use vector, mass, custody, burn, rescue, and arrival-window language. Venusian characters use altitude, lift margin, drift, corrosion, and shared-load language. Mercurian characters use shade-hours, heat debt, radiator capacity, cold reserve, and launch-window language.

Cynthia characters distinguish domestic independence from withdrawal from the Union. Martian Meridian characters use water districts, corridors, civic kinship, and maintenance sovereignty. Mafia, cartel, pirate, corporate, and cooperative crews may share logistics vocabulary while assigning incompatible meanings to protection, debt, rescue, custody, and consent.

Jovian characters use shielding depth, convoy sequence, moon jurisdiction, and radiation weather. Saturnian characters use chemistry, atmospheric weather, route interval, and distributed obligation. Uranian and Neptunian specialists distinguish energy input, production rate, containment margin, and stored antimatter. Kuiper and Watchtower characters use bearing, parallax, uncertainty, warning time, and false-positive cost.

These are settlement registers, not accents every resident must perform. A teacher, miner, child, courier, committee delegate, and Immortal technical witness should still sound different inside the same settlement.

### Scene Rule

When mortal, Immortal, and uplifted characters share a scene, disagreement must arise from material stakes and lived horizons. Do not identify speakers by adding species exposition to every line; let syntax, chosen evidence, interruption patterns, sensory attention, and the response of the room carry the distinction.

## Independence and First Solar War Spine

The detailed political and military chronology is maintained in [[Sol Independence Movements and First Solar War]].

### Required Sequence

1. O'Neill cylinders distribute permanent populations through inner-system Lagrange regions;
2. fusion drives and fueling stations make regular inner-system and Belt routes possible;
3. the Colonial Union Postal Service creates authenticated civic continuity across those routes;
4. the Belt develops asymmetrically and mining fleets arm against corporate claim fleets;
5. the Sea of Tranquility Corporate Conflict demonstrates the limits of Lunar self-government;
6. Cynthia becomes autonomous within the Union;
7. Earth's orbital freight ring becomes operational;
8. Belt mega-stations gain practical and then constitutional semi-independence;
9. Jovian footholds are recognized;
10. the Martian Meridians Ascendant secures independence for the Kindred of Mars;
11. the Saturnian foothold is recognized as the First Solar War begins;
12. the war confirms a plural Solar order while preserving Union emergency powers.

### Faction Rule

No faction is socially or politically uniform.

- The Union contains civil administrators, defense commands, local committees, Cynthia allies, corporate partners, and dissenters.
- Revolutionaries include democratic secessionists, labor fleets, anti-corporate militias, authoritarians, and opportunists.
- Belt mining fleets can be cooperative, corporate, syndicate-controlled, or temporarily allied.
- Pirates, gangs, mafia houses, and cartels may provide real services while remaining coercive and predatory.
- Uplifted and synthetic people participate across all factions.

### War Rule

Every engagement must be written through a survival or civic system: fuel, mail, rescue, air, heat, shade, lift, water, schooling, medicine, household movement, or burial. Fleet action without a civilian consequence does not carry the chapter.

## Outer Systems and Interstellar Spine

The detailed chronology is maintained in [[Sol Outer Systems, Second Solar War, and Craterheart Mission]].

### Required Sequence

1. Jupiter and Saturn mature into the major outer-system powers;
2. Uranian colonies establish cryogenic containment, storage, and deep-route infrastructure;
3. Neptunian and Triton facilities produce mission-grade antimatter;
4. the Outer Systems Compact demands independence;
5. the Kuiper Watchtower Network begins as civil hazard-warning infrastructure;
6. the Second Solar War divides inner and outer coalitions over independence, antimatter, convoys, and Watchtower command;
7. the postwar settlement recognizes the Compact as a sovereign associated bloc and places Watchtower under joint authority;
8. Pluto-Charon becomes neutral associated staging territory;
9. the *C.U.C. Deliverance* departs in 3600 under joint authorization;
10. the ark crash-founds Craterheart Colony and the Proxima Commonwealth in 3608.

### Antimatter Rule

Antimatter must remain expensive, manufactured, magnetically confined, remotely handled, distributed across storage cells, and dangerous to move near inhabited volume. It cannot function as ordinary liquid fuel.

### Watchtower Rule

Watchtower detects; it does not guarantee interception. Coverage depends on geometry, sensitivity, baseline, classification, and human or artificial interpretation. Natural-hazard work continues during war even when both sides misuse the same sensors.

### Independence Rule

Jupiter and Saturn lead the Compact but do not speak automatically for every moon. The Second Solar War has a stronger inner-outer axis than the First without becoming culturally or militarily uniform.

## Part Questions

### Part I - After the Fire

Can routine become law without turning survival authority into permanent rule?

### Part II - The Rebuilders

Who owns recovery once survival tools become powerful enough to redesign life?

### Part III - Federation

Can planetary unity remain accountable to the places that made it possible?

### Part IV - Leaving Earth Again

Does leaving Earth distribute humanity's future or merely extend Earth's hierarchy?

### Part V - The Solar Civilization

Can a civilization spread across distance without making delay, dependency, and fear into reasons for war?

## Motif Progression

### Maps

Wrong borders -> corrected routes -> restored bioregions -> constitutional jurisdictions -> transfer windows -> interstellar navigation.

### Air

Filtered survival -> public utility -> recovered atmosphere -> habitat commodity -> shared vulnerability.

### Doors

Burned for heat -> controlled by technicians -> city gates -> airlocks -> docking rights -> departure hatch.

### Names

Names over graves -> refugee records -> citizenship rolls -> enhanced and uplift status -> station addresses -> crew manifest.

### Delay

Missed rescue -> caravan weeks -> council procedure -> orbital communication -> outer-system politics -> interstellar separation.

## Drafting Guardrails

- No chapter may rely only on a council meeting or report.
- Every milestone requires a meal, shift, lesson, medical decision, household argument, repair, or burial.
- Dialogue voices must vary by profession, generation, and region.
- At least one competent character must be wrong in every chapter.
- Do not make survival communities morally pure because they suffered.
- Do not make later abundance erase maintenance, class, grief, religion, or family.
- Do not describe the Immortals as uniquely rational. Their horizon is longer, not neutral.
- Every Immortal must be wrong about at least one long-term consequence of their own specialty.
- Do not let a House's private reverence become legal authority without showing resistance.
- Age mortal relatives on the page through marriages, work, illness, funerals, inheritance, and estrangement.
- Return to each Great City at multiple stages so environmental and institutional change remains visible.
- Give uplifted peoples independent goals, internal disagreements, settlements, and viewpoint scenes.
- Full personhood does not require cultural assimilation or cooperation with humanity.
- Restoration must show labor, waste, displacement, failed interventions, and ecological agency alongside wonder.
- Keep technology visible through use, failure, training, and ownership.
- Preserve uncertainty where the canon note is only a dated scaffold.
- Do not portray every Belt family as criminal or criminal rule as authentic frontier freedom.
- Do not make pirates strategically omnipotent; their power comes from route knowledge, captured access, concealment, and fragmented enforcement.
- Do not turn the Kindred of Mars into an ancestry-based nation or Cynthia into a state outside the Colonial Union umbrella.
- Do not make Jupiter and Saturn one outer culture or treat every moon as consenting to system independence.
- Do not describe antimatter as mined, casually stored, or harmless near inhabited volume.
- Do not make Watchtower omniscient or reduce Craterheart to a triumphant planned landing.
