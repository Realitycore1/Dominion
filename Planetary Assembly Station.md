## Summary

## Details

A Planetary Assembly Station is a 
space station or facility that is used to assemble and construct various spacecraft, satellites, and other hardware components. Planetary Assembly Stations are often located in low-[[Earth (Terra)]] orbit, near spaceports or other facilities that can provide access to resources and equipment needed for construction. They are typically equipped with advanced manufacturing and assembly facilities, including fabrication machines, robotic [[Arms]], and other specialized tools. The purpose of a Planetary Assembly Station is to bring together the various components of a spacecraft or other space hardware and assemble them into a functional unit. This can include everything from small sub-[[Systems]] such as thrusters or solar panels, to large components such as modules or sections of a space station. The Planetary Assembly Station can be a critical element in the development of new spacecraft and space [[Infrastructure]], providing a convenient and efficient way to build and test new hardware before launching it into space. The design of a Planetary Assembly Station would depend on the specific needs of the hardware being constructed. For example, a facility that assembles large sections of a space station might need to be larger and more complex than one that assembles small sub-[[Systems]]. Nonetheless, the Planetary Assembly Station is an essential component of the modern space industry, facilitating the creation of new and advanced technologies that will continue to shape our future explorations of the universe.
