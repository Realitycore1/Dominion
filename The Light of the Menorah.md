Jerusalem, 2045
[[Miriam Ben-David]] had not meant to take the children into the city that day. The parades were dangerous, and crowds made her stomach clench. But when word spread that the Menorah — the Menorah — would be displayed at the Kotel Plaza for the first [[Time]] in millennia, she folded her doubts like laundry and bundled her children onto the tram.

The square was suffocating. Pilgrims wept, [[Soldiers]] sang psalms, drones buzzed overhead projecting golden flames into the sky. And then it appeared — the great seven-branched lamp, hoisted on a frame, gleaming under floodlights. Miriam’s daughter Yael gasped, clutching her mother’s hand. Her son Aaron whispered, “We’re safe now, right?”

Miriam couldn’t answer. She wanted to believe the glow of the Menorah was protection, but in her bones it felt like tinder.

⸻

Jerusalem, 2055
The first siege lasted two weeks. Miriam had trained as a nurse but had not expected to become a medic under shellfire. She boiled bandages in rainwater, held tourniquets in place while walls shook around her. The Menorah had been moved to an underground vault, but every night, in basements and shelters, she lit a little candelabrum. Children huddled around it as if the tiny flames might keep the missiles at bay.

One boy, blinded by shrapnel, touched the wax with trembling fingers and asked if it was the same light Solomon once prayed before. Miriam lied and said yes.

⸻

Jerusalem, 2065
By then, her own children were grown. Yael slipped across borders with refugee networks, whispering to her mother that [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Gnostic Expanse/Mirror Earth (2076)/Relics|Relics]] were curses, not blessings. Aaron stayed. He joined a Zoharist brigade, convinced he had seen [[Angels]] in firelight, that the Menorah’s glow was destiny.

Miriam never saw him again, except on posters pasted to concrete: “Join the Light. Defend Jerusalem.” His face was a weapon now.

⸻

Jerusalem, 2078
The sirens were late that night. Miriam had only seconds to gather her grandchildren — Yael’s children, left in her care — and herd them into the bunker beneath their apartment. The air reeked of dust and iron. Outside, the world roared.

She lit seven candles, trembling. The children whimpered. One asked if they would be safe, if the Menorah would protect them. Miriam kissed their foreheads. She could not tell them the truth — that the Menorah had been protection, then banner, then curse. That [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Gnostic Expanse/Mirror Earth (2076)/Relics|Relics]] had saved no one.

The light flickered once. Then came a white [[Fire]] brighter than all seven candles combined.

When the flames faded, no one was left to tend the wicks.
