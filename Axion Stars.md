I. Physical Definition

An [[Axion]] Star is a stellar-scale object composed primarily of axions — hypothetical, low-mass bosonic [[Particles]] that are prime candidates for dark matter.
In this configuration, axions form a self-gravitating Bose–Einstein condensate, allowing the star to exist as a dark, weakly interacting, but gravitationally significant structure.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Low Electromagnetic Signature — Virtually invisible in the EM spectrum except for rare [[Axion]]–photon conversion events.
	•	Bose–Einstein Condensate Core — All axions occupy the same quantum state, behaving as a single macroscopic wavefunction.
	•	Weak Interaction Transparency — Normal matter and radiation pass through with minimal scattering.
	•	Gravitational Lensing — Detectable by its bending of light from background objects.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), [[Axion]] [[Stars]] are classified as Dark-Matter Anchors, functioning as invisible gravitational nodes that subtly shape galactic and inter-[[Verse]] structure.
