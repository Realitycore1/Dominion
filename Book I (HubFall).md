Draft 1

The ballroom of Ferox Palace shimmered like a jewel suspended inside the [[O’Neill Cylinder]], chandeliers casting a thousand points of light against marble floors. The nobles of the [[Equilon Union]] had gathered in their finest silks and armor-polished uniforms, each face a mask of calculated civility. Amber Zohair moved through them like a ghost in her own house, the music a muffled tide she could not quite step into.

Her father, King Armand, sat enthroned at the dais. His eyes were sharp, measuring, always searching for weakness. Her umother, Cathrine, perched beside him like a statue draped in emerald, lips curved in a smile that never reached her eyes. Evelynn lingered nearby, enjoying the courtly whispers, while Darius stood slightly apart — the only one who ever spared Amber a look that wasn’t veiled with disdain.

Amber had learned young that affection in the Zohair line was a dangerous fiction. She had grown careful, guarded, and when that failed her, she had grown invisible. Music and books were safer companions than her family ever was.

When the herald’s staff struck marble, [[Silence]] fell.

“My daughter,” Armand’s voice thundered, “has reached an age where her duty must be bound to the future of our house. Tonight, I reveal her betrothal.”

Amber’s chest constricted. No one had told her. Not her mother, not Evelynn, not even Darius. She stood frozen as servants drew back velvet curtains to reveal [[Lord Axel Highmore]], tall and broad, his face carved in smug satisfaction.

Amber’s hands trembled. Axel’s gaze met hers, and his smile deepened — the smile of a [[Man]] who saw her not as a partner, but as a prize to be broken.

“Lord Axel,” Armand continued, “whose house has long stood loyal, shall be bound to mine through this union.”

A ripple of approval coursed through the hall. Evelynn’s smirk cut like glass.

Heat rushed to Amber’s cheeks. She felt the walls [[Close]] in, the music mocking her, the eyes of the nobles cutting deep. The shame of it — being unveiled like a trinket, bartered without a word — drove her breath ragged. When her father’s voice lowered, sharp enough only for her ears, the cruelty was complete.

“Do not shame me, girl. He is what you deserve.”

Amber could not breathe. Could not stand another heartbeat in that chamber. She fled, skirts whispering over marble, into the cooler corridors beyond.

The garden balcony lay quiet, its night-blooming flowers glowing pale beneath the cylinder’s inner [[Stars]]. Amber pressed trembling hands against the balustrade, dragging air into her lungs. Her shame burned hotter than her father’s gaze — she wanted to rip the jeweled gown from her body, shed the Zohair name, vanish into [[Silence]].

Footsteps approached. She turned, startled.

“Running already?” The voice was Axel’s, smug and heavy. His shadow filled the archway. “A poor look for a future [[Queen]].”

Amber backed against the railing. “Leave me.”

“Or what? You’ll pout? You’ll sulk until someone notices?” His laugh was low, cruel. “You should be grateful. I could have had Evelynn. Instead, I will take you.”

Amber’s throat closed. She clutched the rail — then felt the world pitch.

A shove. Sudden. Hard. The marble edge vanished beneath her hands. She fell.

The impact tore the breath from her lungs, darkness crowding her vision. Somewhere above, the music still played. The court still danced.

Then — a face, not Axel’s. Not Evelynn’s. Not her father’s.

A boy — no, a [[Man]], though only just. His clothes were plain, his hands calloused. His eyes, steady.

“Stay with me,” he whispered. His voice carried no mockery, no command. Only concern.

Amber’s lips parted, but no words came. She had never been held this way before, cradled not as a daughter of kings but as a fragile, wounded girl.

“What’s your name?” she managed, faintly.

“Demetrius,” he said. “Orfano.”

Her thoughts blurred, her heart clinging desperately to that one kindness. She reached for his hand as the darkness pulled her under.

Amber woke in her bedchamber to the cold light of morning. [[Pain]] lanced through her ribs, but the greater [[Pain]] came with the servant’s words:

“The boy who attacked you has been dealt with, my lady. His execution is this evening.”

Amber froze. “Executed?”

The servant bowed. “By order of the king. Mercy cannot be shown to such treachery.”

Her hands trembled. She remembered his eyes, the steadiness, the first [[Human]] kindness she had ever known. He had not attacked her. He had saved her.

But the Palace walls did not listen. In their [[Silence]], Amber heard the verdict loud and clear:
In Ferox Palace, kindness was treason.

The day the courtyard closed on Demetrius, the sky over the cylinder was a thin, indifferent blue. The nobles arranged themselves like ornaments along the stone gallery, voices hushed into the polite hum of a machine grinding through duty. Amber was brought to the east gallery by a steward with hands that trembled only slightly. Lysander had argued, pleaded—his words simmered behind his teeth when he left her with the steward. Darius had tried once to catch her eye; Amber could not meet it. The palace had a way of making people small. Today she felt smaller than a [[Child]]’s coin.

They brought Demetrius in like a thing meant for display: shackled, silent, his head bowed. The Novak brand had not dirtied his face; it lay like a bruise on the world that soft people pretended not to see. When the [[Judge]] read the indictment—treason against the crown, an attempted assault on the princess in the royal gardens—Amber felt each syllable move across her like a blade she could not duck.

It was absurd, and it was final.

He did not plead. He did not lift his head to look at her. He let them take him as if, in accepting that fate, he might also smooth something ragged inside the palace. When the order was given, it was practiced and neat, the way a state erases a thing it has decided is inconvenient. The method was a quiet thing—no fanfare, no need. The executioner’s work ensured that the sound was ordinary, a metallic thing within the catalogue of unavoidable noises.

Amber did not scream. How could she? The sound she wanted swallowed itself before it formed, sucked into her chest and folded inward until it was a small coldstone. The nobles clapped once, perfunctorily—applause for order, for the spectacle of [[law]] upheld. Evelynn smiled like a prize. Her father sat impassive, satisfied. Darius mouthed a name—Demetrius—then hid his face. Lysander’s fingers flexed around the hilt of a ceremonial dagger until his knuckles whitened.

After they took the body away, Amber found she could not move. The world around her assumed its routines as if nothing had happened at all. Servants began to rearrange flowers; the choir rehearsed; a steward upset a tray and muttered apologies that were already out of date. She thought of the [[Man]] who had touched her hand in the garden with no calculation, only steadiness, and somewhere in that thought something in her cracked free.

She withdrew from the court and retreated to the private wing of her rooms. For hours she sat with music she could not hear, staring at the small square of garden visible from her window. The wound in her chest was no longer physical; it was an absence so vast the palace could not contain it. Each remembered kindness of Demetrius was a lit match that flared and went out. Each recollection was answered by the palace with a certificate of its own correctness: the execution, the [[Silence]], the neatness with which cruelty was administered as governance.

Days blurred. Lysander brought books and insisted she read; Amber turned the pages but could not find words to hold the hollow beneath her ribs. Food tasted like ash. Sleep would not come except in short, sharp fits that left her raw and more awake afterward. She began to feel watched not by the court, but by the memory of that [[Man]]’s steady eyes. She felt ashamed at needing them to feel anything at all. The notion that kindness could be punished became a [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Interstice City/Doctrine|Doctrine]] in her head; the palace had codified it.

On the seventh night after the execution, when the corridors were quiet and even the palace dogs had settled into livelihood’s resignation, Amber stole away. She wrapped a thin cloak about her shoulders, passed the [[Sentinel]] with the indifference of someone who had ceased to belong to anything, and climbed the eastern wall. The balustrade she reached that night felt familiar and clean under her palms, moonlight pooling on the stone like a shallow, steady pool. She stood there for a long [[Time]], thinking of Demetrius, of Lysander’s half-broken counsel, of Darius’s shadowed face. The thought of waking one morning and being anything other than the sum of enforced humiliation was unbearable.

She tested the air, the height. In the quiet the decision was as if it had been laid down for her by someone kinder than the palace: a clean cessation, an end to being shorn and fitted into other people’s designs. She stepped onto the lip as if stepping into a cool bath. For an impossible second she imagined the splash and the surrender—a sound like water taking in a voice—and then she let herself go.

The fall was not cinematic. It was a stain of sound and the sudden, lurching spray of rooftop-smelled air. For a heartbeat she tasted iron and thought of Demetrius’s hand. The world folded to a pinprick of light.

Her body hit and the world went entirely quiet; thunder followed, but it belonged to someone else. The guards found her crumpled in the garden as before—again—this [[Time]] a tangle of limbs and broken bone. This [[Time]] the palace attendants did not hurry to declare; they hesitated as though the answer was too sharp to be named.

They carried her inside. Lysander stayed, kneeling, his face wet with tears that were not to be shed. Darius’s hands shook as he touched a bandage. Armand’s face was a cliff of practiced emotions. Cathrine’s mouth was a thin, distant line. No one said the word they had learned to avoid; in the hush, a [[Silence]] with shape, Amber understood that what she had attempted would be labeled something else until the truth would no longer be possible to own.

They summoned Adams because the palace always summoned men who could fix what the palace could not afford to show as broken. [[Dr. Augustus Adams]] arrived with a quietness of his own: not ceremonial, not grand, but a focused, unhurried competence. He smelled of antiseptic and an old book. He had the hands of one who had learned patience at a cost.

He worked through the night. Amber lay in a fever of [[Pain]] and something worse: a mind half-slipping from the tether of [[Continuity]], knotted memories loosening like frayed thread. She remembered a face—Demetrius’s steady eyes—and it thinned. She caught at it as if at a rope; fingers closed on damp air.

Adams spoke to her as he worked, the syllables low and steady. “You’re not finished,” he said once, as if stating a fact. “You’re not done.”

She could not answer.

He performed things the court surgeons called miraculous and the palace clerks called practical. He tended her ribs and stemmed the bleeding, but more than that: he tended the place in her mind where tenderness had lodged and been cut out. It was not magic; it was method—delicate neurosurgical techniques, experimental mnemonic stabilization, [[Aetheric]] dampening that kept recursive grief from unraveling into something else. He stitched more than bone. He steadied the columns upon which her small fragile sense of self rested.

When she woke days later, the world was filtered through cotton and distance. Her body was bandaged and heavy; her thoughts were a pool with stones dropped in. Dr. Adams sat at the foot of the bed like someone who had read the pages of a troubling book and refused to [[Close]] it. He had a thin, gentle face with eyes that did not [[Judge]]. When she said his name, he smiled almost sadly.

“You held on,” he said. “By whatever it is you used to hold somebody’s hand.”

She tried to remember the [[Man]] who had held her in the garden. The memory had been preserved in a small [[Blackstone]] of feeling; it glowed, but no light would pour from it yet.

“Why?” she asked weakly. “Why save me?”

He did not answer the politics. He said instead—something quiet, almost private—“Because someone should have.” His voice had a weight to it that suggested more than a passing compassion. He checked the monitors with an ease that mocked the ceremonial bureaucrats and then, when he looked up, his eyes were steady and skilled.

“You will be watched,” he added. “Not because you are broken, but because this is a palace. It is their policy.”

Amber managed a bitter, small laugh. “Do you think I care?”

He inclined his head as if to a truth that was both terrible and inevitable. “You will outlive them if you want to. You will survive if you choose.”

In the weeks that followed—months, perhaps, for the order of the palace distorted calendar into something pliable—[[Dr. Augustus Adams]] came often. He altered bandages, adjusted medicines, spoke rarely of the Union or of kings. He asked questions about books. He listened when she said nothing at all. His presence was a cord thrown under a collapsing bridge. Where she had expected to find pity, she found only a professional steadiness, an insistence that something fragile be preserved.

It would be years before Amber learned that the [[Man]] who had saved her mind and grafted the edges of her brokenness back together was not merely a healer in a palace. He would become, in whispered logs and hidden cells, the [[Man]] who stitched revolts into being—the patient architect of resistance. For now, though, he was simply a surgeon with steady hands and a willingness, inexplicably, to keep one broken girl breathing.

When she first opened her eyes to the sterile light and saw him watching the monitors, she wanted, for the first [[Time]] since the ballroom, to ask a single question that had no diplomatic answer: “Am I owed anything for the mercy I was given?”

Dr. Adams’s reply did not comfort. It was a promise in a voice that did not intend to be gentle. “You are alive. That is enough to begin. What you do with it will be yours.”

The first riots began over food.
A shipment lost in transit, a warehouse sealed for noble stockpiles, a crowd that refused to disperse — the spark was small, but the [[Fire]] was not. Within weeks, shortages spread to air filtration and power rations. The nobles whispered that the people had grown entitled; the people shouted that the nobles had grown fat on their breath.

From her balcony, Amber watched the smoldering streets. Once, Ferox had seemed untouchable, a palace in a cylinder above an endless ocean. Now she heard chants through the night, echoing through the vast structure like the groaning of an old ship.

It was Emilia who came to her then. [[Emilia Navarro]] — a woman with sharp eyes and a voice soft enough to disarm. She was introduced as a companion, someone to keep the princess from drowning in her solitude. Amber had expected another watchful servant, another pawn of her father’s court. Instead, Emilia listened.

She listened when Amber raged. She listened when Amber wept without words. She listened when Amber clawed against her own thoughts, torn between wanting closeness and wanting no one near her at all.

“You think I don’t see it?” Emilia said one night, their chairs angled toward the glow of protests below. “You’re breaking under them. But you don’t have to.”

Amber’s jaw tightened. “And what? Replace one leash with another?”

Emilia’s hand found hers. It was not Demetrius’s hand, but it was steady. Warm. Amber flinched, then clutched it as if to punish herself.

“Not a leash,” Emilia murmured. “A choice.”

Amber wanted to believe it. She wanted to believe anything that promised her the palace did not own her bones, her breath, her life. But the RAD twisted like a knife: if Emilia meant this, she was salvation; if she lied, she was worse than the court.

The riots grew louder. The air smelled of smoke. The nobles gathered [[Soldiers]] in polished lines.

Dr. Adams received [[Reports]] through [[Maxine Hyden]] and [[Jordan Ortega]]: supply [[Chains]] cracking, [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Military|Military]] officers deserting, resistance cells forming. The city was turning.

And at its heart, Amber stood torn — between the hollow cruelty of her father’s crown and the dangerous intimacy of the woman holding her hand.

She closed her eyes, and Demetrius’s steady gaze flashed once more in her memory.

“I will help you,” she whispered at last. “But not because you asked. Because I refuse to let them own me again.”

The first [[Time]] [[Emilia Navarro]] touched her hand, Amber flinched as if burned. The second [[Time]], she held on too tightly, as though Emilia might vanish if she let go. By the third, it became their language: a silent exchange in the palace shadows while chants of protest bled upward from the streets.

Emilia was patient. She was not Demetrius, but she was steady. When Amber pulled away with words like knives — You’re using me. You’re like them. Leave me alone. — Emilia did not leave. When Amber begged in whispers for someone not to abandon her, Emilia stayed. It was not romance; it was survival, a binding of two people on opposite sides of a crumbling crown.

Meanwhile, the world below tightened. The first riots were about food. Then air. Then freedom itself. Cylindrical habitats that had once glittered as the jewel of Equilon cracked under shortages and decrees. [[Soldiers]] marched to suppress demonstrations, and still the people gathered, voices swelling against the palace like an oncoming tide.

Dr. Adams stitched the unrest into a map with the help of [[Maxine Hyden]] and [[Jordan Ortega]]. They sent troop schedules, ration records, whispers of officers ready to defect. Each report drew red lines across Adams’s ledgers — arteries of a [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Yauturn Sovereignty/System|System]] slowly bleeding itself dry.

Amber watched all of this from within the palace, torn between [[Silence]] and the storm. Emilia sat with her on nights when the chants grew too loud to ignore.

“You can’t pretend you’re apart from it,” Emilia said one evening, the glow of firelight from riots reflecting in her eyes. “Your family is the crown. Their cruelty is stamped with your name.”

Amber’s throat tightened. “And what would you have me do? Betray them and become what — your tool?”

Emilia didn’t flinch. “Not mine. Your own. Help us because you choose it. Or stay, and drown with them.”

Amber trembled. The RAD inside her twisted everything: Emilia was lifeline and knife, comfort and danger. She wanted to trust her — and feared that trust more than she feared the crown.

Days later, Amber walked into a hidden chamber beneath the palace — resistance gathered in shadow, Dr. Adams at its center. Gas lamps flickered on iron walls, casting long shadows over Maxine and Jordan as they unrolled stolen maps.

When Adams looked up, his face was grave, but his words gentle. “You’re here of your own will?”

Amber hesitated. She remembered Demetrius’s eyes, the way he had steadied her before the darkness claimed her. She remembered his execution, the applause of nobles, her own fall from the palace wall.

Her voice shook, but she spoke anyway: “Yes. Because I refuse to let them own me again.”

The room held its breath. Emilia’s hand found hers, steady as ever.

The uprisings swelled. Streets burned. [[Soldiers]] cracked down, but cracks formed within their ranks — deserters slipping messages to Adams through Hyden and Ortega. Whole districts rebelled when their air was cut off, raising barricades from market stalls and metal scraps.

From the safety of the palace walls, [[Armand Zohair]] declared the people ungrateful, their rebellion a disease. But in hidden rooms, Amber passed lists of troop deployments, noble conspiracies, and weaknesses in palace security. Each secret she gave was a thread pulled from her family’s power.

The crown struck back with violence — executions staged, rationing weaponized. Amber’s RAD made her falter, retreating into self-doubt, sometimes lashing out even at Emilia. But Emilia remained, unshaken, drawing her back each [[Time]].

One night, as the riots thundered outside and the palace shook with distant detonations, Amber pressed her forehead against Emilia’s shoulder and whispered:

“They will hate me forever. My family, the people… all of them. Even you.”

Emilia’s hand smoothed the trembling from hers. “Maybe. But when [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]] turns, hate is lighter than regret.”

For the first [[Time]], Amber did not argue.

She chose.

And the uprising roared louder, carrying her name in whispers through the alleys of Ferox — not as the princess of the crown, but as the ember that might yet burn the palace down.

Amber’s breath caught as the room hushed around her. Resistance eyes fixed on her, waiting, measuring. The daughter of [[Armand Zohair]] stood in their midst, fragile as glass and yet holding something they could not seize: choice.

“I will help you,” she said. Her voice quavered, but the words carved like knives. “Not because you asked. Not because you ordered. Because I refuse to let them own me again.”

Emilia’s hand brushed hers, steady. Dr. Adams inclined his head, as if he had been waiting for this moment since the night he stitched her back together.

So it began.

Ferox began to fracture.
Markets closed in fear. Air rationing turned into punishment — entire blocks of civilians cut off, gasping behind sealed filters until resistance crews tore the locks away.

Amber carried maps from the palace in folded silks, passed troop timetables whispered between guards, even names of nobles scheming behind her father’s back. Each secret was a spark, and the sparks became flame.

Her father raged in council chambers, Evelynn fanned noble paranoia, and Cathrine sharpened her smiles into daggers. Darius alone looked away, his [[Silence]] louder than words.

Amber withdrew each night to Emilia’s company. Sometimes she clung so tightly her nails left marks; sometimes she hurled words like weapons, accusing Emilia of manipulation. Each [[Time]] Emilia stayed. “Hate me if you must,” she would say, “but don’t stop.”

Outside, [[Fire]] bled into the streets.

The uprisings broke the city like a fever. Barricades rose, [[Soldiers]] deserted, chants rolled like thunder through the cylinder’s hollow skies.

Armand declared martial [[law]]. His decree was iron: no food, no air, no mercy until obedience is restored.

Amber heard the screams from her balcony. She saw [[Soldiers]] [[Fire]] into crowds, saw bodies dragged away. Each face was Demetrius. Each body was his execution repeated, a thousand times over.

She stormed into her father’s chambers, rage trembling through her voice.

“You are killing them.”

Armand turned, unmoved, his eyes cutting. “They are cattle. Cattle forget their place. They will learn again.”

“They will never forget this,” Amber hissed.

He stepped closer, towering. “You are mine, girl. You will marry as I command, live as I command. The blood of Zohair does not betray itself.”

Amber’s hands clenched. For a moment, she was a [[Child]] again — small, invisible. Then Emilia’s words rang in her memory: Not a leash. A choice.

The next night, as the riots reached the palace gates, Amber stood on a balcony before the roaring masses. [[Soldiers]] raised weapons, nobles screamed for order, but Amber’s voice cut through the chaos.

“I am not my father’s heir,” she cried. “I will not be chained to his cruelty. The blood you spill is the blood of your own people — and I will not stand with it!”

The words echoed across the cylinder. A hush swept the crowd, followed by a roar greater than before. [[Soldiers]] faltered, some lowering weapons, others throwing them aside. The people surged forward, not in blind rage but with purpose: they had a voice, and it was hers.

Inside, Armand’s fury burned, Evelynn’s smile turned sharp as knives, and Cathrine whispered poison. But outside, the palace trembled with the sound of revolution.

Amber’s chest heaved, her throat raw. She turned to Emilia, who stood steady beside her. “They will never forgive me,” Amber whispered.

Emilia’s gaze held hers. “They don’t need to forgive you. They need you to keep going.”

And as the palace walls shuddered under the force of the uprising, Amber understood: she had crossed the line forever.

Ferox did not fall in a night. It tore itself apart in weeks.

The first barricades hardened into borders. Neighborhoods flew makeshift banners, some painted with the seal of the resistance, others marked with the sigil of House Zohair. Loyalist [[Soldiers]] marched in polished columns through noble districts, while the outer blocks roared with chants of freedom. The cylinder itself groaned under the strain — air processors strained, power grids flickered, gardens turned brown.

[[Maxine Hyden]]’s [[Reports]] detailed which regiments were bleeding deserters, which captains could be bribed, and which would fight to the last. [[Jordan Ortega]] mapped the choke points where resistance fighters could hold ground against palace troops. Their whispers reached Adams, and through Adams, Amber.

Each night, she spread maps across her chamber floor with Emilia at her side. By candlelight she traced red lines across the city, her hands trembling as she committed betrayals in ink. Each note she passed was another thread cut from her family’s rule.

But guilt clung like a second skin. Every burned market, every dead protester whispered in her mind: This is your fault. You opened the gates. You fed them blood. She clawed at herself with questions she could not answer, lashing at Emilia in panic.

“You’re using me,” she spat one night, knocking maps from the table. “You’re lying. You’re all lying. You’ll leave me the moment I’ve served my purpose.”

Emilia didn’t flinch. She sat in the wreckage, voice low, steady. “Then I’ll keep lying, if it means you stay alive long enough to see them free.”

Amber hated her for it. She clung to her for it. Both truths lived in her chest like [[Fire]] and ice, neither extinguishing the other.

When Dr. Adams stepped into the chamber one night, he did not carry bandages or medicine. He carried plans.

“The eastern air grid has been seized,” he said, spreading diagrams across the floor. “Without it, the palace suffocates in a week. With it, they control every breath in Ferox.”

Amber stared at him, realization dawning sharp as glass. He was not only the surgeon who had stitched her back together, not only the [[Man]] who had whispered her into survival. He was here as a commander.

“You knew,” she whispered. “You knew from the start.”

Adams’s gaze met hers, calm as stone. “I saved you because you mattered. To me, yes. To them, more. You were never just a patient, Amber. You are a fulcrum.”

Her breath caught. “So it was design. All of it.”

He shook his head. “Not design. Choice. Yours.”

The words struck her harder than her father’s hands ever had. She wanted to scream, to collapse, to clutch Emilia and demand she never let go. Instead she pressed trembling fingers to the map, marking a choke point with ink that shook like spilled blood.

“If I am a fulcrum,” she said, voice brittle but clear, “then I will break the crown.”

The civil war raged. Ferox’s marble avenues ran black with smoke and red with blood. Amber’s name spread in whispers among rioters: the princess who betrayed her father, the ember at the palace heart.

But in the shadows, she trembled. Every night she felt Demetrius’s eyes watching from [[The Void]], judging. Every morning she woke to Emilia’s steadiness, half convinced she would be gone. And each [[Time]] Adams appeared, she wondered whether she was his chosen ally — or simply his greatest experiment.

The walls of Ferox shook, and within them, Amber Zohair’s fragile soul shook harder still.

The palace fell with thunder — but it was thunder her father had been waiting for.

Amber pushed forward with the resistance, her voice breaking the gates as [[Soldiers]] faltered. [[Fire]] and chants rose around her. For a heartbeat she believed this was the end, the crown broken, Ferox free.

Then the gates opened too easily.

Steel slammed shut behind them. Loyalist troops poured in from hidden corridors. She heard Adams shouting orders, Emilia’s voice crying her name — then [[Arms]] seized her, iron-strong, dragging her down.

When she lifted her gaze, she saw her father on the dais, smiling as if she were a lesson he had always known she would learn. Evelynn’s smirk gleamed beside him. Darius turned his eyes away.

“Did you think we were blind, girl?” Armand’s voice cut like stone. “Did you think your treachery went unseen?”

They bound her in [[Chains]] and dragged her from the light.

⸻

The Black Cells swallowed her.

Beneath the palace, beyond stone carved before the Union itself, the air was damp and stank of rot. Torches burned only for her captors; for Amber, there was only dark.

The first days blurred with [[Hunger]]. Then came the voices: guards mocking her, whispering that Emilia had abandoned her, that Darius had denounced her, that Adams had never cared. They mimicked her confidante’s voice in the dark until she screamed.

When screams failed, they beat her. When beatings failed, they humiliated her. They used her body as they used her name — to make her less than [[Human]], a vessel to be filled with shame until her will snapped.

Her RAD tore her apart: one moment clinging to imagined kindness, the next lashing in feral despair. She begged for Emilia in the dark, then cursed her name, then begged again.

Weeks passed. [[Time]] was a wound.

Amber’s mind frayed. Demetrius’s eyes haunted her dreams. Emilia’s steadiness became a phantom. She began to believe the lies — that she was abandoned, that no one would come, that her blood doomed her to this fate.

And then, one night, the darkness cracked.

⸻

The first gunshot echoed like thunder. Shouts followed — not guards’ voices, but resistance.

A flash of light seared her eyes as figures stormed into her cell. Helmets, armor blackened, rifles burning with muzzle flash. One knelt at her side.

“Princess,” a voice said through a vocoder, urgent but steady. “We have you. Hold on.”

Hands pulled her up, cradled her weight as they fought their way through twisting corridors slick with blood. Loyalist guards fell under silenced [[Fire]], others melted back in terror.

Amber could not run, but she clung to her rescuer with a grip like [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Oneiroverse Multiverse/Chorus of Archetypes/Death]]. For the first [[Time]] in weeks, she felt air that was not fetid — smoke and [[Fire]], yes, but air that meant freedom.

⸻

She woke again on a bed of clean sheets. The light was too bright.

Dr. Adams stood at her side, older somehow, eyes lined with worry. Emilia sat at the other edge, her hand trembling as it reached for Amber’s.

“You’re safe,” Adams said. “We have you back.”

Amber’s lips cracked with the ghost of a laugh. “Safe? No. Not anymore.”

Her voice was hoarse, hollow, but burning with something new.

Emilia touched her hand — the same gesture as always — but this [[Time]] Amber’s grip was iron, not desperation. Her eyes no longer begged. They promised.

“I will burn them for this,” Amber whispered. “Every last one of my blood.”

The room fell into [[Silence]]. Adams did not argue. Emilia did not pull away.

For in that moment, the broken girl of Ferox Palace was gone. In her place was a figure carved from pain, humiliation, and rage — the ember that would one day ignite into flame.

Amber’s body healed in weeks. Her mind did not.

Dr. Adams kept her alive with skill and patience, but Emilia kept her breathing. She sat through Amber’s terrors, the screams in the night when whispers of the Black Cells clawed back into her dreams. Sometimes Amber clung to her so fiercely Emilia’s arms bruised; sometimes she shoved her away, demanding to be left to rot. Emilia never left.

Whispers spread faster than the truth. They called her the Princess in Chains, the heir who had suffered beneath her own father’s cruelty. To the people, she became living proof of the monarchy’s rot. To the nobles, a cursed figure they whispered of in fear.

Amber did not want the name. But when she heard the chants of it echo through the streets, she decided she would not deny it. If they saw her as the ember that could burn the palace — then burn it would.

When Darius came to her, it was not with soldiers but with quiet desperation. He met her in the shadow of the cylinder’s abandoned gardens, voice trembling.

“Sister,” he said, “I never wanted this for you. You know I tried to shield you.”

Amber studied him with eyes that no longer trembled. “Then prove it.”

She invited him to a safe house, promising sanctuary, a chance to speak freely. Darius followed, hopeful, perhaps even trusting.

The moment he stepped inside, resistance fighters closed the doors. Shackles clamped his wrists. He struggled, shouted her name, but Amber only watched.

“Amber,” he pleaded, “you’re not like them. You don’t have to be.”

Her voice was low, steady, cold. “I am not like them. I am worse. And it is because of them.”

The execution was swift. Amber did not flinch as her brother’s blood stained the floor. She did not cry. She only felt the hollow in her chest grow sharper, cleaner.

When the people rose again, they rose with fury. Not just riots — an entire population, surging as one tide. Armed with stolen rifles, torches, and sheer rage, they stormed Ferox Palace.

Amber walked at the front.

Inside the grand halls, her family waited. Cathrine, pale but composed; Evelynn, sneering, venom on her lips; Armand, standing like a mountain.

One by one, she cut them down. Cathrine begged — Amber silenced her. Evelynn cursed her — Amber made it quick.

At last only Armand remained.

“You think you have won,” he said, his voice unshaken. “But you are mine. You will always be mine. Blood does not wash away with blood.”

Amber raised the blade, her hands trembling not with fear but with fury. “No,” she whispered. “I am not yours. I am no one’s.”

The blade fell. Armand Zohair’s reign ended in silence.

Lysander was there. He had followed her since childhood, through cruelty, through rebellion. He had stayed even when she spiraled, even when she broke. But now he saw her standing over the corpses of her blood, eyes hard as stone, breath steady in the reek of blood.

“This is not who you were,” he said, voice heavy with grief.

Amber’s gaze met his. For a heartbeat, the girl he had once known flickered. Then it was gone.

“Then you never knew me,” she answered.

Lysander turned away. His footsteps echoed through the ruined palace as he left her forever.

Amber stood amid the ashes of her family, Emilia at her side, Adams watching with unreadable eyes. The chants of the people thundered outside.

She was no longer the princess of Ferox. She was something else now — forged by cruelty, tempered by blood, carried forward by rage.

The palace burned for three days. Smoke rose through the cylinder skies until it stained the stars. The chants of the people thundered like tides: Amber! Ember! Princess in Chains!

She stood on the steps where her father once decreed death and felt the weight of a thousand eyes. Emilia at her shoulder, Adams at her side, Maxine and Ortega among the crowd. All of them waiting.

“You are their queen now,” Emilia whispered.

Amber’s throat closed. The word was a chain. She saw Demetrius’s eyes, her father’s sneer, her own blood spilled in shadows. She shook her head.

“No. I did not burn my family to wear their crown. Let them build their own republic. I will not rule.”

The resistance leaders met that night. By dawn, a proclamation spread through Ferox: the monarchy of House Zohair was abolished. In its place, the Equilon Republic was born. Not perfect, not stable, but free.

Amber did not attend the signing. She walked the quiet ruins of the garden where Demetrius once held her hand, her fingers tracing broken stone. She was a symbol now, nothing more. She had given them her blood, her pain, her vengeance. That was enough.

Then came the call.

Sensors lit with the approach of ships — not resistance, not loyalist, but foreign. The Colonial Union investigation fleet. Sleek cruisers, broadcasting old codes of order and authority.

From the balcony of the ruined palace, Amber watched their formation break the stars. The people cheered in hope, whispered in fear. The Republic stood barely formed, and now the eyes of the Union turned upon them.

Amber closed her eyes. She had set the fire. The rest was no longer hers to command.

The wreckage of the first fleet still drifted when the second arrived.

The people of Equilon had cheered, cried, even sung as the last Colonial Union cruiser broke apart over Ferox’s skies. For a heartbeat, it felt as though freedom had been won. Then the horizon split again, and the new fleet appeared. Sleeker, faster, too soon.

“They should not be here for years,” Ortega whispered as alarms blared. “Not months. Years.”

Adams stared at the readings. “They have new drives. Faster than anything we’ve seen.”

The Republic council argued until their voices frayed, but the truth was plain: they could not fight again. The fleet outside would burn them from existence.

It was Maxine who found the answer, buried in the wreckage of the first fleet: a prototype FTL drive, fractured but intact. It was untested, unstable, a thing the Union had not meant for anyone else to wield.

They welded it to the frame of their largest O’Neill cylinder. Whole districts were stripped for parts. Families crowded into the hollowed shell, bringing only what they could carry. The rest of the Republic’s fleet readied for battle, knowing they would not return.

Amber walked the decks as the evacuation swelled. Children clutched scraps of memory, old men kissed the steel walls as though saying farewell to the home they had fought for. Everywhere she went, they reached for her hand, whispered her name. Ember. Princess in Chains. Savior.

She said nothing. She only walked with Emilia beside her, steady as always.

When the last doors sealed, the cylinder shuddered. Energy built like a scream. The fleet outside opened fire. Republic ships died one by one, burning in brilliant silence.

Then the drive ignited.

The stars blurred. Reality twisted, tore, collapsed into a tunnel of unlight. People screamed, prayed, clutched each other as the world itself stretched and broke.

And then — silence.

When the stars returned, they were not the same. No familiar constellations, no Milky Way. Just blackness. The endless, yawning Bootes Void.

A billion light-years from home.

Amber stood at the viewport, her reflection pale against the void. Her breath fogged the glass.

Emilia slipped her hand into hers. Adams lingered at her back, quiet, already considering what survival would require.

“We escaped,” Emilia whispered.

Amber’s eyes did not leave the darkness. “No,” she said. “We were cast out.”

Behind her, the people of Equilon stirred — frightened, angry, but alive. Already the word spread: Exiles. Survivors. Republic.

The stars gave no answer.
