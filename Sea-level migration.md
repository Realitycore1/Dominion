I. The Long Retreat (2030 – 2070)

Between 2030 and 2070 the ocean rises less than two meters, but the variability of coastlines increases tenfold.
Storm surge plus subsidence means cities flood unpredictably: one year safe, the next knee-deep in saltwater.
Insurance collapses before [[Infrastructure]] does.
The result isn’t one great exodus but a century of micro-migrations—families, neighborhoods, whole ports giving up block by block.

⸻

II. How It Unfolds

1. The Insurance Collapse (2030s)

Private insurers abandon coastal real estate; governments quietly nationalize disaster relief.
Middle-class families take buyouts and drift inland, beginning the first voluntary climate migration.
This becomes the seed population of the inland megacities.

2. The Logistics Failure (2040s)

Ports built for 20th-century trade can’t adapt to constant dredging and electrical corrosion.
[[Supply-chain addiction]] turns the ocean itself into a choke point—salt intrusion wrecks data cables and fuel terminals.
Shipping conglomerates abandon legacy harbors and build off-shore floating platforms run by AI—tax-free, sovereign in all but name.

3. The Political Vacuum (2040s–2050s)

Abandoned coastal zones mutate into Free Ports—grey economies mixing refugees, mercenaries, and data [[Pirates]].
States try to govern from floating embassies; local militias seize desal plants and wind farms.
Smuggling merges with humanitarian rescue.

4. The Urban Overload (2050s–2060s)

Inland cities triple their population without tripling [[Infrastructure]].
Water scarcity meets refugee stress; disease and riots turn civic management over to automated [[Systems]]—the birth of the Vertical City State.
Gated “hydrocities” rise atop aquifers; everything outside becomes informal sprawl.

5. Cultural Inversion (2060s–2070s)

Coastal life becomes nostalgia, myth, and class marker.
The wealthy fund “Reclaimed Coast Projects” behind sea walls, while most of [[Humanity]] lives in dust basins or arcologies.
Tourism morphs into pilgrimage: visiting drowned cities by submarine like [[Temples]].

IV. Technologies & [[Infrastructure]]
	1.	Amphibious Architecture:
Modular buildings float within flooded districts; emergency shelters evolve into permanent hydro-slums.
	2.	Seawall Economics:
Nations pour trillions into barriers that last decades at best; when they fail, the debt itself becomes unpayable—triggering the 2050s financial crisis.
	3.	Migration Algorithms:
AI [[Systems]] manage relocation quotas and resource allocation; migrants are tagged as “biometric citizens.”
Algorithmic bias hard-codes inequality: certain genotypes or professions prioritized for resettlement.
	4.	Aquatic Agriculture:
Floating kelp and rice farms feed millions; later weaponized by blockades—“food currents” replace trade routes.
	5.	Memory Vaults:
Before abandonment, cities digitize themselves—3-D scans, cultural data, DNA archives—so that civilization can later be “restored” in VR.
This ensures your world never loses its ghosts.
