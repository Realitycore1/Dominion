The concept of "Sixty seconds to midnight" refers to a symbolic measure of the perceived danger of nuclear war, created by the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists. The organization rates the probability of nuclear war on a scale from one to seven, with one being the most dangerous and seven being the safest. The phrase "Sixty seconds to midnight" is used to represent a state of extreme tension or danger, where a nuclear war could potentially occur within a very short period of [[Time]]. It is meant to evoke a sense of urgency and emphasize the need for action to prevent such a catastrophe. When the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists uses this term, it often indicates that the probability of a nuclear war is high, and that world [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Galactic Federation/Government/Leaders|Leaders]] must take immediate steps to prevent it. It can be seen as a call for cooperation, dialogue, and negotiation between nations to reduce tensions and avoid a catastrophic conflict.

Nuclear propagation is the potential spread of nuclear weapons or fallout from a nuclear explosion. It can occur due to various factors, including the ever-increasing tensions between various powers and their respective arsenals of nuclear weapons. When nuclear tensions are high, nations may increase their readiness to use nuclear weapons or conduct exercises to test their capabilities. This can lead to a buildup of radiation and other harmful substances in the atmosphere, which could eventually cause radiation fallout and environmental damage. The possibility of nuclear propagation is particularly concerning because even small amounts of radioactive material can have significant health and environmental effects over [[Time]]. Nuclear fallout can travel long distances, affecting people and ecosystems in areas far from the site of the explosion. To prevent nuclear propagation, it is important for nations to take measures to reduce tensions and avoid conflicts that could lead to the use of nuclear weapons. This includes diplomatic efforts to resolve disputes peacefully, as well as robust international treaties and monitoring mechanisms to ensure compliance with nuclear disarmament agreements. Additionally, nations must prioritize investing in emergency response plans and contingencies in the event of a nuclear incident, so that they can mitigate the effects of radiation and protect public health and safety.
