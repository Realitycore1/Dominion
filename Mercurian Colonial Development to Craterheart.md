# Mercury: public shadow and industrial government

## Why Mercury is settled

Mercury is founded for metals, solar power, refining, and extreme-environment engineering. It feels less inhabited than operated.

Gigantic mirror fields turn above the horizon. Heat exchangers rise from forge pits in black fins. Tunnel settlements follow cold seams beneath the surface. Forge cities occupy managed twilight where concentrated light can feed industry and public shade can keep a route home survivable.

Every visible surface reflects, rejects, transfers, stores, or endures heat. Decorative exteriors are rare because exterior area is thermal machinery.

## The Colonial Committee, 3180

Mercury’s decisive committee crisis occurs in 3180. A corporate shade schedule shifts one settlement’s shadow to preserve a refining window. Interior temperature remains safe; exterior routes do not. A worker receives the notice too late and burns her hand on a wall that had recently been dark.

The committee places solar shades, radiators, polar reserves, and cold stores under public control. It retains worker movement records for safety, despite their capacity for labor surveillance.

Mercury’s founding political bargain is therefore incomplete but durable: residents gain public shadow while continuing to argue over the data required to provide it.

## From committee to Directorate

No single committee can independently schedule mirror light, cold storage, tunnel access, forge demand, surface rescue, and shade transit. A delayed choice in one system can cook another.

The Mercurian Directorate develops as industrial government. Seats belong to elected settlements, worker councils, engineering offices, and production districts. Every motion requires a thermal consequence statement. Debate pauses during shade transitions because representatives may need to move before their routes become lethal.

The executive is called the Warden rather than governor. A governor rules territory. A Warden watches heat.

Deka Nuru becomes the First Warden of Mercury. She accepts office in the cold store where the burned worker was treated. Later histories call her “the first governor who survived,” a description Mercurians understand literally.

## Forge Lords and public infrastructure

The Forge Lords are not legal nobility. They are founders and families whose firms control mirror arrays, refining contracts, heat patents, training pipelines, and the oldest forge districts. Their power is industrial coordination: one cannot command Mercury, but several can darken a city by redirecting mirrors for maintenance.

The Directorate places primary exchangers, mirrors, and shade corridors under common scheduling while leaving production facilities private. Government controls light; industrial aristocrats control much of what the light makes. Neither can operate Mercury safely without the other.

## Culture and war

Mercurian language becomes clipped because emergency communication rewards brevity. Calibration has ceremonial weight. Residents recognize failing bearings through changed vibration in cups and floors. Heat is remembered as history: a dark surface may still be lethal because it faced the Sun an hour earlier.

During the First Solar War, shade becomes a weapon. Military command shifts thermal schedules to conceal launches, forcing workers into shelter and accumulating heat debt in radiators. The conflict proves that thermal authority is military authority even when no weapon points at a habitat.

## Position by 3600

Mercury enters the interstellar era as the Inner Sphere’s forge and thermal laboratory. It contributes high-temperature refining, mirror control, cooling systems, slow-warning culture, and administrators trained to count hidden heat before approving visible output.

Its departure counsel to the *Deliverance* is concise: “Count the heat twice.”
