In real-world astrophysics, a Thorne–Żytkow Object is a rare stellar configuration in which a neutron star resides at the core of a red supergiant. The neutron star spirals inward during a merger event until it replaces the supergiant’s core, producing unusual nucleosynthesis signatures — particularly heavy element [[Production]] via rapid proton capture.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), a TZO is considered a naturally occurring trans-phase stellar engine, combining the high-density, degenerate-matter [[Physics]] of a neutron star with the vast convective envelope of a red supergiant.

This arrangement creates multi-layered energy gradients that couple gravitational, nuclear, and in advanced cases, [[Aetheric]] processes.

• Envelope: Hydrogen/helium-rich convective supergiant shell.

• [[Key]] [[Physics]]: Extreme pressure-driven nucleosynthesis, gravitational potential gradients, cross-[[Domain]] phase stability.
