Reader Q&A – Part I

Cosmology, Reality Structure, and Dominion Foundations

⸻

Purpose of This File

This document exists to answer the kinds of questions a new reader would naturally ask when first encountering the structure of reality, the rise of the Dominion, and the foundational events that shaped its worldview.

This is Part I only.

It covers:
	•	the structure of reality
	•	how verses, multiverses, and basins function
	•	severance and instability
	•	how realities interact
	•	why humanity became dangerous
	•	and the early origins of the future Dominion

⸻

SECTION I — THE STRUCTURE OF REALITY

⸻

What is the Omniverse?

The Omniverse is the highest known structure of reality.

It can be understood as a set of sets: a total framework in which realities emerge, differentiate, stabilize, decay, and are replaced.

It is not a single universe or even a single multiverse.

It is the total environment in which all major structures of existence are generated.

At the broadest level, the Omniverse behaves like a computational reality framework, constantly generating and expressing new structures through what can be called novelty generation.

It is not conscious, moral, or teleological.

It does not “want” anything.

It simply continues what is structurally possible.

⸻

What exists directly beneath the Omniverse?

Directly beneath the Omniverse are Megaverses, more commonly referred to as Basins.

A basin is a large-scale reality structure that expresses a particular foundational rule set.

These basins are categorized by broad behavioral families such as:
	•	Forest
	•	Stone
	•	Ocean
	•	and others

These categories are not merely aesthetic. They describe how reality behaves at a deep structural level.

For reader simplicity, only the major categories are necessary at first.

⸻

What is a Basin?

A Basin is the large-scale expression of a foundational rule set known as an Axiom.

A basin is the environment in which related multiverses, verses, and reality structures are generated.

It can be understood as the “body” or “region” through which one major rule space is being expressed.

A basin is generally the expression of one axiom, though there are engineered basin types capable of hosting more than one.

⸻

What is an Axiom?

An Axiom is the fundamental rule set that determines what can and cannot exist within a basin and the realities inside it.

An axiom governs:
	•	what kinds of physics are possible
	•	what kinds of structures can form
	•	what kinds of entities may arise
	•	how continuity behaves
	•	and what counts as a viable reality expression

Examples of axiom-level variation could include:
	•	gravity behaving differently
	•	cooperation being more structurally favored than competition
	•	radically different assumptions of matter, causality, or identity

An axiom is not just a “law of physics.”

It is the deepest operating rule space of a reality family.

⸻

Where do Axioms come from?

Axioms are generated through novelty generation.

As the Omniverse produces new information through emergent and accumulated structure, new rule spaces become possible.

These are then expressed as new axioms.

Axioms are:
	•	generated
	•	self-enforcing
	•	and structurally maintained by the system itself

They are not chosen by an intelligence or deity.

They simply emerge when conditions for their expression become viable.

⸻

Do Axioms live forever?

No.

Axioms have a life cycle.

Their lifecycle can be understood as:
	•	Birth — a new axiom emerges
	•	Expression — it generates realities and possible structures
	•	Exhaustion — it eventually runs out of viable expressions
	•	Death — it loses structural stability and detaches from the greater Omniverse

Once an axiom has exhausted all viable and stable expressions of its rule set, it can no longer continue meaningfully.

When this happens, the basin and realities it generated begin to fail structurally.

⸻

What counts as a “successful” reality expression?

Not every possible reality expression is equally meaningful or viable.

A stable expression is one that can:
	•	sustain structure
	•	generate continuity
	•	and produce novelty over time

The most effective generators of novelty are intelligences, especially those capable of:
	•	long-term continuity
	•	adaptation
	•	recursive creation
	•	and civilization-scale persistence

This does not mean the Omniverse is teleological or “wants” intelligence.

It means intelligence is one of the most effective natural solutions for generating novelty.

That creates a functional bias, not a purpose.

⸻

SECTION II — MULTIVERSES, VERSES, AND TIMELINES

⸻

What is a Multiverse?

A Multiverse is a collection of Verses that share similar foundational assumptions.

These assumptions can include:
	•	related physics
	•	compatible structural behavior
	•	recognizable continuity patterns
	•	and similar ways reality is expressed

A multiverse is not “everything.”

It is one cluster of related realities inside a larger basin.

⸻

What is a Verse?

A Verse is one complete, self-contained reality.

A verse is what most people would think of as a universe, but in this framework it is specifically one full enclosed reality structure.

A verse contains:
	•	its own internal physical and structural rules
	•	its own continuity
	•	its own timeline structure
	•	and its own boundary shell

A verse is not just a world or a galaxy.

It is the entire enclosed reality that contains them.

⸻

What is Eos?

Eos is the local multiverse to which Totum belongs.

It is the home multiverse of the setting’s primary human continuity.

Eos is made up of an enormous but finite number of verses that all share enough common assumptions to remain legible to minds like ours.

In practical terms, Eos includes the family of realities that can produce things such as:
	•	alternate scientific worlds
	•	fantasy-like worlds
	•	superhero-like realities
	•	and many other expressions of familiar possibility

Eos is not limited to human imagination.

It contains far more than humanity has ever imagined.

But it is also the multiversal region in which many familiar imagined structures can arise.

It is, effectively:

our home multiverse

and

the nearest family of realities humanity can eventually grow into understanding

⸻

What is Totum?

Totum is the name of the local verse that contains the primary human continuity.

It is “our” verse.

Totum exists within Eos, which exists within a larger Forest Basin.

Totum is held structurally within reality through deeper support systems such as:
	•	strange matter filaments
	•	verseon boundary structures
	•	and the broader computational logic of its basin

⸻

Are all fictional worlds in Totum automatically real somewhere in Eos?

No.

Not every fictional world automatically exists as a real neighboring verse.

Some ideas are simply:
	•	imagination
	•	coincidence
	•	structural convergence
	•	or recombination of familiar patterns

However, because realities are not perfectly sealed, some fictional concepts may partially resemble real structures elsewhere due to:
	•	subconscious bleed-through
	•	symbolic recurrence
	•	shared structural assumptions
	•	and indirect cross-verse influence

This means:
	•	some fictional ideas are just fiction
	•	some accidentally resemble reality elsewhere
	•	and some may partially reflect actual bleed-through

Important distinction:

Just because something could exist does not mean it does exist.

⸻

What is the difference between an alternate timeline and a separate verse?

A Verse is the entire reality.

A Timeline is one internal expression of that reality.

This means:
	•	a separate verse = a different reality entirely
	•	an alternate timeline = a different history inside the same reality

Even if events diverge dramatically, they are still part of the same verse as long as they remain internal expressions of that verse’s structure.

⸻

Can one verse contain many alternate timelines?

Yes.

A single verse can contain an enormous number of alternate timelines.

These are not all merged together.

They exist as structurally separated internal strands.

⸻

Are alternate timelines easier to access than other verses?

No.

Counterintuitively, timeline traversal is harder than multiverse traversal.

Timeline branches are hidden inside a more isolated internal structural regime.

Separate verses are “outside” enough to be reached through the larger reality structure.

Timeline branches are buried inside the same verse and are therefore harder to access directly.

⸻

SECTION III — LOOM THREADS AND SEVERANCE

⸻

What are Loom Threads?

A Loom Thread is a single stable timeline within a verse.

Each timeline is one coherent causal strand—one stable worldline.

The easiest way to picture them is as threads in a loom:
	•	each strand is separate
	•	each strand is structurally coherent
	•	and the whole woven structure makes up the timeline complexity of the verse

Each loom thread is:
	•	one stable historical path
	•	one internal expression of the verse
	•	and one continuity strand inside the larger reality

⸻

Can Loom Threads be connected?

Yes.

Loom threads can be knotted together artificially.

This does not fully merge them into one timeline, but it can bind them tightly enough to behave as if they are strongly linked.

This is usually done to improve structural resilience and reduce the risk of failure.

However, knotting comes with tradeoffs:
	•	paradox risk
	•	instability
	•	abnormal cross-thread effects
	•	and long-term maintenance cost

⸻

What is a Severance Event?

A Severance Event occurs when a timeline (loom thread) becomes too unstable to remain safely attached to the larger verse.

When that happens, it is eventually cut loose.

Severance can happen because of:
	•	instability
	•	contradiction
	•	low novelty generation
	•	structural weakness
	•	paradox overload
	•	verseon collapse

It can also be caused artificially by advanced civilizations.

⸻

Why does severance happen?

Because reality prioritizes overall structural integrity.

When a timeline becomes unstable, the system first attempts to correct it.

If correction fails, continued instability can threaten the larger structure.

In those cases, severance acts as a form of containment.

It is not moral or intentional.

It is simply the system preserving itself.

The logic is:
	1.	Correction
	2.	Containment
	3.	Severance
	4.	Drift
	5.	Collapse

⸻

What happens after severance?

A severed timeline does not vanish instantly.

Instead, it:
	•	detaches
	•	drifts
	•	loses coherence
	•	destabilizes over time
	•	and eventually degrades into oblivion

It is a slow death, not immediate deletion.

⸻

Can severed timelines be saved?

Artificially, yes—but at high cost.

It requires:
	•	sufficient intelligence
	•	infrastructure
	•	stabilization systems
	•	and often long-term intervention

This is difficult and usually temporary unless deep structural correction is achieved.

⸻

What does severance feel like from inside a timeline?

From within a timeline, severance appears as foundational instability.

It can manifest as:
	•	reality becoming “playable”
	•	iridescent visual shifts
	•	contrast distortion
	•	objective reality drifting
	•	environmental inconsistency
	•	paradox-like events
	•	and local physical constants becoming unreliable

In extreme cases, reality temporarily behaves less like a rigid structure and more like a partially loosened system.

This is not stable.

It is a dangerous breakdown state.

⸻

How common are severance events?

At Omniversal scale, natural severance events happen constantly.

But from any local perspective, they seem rare.

The easiest comparison is a supernova:
	•	common across the total system
	•	rare from any one point of view

Artificial severance events are much rarer and usually require extremely advanced intervention.

⸻

SECTION IV — THE VOID, UNREALITY, AND OBLIVION

⸻

What is the Void?

The Void is the low-density outer medium that exists outside and between verses.

It is not empty nothingness.

It is a sparse and partially real form of reality that still retains enough structural integrity to allow:
	•	existence
	•	movement
	•	and limited continuity

It can be traversed.

Things can exist in it.

It is effectively a weak but still functional outer reality medium.

⸻

What is Unreality?

Unreality is the unstable zone beyond the Void.

It is the region where reality becomes too weak to reliably support structure.

In Unreality:
	•	coherence breaks down
	•	structure corrodes
	•	continuity becomes harder to maintain
	•	and detached realities begin to die

Unreality is not instant erasure.

It is slow structural death.

⸻

What is Oblivion?

Oblivion is the terminal failure state beyond Unreality.

Where Unreality is slow breakdown, Oblivion is final nonexistence.

Once something fully collapses into Oblivion, it is permanently gone.

⸻

How are the Void, Unreality, and Oblivion different?

Void
	•	low-density reality
	•	traversable
	•	still supports existence

Unreality
	•	corrosive instability
	•	structure degrades over time
	•	survival becomes increasingly difficult

Oblivion
	•	total collapse
	•	terminal erasure
	•	no recoverable structure

⸻

Can the Void be traveled through?

Yes.

But only by sufficiently advanced or powerful civilizations.

Void travel is possible and often faster than ordinary in-verse transit, but it is extreme, dangerous, and not efficient for routine use.

It is best understood as overkill transit—useful, but excessive unless absolutely needed.

⸻

How do things survive in the Void?

Survival in the Void requires protection.

Methods include:
	•	shielding
	•	specialized materials
	•	field containment
	•	and certain biological or quasi-biological adaptations

Without sufficient protection, Void exposure can produce:
	•	physical instability
	•	identity drift
	•	structural degradation
	•	and drift toward Unreality

⸻

SECTION V — FILAMENTS, VERSEONS, AND REALITY BOUNDARIES

⸻

What are Strange Matter Filaments?

Strange Matter Filaments are the natural structural supports of reality.

They are the bones and nerves of a basin.

Their functions include:
	•	holding verses together
	•	maintaining structural coherence
	•	keeping Oblivion at bay
	•	transmitting information
	•	and allowing axioms to compute without external collapse

They occur naturally, but sufficiently advanced civilizations can also interact with or engineer them.

They are also used for:
	•	navigation
	•	structural mapping
	•	and interverse transit

⸻

What kind of information do the filaments transmit?

Their primary role is to transmit stress-response information.

They function like a vast wired bus network across reality.

Their main job is structural coherence.

However, because they can carry information, they can also transmit:
	•	patterned data
	•	symbolic recurrence
	•	structural changes
	•	and under rare conditions, indirect cognitive or anomalous effects

This means one verse can sometimes influence another indirectly through:
	•	dreams
	•	manifestations
	•	symbolic recurrence
	•	or anomaly bleed-through

⸻

Can one reality affect another?

Yes—but not infinitely.

How much influence one verse can exert on another depends on boundary permeability.

A reality with low permeability is hard to affect.

A reality with high permeability is more vulnerable to:
	•	dream bleed
	•	symbolic overlap
	•	manifestations
	•	and cross-verse anomaly

This is one of the reasons similar concepts can appear across different realities without direct conventional contact.

⸻

What is Boundary Permeability?

Boundary Permeability is the degree to which a verse can be influenced by external structures.

It determines:
	•	how easily outside influence can enter
	•	how easily things can leave
	•	how difficult travel is
	•	and how likely anomalous bleed-through becomes

It is not uniform across a verse.

There are:
	•	soft spots
	•	dense spots
	•	and varying levels of resistance

⸻

What is the Verseon Field?

The Verseon Field is the structural boundary system that encloses a verse and keeps it self-contained.

It is made of Verseons, non-traditional particles that form a stabilizing lattice around a reality.

This lattice separates the verse from:
	•	the larger multiverse
	•	the basin
	•	and the wider uncertainty beyond

A verse can be understood as an island of stability enclosed by a verseon shell.

⸻

What does Verseon Density do?

Verseon density determines how sealed or open a verse is.

High verseon density

means:
	•	stronger containment
	•	harder traversal
	•	less outside influence
	•	more stable local continuity

Low verseon density

means:
	•	weaker containment
	•	easier traversal
	•	more anomalies
	•	more external bleed-through

Verseon density is not uniform.

It varies across the verse.

⸻

What affects Verseon Density and Permeability?

Major factors include:
	•	proximity to strange matter filaments
	•	the age of the verse
	•	significant internal events
	•	artificial interference
	•	and local structural history

These variations can be:
	•	natural
	•	or intentionally manipulated by advanced intelligences

This means reality boundaries can be:
	•	hardened
	•	weakened
	•	exploited
	•	or measured

⸻

SECTION VI — HUMANITY, THE DRACOSHIA, AND WHY MANKIND WAS TARGETED

⸻

Who are the Dracoshia?

The Dracoshia are a hostile alien civilization.

They are one of the major powers that recognized the long-term danger of humanity before humanity had fully reached that scale.

They are not simply enemies by accident.

They are one of the first civilizations to correctly identify what mankind could eventually become.

⸻

Why did the Dracoshia try to stop mankind?

Because they projected that if humanity was left unchecked, it would not merely join the larger multiversal community.

It would eventually attempt to dominate it.

Once humanity reached the multiverse stage, it was expected to develop:
	•	interverse travel
	•	filament engineering
	•	verseon control
	•	and eventually much larger structural influence

Dracoshian projections indicated that mankind would not stop there.

It would continue outward and attempt to build dominance at larger and larger scales.

From their perspective, humanity represented:
	•	competition
	•	loss of dominance
	•	and a future hegemonic threat

⸻

Were the Dracoshia wrong?

Only partially.

They were:
	•	paranoid
	•	and correct

Their fear of what humanity could become was justified.

What they did was extreme, but their assessment of the danger was not baseless.

⸻

Did the Dracoshia succeed in stopping humanity?

No.

Their intervention failed in its ultimate purpose.

In fact, it accelerated the process they were trying to prevent.

⸻

What made humanity especially dangerous?

Not humanity in the generic biological sense.

The danger came from a specific civilizational trajectory.

What made that trajectory dangerous was not just intelligence, but a combination of:
	•	adaptability
	•	ambition
	•	scalability
	•	continuity focus
	•	and above all:

perseverance of its goals

This was not the only possible future for mankind.

It was one possible outcome among several.

But it was the one the Dracoshia believed would become the most dangerous.

⸻

SECTION VII — THE DOMINION MINDSET

⸻

Was humanity always destined to become the Dominion?

No.

The Dominion was not the inevitable or only outcome of mankind.

Other human trajectories also exist or can exist, including examples such as:
	•	the Domain
	•	the Federation
	•	the Empire

Different human futures pursue “ascendancy” in different ways.

The Dominion was one particular civilizational expression—not an unavoidable one.

⸻

How is the Dominion different from other human trajectories?

The Dominion

Believes ascendancy must be built.

Its focus is on:
	•	continuity
	•	infrastructure
	•	control
	•	structural understanding
	•	and permanent survivability

The Domain

Believes ascendancy comes through unlimited biological potential.

The Federation

Believes ascendancy comes through cooperation and collective integration.

The Empire

Believes ascendancy can be taken.

The Dominion’s unique distinction is that it seeks to construct continuity at scale rather than simply seize or embody power.

⸻

What mindset eventually produced the Dominion?

The Dominion emerged from a civilization-wide belief that:

continuity must be secured at scale

Its worldview is rooted in survival, but not survival in a small or temporary sense.

It seeks continuity across:
	•	time
	•	civilization
	•	and reality itself

From this perspective:
	•	ignorance is dangerous
	•	instability is dangerous
	•	dependency is dangerous
	•	and anything not structurally secured remains a threat

⸻

What is the Dominion trying to do?

The Dominion seeks to:
	•	survive
	•	persist
	•	understand the greater structure
	•	and build enough infrastructure that continuity cannot easily be taken away again

It does not pursue transcendence merely for glory.

It seeks to make sense of the larger structure and secure itself permanently within it.

⸻

What is one of the core founding beliefs of the Dominion?

One of the most important founding principles is:

“No one will save us, and it will not be given willingly.”

This belief became foundational because the future Dominion leadership concluded that:
	•	survival cannot depend on outside mercy
	•	continuity must be built
	•	and freedom must be defended, not expected

A second closely related practical lesson is:

“Avoid crippling dependency.”

Together, these two beliefs form a major part of the Dominion’s civilizational logic.

⸻

SECTION VIII — AMBER ZOHAIR

⸻

Who is Amber Zohair?

Amber Zohair is the foundational figure behind the Dominion mindset and the first Celestial Empress of the Dominion.

She is one of the central figures in the entire setting.

Amber is not remembered merely as a ruler.

She is remembered as the individual whose worldview scaled from:
	•	personal conviction
	•	to faction
	•	to state
	•	to civilization

⸻

What kind of person was Amber Zohair?

Amber was, in different ways:
	•	a survivor
	•	a ruler
	•	and a philosopher

She is remembered differently depending on perspective.

To many outside the Dominion, she may be seen as:
	•	a tyrant
	•	conqueror
	•	or dangerous founder

To much of the Dominion, she is seen as:
	•	a saintly authority
	•	divinely sanctioned ruler
	•	and eventually a God-Empress

⸻

What shaped Amber’s worldview?

Amber’s worldview was formed through repeated exposure to systemic failure and existential vulnerability.

Her life included:
	•	war
	•	collapse
	•	abandonment
	•	betrayal
	•	torture
	•	leadership under pressure
	•	and attempted extermination by corrupt power structures

From these experiences, she concluded that corruption at scale repeatedly destroys mankind’s ambitions.

That belief became central to the Dominion worldview.

⸻

What lesson did Amber carry into the Dominion?

The core lesson Amber carried forward was:

No one will save us, and it will not be given willingly.

She also learned that:
	•	dependency leads to capture
	•	corruption scales faster than virtue
	•	and survival requires preparation before crisis arrives

These beliefs became the seed of Dominion doctrine.

⸻

SECTION IX — EQUILON, HARMINA, AND THE FOUNDING ESCAPE

⸻

What was the Equilon Union?

The Equilon Union was the interstellar human colony polity that would eventually become the precursor to the Dominion.

It was a distant colony system that sought genuine independence from outside control.

Its leadership did not want to remain subject to the political and structural influence of larger powers during its vulnerable developmental stage.

⸻

Who was the Equilon Union trying to break away from?

The Equilon Union was in conflict with a larger human authority known as the Colonial Union.

The Colonial Union demanded submission to Earth-centered authority and tightly controlled both governance and strategic communications.

This eventually brought it into direct conflict with the Equilon Union’s desire for sovereignty.

⸻

Why did Equilon want independence?

Because it wanted true self-determination and did not want to become structurally dependent on outside powers.

Its leadership recognized that if it remained under external authority while still vulnerable, it could be permanently shaped into dependency and subordination.

This concern would later become one of the deepest foundations of Dominion thought.

⸻

What triggered the final break?

Tensions escalated after the Equilon Union resisted Colonial Union authority and destroyed the first investigation fleet sent to reassert control.

In response, the Colonial Union deployed a stronger interdiction fleet equipped with the first known FTL warp drives.

This sharply increased the threat to Equilon’s survival.

⸻

How did Equilon develop the technology that led to its escape?

Using plans and recovered data from the destroyed investigation fleet, the Equilon Union attempted to replicate warp-drive technology.

However, instead of reproducing a refined warp drive, they created something much more unstable:
	•	multiple drives were coupled together
	•	in an attempt to compensate for technical limitations
	•	and the result was not a proper warp system

Instead, this accidental overbuilt solution became the first Jump Drive.

⸻

Why did humanity leave the Milky Way?

Humanity did not leave the Milky Way as part of a grand planned expansion.

It happened during a survival-driven attempt to escape destruction and subjugation.

The future Dominion settlers were trying to survive political and military extinction.

Instead, they accidentally crossed a technological threshold no one had intended.

⸻

What was the first Jump like?

The first jump was a completely blind jump.

It was believed at first to be a failed or malformed warp transit event.

But after the jump, something was clearly wrong:

the stars were missing

The familiar galactic backdrop was gone.

The survivors realized they had not merely moved within the Milky Way.

They had left it entirely.

⸻

What happened physically during the Jump?

The jump caused:
	•	overloaded systems
	•	severe spacetime stress on components
	•	and injuries among the survivors

It was not a clean or elegant transit.

It was a dangerous rupture into the unknown.

⸻

What was the most important consequence of the Jump?

The most important consequence was not just displacement.

It was realization.

The survivors understood that humanity had accidentally developed a class of transit technology that even larger surrounding powers did not seem to possess.

This produced a deep and lasting paranoia.

It was a civilization-defining moment.

In significance, it can be compared to the discovery of nuclear energy.

⸻

SECTION X — WHY THEY SURVIVED

⸻

Why didn’t the Colonial Union find them after the Jump?

Because the jump did not obviously look like “humanity has left the galaxy.”

The only major detectable sign was a gravitational anomaly that was interpreted as an unusual warp event.

To the Colonial Union, it appeared as if the rogue colony had simply escaped by more conventional means.

So they searched:
	•	nearby systems
	•	plausible escape routes
	•	and surrounding stellar regions

…and found nothing.

To them, Equilon appeared lost.

⸻

Did the Federation understand what happened?

No.

The larger galactic Federation only detected a focused gravitational anomaly.

It was unusual enough to eventually warrant attention, but not enough for them to correctly understand what had occurred.

They did not possess this class of transit technology and did not recognize its full implications.

⸻

What was the Federation?

The Federation was a galaxy-spanning collective of civilizations.

Harmina was only one world among billions within its broader territory.

However, the Federation was not a neutral or benevolent order.

At its core, it functioned as a parasitic client-state structure dominated by its founding member civilizations.

⸻

Why did the future Dominion distrust the Federation?

Because they recognized it as a system that prevented true sovereignty.

The Federation maintained control in part through enforced material specialization.

This meant worlds or civilizations were pressured into focusing on only a narrow range of strategic outputs, making them dependent on the larger Federation system for everything else.

That created:
	•	economic dependency
	•	political subordination
	•	strategic weakness
	•	and long-term structural capture

The future Dominion leadership understood this as a trap.

⸻

Why was Harmina a good place to hide?

Harmina worked because it was ignored, not because it was secretly ideal.

The Federation classified it as a Deathworld.

Its native life and environmental conditions were considered too hostile, too dangerous, and too unprofitable for normal large-scale civilizational development.

That meant it was treated as restricted and undesirable space.

That gave the future Dominion something invaluable:
	•	obscurity
	•	time
	•	and room to build

⸻

How did Harmina survive and succeed?

Because the settlers arrived with exceptional preparation.

The mission was effectively a successor-state rebuild operation.

The goal was not simply survival.

The goal was to:
	•	settle Harmina
	•	rebuild infrastructure
	•	and become an independent interstellar civilization again within only a few generations

Their success was made possible by:
	•	preparation
	•	technological capability
	•	strategic caution
	•	and refusal to become dependent

⸻

How long did they remain hidden long enough to rebuild?

On average, they had a few centuries of relative breathing room before outside discovery became a serious concern.

That developmental window was critical.

It allowed them to harden into something far more dangerous than a lost colony.

It allowed them to become the early foundation of the future Dominion.
