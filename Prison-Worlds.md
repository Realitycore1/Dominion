Prison Worlds

Summary

A Prison World is a planet, moon, habitat, asteroid complex, artificial world, or isolated colony whose dominant purpose is the containment, punishment, rehabilitation, exile, labor control, quarantine, or political disappearance of prisoners.

In simple terms:

A Prison World is a civilization’s decision about what to do with people it refuses to trust.

That makes this class morally volatile.

A Prison World can be:

* a secure rehabilitation colony
* a penal labor world
* a fortress prison
* an exile planet
* a political blacksite
* a quarantine prison
* a debt-labor colony
* a war-criminal holding world
* a maximum-security containment site
* a population dumping ground

The difference between justice and atrocity depends almost entirely on governance, oversight, legal access, and prisoner status.

⸻

Defining Traits

Prison Worlds commonly contain:

* planetary detention zones
* orbital interdiction grids
* forced labor camps
* exile settlements
* maximum-security citadels
* correctional megastructures
* surveillance networks
* automated perimeter systems
* penal colonies
* prison cities
* rehabilitation districts
* execution grounds
* judicial archives
* interrogation facilities
* labor extraction zones
* guard academies
* identity-control systems
* restricted transit ports
* anti-escape orbital defenses
* bio-tracking infrastructure
* remote medical facilities
* prisoner classification centers

Some Prison Worlds look like cages.

Others look like ordinary colonies where no one is allowed to leave.

The second kind is usually more unsettling.

⸻

Primary Function

Prison Worlds may serve as:

* criminal detention sites
* political exile worlds
* penal labor colonies
* rehabilitation settlements
* high-security containment zones
* war-criminal prisons
* debt-labor worlds
* quarantine prisons
* ideological reeducation sites
* dangerous offender containment worlds
* military prisoner-of-war sites
* anti-technomagic detention sites
* Aether-sensitive containment worlds
* blacksite disappearance zones

The central function is controlled removal from normal society.

That removal may be temporary, permanent, lawful, corrupt, humane, or monstrous.

⸻

Governmental Role

Prison Worlds are always legal stress tests.

They reveal what a civilization actually believes about:

* guilt
* punishment
* mercy
* rights
* labor
* citizenship
* rehabilitation
* social danger
* political dissent
* due process
* bodily autonomy
* death penalties
* exile
* memory and identity

A healthy state treats Prison Worlds as accountable legal institutions.

A corrupt state treats them as places where law goes to stop being seen.

That is the key line.

⸻

Economic Role

Prison Worlds often produce value through controlled labor or specialized containment.

Common exports include:

* mined resources
* manufactured goods
* hazardous labor output
* recycled materials
* penal agricultural output
* construction labor
* industrial parts
* military support goods
* waste processing
* dangerous research labor
* correctional expertise
* security doctrine
* containment technologies

They import:

* guards
* food
* medicine
* surveillance systems
* restraint technology
* legal inspectors
* weapons
* prison administrators
* mental health personnel
* replacement machinery
* orbital security systems
* disciplinary AI
* judicial data
* anti-riot equipment

The danger is obvious:

Once punishment becomes profitable, innocence becomes economically inconvenient.

That should be a major warning attached to the class.

⸻

Infrastructure

Prison World infrastructure depends on containment philosophy.

Fortress Prison Model

Features:

* sealed citadels
* orbital guns
* shielded walls
* guard towers
* kill zones
* surveillance grids
* underground cells
* layered checkpoints

Penal Colony Model

Features:

* prisoner settlements
* work zones
* ration stations
* limited self-governance
* controlled agriculture
* forced or semi-forced labor
* distant guard cities

Exile World Model

Features:

* minimal guards
* hostile environment
* no easy exit
* monitored orbital blockade
* prisoner-built society
* survival as containment

Rehabilitation World Model

Features:

* therapy centers
* education blocks
* restorative justice courts
* monitored communities
* civic labor programs
* reintegration testing
* psychological care systems

Blacksite Model

Features:

* no public records
* sealed transit
* hidden locations
* interrogation wings
* identity erasure
* classified prisoners
* no legal visibility

The model says more about the civilization than the prisoners do.

⸻

Social Character

Prison World societies are shaped by status, control, scarcity, violence, and institutional distrust.

Common traits:

* rigid hierarchy
* black markets
* guard/prisoner caste separation
* coded speech
* survival networks
* institutional brutality or trauma
* suspicion of informants
* intense local loyalties
* ritualized defiance
* fear of transfer
* legal hopelessness
* underground faiths
* prison economies
* scars as social records
* improvised families
* hatred of administrators

In more humane rehabilitation worlds, the culture may instead center on:

* restitution
* monitored autonomy
* psychological recovery
* skill training
* civic reintegration
* survivor-offender mediation
* strict but transparent law

But even humane Prison Worlds carry stigma.

A person released from one may never fully escape the classification.

⸻

Military Value

Prison Worlds can have high strategic value despite not being conventional military centers.

They may contain:

* dangerous prisoners
* captured officers
* political hostages
* war criminals
* enemy intelligence assets
* penal battalions
* forced labor industries
* hostile Aether users
* captured entities
* illegal weapons researchers
* insurgent leaders
* black project witnesses

Common wartime uses include:

* POW containment
* penal military recruitment
* political hostage storage
* forced industrial production
* interrogation centers
* war-crime tribunals
* exile of defeated elites
* containment of dangerous technologies or beings

Common wartime threats:

* prison uprising
* enemy rescue raid
* guard mutiny
* prisoner radicalization
* orbital blockade breach
* mass execution cover-up
* hostage crisis
* evidence destruction
* forced recruitment revolt
* prison-world secession

A Prison World can become an army if its prisoners organize.

That is a great plot engine.

⸻

Common Failure Modes

Legal Disappearance

Prisoners lose access to courts, names, records, appeals, or outside witnesses.

Penal Profit

The economy depends on prisoner labor, creating incentives for mass incarceration.

Guard Sovereignty

Prison staff become the true rulers and operate beyond civilian law.

Prisoner Kingdoms

Gangs, cults, warlords, or ideological factions control internal life.

Rehabilitation Fraud

The world claims to reform prisoners but actually breaks them into compliance.

Political Purge

The prison system is used to remove dissidents, rivals, journalists, scholars, or inconvenient officials.

Generational Captivity

Children are born into prison settlements and inherit stigma or confinement.

Containment Collapse

A riot, war, plague, or infrastructure failure releases dangerous prisoners or entities.

Dehumanization Culture

Prisoners stop being treated as citizens, persons, or moral subjects.

Exile State Formation

The prison population builds its own identity and eventually becomes a hostile polity.

⸻

Variants

Penal Labor World

Prisoners work mines, factories, farms, construction, or hazardous industries.

Rehabilitation World

Designed for reform, monitored autonomy, education, restitution, and reintegration.

Exile World

Prisoners are deposited on a remote world and prevented from leaving.

Fortress Prison World

Maximum-security planetary prison with heavy guard infrastructure.

Political Prison World

Used for dissidents, nobles, rebels, intellectuals, religious leaders, or ideological enemies.

Blacksite Prison World

Classified detention site outside normal legal oversight.

POW World

Military prisoner world for captured combatants.

Quarantine Prison World

Contains prisoners who are biologically, metaphysically, cognitively, or Aetherically dangerous.

Null Prison World

A Prison World with Null conditions to suppress technomagic, Aether abilities, entities, or reality manipulation.

Death Sentence World

A hostile world where survival itself is punishment; often legally disguised execution.
