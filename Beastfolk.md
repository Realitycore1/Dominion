Classification: Sentient Hybrids & Divergent Peoples
Subtype: Permanent Therian Lineages
Status: Active, Culturally Diverse, Persistent

⸻

Core Description

Beastfolk are sapient beings whose bodies and cultures permanently diverged from baseline humanity through ancient curses, forced adaptation, ritual binding, or long-term exposure to altered environments. Unlike shapeshifters or curse-victims, Beastfolk do not revert.

They are born as they are.
They live as they are.
They die as they are.

Their forms range from near-human with subtle animal traits to fully bestial humanoids, depending on lineage and age.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Beastfolk are stabilized outcomes, not active transformations.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Material Continuum
	•	Secondary Influence: Vital Expanse (adaptive biology)
	•	Conditional Influences: Curse residue, environmental saturation, ancestral pacts

They emerge when:
	•	a curse persists long enough to stabilize genetically,
	•	survival pressure reshapes a population irreversibly,
	•	ritual or planar exposure affects multiple generations.

They are not monsters by default.
They are people shaped by non-human answers to survival.

⸻

Intelligence & Psychology

Beastfolk possess full sapience.

However, cognition often reflects:
	•	heightened instinctual processing,
	•	altered social bonding,
	•	non-human sensory priorities.

This does not reduce intelligence—it reorients it.

A ratkin mind values networks.
A dogman mind values hierarchy and loyalty.
A cervine beastfolk mind values territory and cycles.

⸻

Social Structures

Beastfolk societies tend toward:
	•	clans, packs, nests, or warrens,
	•	rigid social roles,
	•	ancestral memory preservation.

Many Beastfolk cultures:
	•	predate human nation-states,
	•	retain oral or ritual histories,
	•	distrust centralized authority.

Some form empires.
Others remain isolationist by design.

⸻

Origins & Lineages

Common Beastfolk origins include:
	•	Curse-Stabilized Lineages
(e.g., permanent lycanthropic inheritance)
	•	Environmental Divergence
(e.g., subterranean ratkin, ashland dogmen)
	•	Ritual-Bound Peoples
(e.g., guardian clans fused with animal spirits)
	•	Post-Collapse Evolution
(e.g., Europolis ashland beast-tribes)

Once stabilized, Beastfolk breed true.

⸻

Relationship to Curses

Beastfolk are not actively cursed, but many descend from curses.

This distinction matters:
	•	They do not spread transformation by contact.
	•	They do not experience forced change.
	•	They cannot be “cured” without annihilation.

Attempts to “restore” Beastfolk to humanity often result in:
	•	death,
	•	madness,
	•	monstrous regression.

⸻

Variants (Examples)
	•	Dogmen: Pack-based, territorial, loyalty-driven
	•	Ratkin: Networked, urban or subterranean survivalists
	•	Boarfolk: Aggressive, clan-war oriented
	•	Feline Folk: Solitary, trade-focused, predatory
	•	Avian Folk: High-mobility, sky-adapted cultures
	•	Hybrid Beastfolk: Result of layered lineage or collapse-era experimentation

Hybrids often face internal instability or cultural schisms.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Heightened vulnerability to prejudice and extermination campaigns
	•	Physiological specialization limits adaptability
	•	Often targeted by religious or purist movements
	•	Hybridization can destabilize lineage coherence

Despite this, Beastfolk endure because they adapt faster than ideology.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Humans: Often hostile, sometimes allied
	•	Shapeshifters: Viewed as unreliable or dangerous
	•	Wendigo/Rugaru: Seen as existential threats
	•	Domovoi & Land Spirits: Frequently allied
	•	Vampires: Rare overlords or mutual predators

Beastfolk remember when humans were weaker.
