🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: Ashoka’s Pillars
Discovery Location: Multiple sites across the Indian subcontinent, with a significant rediscovery in Vaishali, Bihar, during metro excavations in 2039.
Summary:

Material: Polished sandstone monoliths, average height 12–15 meters.

Known Inscriptions: Edicts of Emperor Ashoka (3rd century BCE) — early moral-legal codes promoting dharma, tolerance, and nonviolence.

New Findings:

Radiometric scans suggest subsurface chambers beneath the Vaishali pillar base, previously undetected.

Inscriptions discovered in an unknown Brahmi variant, provisionally dated to late Mauryan period.

Trace residues of cinnabar and gold leaf hint that some pillars were once richly decorated — challenging assumptions of austere presentation.

Archaeological Significance: Confirms Ashoka’s edicts as both religious and state propaganda; possible hidden codices suggest an attempt to preserve imperial legitimacy across ages.
