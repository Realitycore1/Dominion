One of the most massive galaxy–cluster concentrations in the nearby universe (often called the Shapley Concentration). It’s a supercluster complex—multiple rich clusters and filaments bound in a sprawling node of the cosmic web.

Where/scale.
	•	Direction: Centaurus/Norma region (behind parts of the Milky Way).
	•	Distance: 200 Mpc (650 million ly).
	•	Extent/mass: spans tens of Mpc with total mass of ~10¹⁶–10¹⁷ M☉ spread across many clusters and connecting filaments.

[[Key]] members & structure.
	•	Rich [[X-Ray]] clusters (e.g., Abell 3558, A3562, A3556, A3559) plus groups and walls.
	•	Not a single virialized object—still collapsing along filaments; strong substructure and merging activity.

Cosmic-flow role.
	•	Acts as a major attractor for galaxy motions on 100–300 Mpc scales.
	•	Our Local Group’s bulk flow is well described by a push–pull picture: pushed from nearby voids/repellers and pulled toward the [[Great Attractor]] region and onward to Shapley.

How we study it.
	•	Redshift surveys (optical/IR/radio) through [[The Zone of Avoidance]] to map membership.
	•	[[X-Ray]]/SZ to weigh hot intracluster gas and merger shocks.
	•	Peculiar-velocity reconstructions to track the converging flow into the node.
	•	Weak lensing to trace dark-matter mass in the densest cores.

Why it matters.
	•	Benchmarks cluster assembly and mergers in a high-density environment.
	•	Anchors models of local large-scale flows (CMB dipole context).
	•	Serves as a laboratory for cosmic web dynamics, CR/shock [[Physics]], and feedback in extreme clusters.
