The Destiny of the Integral

•	Self-Perception
	•	[[Estela Integralism]] believes it is the vanguard of [[Human]] destiny, chosen to unify the [[Stars]] under a single will.
	•	Citizens are taught from birth that their state is the only true civilization, all others being weak, impure, or decadent.
	•	Their propaganda calls them the “Forge of Purity”—a people tempered in struggle, made unbreakable by devotion to the Caudillo.
	•	View of Other [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Colonies]]
	•	Oath-bound societies like [[Austro Guard]] are dismissed as shackled by weakness, unable to seize destiny.
	•	Hierarchical states like [[Eprea Hindutva]] are respected for their order but condemned as spiritually stagnant.
	•	Liberal or pluralistic [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Colonies]] are considered enemies of destiny, ripe for conquest or annihilation.
	•	Collective Ethos
	•	Unity in Submission: individuality is treason; greatness is only found in collective obedience.
	•	Expansion as Proof: conquest is not optional—it is the colony’s sacred purpose.
	•	Fear as Glue: loyalty is less about belief and more about survival; terror binds society as tightly as devotion.
	•	Symbols of Self
	•	The Black Sun: their emblem, symbolizing eternal radiance and inevitable destiny.
	•	Twin Comets: representing struggle and victory, always flanking the Black Sun in art.
	•	The Caudillo’s Image: omnipresent, painted, sculpted, projected—his visage is synonymous with the state itself.
	•	Foreign Reputation
	•	To allies, they are dangerous but undeniably effective: their fleets arrive on [[Time]], their [[Soldiers]] obey without hesitation.
	•	To enemies, they are fanatics, terrifying in their willingness to die en masse for spectacle and ideology.
	•	To neutral observers, they are a civilization that burns bright but seems destined to devour itself.

[[Estela Integralism]] sees itself as destiny embodied, a colony where loyalty and fear merge into one identity: the people as weapon, the leader as destiny, the state as eternity.
