I. Physical Definition

A Holographic Star is a stellar-scale phenomenon whose observable structure and luminosity are not the actual source of its mass–energy, but rather a projection or encoded manifestation derived from information stored on a lower-dimensional [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Oneiroverse Multiverse/Chorus of Archetypes/Boundary]] surface.

[[Key]] attributes:
	•	Core Reality Layer — The “true” physical object may be compact (planetary- to asteroid-scale) or exist in another dimension entirely.
	•	Holographic Envelope — The visible star is an energy–information reconstruction that behaves like a normal star to external observers.
	•	Encoded Surface [[Physics]] — Stellar behavior is generated from data stored on a 2D or n-D [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Oneiroverse Multiverse/Chorus of Archetypes/Boundary]], following the holographic principle.
	•	No Traditional Fusion Core — Energy output is drawn from computation, exotic particle decay, or direct [[Verseon]] lattice coupling.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Holographic [[Stars]] are considered Energy–Information Projection Nodes, functioning as light sources, navigation markers, and information [[Carriers]] while requiring only a fraction of the mass of a conventional star.
