Classification: Liminal Beasts & Territorial Spirits
Subtype: Guardian-Predator / Geomorphic Entity
Status: Active, Site-Bound, Persistent

⸻

Core Description

Taniwha are massive, serpentine or draconic entities bound to specific landscapes—rivers, caves, lakes, reefs, gorges, or mountain passes. Their forms vary widely: eel-like leviathans, crocodilian giants, horned serpents, or stone-scaled beasts whose bodies echo the terrain they inhabit.

A Taniwha is not defined by its body.
It is defined by where it is allowed to exist.

To enter a Taniwha’s domain is to accept terms you may not understand.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Taniwha are liminal territorial entities, born where Vital Expanse, local myth, and geography overlap long enough to stabilize.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Liminal Thresholds (Material ↔ Spirit)
	•	Secondary Influence: Vital Expanse (endurance), Localized Mythic Resonance
	•	Realm Interface: Shadow Realms (Umbrae), Bleeding Wilds (localized), river-realms

They are neither gods nor simple animals.
They are embodied boundary conditions.

⸻

Intelligence & Will

Taniwha possess situational sapience.

They understand:
	•	territory,
	•	ritual behavior,
	•	obligation,
	•	patterns of respect or violation.

They do not think globally.
They think locally and absolutely.

A Taniwha may:
	•	protect travelers who follow custom,
	•	annihilate those who break taboo,
	•	ignore entire generations,
	•	awaken suddenly when balance is disturbed.

⸻

Behavior & Ecology

Taniwha inhabit:
	•	river mouths,
	•	deep caves,
	•	coastal reefs,
	•	volcanic tunnels,
	•	sacred valleys.

They regulate:
	•	flood cycles,
	•	predator access,
	•	migration routes,
	•	spiritual contamination.

They may act as:
	•	guardians,
	•	punishers,
	•	deterrents,
	•	ecological stabilizers.

When a Taniwha leaves its domain, it weakens rapidly.

⸻

Feeding & Sustenance

Taniwha feed on:
	•	large prey,
	•	spiritual residue,
	•	offerings,
	•	intruders who violate their bounds.

Some subsist almost entirely on ritual interaction rather than flesh.

Starving a Taniwha by breaking tradition often results in violent reassertion of presence.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Cannot stray far from their bound domain
	•	Severely weakened by removal of cultural memory
	•	Vulnerable to nullification of local myth structures
	•	Susceptible to deliberate desecration—but at catastrophic cost

Destroying a Taniwha usually destabilizes the region it guarded.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Sea Serpents: Occasionally mistaken for Taniwha, but lack territorial binding
	•	Kami: Closest relatives; Kami are abstract, Taniwha are physical
	•	Dragons: Dragons dominate broadly; Taniwha dominate precisely
	•	Humans: Long-term cohabitation possible through ritual
	•	Titans: Titans override; Taniwha resist until destroyed

Taniwha do not rule.
They enforce.

⸻

Variants
	•	River Taniwha: Flood-bringers or protectors
	•	Cave Taniwha: Subterranean guardians
	•	Reef Taniwha: Coastal wardens, ship-breakers
	•	Mountain Taniwha: Pass-keepers and avalanche spirits
	•	Urban Taniwha: Rare; bound to infrastructure or buried rivers

Urban Taniwha are volatile and often misidentified as disasters.
