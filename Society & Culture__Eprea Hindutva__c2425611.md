Life in the Frozen Order

The [[Eprea Hindutva]] is less a civilization than a living hierarchy. Every citizen is born into a slot and dies in it, their place bound by blood, biometric tag, and the [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Interstice City/Doctrine|Doctrine]] of purity.
	•	Caste as Destiny
	•	Brahmic Caste – priestly-technocrats, custodians of ideology and knowledge. They monopolize philosophy, education, and advanced sciences.
	•	Kshatriya Caste – warriors, officers, and enforcers of order. They are the sword of the regime, drawn against both external enemies and internal dissent.
	•	Vaishya Caste – merchants, [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/Biology & Lifecycle/Engineers|Engineers]], administrators of [[Production]]. They run the industrial machine but are excluded from policy.
	•	Shudra Caste – laborers and menial [[Workers]], the unyielding base of society.
	•	Dalits/Untouchables – outcastes, forced into the most dangerous frontier tasks (asteroid mining, plasma-waste disposal). Officially invisible, yet their suffering props up the [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/United Authority of Systems/System|System]].
	•	Education & Indoctrination
	•	Children are separated into caste academies by age 6, where indoctrination begins.
	•	Brahmic schools teach philosophy and governance, while Shudra schools drill obedience and toil.
	•	Daily lessons end with recitations of the Mantra of Purity, affirming loyalty to caste and Pradhān.
	•	Family Life
	•	Marriages are arranged within caste, negotiated by councils and overseers. Inter-caste marriage is forbidden and punished by exile.
	•	Families take pride not in honor or wealth, but in purity of lineage—a family unbroken by scandal or “impurity” is a jewel in caste politics.
	•	[[Ritual]] & Ceremony
	•	Public life is dominated by caste ceremonies:
	•	Processions of Purity – annual marches of Brahmic [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Synarchate/Government/Leaders|Leaders]] through cities, where lower castes kneel as they pass.
	•	Ash Oaths – Kshatriya initiation rituals where initiates swear fealty while branded with heated iron sigils.
	•	Silent Days – Vaishya-led civic shutdowns where commerce halts for state-[[Wide]] reflection.
	•	[[culture]] & Expression
	•	Music and art glorify the cosmic order, often depicting tiers, pyramids, or concentric circles.
	•	Poetry is dominated by hymns to order and destiny, though forbidden “low-caste ballads” circulate underground, telling stories of rebellion.
	•	Architecture mirrors hierarchy—cities are concentric: Brahmic [[Temples]] in the high spires, Shudra blocks at the periphery, Dalits pushed underground or to orbital ghettos.
	•	Social Discipline
	•	Public honor is displayed by obedience: bowing in the presence of higher caste, wearing caste-marked colors, reciting chants when summoned.
	•	To question the hierarchy is to brand oneself an “impure.” Most dissenters vanish into exile camps or frontier mines.
