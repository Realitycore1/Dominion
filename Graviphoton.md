I. Physical Definition

In high-energy theoretical [[Physics]], particularly in supergravity and certain string theory models, the graviphoton is a vector boson arising from the unification of the electromagnetic and gravitational sectors in higher-dimensional field [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Synarchate/Science/Frameworks|frameworks]]. It can be viewed as the Kaluza-Klein excitation of a photon propagating through compactified extra dimensions, inheriting properties from both the [[Graviton]] and photon.

Within the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), graviphotons are multi-field resonance quanta of the Unified Lattice Field (ULF), existing where the gravitational curvature field and the electromagnetic gauge field interlock. They carry both gravitic curvature influence and EM vector potential, allowing for manipulation of both mass-energy and charge distributions simultaneously.
	•	Spin: 1 (Vector boson)
	•	Mass: 0 in unbroken symmetry states; can acquire effective mass via lattice tension in compactified [[Domains]].
	•	Charge: Neutral under EM charge but exhibits effective EM-gravitic dual charge in [[VFF]] terms.
	•	Interaction: Couples to both EM and gravitational fields; weakly interacts with standard matter except in high-curvature or strong-field [[Regions]].
