Core Concept (Why this works)

They survive in:
	•	Interstellar medium
	•	Oort clouds
	•	Debris halos around stars
	•	Post-supernova dust fields
	•	Accretion skirts near black holes (outer, survivable zones)

They filter:
	•	Micrometeoroids
	•	Carbon-rich dust
	•	Ice grains
	•	Ionized particles
	•	Exotic radiation byproducts

They don’t hunt.
They harvest entropy gradients.

⸻

Biology That Makes Sense

Structure
	•	Bell / Mantle
A semi-rigid, radiation-tolerant membrane that acts like a sail and funnel.
	•	Filtration Tendrils
Kilometer-scale strands with electrostatic charge differentials that attract dust, ions, and micro-debris.
	•	Metabolic Core
Converts matter + radiation into long-term chemical and electrical energy storage.
	•	Neural Simplicity
No centralized brain. Reflexive, distributed signaling—closer to coral or slime molds.

This keeps them:
	•	Non-sentient (mostly)
	•	Non-strategic
	•	Non-anthropomorphic

Which is good.

⸻

How They “Eat”

They exploit physics humans usually avoid:
	•	Electrostatic capture – Dust clings naturally.
	•	Radiolysis – Radiation breaks molecules into usable forms.
	•	Piezoelectric flex – Motion through particle clouds generates charge.
	•	Slow fusion catalysis (rare variants) – Not reactors, but passive isotopic energy extraction.

They’re basically living particle collectors.

⸻

Lifecycle
	•	Spawning phase
Occurs in dense debris fields after stellar death or planetary collisions.
	•	Drift phase
Millions of years of slow migration along gravitational gradients.
	•	Bloom events
When conditions spike (supernova remnants, collapsing systems), populations explode.
	•	Senescence
Eventually fragment into nutrient dust, feeding the next generation.

This makes them:
	•	Ancient
	•	Persistent
	•	Largely indifferent to civilizations

Which explains why they predate and outlast empires.

Interaction With Civilizations

This is where your setting shines.

Early Humanity
	•	Mistaken for omens, gods, angels, demons.
	•	Associated with stellar disasters.

Spacefaring Civilizations
	•	Navigation hazards.
	•	Communication interference.
	•	Radiation shielding anomalies.

Advanced Powers (Dominion-tier)
	•	Studied as cosmic keystone species.
	•	Sometimes protected.
	•	Sometimes harvested carefully.
	•	Never fully controlled.

Because killing them destabilizes dust flows, star formation regions, and long-term orbital mechanics.

They are ecological load-bearing entities.

Aetherophaga dispersa
	•	Common interstellar drifters
	•	Low interaction, dust-feeders

Aetherophaga gravitalis
	•	Found near stars, debris disks, gravity wells

Aetherophaga postnovae
	•	Blooming populations in supernova remnants

Aetherophaga liminalis
	•	Rare edge-cases interacting with exotic fields
	•	Heavily debated, often misclassified

This lets you write clinical reports without myth creeping in.
