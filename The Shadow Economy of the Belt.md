I. [[Corporate Fleets]] & Syndicates
	•	[[Corporate Fleets]]:
	•	Mega-firms operate heavily armed mining convoys, complete with escort craft and modular refineries.
	•	They often double as “private navies,” imposing monopolies and blockades on smaller guilds.
	•	Syndicates & Cartels:
	•	Emerged in the power vacuum after the [[Ceres]] [[Schism]].
	•	Specialize in controlling chokepoints: ice shipments, oxygen recyclers, or fusion fuel.
	•	Some present themselves as “protection guilds,” others openly run as mafias.

⸻

II. PMCs & Mafias
	•	PMCs (Private [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Military|Military]] Companies):
	•	Rent themselves to both guilds and corporates, often switching sides.
	•	Keep orbital shipyards busy with constant demand for escort craft, drones, and boarding pods.
	•	Mafias:
	•	Operate inside habitats, controlling smuggling, gambling, and black markets.
	•	Specialize in “luxury crimes”: extortion for air, water, and food supplies.
	•	Entire habs have been turned into mafia strongholds, with oxygen held as ransom.

⸻

III. The Rarest Commodities: Art & Organics
	•	Wood, Leather, Cloth: Genuine [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] organics become priceless. A chair made of oak or a painting on canvas can spark bidding wars across the Belt.
	•	Art Theft: Syndicates steal cultural artifacts from [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] vaults, smuggling them into the Belt where they become status symbols in habitat courts.
	•	Organics Black Market:
	•	Smuggled seeds, pets, or even soil from [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] → luxury contraband.
	•	Cartels splice organics into [[Hydroponics]] lines to sell “authentic” food experiences (a tomato grown in soil instead of nutrient gel might cost a year’s wages).

⸻

IV. [[culture]] of Smuggling
	•	The Chainfolk learn to live in the cracks: small freighters running contraband between habs, bribing customs with oxygen and favors.
	•	Smugglers become folk heroes, celebrated in song — balancing survival with defiance against cartels and corporates.
	•	A black-market lingua franca develops: hand-sign codes, creole slang, and encrypted glyphs stenciled on hulls.

⸻

V. Long-Term Consequences
	•	The Belt Confederacy never fully eradicates crime — it incorporates it. Cartels and mafias often hold official seats in local councils, disguised as “resource guilds.”
	•	Art and organics crime shapes [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Identity|Identity]]: Chainfolk value authenticity so much that a piece of [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] wood or handwoven cloth becomes sacred.
	•	Over centuries, this black [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Kuomin Empire/Economy|Economy]] helps define Belt [[culture]] as much as oxygen-sharing and guild [[law]]: to be Belt is to live between survival and shadow.
