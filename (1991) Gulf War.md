The Gulf War was a conflict that occurred between Iraq and a coalition of international forces in 1990-1991. The war began after Iraq invaded and occupied the small country of Kuwait, which was an ally of the United States and other Western countries. The coalition, which included the United States, Saudi Arabia, and other countries, responded with a military intervention aimed at removing Iraq from Kuwait and restoring the country's independence. The war involved extensive aerial bombardment and ground combat operations, resulting in significant damage to both Iraq and Kuwait. The war also had a profound impact on Iraq, with the destruction of much of the country's infrastructure and a severe economic crisis that lasted for years afterward. It also led to a massive humanitarian crisis, with millions of people displaced and facing severe poverty and health problems. Despite its relatively short duration, the Gulf War remains a significant event in modern history, with its legacy continuing to shape regional politics and relations between the United States and its allies on one hand, and Iraq and its neighbors on the other.
