I. Physical Definition

A Supernova Star is a stellar-mass object in the critical pre-supernova stage — a short-lived state where extreme core instability precedes catastrophic detonation — followed by the supernova event itself.
In astrophysical terms, this occurs when the stellar core can no longer support itself against gravity, leading to sudden collapse and violent outer-envelope ejection.

There are multiple [[Natural]] pathways:
	•	Type II Supernovae — Massive [[Stars]] (>8 M☉) exhausting nuclear fuel, collapsing core, and rebound shock.
	•	Type Ia Supernovae — White dwarfs exceeding the Chandrasekhar limit due to mass transfer from a companion.
	•	Pair-Instability Supernovae — Core photons converting to electron–positron pairs, removing pressure support and triggering collapse.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), a Supernova Star is classified as a Terminal-State Stellar Engine — not just an astrophysical event, but also a cosmic energy release node capable of reshaping local [[Verse]] topology.
