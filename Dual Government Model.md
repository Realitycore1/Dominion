# Dual Government Model

## Summary

The Dominion is a dual government built around two different time horizons.

The Federal Government governs the now: daily law, representation, services, courts, budgets, provincial administration, civic rights, ordinary security, and the practical needs of living citizens.

The Royal Government governs the future: continuity, existential planning, civilizational memory, long-horizon defense, succession, VFF and MOT risk, post-collapse recovery, and threats too large or slow for normal politics to handle.

This is not a contradiction between republic and monarchy. It is the core design.

The Federal Government protects mankind from being ruled only by myth, emergency, and distant strategy.

The Royal Government protects mankind from being ruined by short-term politics, local incentives, and the inability of ordinary institutions to plan across centuries or catastrophes.

## The Two Clocks

### The Federal Clock: The Now

The Federal Government is best suited for:

- civil law;
- budgets and taxation;
- courts and ordinary justice;
- provincial, state, and local administration;
- public services;
- healthcare distribution;
- education policy;
- infrastructure maintenance;
- citizen rights and franchise;
- routine defense authorization;
- oversight of ministries and agencies;
- public consent and representation.

Its strength is proximity. It understands daily life because it is built from people who live inside daily life.

Its weakness is horizon. Elections, factions, budgets, local emergencies, and public pressure make it poorly suited for threats that unfold over centuries or across unstable reality conditions.

### The Royal Clock: The Future

The Royal Government is best suited for:

- continuity doctrine;
- existential threat planning;
- civilizational memory;
- succession integrity;
- long-horizon defense;
- VFF and MOT instability;
- relic and forbidden-technology containment;
- evacuation chains;
- post-collapse restoration;
- preservation of mankind across discontinuity;
- threats that ordinary politics will underfund until it is too late.

Its strength is horizon. It can remember beyond a term, a faction, a market cycle, or a single generation.

Its weakness is abstraction. If unchecked, it can treat living citizens as future variables, justify emergency permanence, or overshadow the local life it claims to protect.

## Boundary Principle

The Dominion works only when each government respects the other's time scale.

The Federal Government may not sacrifice mankind's future for immediate comfort, political popularity, or administrative convenience.

The Royal Government may not consume daily life in the name of a future so distant that living people become tools.

The present is not lesser than the future.

The future is not optional because the present is busy.

## How Power Is Divided

### Federal Powers

Federal power covers ordinary governance:

- legislation;
- court systems;
- civil rights;
- public service;
- franchise and elections;
- provincial and local administration;
- social welfare;
- ordinary economic regulation;
- non-existential military oversight;
- civilian agency management.

The Federal Government can resist the Royal Government through lawful procedure, witness demands, review, public record, budget refusal, and constitutional challenge.

### Royal Powers

Royal power covers continuity governance:

- existential emergency authority;
- continuity archives;
- sacred-civic rites;
- succession and witness-chain integrity;
- continuity defense forces;
- memory courts;
- civilizational restoration mandates;
- long-horizon infrastructure obligations;
- reality-stability and VFF safety;
- final appeal in species-level disputes.

The Royal Government can override ordinary procedure only when continuity is immediately threatened. Such action must be witnessed, named, archived, and reviewed after the crisis.

## Institutional Tension

The Dominion deliberately preserves tension between the two governments.

If the Federal Government always wins, the Dominion becomes short-sighted, popular, fragmented, and unable to prepare for existential threats until they arrive.

If the Royal Government always wins, the Dominion becomes ceremonial autocracy, where every present hardship can be excused as a future necessity.

The system survives by making conflict lawful.

Loyal opposition is not treason when it protects the proper boundary between now and future.

## Amber's Role

Amber is not simply a monarch above a republic. She is the living holder of the future-facing government.

Her danger is not ordinary ambition. Her danger is that the machinery around her can invoke continuity in her name until the person disappears inside the office.

Amber's chosen restraint gives the Federal Government room to govern the present without forcing every dispute through the throne.

## Emilia's Role

As [[Voice of the Throne]], Emilia is the human corrective between the two clocks.

She asks what continuity means to the household, child, patient, refugee, worker, believer, dissenter, and frontier community standing directly in front of the state.

She is most important when the Royal Government is technically correct and morally drifting.

## Vel's Role

As [[Shield of Continuity]], Vel protects the witness-chain around Amber and the Royal Government.

His office matters because long-horizon power creates a special vulnerability: orders may be issued in the sovereign's name by ritual, panic, bureaucracy, security machinery, or court myth.

The Shield distinguishes the living will from the machinery that claims to speak for it.

## Failure Modes

### Federal Failure

The Federal Government can fail through:

- short-termism;
- factional paralysis;
- popular denial of distant threats;
- underfunding future defense;
- provincial capture;
- rights language used to avoid shared sacrifice;
- inability to act until crisis becomes visible.

### Royal Failure

The Royal Government can fail through:

- emergency permanence;
- symbolic overreach;
- over-centralization;
- treating citizens as continuity material;
- suppressing local autonomy;
- hiding costs from the Federal Government;
- confusing the Empress's image with her judgment.

## Story Use

This model gives the Dominion its central political conflict.

Useful conflicts:

- The Federal Government refuses a Royal continuity order because the local cost is too high.
- The Royal Government overrides federal delay because a century-scale threat has become immediate.
- A province wants day-to-day autonomy while depending on Royal long-horizon defense.
- Amber submits an emergency act to review after saving millions, and the review still wounds her politically.
- Emilia argues that a technically valid continuity plan is destroying the people it exists to save.
- Vel exposes a ministry using Amber's seal to bypass Federal resistance.

## Canon Formula

Federal Government: governs the living present.

Royal Government: guards the endangered future.

The Dominion is stable only when both are strong enough to resist the other.
