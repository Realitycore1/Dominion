Riyadh, 2039
The first [[Time]] Yasin saw the Black Stone, he was seventeen. Not the real one — only the projection. Drones hummed overhead, stitching a hologram across the plaza: a glowing cube crowned by a fragment of meteorite. The crowd surged around him, chanting, sweating, pressing [[Close]].

“Repeat the words!” his uncle hissed, shoving his elbow. Inspectors with rifles prowled the square. Yasin mouthed the slogans half-heartedly. The Stone is eternal. The Stone is our strength.

But inside, he wondered if any of them believed — or if, like him, they feared the fines, the beatings, the shame of being marked unfaithful.

That night, his friends traded jokes on a secret forum: Our god is a rock, and our stomachs are empty. Yasin laughed until he cried.

⸻

Riyadh, 2047
Draft papers arrived on a hot wind. His father wept quietly. His mother pressed a scrap of cloth into his hand: “Touch the Stone with this, bring back blessing.”

By the [[Time]] he reached Jerusalem, the city was already a carcass. Streets boiled with gunfire, relic banners flapped from every shattered window. Yasin marched in a column of boys like himself, rifles too heavy, eyes too [[Wide]]. Commanders told them they were the army of God. He felt like fodder.

One night, they stormed a district lit by flickering candles. The civilians didn’t fight — they cowered. A medic in a blood-stained apron guided children into cellars. For a moment her eyes met his. She looked at him not with hatred but with exhaustion, as if he were another lost boy she had to protect.

He hesitated. A [[Child]] clutched her apron. Shellfire cracked. Yasin turned away, firing into empty air.

⸻

Jerusalem, 2055
The siege dragged on. [[Hunger]] hollowed him. His comrades prayed loudly, but he no longer joined. He thought of the children in cellars. He thought of the Stone, hidden in its Kaaba, untouchable, indifferent.

One evening he heard his younger brother’s voice crackle over a radio broadcast — zeal bright, chanting relic hymns with the others. His brother had followed in his footsteps. Pride should have stirred. Instead, bile rose in his throat.

That night, Yasin slipped away. Desertion meant [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Oneiroverse Multiverse/Chorus of Archetypes/Death|Death]]. But staying meant worse.

⸻

The Desert Road, 2078
The sky burned white in the west. Yasin was older now, thin as a reed, living on stolen bread and [[Silence]]. He had survived raids, militias, desert patrols. He had not survived himself.

He lay on the road, watching the horizon bleed [[Fire]]. The Stone would endure. The Caliphate would claim victory even as ashes fell. His brother would die believing.

He smiled once, bitter, when he thought of the medic’s eyes. He had seen more faith in her exhaustion than in all the sermons of the Stone.

Above him, the [[Stars]] flickered. For the last [[Time]], he looked without slogans, without fear.

Then the world ended.
