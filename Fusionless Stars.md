I. Physical Definition

A Fusionless Star is any stellar-mass luminous object that does not generate its energy through nuclear fusion in its core.
Instead, its brightness and thermal output come from alter[[Infrastructure]] which can include:
	•	Gravitational Contraction (Kelvin–Helmholtz mechanism) — as in pre-main-sequence protostars or post-collapse remnants.
	•	Matter–[[Antimatter]] Annihilation — engineered or naturally seeded [[Antimatter]] streams sustaining luminosity.
	•	Dark Matter Annihilation — as in dark [[Stars]], where WIMP collisions provide heating.
	•	Exotic Particle Decay — continuous emission from unstable [[Exotic Matter]] cores.
	•	Vacuum Energy Extraction — tapping background spacetime fluctuations (Quantum Foam-type).

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Fusionless [[Stars]] are recognized as non-fusion, energy-radiating stellar bodies that can occur naturally in certain epochs (e.g., early-universe dark [[Stars]]) or be engineered for specialized Omniversal [[Infrastructure]] roles.
