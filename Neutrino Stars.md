I. Physical Definition

A Neutrino Star is a stellar-scale object composed primarily of neutrino-degenerate matter, where neutrino pressure counterbalances gravitational collapse in place of electron or neutron degeneracy.
In this configuration, the star’s structure and stability are governed by weak-force interactions, making it unlike any conventional fusion-powered star.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Neutrino Degeneracy Pressure — Extremely high-density neutrino gas forms a quantum-degenerate state, resisting further compression.

	•	High Transparency — Ordinary matter and photons pass through with little interaction, making them visually “ghost [[Stars]].”
	•	Ultra-Low Thermal Radiation — Energy loss primarily via neutrino emission rather than light or heat.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Neutrino [[Stars]] are classified as Weak-Interaction Anchors, notable for their near-invisibility in the electromagnetic spectrum and their role in energy/mass balancing in deep cosmic [[Systems]].
