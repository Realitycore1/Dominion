Classification: Undead Sovereigns & Artificial Continuance
Subtype: Phylactery-Bound Intellects / Self-Sustaining Undead
Status: Rare, High-Threat, Non-Natural

⸻

Core Description

A Lich is a sentient being that has deliberately severed the natural death transition and replaced it with an engineered feedback loop anchoring identity, memory, and will to an external vessel—commonly known as a phylactery.

The body is no longer the self.
The soul is no longer free.
The mind persists because it refuses to stop.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Liches are artificially stabilized deathbound entities.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Death (controlled), Shadow Realms
	•	Secondary Influence: Malice (discipline-focused), Arcane Law
	•	Realm Interface: Null Shores (resisted), Abyssal Middle (exploited)

They are not alive.
They are not properly dead.
They are processes pretending to be beings.

⸻

Creation (The Lich Ascension)

Becoming a lich requires:
	•	extreme magical competence,
	•	precise metaphysical knowledge,
	•	willingness to destroy one’s natural soul-flow.

Core steps include:
	1.	Soul extraction and partition
	2.	Phylactery construction
	3.	Identity anchoring ritual
	4.	Controlled bodily death
	5.	Reanimation under artificial law

Failure results in:
	•	soul loss,
	•	Null dissolution,
	•	uncontrolled wraithhood,
	•	or catastrophic undead abominations.

⸻

Phylactery Function

The phylactery is not a container—it is a regulator.

It:
	•	stabilizes identity,
	•	prevents soul dissipation,
	•	reconstitutes the body when destroyed,
	•	anchors memory across centuries.

Destroying the phylactery is the only way to truly kill a lich.

⸻

Intelligence & Psychology

Liches retain full sapience, often exceeding their living selves.

However, over time:
	•	empathy atrophies,
	•	patience grows inhuman,
	•	perspective detaches from mortality.

Some liches become:
	•	cold scholars,
	•	tyrants,
	•	archivists,
	•	reality engineers.

Others degrade into obsession loops.

A lich does not go mad quickly.
It goes wrong slowly.

⸻

Behavior & Influence

Liches often:
	•	build layered defenses,
	•	manipulate civilizations indirectly,
	•	hoard knowledge, not gold,
	•	use undead as infrastructure.

They prefer:
	•	preparation over confrontation,
	•	redundancy over risk,
	•	influence over visibility.

A visible lich is usually a mistake—yours or theirs.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Phylactery destruction = true death
	•	Vulnerable to Phoenix-grade erasure
	•	Destabilized by Grace saturation
	•	Severely disrupted by Null resonance

Liches fear:
	•	entropy,
	•	erasure,
	•	anything they cannot model.

⸻

Variants
	•	Archlich: Perfected lich, minimal decay
	•	Vampiric Lich: Feeds on blood or life-force
	•	Ascetic Lich: Refuses domination, pure survival
	•	Machine Lich: Soul bound to construct systems
	•	Collective Lich: Distributed phylactery network

Collective liches are existential threats.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Vampires: Rival immortals; mutual disdain
	•	Phoenixes: Existential enemies
	•	Reapers: Actively hunt liches
	•	Gods: Uneasy tolerance or outright hostility
	•	Necromancers: Students, tools, or rivals

Liches are rarely loyal to anything but continuity.

⸻

Distinction from Similar Entities
	•	vs Ghosts: Ghosts are accidents; liches are choices
	•	vs Wights: Wights are bound; liches are free
	•	vs Deathless Spirits: Liches are artificial
	•	vs Gods: Liches require maintenance

This distinction preserves hierarchy clarity.
