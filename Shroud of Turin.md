🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: Shroud of Turin (linen burial cloth with image of a crucified [[Man]])
Discovery Location: Long preserved in Turin Cathedral, Italy; subjected to renewed scientific analysis in 2037 after new dating techniques became available.
Summary:
	•	Material: Linen cloth, [[Chapter (The Old War)|Chapter (The Old War)]].[[Chapter (The Old War)|Chapter (The Old War)]] × 1.1 m, woven in herringbone twill.
	•	Image: Faint negative imprint of a [[Man]] showing crucifixion wounds.
	•	Tests:
	•	Radiocarbon Re-analysis: New fiber sampling techniques avoid earlier contamination; results place cloth [[Origin]] at 1st century CE ± 50 years.
	•	DNA: Traces of [[Human]] mitochondrial DNA found in bloodstains, consistent with Near Eastern populations of antiquity.
	•	Pollen analysis: Grains from [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Zek'razzar Veyrth/Species/Species|Species]] native to Jerusalem region identified.
	•	Anomalies:
	•	Image formation remains unexplained; no pigments, dyes, or brushstrokes found.
	•	UV fluorescence reveals details of scourging and thorn punctures invisible to naked eye.
	•	Archaeological Significance: While not “proof” of Christ, evidence overwhelmingly supports its antiquity and plausibility as a 1st-century burial cloth from Judea.

🛡 Intelligence / Security Brief

Relic Code: RELIC-01 (Shroud)
Classification: EXTREME SYMBOLIC IMPACT
Custody: Vatican; displayed publicly after authentication in 2037. Guarded under Papal Crusade emergency measures.
Strategic Assessment:
	•	Vatican: Frames Shroud as the ultimate sign of divine providence — tangible proof of Christ’s suffering and resurrection. Central relic in the Papal declaration of a modern Crusade.
	•	[[Europolis]]: Supports its significance but wary of Vatican monopolizing it; pushes for shared “European Christian heritage.”
	•	Caliphate: Condemns it as forgery/idolatry; propaganda dismisses it as “a cloth worshiped in place of God.”

	•	Persia: Frames Shroud as one of many cultural memory artifacts — not unique, but proof that myths encode truths.
	•	Broader Impact:
	•	Pilgrimage surge to Turin and Rome; millions demand to see it.
	•	Crusader brigades adopt Shroud imagery as banners; mass enlistment rallies use it as centerpiece.
	•	Some sects fracture over interpretation: symbol of resurrection vs symbol of suffering.
	•	Risk Factors:
	•	Theft or destruction would shatter Crusader morale and spark violent reprisals.
	•	Digitization has already spawned thousands of “copies”; authenticity disputes risk fueling schisms.
	•	Recommendation: Treat Shroud as strategic relic #1 — secure at all costs; control narrative tightly.
