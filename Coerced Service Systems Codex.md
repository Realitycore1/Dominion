Indenture & Penal Service Classification (Foundational Civic Law)

Document Type: Civic–Legal Codex Entry
Scope: All recognized forms of forced and voluntary service within the State
Purpose: Maintain labor stability, reintegration mechanisms, wartime continuity, and internal security without reliance upon permanent mass slavery.

⸻

I. Definitions (Legal Terms)

Service

Any binding contract, decree, or sentence compelling labor, duty, or performance in exchange for:
	•	payment, sustenance, or shelter
	•	debt dissolution
	•	protective residency
	•	political enfranchisement
	•	legal clemency
	•	wartime settlement status

Indenture

A term-limited service obligation recognized as a civil contract or state-imposed compact, in which the indentured retains legal personhood (unless otherwise revoked under Penal Servitude statutes).

Civil Personhood

A recognized status granting:
	•	bodily autonomy under law
	•	right of petition
	•	right to medical care consistent with caste
	•	limited protection against unlawful injury
	•	legal recognition of identity and familial ties

Civil Death

A terminal legal revocation of personhood, applied only under high crimes or existential threat rulings. Civil Death converts the sentenced individual into a Permanent Service Asset, forfeiting nearly all civil protections.

⸻

II. The Three Primary Classes of Service

Class A: Voluntary Indenture

Also called: Contract Indenture, Sponsored Service
Nature: civil contract
Typical term: 3–12 years
Primary function: economic mobility + controlled immigration + workforce allocation

A.1 Entry Conditions
Voluntary Indenture may be entered via:
	•	debt repayment agreement
	•	skill sponsorship (guild, house, military)
	•	passage contract (relocation / resettlement)
	•	citizenship-track enrollment

A.2 Rights Retained
Indentured persons retain:
	•	full name and identity
	•	bodily autonomy
	•	right of petition to magistrates
	•	fixed work hour ceilings (varies by occupation)
	•	injury compensation schedule
	•	protections against lethal discipline

A.3 Restrictions
	•	restricted movement without writ
	•	limited property ownership (usually personal effects only)
	•	mandatory residency at assigned quarters
	•	limits on marriage outside caste without sponsorship consent

A.4 Completion Outcomes
Upon term completion:
	•	issuance of Release Seal
	•	return of legal mobility rights
	•	optional transition to:
	•	Free Labor Citizen
	•	Protected Resident
	•	Military Auxiliary Enlistment
	•	Renewal Contract (optional)

Note: Many elect renewal due to stable housing, guaranteed rations, and social belonging.

⸻

Class B: War Indenture

Also called: Captive Indenture, Reconstruction Service
Nature: wartime civic coercion
Typical term: 7–25 years
Primary function: manage POWs & conquered populations without permanent slavery

B.1 Eligible Populations
	•	surrendered combatants
	•	captured irregulars (depending on classification)
	•	conquered civilian populations under wartime proclamation
	•	collaborators sentenced under wartime tribunal

B.2 Key Legal Principle
War Indenture is not intended as hereditary slavery.

It is framed publicly as:

“Restitution through work, with a pathway to reintegration.”

B.3 Rights Retained
War Indentures retain:
	•	legal identity (assigned registry)
	•	regulated medical access
	•	restricted petition rights
	•	protections against casual homicide (execution requires writ)

B.4 Restrictions
	•	movement bound to assigned zones
	•	restricted reproduction rights (varies by era/culture)
	•	mandatory ideological instruction
	•	communications controlled

B.5 Assimilation Ladder
War Indentures may progress via:
	•	loyal service marks
	•	skill demonstration
	•	military auxiliary enlistment
	•	state sponsorship by a House / Guild

Result: elevated to Contract Indenture or Resident Status.

This ladder is vital: it reduces revolt risk and converts enemy population into infrastructure.

⸻

Class C: Penal Servitude

Also called: Sentence Service, Irrevocable Service
Nature: criminal sentence
Typical term: 10 years to life
Primary function: punishment + containment + deterrence

C.1 Crimes Associated
Penal Servitude applies to:
	•	murder
	•	rape / predation
	•	treason
	•	sabotage against state infrastructure
	•	repeated violent offenses
	•	war crimes and atrocities

C.2 Two Subclasses
C.2.a Limited Penal Servitude (LPS)
	•	fixed term
	•	retains personhood
	•	may petition after minimum service threshold

C.2.b Permanent Penal Servitude (PPS)
	•	functional life slavery
	•	may be framed as “until death”
	•	includes Civil Death rulings

C.3 Civil Death Doctrine (Rare)
Civil Death applies only to:
	•	existential threats
	•	insurgency leadership
	•	mass atrocity offenders
	•	repeated treason
	•	irredeemable predation

Civil Death converts the sentenced into a Permanent Service Asset:
	•	no personal property
	•	no legal family status
	•	no right of petition
	•	identity reduced to registry designation

State justification: “They have revoked their right to be a citizen of the human species.”

⸻

III. Secondary & Covert Classes (Not Public-Facing)

These exist but are legally minimized, often euphemized.

Class D: Compulsory Indenture

Nature: “voluntary in law, forced in outcome”
Purpose: absorb displaced populations without open slavery

Common triggers:
	•	unpaid taxes
	•	land forfeiture
	•	disaster displacement
	•	medical debt
	•	starvation clause enactment

This is the state’s pressure valve:
	•	stabilizes society during crises
	•	creates exploitability without admitting coercion

⸻

IV. Prohibitions (What the State Claims It Does Not Permit)

To maintain legitimacy, the state explicitly prohibits:
	•	Hereditary Indenture
No child inherits status automatically (exceptions exist in practice via guardianship assignments)
	•	Open Slave Markets
Trading of persons as commodities is “illegal,” except under classified Penal Asset transfer statutes
	•	Unauthorized Punishment
Private violence against indentures is prohibited; only approved disciplinary officers may act (this is where corruption happens)
	•	Sexual Exploitation
Publicly criminalized—even if enforcement is uneven

The state’s public stance: coercion is permitted; excess is not.

⸻

V. Social Reality (Unwritten Truths)

1) Indenture is a pipeline

Voluntary Indenture often becomes multi-generational in practice because:
	•	poverty renews contracts
	•	children lack resources to exit caste zones
	•	Houses control opportunity

2) War Indentures are the assimilation engine

Conquest cultures survive long-term by:
	•	stealing labor
	•	converting talent
	•	absorbing families
	•	normalizing “former enemies” into civic identity

3) Penal Servitude becomes political

During unstable eras, “treason” expands to include:
	•	dissent
	•	journalism
	•	unauthorized organizing
	•	religious deviation
	•	competing loyalty networks

This is where functional systems become monstrous.

⸻

VI. Suggested Cultural Language (Euphemisms)

For dialogue realism and propaganda tone.
	•	slavery → service
	•	captive → restitution citizen
	•	slave → asset
	•	breeder → demographic service unit / population quota labor
	•	punishment → correction
	•	camps → service districts
	•	war captive → reconstruction class
	•	chattel → irrevocable sentence asset

⸻

VII. Story Hooks (Built-in Drama)
	•	A Voluntary Indenture becomes Compulsory after a law revision.
	•	War Indentures gain citizenship and form a loyal auxiliary caste (resented by “pure” citizens).
	•	A character fights to prevent Civil Death from being abused politically.
	•	A freed indenture chooses to stay because their old homeland no longer exists.
	•	A high-ranking officer was once a War Indenture (a secret capable of destabilizing the state).
