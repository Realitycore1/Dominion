# (2073) Indo-China Erupts

## Definitive Event

In 2073, the Fourth International fractures openly between India and China.

The immediate trigger is a dispute over missile trains held at an Indian-controlled depot. Chinese command orders the trains into a shared eastern theater. Indian authorities refuse transfer without national release codes. Integrated CRINK software recognizes the Chinese priority, so Indian crews physically remove the couplers.

The dispute escalates into the renewed Indo-Chinese war:

- Chinese security detains Indian liaison officers;
- India closes the depot and suspends integrated command participation;
- artillery strikes an Indian sensor station;
- India attacks the supplying road;
- Chinese forces cross a mountain line left provisional by the original CRINK treaty.

India invokes Ashoka's Pillars and declares a third strategic path outside western, Caliphal, and Chinese control. It restores exclusive national authority over ports, missile forces, rail, and communications.

China formally declares the **Neo Xia Sovereignty**, reframing the Heirloom Seal, Mandate doctrine, and CRINK infrastructure as evidence of Chinese civilizational leadership rather than equal alliance.

Russia attempts to retain access to both systems. North Korea publicly aligns with Neo Xia while Chairwoman Kim Yo-jong refuses Chinese supervision of her launch forces. North Korean attacks against South Korean, Japanese, and western Pacific targets intensify.

The political bloc fractures, but its infrastructure does not. Shared satellites, targeting software, warning models, and launch protocols continue operating across hostile commands.

This is a direct escalation mechanism toward the Exchange.
