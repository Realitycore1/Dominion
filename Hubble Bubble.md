The idea that we live inside a local underdensity (“void”) where galaxies are slightly farther apart than average. That would make the locally measured Hubble rate (H₀) look too high, because galaxies near us recede a bit faster than the cosmic mean.

How big/what kind.
Two common versions:
	•	Small “bubble” (~70–100 Mpc): early SN Ia work hinted at a step in H₀ between very nearby and more distant supernovae.
	•	Large “KBC-style” void (~200–300 Mpc): a gentler underdensity over a bigger volume.

What it would do.
	•	Boost local H₀ by ~1–[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]% for realistic voids; to explain the full H₀ tension (~5–10%) you’d need an implausibly deep, large void with special alignment.

How we test it.
	•	Galaxy counts & weak lensing: map local density out to a few hundred Mpc.
	•	Type Ia supernova Hubble diagram: look for a step in distance moduli vs redshift.
	•	Peculiar velocities / flows: check if local expansion is coherently faster.
	•	Independent H₀ methods: strong-lens [[Time]] delays, BAO+BBN, standard sirens.

Current status (consensus).
	•	A modest local underdensity likely exists but is too small to solve H₀ on its own.
	•	The classic “Hubble bubble” step in SN data shrinks or vanishes with improved calibration and larger samples.
	•	Void-based explanations can nudge H₀ by a percent or so, but do not remove the H₀ tension.

Why it still matters.
	•	Sets the cosmic-variance floor on local H₀ measurements.
	•	Reminds us to model environmental systematics (survey depth, selection, peculiar velocities) when comparing distance ladders.
