I. Definition — The Energy Plateau

Between roughly 2030 and 2060, global net-energy growth stops accelerating. [[Humanity]] produces more total energy each year, but the energy surplus per capita and per dollar of GDP stagnates. Civilization hits a thermodynamic ceiling without realizing it. Economies keep expanding on paper; physical throughput flatlines.

The plateau is invisible at first — power still flows, lights stay on — but it eats the future from the inside.

⸻

II. How It Happens (Plausible Mechanics)

1. Fusion Delay Syndrome
	•	By 2030, fusion prototypes (ITER, SPARC successors, Chinese Tokamak series) achieve plasma stability, but not economically sustained gain.
	•	Investment fatigue sets in. Politicians see “clean energy” headlines and quietly pull funding; private capital chases shorter-return AI and biotech.
	•	Experimental breakthroughs remain laboratory curiosities; commercialization never scales.

2. Renewables Meet Material Limits
	•	Global deployment saturates the easy sites.
	•	Rare-earth extraction bottlenecks, copper shortages, and lithium-brine contamination drive up costs.
	•	Grid storage depends on heavy metals; recycling loops are slower than expansion rates.
	•	Energy-return-on-investment (EROI) declines: we spend more energy building the [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/United Authority of Systems/System|System]] than we gain from its expansion.

3. Fossil Plateau Instead of Peak
	•	New extraction tech (super-fracking, Arctic drilling) prevents absolute depletion but keeps carbon locked in the economic bloodstream.
	•	Each barrel takes more energy to get than the last.
	•	Net energy from hydrocarbons slowly falls even while gross [[Production]] looks healthy — the illusion of abundance masking exhaustion.

4. [[Infrastructure]] Drag
	•	Aging grids leak 10–20% through inefficiency and transmission losses.
	•	Maintenance eats the same budgets once meant for innovation.
	•	Decentralized “smart” grids fragment into regional fiefdoms; politics replaces engineering as the deciding factor for power allocation.

5. Political Capture
	•	Energy giants rebrand as “transition partners,” lobbying to slow disruptive tech that threatens stranded assets.
	•	Governments, addicted to fuel royalties, collude in stagnation.
	•	The world slides into an energy cartel equilibrium: stability through mutual underperformance.

⸻

III. Observable Consequences (2040s–2050s)
	1.	Economic Stagnation Behind a Digital Façade
Stock markets soar thanks to AI speculation, but real productivity per joule drops. Nations live off data bubbles while physical [[Infrastructure]] decays.
	2.	Food & Water Tightening
Fertilizer costs rise with [[Natural]]-gas Inputs; desalination plants strain to power themselves.
Agriculture becomes the first visible casualty of the plateau — yields steady, populations keep growing.
	3.	Geo-engineering Temptation
With green transitions stuck, governments experiment with stratospheric aerosol injection and orbital mirrors, creating the climate technocracy that later fractures into [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Attea Matil/Military/Military|Military]] control.
	4.	Energy Nationalism
States guard domestic generation as strategic assets.
Grid wars replace oil wars: sabotage of transmission corridors, AI-driven energy raids, cyber-blackouts.
This seeds the habit of deniable [[Infrastructure]] warfare that defines the pre-WWIII decades.
	5.	Public Psychology
The public never experiences a dramatic shortage—only the erosion of convenience: rolling brownouts, rationed charging, expensive flights.
A generation grows up equating “normal” with scarcity management, primed for authoritarian solutions.
