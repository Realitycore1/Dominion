Classification: Liminal Entities & Shapeshifting Intelligences
Subtype: Spirit-Bound Sorcerers / Dual-Form Sentients
Status: Active, Rare, Culturally Anchored

⸻

Core Description

Nagual are sapient beings capable of assuming animal forms, not through curse or infection, but through deliberate spiritual mastery. Their transformations are precise, controlled, and purposeful—often limited to one or a small number of animal aspects tied to identity, lineage, or pact.

A Nagual does not become an animal.
It expresses another self.

In human form, Nagual appear unremarkable. In animal form, they are uncanny—too aware, too deliberate, too focused.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Nagual exist at the intersection of identity, spirit, and form.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Liminal Thresholds (Material ↔ Spirit)
	•	Secondary Influence: Oneiroverse (self-concept), Vital Expanse (biological adaptation)
	•	Realm Interface: Dream-Wastes, Shadow Realms, ritual-bound local realms

They are not cursed.
They are not born transformed.
They are made.

⸻

Origins

Nagual arise through:
	•	long-term spiritual discipline,
	•	inherited ritual traditions,
	•	pacts with animal spirits or archetypes,
	•	mastery of dream-self manipulation.

Some cultures view Nagual as:
	•	protectors,
	•	witches,
	•	assassins,
	•	spiritual guides.

Others hunt them relentlessly.

⸻

Intelligence & Psychology

Nagual possess full sapience, but their cognition is dual-layered.

They retain:
	•	human reasoning,
	•	memory,
	•	language.

They also adopt:
	•	instinctual processing from their animal aspect,
	•	altered sensory hierarchies,
	•	non-human emotional responses while transformed.

Extended time in animal form risks identity bleed.

⸻

Transformation Rules

Nagual transformations:
	•	are voluntary,
	•	require focus or ritual triggers,
	•	are energetically taxing,
	•	do not spread through contact.

Most Nagual are limited to:
	•	one primary animal form,
	•	one secondary (rare),
	•	symbolic or dream-forms under extreme conditions.

Forced transformation is possible—but dangerous and often fatal.

⸻

Behavior & Ecology

Nagual operate as:
	•	solitary agents,
	•	covert observers,
	•	cultural enforcers,
	•	assassins or guardians.

They thrive in:
	•	rural or wilderness regions,
	•	societies with strong mythic continuity,
	•	places where animal symbolism retains meaning.

Urban environments erode Nagual stability over time.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Identity fragmentation from overuse
	•	Vulnerability during forced transitions
	•	Disruption by Null or anti-spiritual zones
	•	Strong Grace fields can suppress transformation

A Nagual who loses their sense of self may degenerate into a feral shapeshifter.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Beastfolk: Viewed as kin-adjacent but distinct
	•	Shapeshifters (Curseborn): Despised as unstable
	•	Wendigo/Rugaru: Actively hunted
	•	Kami & Land Spirits: Frequent allies
	•	Vampires: Mutual wariness; Nagual resist domination

Nagual do not form large societies.
They form lineages and circles.

⸻

Variants
	•	Jaguar Nagual: Stealth, authority, night dominance
	•	Coyote Nagual: Trickery, liminality, boundary-breaking
	•	Eagle Nagual: Surveillance, sky-bound ritual
	•	Serpent Nagual: Knowledge, subterranean pathways
	•	Urban Nagual: Rare, psychologically strained

The animal is not chosen lightly—it defines the Nagual’s fate.
