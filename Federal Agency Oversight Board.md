# Federal Agency Oversight Board

## Summary

The Federal Agency Oversight Board is the civilian audit body responsible for keeping Federal agencies inside their legal mandates.

It does not run the agencies. It reviews them.

Its existence is one of the major reasons the Dominion's Federal Branch can operate powerful administrative bodies without immediately becoming an unaccountable security state.

## Mandate

The Board investigates:

- interagency jurisdiction disputes;
- misuse of classified authority;
- emergency powers that overrun their sunset dates;
- budget concealment;
- automated decision systems with no appeal path;
- citizen-rights complaints against Federal agencies;
- improper coordination with Royal ministries;
- coercive service-track practices;
- administrative retaliation against dissidents.

## Powers

The Board may:

- subpoena records;
- compel agency testimony;
- order temporary suspension of a policy;
- refer criminal matters to Federal courts;
- demand public summaries when full disclosure is impossible;
- require archival preservation of controversial decisions;
- issue binding compliance plans.

## Limits

The Board cannot command military forces, override courts, publish legitimately sensitive continuity information, or nullify a lawful Royal emergency order during an active existential crisis.

It can, however, preserve records and force review after the emergency passes.

## Public Face

Most citizens know the Board through appeal portals, civic-rights clinics, and public hearings. Its hearings are often dull, technical, and politically bitter. That dullness is part of its value.

The Board makes power explain itself.

## Story Use

Use the Board when a citizen, journalist, court clerk, provincial official, or dissident needs a lawful route to challenge the system without joining a rebellion.

The Board is not glamorous. It is the sound of a huge civilization trying to remain governable.
