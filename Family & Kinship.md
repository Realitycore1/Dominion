In the [[Austro Guard]], families are more than households—they are the custodians of honor across generations. Kinship is the loom upon which the colony weaves [[Continuity]] between ancestors, present Oathkeepers, and unborn heirs.
	•	Line Banners
Every family maintains a Line Banner, embroidered with sigils for each member who has sworn the Oath and, especially, those who have served as Oathkeepers.
	•	During [[Oath Ceremonies]], these banners are paraded to the Pillar of Duty.
	•	At funerals, they are draped over coffins, carrying the lineage’s pride into the grave.
	•	Rival families measure status not by coin, but by how crowded their banners are with sigils.
	•	Cross-Heritage Marriages
Since the colony’s founding blended European and Chinese settlers, intermarriage was encouraged as a stabilizing practice.
	•	Lineages often weave dual symbols—[[II - Companion Narratives/Syl'Cothan Dominion/4 - Equilion Union/Book I (HubFall)/Characters/Characters]] from both Han calligraphy and Central European heraldry—into banners, rings, and family shrines.
	•	Children take on hyphenated honor-names, marking both ancestries. This practice reduces factionalism and creates a tangled web of kinship alliances.
	•	Household Duty Structure
Each household is expected to “supply” at least one adult to civic burden rotations at any given [[Time]]. Families organize themselves around this:
	•	Younger siblings or cousins often step in to care for children when a parent is selected as Oathkeeper.
	•	Honor-rich families compete to take on the hardest burdens, while honor-poor families strive to match them in small but public ways.
	•	Ancestor Veneration
Families keep shrines not for gods but for Oathkeepers past.
	•	Names are etched on basalt plates beside offerings of food, steel, or tools.
	•	It is common for a family meal to begin with a toast “to those who bore the burden before us.”
	•	Stories of ancestors’ terms as Oathkeepers are retold as moral lessons for children, ensuring [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]] is lived in the present tense.
	•	Social Interweaving
Kinship webs are deliberately tangled. Families seek marriage across trades and guilds to diversify honor capital and reduce insularity. This practice ensures that no single clan can dominate politics, since everyone’s kin are spread through multiple strata of the colony.

The result is a society where family is the first civic classroom. To be born [[Austro Guard]] is to be born into a lineage already measured against the Pillars of Duty. Every household is a small fortress of memory, honor, and obligation.
