I. Physical Definition

A Torus Star is a stellar-scale object whose plasma and fusion [[Regions]] are arranged in a ring-shaped (toroidal) geometry rather than a sphere.
The structure is sustained through complex balancing of gravity, rotation, magnetic confinement, and [[Exotic Matter]] effects, either naturally in rare astrophysical scenarios or through deliberate engineering.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Toroidal Plasma Flow — Fusion and energy generation occur in a doughnut-shaped band around a central void.
	•	Rotational Stability — Rapid rotation combined with magnetic pressure maintains the shape.
	•	Central Void Phenomena — May contain vacuum, [[Exotic Matter]], or even a smaller compact object such as a black hole or wormhole.
	•	Multi-Axis Magnetic Fields — Generate confinement zones preventing plasma collapse into a sphere.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Torus [[Stars]] are categorized as Morphological Anomalies, valued for their unique gravitational, magnetic, and radiation patterns.
