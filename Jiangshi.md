Classification

Corpse-Reanimated Undead / Breath-Drainers / Ritual-Stabilized Revenants

⸻

CORE IDEA

Jiangshi are bodies animated by misaligned life-force.

They are not vampires in the blood sense and not ghosts wearing flesh. A Jiangshi exists because qi (vital breath) failed to disperse correctly at death. The corpse becomes a pressure vessel—sealed, charged, and dangerous.

They don’t hunger for blood.
They steal breath.

⸻

ORIGIN

Jiangshi form through funerary disruption and energetic imbalance:
	•	improper burial or transport of bodies
	•	deaths far from ancestral ground
	•	ritual negligence or sabotage
	•	lightning strikes, geomantic faults, or necromantic meddling
	•	corpses sealed too tightly, trapping qi

The soul departs.
The vital engine does not.

⸻

PHYSICAL MANIFESTATION

Jiangshi are unmistakable and rigid.

Common traits:
	•	Stiff, corpse-rigid bodies unable to bend joints
	•	Pale greenish or ashen skin tone
	•	Elongated fingernails and sharpened teeth
	•	Clouded or glowing eyes
	•	Qing-dynasty–style burial garments or robes
	•	Yellow talisman (fu) affixed to the forehead in controlled states

They move by hopping—both feet leaving the ground simultaneously—arms extended forward.

⸻

BEHAVIOR & TEMPERAMENT

Jiangshi are predatory but procedural.
	•	Attracted to living breath and warmth
	•	Move directly toward sensed qi sources
	•	Poor at navigation, excellent at persistence
	•	Can be halted, redirected, or suppressed by proper rites

They are not sadistic.
They are imbalanced.

⸻

INTELLIGENCE

Low to moderate.
	•	Most operate on instinct
	•	Can recognize repeated targets
	•	Rare elder Jiangshi retain fragments of memory
	•	Do not strategize beyond pursuit

Advanced forms may speak in broken phrases or mimic ritual responses.

⸻

METAPHYSICAL TRAITS
	•	Qi Drain: contact siphons life-breath, causing weakness or death
	•	Rigid Animation: joints cannot flex; terrain limits movement
	•	Grace-Resistant: divine light disrupts but does not command
	•	Malice-Reactive: corruption accelerates aggression and spread
	•	Talisman Control: proper seals suppress motion and hunger

Breaking a control talisman releases stored energy violently.

⸻

ECOLOGY
	•	Found near grave routes, mountain passes, abandoned villages
	•	Often appear in clusters due to mass transport disasters
	•	Do not reproduce biologically; new Jiangshi arise from new failures
	•	Surroundings show geomantic disturbance

Villages plagued by Jiangshi feel airless, even outdoors.

⸻

ALPHA JIANGSHI

An Alpha Jiangshi is known as a Corpse King or Golden Jiangshi.

Traits:
	•	Increased flexibility and speed
	•	Partial flight or gliding hops
	•	Ability to drain qi at range
	•	Immunity to basic talismans

Corpse Kings are no longer accidents.
They are systems.

⸻

THREAT ASSESSMENT

Moderate (basic) / High–Severe (Alpha)

You don’t brawl a Jiangshi.
You manage energy, terrain, and ritual control.

⸻

NARRATIVE ROLE

Jiangshi represent:
	•	funerary failure
	•	breath stolen rather than blood
	•	death as energetic malfunction
	•	tradition weaponized by neglect

Where vampires corrupt desire,
where Draugr enforce pride,
Jiangshi are ritual math gone wrong.
