(~0 to ~600 Post-VCA, depending on regional calendars)

VCA, the Veldar Creation Age, ends with the [[Elves]]’ triumph over their kin and their enslavement of humankind. That era collapses in [[Fire]] and rebellion, leaving a continent of ghosts and ashes. What follows is the Age of Reclamation, when no one truly rules and everyone pretends to.

⸻

1. The [[Aftermath]] of the Fall

When the [[Humans]] shattered their [[Chains]] (c. 0 AR on the [[Human]] [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Oneiroverse Multiverse/Umbraxis/Timeline|Timeline]]), the world entered a century of ruin. The old elven cities — once gilded, crystalline metropolises — were either abandoned or half-occupied by [[Human]] militias who mistook libraries for fortresses and [[Temples]] for armories.
Veldar survivors retreated to Esildor, building new academies atop ruins of the forbidden ones, sealing vaults that contained blood-magic artifacts.

The [[Natural]] order broke down: the dragons fled south, the [[Dwarves]] sealed their tunnels, and the lizardmen roamed shattered wetlands glowing faintly from the detonation of the World-Breaker.

The world became quiet, and in that quiet, [[Humanity]] multiplied.

⸻

[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]. The Rise of the [[Human]] Eras

The [[Humans]] inherited a world half-burned and half-enchanted. Their populations boomed where the elven wards failed — the river valleys, coasts, and plains.

The Council Period (~0–30 AR) saw scattered city-assemblies trying to govern together, but their philosophies clashed: some wanted democracy (the future Basingrad), others [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Military|Military]] order (Salesh), others trade neutrality (Scailor).
By the [[Time]] the Empire of Salesh declared itself in 30 AR, the era of unity was over.

These first empires were defined by:
	•	Residual magic leaking from elven ruins (the “Arcane Fallout”).
	•	Weapon [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Gnostic Expanse/Mirror Earth (2076)/Relics|Relics]] forged from dismantled Veldar constructs.
	•	Economic [[Rebirth]] through scavenging ancient armories and libraries.

Each city-state’s power derived from how well it exploited or contained those legacies.

⸻

[[Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)|Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)]]. The Second Collapse — “Wars of the Shattered Crown”

By 150 AR, [[Dragon]] cults, blood mages, and divine pretenders began vying for control of the new world. The Calastine Order emerged — a blood-mage enclave that nearly succeeded in tearing the Veil. The Veldar called down holy [[Fire]], burning entire plains into glass.
The resulting climate disruption led to famine, refugee waves, and the formation of the first mercenary leagues — precursors to Scailor’s democratic tradition.

[[Elves]] became scholars and diplomats again. [[Dwarves]] resurfaced with divine rhetoric. [[Humans]] turned their wars into ideologies.

This chaos lasted roughly two centuries, until exhaustion forced pragmatism.

⸻

[[Chapter (The Old War)|Chapter (The Old War)]]. The Compact of the Nine Cities (c. 370 Post-VCA)

Nine major polities signed the first Tellurian Compact — a promise to preserve knowledge, regulate magic, and stabilize trade. It failed within a decade but left behind the concept of city sovereignty.
The principle: “Each city is a world entire.”
From it arise the independent city-states that dominate later Concordian [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]].

⸻

5. Cultural and Magical Landscape

Magic:
Residual ley density remains high but erratic; pockets of wild surge zones mark old battlefields. Most civilizations develop containment protocols — runic grids, harmonic resonators, and suppression laws. Magic is academic but feared; the word mage oscillates between scholar and criminal.

Religion:
After the dragons’ flight, divine [[Silence]] reigns. Angelic cults reappear, often syncretized with [[Human]] reformist faiths. The Blood Wars imprint the fear of “false [[Ascension]]” — mortals attempting godhood and failing catastrophically.

[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Kuomin Empire/Economy|Economy]]:
Trade routes rebuild around surviving roads, dwarven vaults, and Scailor’s neutrality. The wealth of nations comes from salvage and relic-excavation, leading to archeo-industrial cities.

[[culture]]:
Renaissance-like revival of art, philosophy, and invention occurs, though often as propaganda to glorify each city’s founding myth. Every [[culture]] claims to be the rightful heir of civilization.

⸻

6. Legacy — Why the Post-VCA Period Matters

It defines the Concordian worldview:
	•	Knowledge as inheritance from the gods, not revelation.
	•	Magic as dangerous but indispensable [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Military/Technology|Technology]].
	•	City-states as living philosophies — each embodying a worldview of survival after apocalypse.

Every later age — from the Age of Artificers to the Twilight [[Crusades]] — builds on these ideological fossils.
