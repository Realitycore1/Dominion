# Neptune: distance as infrastructure

## Committee mandate

The Neptune Colonial Committee coordinates inhabited settlements, navigation platforms, remote power stations, accelerator infrastructure, exclusion volumes, worker transit, and antimatter transfer.

The current canon does not assign one exact founding year. The mature system appears during 3300–3425, when Neptune becomes the production center for mission-grade antimatter.

Antimatter is manufactured, not mined. Every useful quantity represents years of power, rejected components, field certification, remote labor, and containment work shared with Uranus.

## A civilization built away from its industry

Neptune’s winds make the atmosphere unsuitable for cities. Habitats remain in orbit or on protected moons while accelerator arrays and production systems occupy remote volumes.

The separation is intentional. Workers may live thirty minutes away by light from the array and only three pressure doors from a communal kitchen. They do not see antimatter. They see diagnostics, power schedules, empty transport chambers, and the red boundary beyond which inhabited traffic is forbidden.

Architecture favors instrument walls over windows, delayed-message rooms over live chambers, and storage larger than current need. Every room displays its communications delay to the places on which it depends.

Neptunians learn to plan for people they may never meet. Distance is one of the load-bearing materials.

## Daily life under the production grid

Settlement life follows accelerator demand. Laundry runs during low draw. Bread ovens heat during containment verification. Residents finish showers before power narrows to life support.

Families converse across delay. A parent asks a question, completes another task, and hears the answer after the original moment has passed. Intimacy becomes the willingness to remain beside a speaker while silence crosses the system.

Security classification creates a persistent political conflict. Workers may know how to maintain a component without being allowed to know why its replacement was delayed.

## The maintenance-disclosure compact

A containment incident exposes that danger. A component exceeds its cycle life, replacement is delayed under classification, and local technicians see temperature drift without access to the maintenance chain. Stored antimatter diverts into a sacrificial trap, but two outer-facility workers die.

Logistics specialist Cael Vale publishes the chronological maintenance record. Union officers argue that disclosure aids attack planning. Workers answer that secrecy already aided neglect.

The resulting compact places maintenance disclosure under local worker review even when mission purpose remains classified. The committee’s role expands from administering production to representing the bodies already inside the strategic target.

## Compact, war, and joint control

Neptune joins the Outer Systems Compact in 3425. Its antimatter production makes the system politically central despite its distance.

During the Second Solar War in 3450, both coalitions target the production chain. A failed inner attempt to seize Neptunian production helps end the war. The settlement recognizes outer independence and places antimatter infrastructure under a joint safety regime.

This is not shared ownership of every Neptunian settlement. It is shared restraint around a system no faction can safely monopolize.

## Position by 3600

The first mission-grade transfer leaves Neptune under Union and Compact guard. Its stored field can support an interstellar ark.

Neptune supplies the *Deliverance* with antimatter, remote-operation doctrine, delayed logistics, and administrators trained to treat empty distance as a wall requiring maintenance. Its departure sentence is: “Distance is one of the walls.”
