I. Physical Definition

A Black Hole Star is an extreme stellar configuration in which a black hole resides at the core of a massive star.
This occurs when:
	1.	A massive star forms around a pre-existing black hole seed.
	[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]].	A massive star collapses but sustains an accretion-supported outer envelope instead of fully dispersing.

In such objects, the black hole accretes stellar material from the inside out, heating the surrounding [[Layers]] and preventing complete collapse.
The balance between inward gravitational infall and outward radiation pressure from accretion heating allows the outer stellar [[Layers]] to persist for extended periods, despite the core being a singularity.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Black Hole [[Stars]] are considered Singularity-Envelope Fusion Hybrids — [[Natural]] or engineered stellar bodies that act as gravitational furnaces with unmatched mass–energy conversion efficiency.
	•	Core: Stellar-mass or intermediate-mass black hole.
	•	Envelope: Fusion-capable plasma surrounding the event horizon.
	•	Energy Source: Combination of accretion disk radiation and fusion in outer [[Layers]].
