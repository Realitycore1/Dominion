gravastar (gravitational vacuum star) is a theoretical alternative to black holes that avoids the singularity and event horizon by replacing them with a stable, layered structure. Proposed by Mazur and Mottola in 2001, a gravastar consists of a vacuum energy core (de Sitter space) surrounded by a thin shell of ultra-dense [[Exotic Matter]], and an exterior that matches the gravitational profile of a black hole. The vacuum core pushes outward with negative pressure, while the shell balances this pressure with its own inward gravitational force, resulting in a stable object without an actual event horizon.

Gravastars aim to solve several paradoxes of black holes, such as infinite density and the information loss problem. From the outside, they would appear almost indistinguishable from black holes—same mass, same gravitational effects—but might show subtle differences in gravitational wave patterns or radiation behavior. In speculative or sci-fi settings, gravastars could be repurposed as controlled cosmic cores, stable energy wells, or even gateways—functioning as a safer, engineered alternative to black holes.
