The September 11 attacks, also known as 9/11, were a series of coordinated terrorist attacks that occurred on September 11, 2001. The attacks were carried out by a group of Islamic extremists who hijacked four commercial passenger aircraft and used them to target key buildings in the United States. Two of the aircraft were flown into the World Trade Center in New York City, while a third aircraft was flown into the Pentagon, a military complex in Washington D.C. The fourth aircraft crashed in a Pennsylvania field after its passengers attempted to regain control of the plane. The attacks caused the deaths of over 3,000 people and had a profound impact on American society and international politics. It led to a massive reorganization of security measures around the world, as well as a shift in American foreign policy towards greater military involvement in conflicts abroad. The attacks also sparked a renewed focus on religious extremism and terrorism, as well as intense debate about the role of intelligence agencies and international relations in preventing such events from occurring again. Today, 9/11 remains a significant event in American history and memory, and it continues to shape perceptions of security, foreign policy, and international relations.
