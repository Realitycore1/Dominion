Classification: Curseborn & Shapeshifters
Subtype: Predatory Curse-Entity / Hunger-Bound Hybrid
Status: Active, Contagious, Regionally Endemic

⸻

Core Description

Rugaru are humanoid predators born from transgression, manifesting as towering, wolf-like or canine-beast figures with elongated limbs, distended jaws, and eyes burning with feral intelligence. Unlike true shapeshifters, the Rugaru’s form is semi-fixed—the transformation is irreversible or only weakly reversible.

Their bodies show signs of spiritual corrosion:
	•	patchy fur over scarred flesh,
	•	exposed bone at joints,
	•	mouths too large for their skulls,
	•	breath steaming even in warm climates.

They are not animals pretending to be men.
They are people punished into predators.

⸻

Ontological Nature

Rugaru arise through curse contagion, not inheritance.

Common triggers include:
	•	violation of sacred taboos,
	•	acts of cannibalism or predatory violence,
	•	broken oaths tied to blood or family,
	•	consumption of malice-tainted flesh,
	•	deliberate use of forbidden rites.
	•	Primary Plane Affinity: Malice-adjacent resonance
	•	Secondary Influence: Vital Expanse (degenerate phase), Umbrae
	•	Realm Interface: Shadow Realms, Bleeding Wilds (predatory zones)

The curse binds hunger to identity. Feeding reinforces the transformation.

⸻

Intelligence & Will

Rugaru retain fragmented human intelligence.

They remember:
	•	names,
	•	places,
	•	relationships.

But these memories are filtered through hunger and paranoia.

Most Rugaru:
	•	hunt deliberately,
	•	stalk former communities,
	•	target kin or familiar prey.

A fully degraded Rugaru loses speech but not cunning.

⸻

Behavior & Ecology

Rugaru are solitary or form temporary packs during lean periods.

They prefer:
	•	rural settlements,
	•	borderlands,
	•	isolated roads,
	•	swamp, forest, or ashland environments.

They do not simply kill.
They terrorize, eroding communities through fear and disappearance.

Regions plagued by Rugaru often develop:
	•	ritual protections,
	•	curfews,
	•	false accusations,
	•	witch-hunts.

The curse spreads as much socially as biologically.

⸻

Transmission

Rugaru curses propagate through:
	•	blood contact,
	•	ritual contamination,
	•	consumption of cursed meat,
	•	inheritance through broken vows.

Bites alone are insufficient—there must be moral or symbolic breach.

This prevents them from becoming simple plagues.

⸻

Weaknesses & Constraints
	•	Vulnerable to ritual cleansing early in transformation
	•	Weakened by Grace-dominant fields
	•	Cannot cross certain symbolic boundaries (consecrated ground, familial wards)
	•	Late-stage Rugaru are nearly impossible to cure

Execution does not always end the curse—sites can remain tainted.

⸻

Relationship to Other Entities
	•	Dogmen: Rugaru are curse-born; Dogmen are disease-stabilized species
	•	Wendigo/Wiendigo: Parallel hunger curses; Rugaru are social predators, not isolationists
	•	Vampires: Mutual hostility; both compete for prey
	•	Unseelie: Sometimes cultivate Rugaru outbreaks as social correction
	•	Domovoi: Actively repel Rugaru from inhabited homes

Rugaru are feared even by other monsters.

⸻

Variants
	•	Ash Rugaru: Born from post-collapse or industrial ruin
	•	Swamp Rugaru: Amphibious, patient, nearly invisible
	•	Urban Rugaru: Rare, extremely dangerous, highly intelligent
	•	Alpha Rugaru: First-generation curse-bearers with near-total control

Alpha Rugaru often become folk demons or regional legends.
