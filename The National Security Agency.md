# The National Security Agency

## Summary

The National Security Agency protects Federal communications, cryptographic systems, secure civic networks, strategic signal infrastructure, and lawful signals-intelligence programs.

Its greatest danger is not incompetence. Its danger is usefulness.

## Mandate

The Agency oversees:

- federal cryptography;
- secure government networks;
- signals intelligence under warrant or foreign-authority rules;
- anti-intrusion defense;
- infrastructure communications security;
- emergency broadcast hardening;
- classified communications standards.

## Powers

It may defend Federal networks, intercept foreign communications under law, issue security standards, and support court-authorized domestic investigations.

## Limits

It cannot conduct mass domestic surveillance without individualized lawful basis, weaken civilian encryption for convenience, or hide illegal programs behind technical secrecy.

## Public Face

Citizens know it through privacy scandals, security updates, public encryption debates, and the fear that someone can always listen.

## Story Use

Use this agency to make the Bill of Rights matter. The strongest surveillance system in the room should also be the one most aggressively audited.
