A Shkadov Thruster works by using a massive reflective structure to redirect a portion of a star’s radiation back toward itself. This creates an imbalance in the radiation pressure, resulting in a net thrust that slowly moves the star (and any gravitationally bound planets or [[Megastructures Portal|megastructures]]) through space.

Reflective Mirror: A parabolic megastructure (usually partial Dyson shell) reflects a segment of this light.

Parameter	Estimate
Mirror size	~1 AU radius
Net thrust	~1 Newton
Star acceleration	~10⁻¹⁰ m/s²
Displacement	~1 light-year per 10 million years
Despite the minuscule thrust, over cosmic timescales, this is sufficient for solar migration, collision avoidance, or galactic repositioning.

 Core Concept
A Shkadov Thruster works by using a massive reflective structure to redirect a portion of a star’s radiation back toward itself. This creates an imbalance in the radiation pressure, resulting in a net thrust that slowly moves the star (and any gravitationally bound planets or [[Megastructures Portal|megastructures]]) through space.

 Operational Principle
Radiation Momentum: Photons from the star carry momentum.

Reflective Mirror: A parabolic megastructure (usually partial Dyson shell) reflects a segment of this light.

Asymmetric Pressure: The reflected radiation exerts a tiny but constant recoil force on the star.

Thrust Vectoring: Orientation of the mirror changes the direction of travel.

Sol type baseline:
