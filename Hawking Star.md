I. Physical Definition

A Hawking Star is a stellar-scale object powered almost entirely by Hawking radiation — the theoretical quantum-mechanical emission from a black hole’s event horizon due to particle–antiparticle pair [[Production]] in curved spacetime.

In astrophysical terms, naturally occurring stellar-mass black holes produce negligible Hawking radiation compared to accretion luminosity, but in [[VFF]]-engineered contexts, Hawking [[Stars]] are:
	•	Small Black Holes (asteroid-mass to planet-mass range) embedded in stellar-mass envelopes.
	•	Tuned so that Hawking radiation output is sufficient to sustain and heat the envelope.
	•	Stabilized via [[Verseon]] lattice gravitational dampers to prevent runaway evaporation or collapse.

These [[Stars]] shine not from fusion, but from thermal radiation emitted directly by the singularity and reprocessed by surrounding material into the electromagnetic spectrum.
	•	Core: Micro- to meso-scale black hole radiating thermally.
	•	Fuel Source: Mass loss of the singularity itself.
	•	Lifespan: Variable — set by initial black hole mass and controlled feeding to counteract mass loss.
