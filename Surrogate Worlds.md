# Domain Surrogate Worlds

## Classification

- **Type:** Transitional planetary organism or provisional worldbody
- **Primary Function:** Conversion, assembly, stabilization, and preparation of a future Womb World or Supernal
- **Secondary Functions:** Resource processing, emergency habitation, military staging, gene cultivation, planetary repair, and mass accumulation
- **Typical Origin:** Captured planet, dead world, shattered moon system, asteroid field, planetary debris, fleet wreckage, or fused megastructure remains
- **Scale:** Moon-sized to terrestrial
- **Mobility:** Limited to none; some advanced examples possess temporary propulsion
- **Command Structure:** Flesh Core clusters, provisional Womb Cores, Seed Choir fragments, Overseers, and one or more supervisory Primes
- **Domain Designation:** Surrogate World
- **External Classification:** Proto-world, conversion biosphere, or unfinished planetary organism

---

# Overview

A **Surrogate World** is a planet or planet-scale body that has not yet achieved the biological, ecological, cognitive, or structural completeness of a true [[Domain Womb Worlds|Womb World]].

It exists between categories.

A Surrogate World may be:

- a natural world undergoing active Domain conversion;
- a dead planet being hollowed and rewritten;
- a shattered world being reassembled;
- a semi-formed planet grown from asteroids, wreckage, and harvested matter;
- a failed Womb World undergoing reconstruction;
- a stationary Supernal seed;
- or a provisional planetary body created to fulfill an immediate need.

Its defining condition is not weakness.

It is incompletion.

> **A Surrogate World is a world being taught how to become alive.**

Some Surrogate Worlds eventually mature into Womb Worlds.

Others become Supernals.

Some remain permanently transitional because their function depends on being adaptable, unfinished, and disposable.

---

# Core Distinction

## Womb World

A complete planetary civilization capable of stable birth, rebirth, ecology, governance, and long-term continuity.

## Supernal

A mature mobile Womb World and gene-forge capable of transporting entire biological civilizations.

## Surrogate World

A provisional or actively converting planetary body that lacks one or more of the following:

- stable global metabolism;
- integrated planetary cognition;
- complete biosphere;
- mature Womb Core;
- coherent planetary command voice;
- closed nutrient cycle;
- independent reproduction;
- or durable civil identity.

A Surrogate World may imitate the functions of a Womb World without yet possessing its full autonomy.

---

# Primary Types

## 1. Conversion Surrogate

A natural world undergoing active biological rewrite.

The Domain gradually replaces or integrates:

- atmosphere;
- hydrosphere;
- crust;
- ecosystems;
- infrastructure;
- and native chemistry.

A Conversion Surrogate may still look mostly natural from orbit while enormous vascular and cognitive systems spread beneath the surface.

## 2. Scrap-Grown Surrogate

A semi-formed world assembled from:

- asteroids;
- shattered moons;
- fleet wreckage;
- harvested stations;
- planetary debris;
- mineral slag;
- dead bioships;
- and reclaimed megastructure material.

These worlds often possess irregular mass distribution, exposed internal anatomy, incomplete crust, and enormous structural braces.

They resemble planets under construction rather than planets under conversion.

## 3. Reclamation Surrogate

A broken or dead Womb World rebuilt after:

- planetary war;
- command collapse;
- genetic corruption;
- heat death;
- ID suppression;
- or Symmorphica contamination.

These worlds contain preserved organs from the original world mixed with newly cultivated replacement systems.

## 4. Siege Surrogate

A rapidly grown military worldbody used to:

- anchor a campaign;
- generate Thralls;
- feed fleets;
- consume battlefield debris;
- and reshape a hostile star system.

Siege Surrogates sacrifice long-term stability for speed.

## 5. Refuge Surrogate

An emergency worldbody built to preserve populations displaced by:

- planetary destruction;
- Severance;
- ecosystem loss;
- Womb World collapse;
- or branch extinction.

Refuge Surrogates prioritize:

- life support;
- memory preservation;
- body reconstruction;
- privacy membranes;
- and continuity archives.

## 6. Seed Surrogate

A compact proto-world intended to be delivered into a new system and expanded over centuries.

It may begin as:

- a moon;
- an asteroid mass;
- a hollow shell;
- or a biological seed-body.

## 7. Experimental Surrogate

A provisional world used to test:

- planetary-scale body plans;
- new ecosystems;
- exotic chemistry;
- cognition architectures;
- alternative command systems;
- and novel forms of Domain personhood.

# Formation Pathways

## Natural Conversion

A planet is gradually rewritten from within.

### Sequence

1. Observer survey.
2. Seed organism deployment.
3. Subsurface vascular colonization.
4. Flesh Core implantation.
5. Atmospheric and hydrological alteration.
6. Crustal ossification.
7. Biospawn activation.
8. Provisional Womb Core formation.
9. Planetary command synchronization.
10. Full Womb World maturity.

The world remains a Surrogate until it can sustain stable planetary continuity without constant external supervision.

## Scrap Assembly

A worldbody is built from disconnected matter.

### Sequence

1. Matter capture.
2. Orbital aggregation.
3. Gravitational stabilization.
4. Structural bone framework.
5. Fleshstone accretion.
6. Internal vascular excavation.
7. Heat-core installation.
8. Atmosphere cultivation.
9. Biosphere seeding.
10. Cognitive integration.

Scrap-Grown Surrogates often contain enormous empty chambers, unhealed seams, and visible artificial geography.

## Emergency Growth

During crisis, the Domain may grow a worldbody before its long-term systems are ready.

These worlds may lack:

- complete archives;
- full ecological balance;
- stable weather;
- reliable tectonics;
- or mature personhood law.

They can survive for centuries while remaining biologically provisional.

# Planetary Anatomy

## Scaffold Mantle

The Scaffold Mantle is a skeletal planetary framework composed of:

- bone megatrusses;
- chitin ribs;
- mineralized connective tissue;
- pressure-support organs;
- and deep load-bearing columns.

On Scrap-Grown Surrogates, the Scaffold Mantle may remain exposed to space.

## Accretion Skin

A living outer layer that captures dust, debris, and mineral matter.

It:

- binds incoming mass;
- sorts useful materials;
- seals impact wounds;
- and gradually forms the future crust.

## Conversion Front

The active boundary between original planetary matter and Domain biology.

This region may contain:

- dissolving rock;
- half-converted cities;
- hybrid ecosystems;
- active Flesh Pits;
- and unstable command zones.

The Conversion Front is often the most dangerous region on the world.

## Provisional Vascular Network

An incomplete planetary circulation system.

Unlike a mature Womb World, a Surrogate may rely on:

- temporary nutrient channels;
- exposed arteries;
- imported coolant;
- fleet-supplied oxygen;
- and modular Flesh Cores.

## Provisional Womb Core

A developing planetary command organ.

It may consist of:

- several linked Flesh Cores;
- one dominant command mass;
- a captive Prime node;
- or a Seed Choir functioning as temporary central intelligence.

A Provisional Womb Core often lacks stable identity.

## Assembly Caverns

Gigantic interior spaces where matter is:

- melted;
- digested;
- sorted;
- reinforced;
- and redistributed.

These caverns may contain:

- ship graveyards;
- asteroid fragments;
- ruined cities;
- and half-grown planetary organs.

## Thermal Spine

A central cooling and energy-transfer axis.

Because Surrogate Worlds are thermally inefficient, the Thermal Spine is usually oversized.

It may connect:

- polar radiators;
- orbital petals;
- deep coolant reservoirs;
- and sacrificial heat organs.

---

# Surface Conditions

A Surrogate World may have radically uneven environments.

One hemisphere may contain:

- completed cities;
- breathable air;
- stable weather;
- and mature ecosystems.

Another may remain:

- vacuum-exposed;
- molten;
- half-ossified;
- geologically unstable;
- or covered in reclamation seas.

Common surface features include:

- incomplete bone mountain ranges;
- exposed vascular trenches;
- membrane canopies;
- unfinished atmosphere;
- continent-scale scaffolds;
- anchored wreckage;
- mobile gestation fields;
- and massive conversion scars.

---

# Population

Surrogate Worlds may host:

- construction Thralls;
- engineers;
- Overseers;
- displaced Domain civilians;
- Seed Choirs;
- branch Primes;
- memory custodians;
- and specialized adaptation organisms.

Many inhabitants consider the world temporary.

Others are born there and develop identity around incompletion.

This can create cultures centered on:

- rebuilding;
- patience;
- migration;
- sacrifice;
- salvage;
- adaptation;
- and the belief that no home must begin complete.

---

# Governance

## Supervisory Prime

Most Surrogate Worlds remain under a supervising Prime.

The Prime controls:

- conversion priority;
- biospawn allocation;
- population movement;
- environmental stabilization;
- and the threshold for world maturity.

## Provisional Councils

Civic Surrogates may form councils representing:

- local collectives;
- construction lineages;
- memory archives;
- displaced populations;
- Flesh Core networks;
- and environmental zones.

## Maturity Disputes

A major political question is:

> When does a Surrogate World become a Womb World?

Possible criteria include:

- stable planetary cognition;
- independent reproduction;
- closed ecology;
- recognized personhood;
- autonomous command voice;
- and the ability to refuse its supervising Prime.

A world may be biologically complete while politically treated as unfinished.

---

# Conversion Ethics

## Native Life

Orthodox Domain law requires assessment of:

- native sapience;
- recoverable ecology;
- protected genetic lineages;
- and existing continuity claims.

Possible outcomes include:

- preservation;
- negotiated integration;
- ecological partition;
- archive recreation;
- or abandonment of conversion.

Militant branches may ignore these safeguards.

## Existing Civilizations

Cities and infrastructure may be:

- preserved inside new anatomy;
- translated into Domain equivalents;
- archived;
- relocated;
- or broken down for matter.

The treatment of existing populations becomes a defining moral test.

## Scrap-Origin Personhood

A semi-formed world may accumulate cognition before planners intended.

This raises questions of whether:

- the world owns itself;
- it may refuse completion;
- its supervising Prime remains legitimate;
- and dismantling unfinished organs constitutes bodily harm.

---

# Capabilities

## Active Conversion

Surrogate Worlds can rewrite:

- terrain;
- atmosphere;
- biospheres;
- infrastructure;
- and captured matter.

## Mass Accretion

Scrap-Grown examples can absorb:

- asteroids;
- wrecked ships;
- moons;
- orbital habitats;
- and planetary fragments.

## Limited Biospawn Production

Most can produce:

- construction organisms;
- maintenance Thralls;
- defense forms;
- environmental processors;
- and limited civilian bodies.

## Fleet Support

They may provide:

- repair tissue;
- nutrient reserves;
- docking cavities;
- replacement organs;
- and temporary command coordination.

## Rapid Adaptation

Their incomplete state makes them easier to redesign than mature Womb Worlds.

This is their greatest advantage.

---

# Limitations

Surrogate Worlds are unstable.

They may suffer from:

- incomplete command integration;
- poor heat rejection;
- inconsistent gravity;
- atmospheric collapse;
- nutrient bottlenecks;
- genetic drift;
- structural failure;
- fractured cognition;
- and external dependence.

A Surrogate World can appear powerful while remaining one systemic failure away from collapse.

---

# Failure States

## Conversion Stall

The world stops developing because of:

- resource shortage;
- command conflict;
- archive corruption;
- heat overload;
- or loss of supervisory intelligence.

## Split World

Different regions evolve incompatible command voices and biological law.

The world becomes several hives sharing one planetary mass.

## Hollow Failure

A Scrap-Grown Surrogate develops insufficient internal support.

Its mantle collapses into assembly caverns.

## Feral Accretion

The world continues absorbing matter without coherent design.

It may become a predatory planetary mass.

## Birth Without Governance

Biospawn production exceeds civic control.

The world fills with organisms faster than identity, law, and command can stabilize them.

## Premature Primehood

The world develops selfhood before its biological systems are complete.

A planetary Prime may awaken inside a body that cannot sustain its own mind.

---

# Relationship to Supernals

Some Surrogate Worlds are intended to become Supernals.

To do so, they must develop:

- complete propulsion organs;
- independent mass balance;
- stellar navigation;
- fleet command;
- long-term heat rejection;
- and a stable planetary Prime or Sovereign architecture.

A mobile Surrogate is not automatically a Supernal.

It remains a Surrogate until it can function as a mature traveling civilization.

---

# Relationship to Womb Worlds

A Surrogate becomes a Womb World when it achieves:

- stable life-production;
- autonomous metabolism;
- mature Womb Core coordination;
- viable ecology;
- continuous personhood law;
- and recognized civil identity.

This transition may be declared through a formal rite called the **First Independent Birth**, when the world creates a complete person or organism without external command, feedstock programming, or Prime intervention.

---

# Strategic Role

Surrogate Worlds serve as:

- conversion anchors;
- colonial seeds;
- siege bases;
- planetary repair platforms;
- temporary homeworlds;
- mass-processing sites;
- and developmental bridges between fleets and permanent worlds.

They allow the Domain to begin planetary-scale life before full completion is possible.

---

# Visual Aesthetic

Surrogate Worlds should embody **unfinished Symmorphic Ossuary Gothic**.

## From Orbit

They may resemble:

- half-built planets;
- exposed skeletal hemispheres;
- patchwork crust;
- asteroid swarms bound by tendons;
- red conversion fissures;
- black vascular seas;
- incomplete membrane atmospheres;
- and enormous external braces.

## Surface Scale

Common features include:

- cities embedded in scaffolds;
- half-grown rib mountains;
- exposed Flesh Cores;
- atmospheric walls;
- drifting construction organisms;
- scrap plains;
- open planetary wounds;
- and unfinished cathedral valleys.

## Interior Scale

Interiors contain:

- assembly caverns;
- scaffold forests;
- active conversion fronts;
- ship graveyards;
- provisional Womb Cores;
- and rivers of liquefied planetary matter.

## Design Principle

> **A Surrogate World should look like a planet caught between corpse, construction site, organism, and civilization.**

A semi-formed world assembled from debris and recovered matter.

## Siege Surrogate

A rapidly grown military worldbody.

## Refuge Surrogate

An emergency planetary habitat for displaced populations.

## Reclamation Surrogate

A damaged Womb World undergoing reconstruction.

## Seed Surrogate

A compact proto-world intended for long-term growth.

## Experimental Surrogate

A world used to test planetary-scale biology and cognition.

## Feral Surrogate

An unfinished world without coherent command.

---

# Canon Summary

- Surrogate Worlds are transitional planetary organisms.
- They include worlds undergoing active conversion and semi-formed worlds grown from scraps.
- They are distinct from mature Womb Worlds and Supernals.
- Their greatest strength is adaptability.
- Their greatest weakness is systemic instability.
- They rely on provisional Womb Cores, Flesh Core networks, Seed Choirs, and supervisory Primes.
- They may mature into Womb Worlds or Supernals.
- Some remain permanently unfinished by design.
- Scrap-Grown Surrogates can be built from asteroid fields, planetary debris, wrecked fleets, and dead megastructures.
- Conversion Surrogates may preserve, integrate, or destroy preexisting worlds depending on Domain doctrine.
- A Surrogate World may develop personhood before completion.

> **A Womb World is life made planetary. A Supernal is life made mobile. A Surrogate World is life still deciding what shape the world should take.**
