Core Ideals:
	•	Survive with elegance: Martians do not merely endure; they transform their trials into art, into [[Systems]], into ceremony.
	•	Build what lasts: [[Infrastructure]] is treated as heritage. Even tools are named.
	•	Honor memory, not myth: Martians reject [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]]-style nationalism, instead creating personal legacies archived into the Line.
	•	[[Silence]] is sacred: Quiet reflection, especially under red skies, is culturally valued. Some debates are conducted in [[Ritual]] stillness, with each citizen submitting a single, silent action instead of speech.
[[Core Identity]]: The Descendants of Ascent
	•	Martians define themselves by survival, precision, and heritage.
	•	Rooted in their shared [[Origin]] aboard Miridian Ascent, their [[culture]] is shaped by:
	•	Scarcity and necessity
	•	Pride in autonomy from the Federation 
	•	Deep reverence for engineering, ritualized maintenance, and the environment
“We are the echo of ascent, forged by red dust and silent sky.”

Architecture Style: Red [[Synthesis]]
A fusion of:
	•	Brutalist utility (thick, shielded forms, low sweeping silhouettes)
	•	Organic adaptation (curved modular units to match Martian wind and terrain)
	•	Sacred symmetry (solar alignments, radial city layouts around core spires)
[[Key]] Elements:
	•	Subsurface Dwellings: Carved into basalt or under domes of Martian glass.
	•	Glow-lit Corridors: Low-light interiors, ringed with ambient bio-luminescent panels or programmable surfaces.
	•	“Cathedral Reactors”: Power stations resemble [[Temples]], decorated with mural-screens showing launch footage, ecological balances, or ancestral AI trees.
	•	Terraced Vertical Farms: Interior garden atriums, visible in every hab complex.

I. The Committee Years (2280–2320)

When the first world house domes rose over Valles Marineris and Arsia Mons, they were not cities — they were lifeboats. The survivors who lived beneath them were governed by the Martian Colonial Committee (MCC), an administrative organ of the [[Colonial Union]].

The Committee’s task was to allocate air, water, and power. Its authority was absolute, but its loyalty was to [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]]. Colonists were taxed, regulated, and surveyed. Even the soil beneath their boots was deemed property of [[Gilgamesh Station]].

Martians obeyed, but resentment grew. They were asked to endure risk while [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] and Luna reaped reward.

⸻

II. The Age of Domes and Shades (2320–2370)

Engineering became [[culture]].
	•	World House Domes spread across valleys and craters, pressurized arcs containing rivers, forests, and cities.
	•	[[Starshades]] were deployed at L1, reflecting sunlight to warm the thin air and accelerate atmospheric thickening.
	•	Comet seeding delivered volatiles to frozen plains, leaving trails of ice that melted into shallow seas.

Each dome-city developed its own character: Arsia the pragmatic, Valles the proud, Elysium the experimental. Though still tied to the Committee, Martians began to call themselves children of domes, not wards of [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]].

⸻

III. Miridian’s Ascent (2378–2410)

The spark came from Miridia Dome, a settlement founded by [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/Biology & Lifecycle/Engineers|Engineers]] and poets. Its matriarch, known simply as Miridian, declared:

“We are not Earthborn, nor Lunars. We are Kindred of Mars.”

Her words spread faster than CU censors could [[Silence]] them. Domes lit their interiors crimson in solidarity. [[Workers]] refused CU tithe quotas. Children drew red sigils on the walls: the first symbols of the Red Clan.

The Committee lost its grip. By 2410, the Martian Assembly replaced it, a council of domes speaking with one voice. Miridian died soon after, but her ascent became myth — she was remembered as the Mother of the Martian Dawn.

⸻

IV. [[The Kindred of Mars]] (2410–2450)

The Martians took a new name: Kindred of Mars, also called the Red Clan.

They rejected [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]]-style nationalism. Instead, they bound themselves by guild and craft. [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/Biology & Lifecycle/Engineers|Engineers]], maintainers, and growers became as honored as warriors once were. [[Silence]] and reflection entered [[Ritual]] life, for in [[Silence]] the domes survived.

Culturally, they wove three values into [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Identity|Identity]]:
	1.	Survive with elegance.
	[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]].	Build what lasts.
	[[Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)|Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)]].	Honor memory, not myth.

Physically, Martians grew tall and lean in low gravity. Red-iron tattoos marked their skin, badges of belonging. They became a people who looked to the dust storms and called them home.

⸻

V. Toward the Red World (2450+)

By mid-25th century, Mars was no longer only domes. [[Starshades]] had warmed the sky, cometary seas shimmered in valleys, and scattered biozones clung to life in the open air.

The Kindred knew their world was scarred, imperfect, but alive. For the first [[Time]] in [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]], a people could say they were born not of [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]], nor of Luna, but of Mars itself.
