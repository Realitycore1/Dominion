Life in the [[Austro Guard]] colony is defined less by personal ambition and more by the quiet, constant hum of duty. Every sunrise reminds citizens that their lives belong partly to themselves, and partly to the Guard.
	•	Education and Upbringing
Children are raised in mixed-lineage cohorts, blending European and Chinese heritage into one civic fabric.
	•	Early Training: From age 7, students drill in civic literacy (laws, [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Ameise Hive/History]] of the Guard) and martial basics (marksmanship, survival, squad tactics).
	•	Honor Lessons: Stories of great Oathkeepers are memorized alongside multiplication tables. “What you owe the Guard” is taught as much as math or science.
	•	Work and Service
The workplace is an extension of the Oath.
	•	A shipwright might leave a signature plate hidden inside a warship’s hull, seen as an offering to the Guard.
	•	Miners [[Time]] their shifts to align with ceremonial bells rung across the colony at dawn and dusk, marking “Oath hours.”
	•	Many professions incorporate voluntary rotations—months of unpaid service maintaining [[Civic Infrastructure]], seen as honor contributions.
	•	Civic Duty as Routine
Because any adult may be chosen as an Oathkeeper, citizens live with an ever-present awareness that they might be called to govern tomorrow.
	•	Taverns and tea houses host “mock councils” where ordinary [[Workers]] practice debate and decision-making.
	•	Citizens keep Duty Journals, small ledgers where they note decisions, observations, and judgments in [[Dominion Omniverse/Dominion Multiverse/Eos Verse/Dominion Verse/Verse Structure/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/Post-WW3/Daily life|Daily life]], as training for potential governance.
	•	Social Life
Leisure is communal rather than individual.
	•	Festivals revolve around oath anniversaries, [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Empyrean Continuum/The Empyrearchy/Military|Military]] victories, and the unveiling of new Walls of Duty.
	•	Music mixes martial chants with folk songs carried from [[Chapter XXII (Earth Primordium)]] lineages, creating a soundscape that feels equal parts battlefield and hearth.
	•	Friendships are strengthened through Shared Burdens—citizens deliberately take difficult shifts together to prove loyalty.
	•	The Burden and the Relief
The Oath weighs heavy, but citizens find relief in knowing everyone carries it. The shared load levels society; even the wealthiest industrialist might be pulled into an unwanted term as Oathkeeper, while the humblest laborer may one day preside over the council.

Every meal, shift, and festival in [[Austro Guard]] is haunted by a single question: “Am I upholding the Oath today?” That quiet self-audit shapes both the discipline and the pride of the colony.
