Prehistoric Age (Survival in the Beastlands)
	•	[[Humanity]] is not the apex predator. The world is dense with Beast Chronicle creatures.
	•	Shamanism is survival tech: chants, bones, blood, and myth bind monsters just enough for people to live.
	•	First contacts with the [[Realms]] (Heaven, Hell, Purgatory, [[The Void]], [[V - Beast Chronicles/Undead & Constructs/The Empty|The Empty]]) are direct — [[Angels]] and [[Demons]] walk openly, demanding oaths.
	•	The cowardly god presents himself as “the Creator,” but his behavior is petty, jealous, and violent. Shamans whisper that something is wrong with god.

⸻

Bronze Age (The Age of [[Ritual]] Kings)
	•	Bronze proves to hold axiomatic resonance — weapons and wards that “fix” laws.
	•	City-states rise as fortified bastions, each ruled by priest-kings who anchor their cities with [[Ritual]] [[law]].
	•	Gilgamesh, Hercules, and other epics are true stories, memories of [[Humans]] facing Beasts and [[Realms]].
	•	[[Angels]] and [[Demons]] openly fight for influence, each trying to monopolize worship.
	•	The jealous god intervenes sporadically — punishing cities that stray, rewarding those who flatter him.

⸻

Iron Age (The Age of Empire and [[law]])
	•	Iron cuts through illusion and spectral [[Entities]]. Writing becomes the ultimate ward — myths pinned into text cannot easily be erased.
	•	Empires expand by imposing their cosmology on conquered peoples: [[law]], religion, and [[Ritual]] unify not just people but reality itself.
	•	The [[Realms]] withdraw slightly, focusing on proxy wars. [[Humanity]] begins to mistake myth for metaphor, though the truth is only just beneath the surface.
	•	The cowardly god enforces orthodoxy: declaring heresies are “unforgivable” because they unravel his fragile authority.

⸻

Medieval Age (The Bastion Era)
	•	The world stabilizes into fortified bastion-cities, each layered with wards, [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Gnostic Expanse/Mirror Earth (2076)/Relics|Relics]], and reliquaries.
	•	Peasants live under rigid [[Ritual]] orthodoxy — planting, marriages, and funerals are all survival codes.
	•	Knights, monks, and orders act as axiomatic champions — their oaths literally hold back Beasts and [[Realms]].
	•	The Church (or equivalents) becomes both a spiritual and ontological authority: faith is as important as food for keeping the city alive.
	•	The cowardly god enjoys this era, as [[Humanity]] worships him without seeing his cowardice.

⸻

Renaissance & Early Modern (Seeds of the Recession)
	•	Old texts are rediscovered; myths are studied as classics, not warnings.
	•	Contact with the [[Realms]] declines — [[Angels]] and [[Demons]] retreat into subtlety. Beasts are rare, sometimes dismissed as superstition.
	•	Secret societies (lodges, covens, cults) preserve real knowledge, but they become background Cold War factions, maneuvering unseen.
	•	The jealous god, threatened by [[Human]] curiosity, begins enforcing the Great [[Silence]]: suppressing contact with [[Realms]], forcing [[Angels]]/[[Demons]] into stalemate.

⸻

Magical Recession (400+ Years)
	•	[[Realms]] retreat or are locked out; Beasts become rare.
	•	[[Humanity]] finally breathes. Cities expand, trade flourishes, wars are fought with steel and gunpowder instead of [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Gnostic Expanse/Mirror Earth (2076)/Relics|Relics]] and wards.
	•	Rationalism, science, and [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Military/Technology|Technology]] rise — not because magic was fake, but because magic receded.
	•	Myths turn into folklore, religions become “faith” rather than literal survival [[law]].
	•	In truth, the [[Realms]] are not gone — they are in stalemate, waging a Cold War beyond sight. [[Humanity]] is left to its own devices.

⸻

Industrial & Modern (Building on Fossilized Myths)
	•	[[Humanity]] industrializes. Factories, trains, and guns dominate. But everything still carries vestiges of myth:
	•	Railroads line tracks with iron because “it’s practical,” not realizing they’re also wards.
	•	[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Estela Integralism/Military|Military]] flags are designed as symbols, not knowing they’re old sigils.
	•	National anthems repeat chants that began as survival hymns.
	•	Wars are fought like ours — but the rituals and myths still unconsciously anchor reality.
	•	The cowardly god grows paranoid. His influence wanes as [[Humans]] no longer need him, yet he cannot directly intervene without exposing his weakness.

⸻

The Return of Magic (Present Setting)
	•	After centuries of [[Silence]], the [[Realms]] stir. [[Angels]], [[Demons]], and Beasts begin appearing again — first subtly, then more openly.
	•	Factions that hoarded knowledge during the Recession emerge as power players: secret orders, cults, bloodlines, and old bastion-cities reveal hidden strengths.
	•	[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Military/Technology|Technology]] and magic collide: runic rifles, industrial wards, leyline reactors. [[Humanity]] now has science hardened by myth.
	•	The cowardly god panics: his old supremacy cannot survive a world where [[Humans]] rediscover they never needed him.
	•	A new era begins: city-states and nations alike brace for open Realm conflict, while [[Humanity]] prepares to take its place as not just survivors — but contenders.
