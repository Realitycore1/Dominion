I. Physical Definition

In real-world particle [[Physics]], a sterile neutrino is a hypothesized right-handed neutrino that does not participate in the standard weak nuclear interaction, interacting only through gravity and possible exotic couplings. Unlike the three known active neutrino flavors (electron, muon, tau), sterile neutrinos are Standard Model singlets, existing outside the SU([[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]) gauge group.

Within the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), sterile neutrinos are modeled as dark sector coupling nodes — neutral leptonic excitations that act as bridges between baryonic matter and hidden-matter [[Domains]]. Their effective role is to modulate dark matter phase-space density, allowing gravitational structures to remain stable even in low-baryon-density [[Regions]].
	•	Spin: ½ (Fermion)
	•	Mass: eV to keV scale in most verses; can scale to MeV or higher in engineered states.
	•	Charge: 0
	•	Interaction: Gravitational, plus potential coupling to [[Axion]], [[Nulliton]], or [[Graviton]] fields in [[VFF]]-modified [[Physics]].
