I. The Age of Wet [[Infrastructure]] (2030 – 2060)

After the AI and energy plateaus, biotech becomes the next “inevitable revolution.”
Governments call it the living [[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Locations/Eos Multiverse/The Dark Forest Zone/Local Cluster/Totum/Verse Structure/Superclusters/Laniakea/Milky Way Galaxy/Sol System/Earth (Terra)/2079-4000/Colonial Union/Interstellar/Colonies/Kuomin Empire/Economy|Economy]]: programmable crops, printable organs, adaptive microbes that clean air and water.
But under scarcity and political tension, biotech matures into a set of pragmatic, morally gray survival tools.

Progress happens, but meaning drains out of it.

⸻

II. Foundations of Realism

1. Constraint-driven, not miraculous
	•	Labs can design sequences cheaply, but grow them only where energy and feedstock allow.
	•	Biotech’s main bottleneck is protein [[Synthesis]] cost—each vat run needs megawatts and sterilized water.
	•	The [[Energy plateau]] keeps everything small, regional, and dirty.

2. Incremental Integration
	•	Synthetic organisms appear first as industrial helpers: bacteria that recover lithium, algae that digest plastics.
	•	Once proven safe, similar organisms enter agriculture and medicine.
	•	Society acclimates—each adoption feels minor until the biosphere itself is semi-engineered.

3. Corporate custodianship
	•	Megacorps patent every viable genome; farmers lease seed lines that self-expire after one season.
	•	Nations trade genetic licenses the way they once traded oil concessions.
	•	“Gene IP” becomes the new diplomacy.

IV. Social Consequences
	1.	The Divide of Flesh and License
The wealthy maintain licensed, monitored genomes—disease-free, modded for climate resilience.
The poor rely on gray-market clinics using unverified edits.
Genetic stratification replaces class visibly: phenotypes become passports.
	2.	The Bio-Clerical Underground
Geneticists and monks share a role: keepers of uncorrupted DNA.
They maintain the “wild vaults,” storing pre-modification genomes for a future that may need them.
	3.	Health Authoritarianism
After multiple viral leaks, states mandate genome registration.
Everyone carries a “biometric seed code”—ostensibly medical, functionally social credit.
	4.	Religious Reaction
Faith movements split: some see gene editing as salvation (return to Eden), others as desecration.
The Ark incident fuels rhetoric that mankind “touched the sacred sequence.”
	5.	[[New Readers Start Here/I - Pleroma Archive/World/Civilizations/Attea Matil/Culture & Society/Identity|Identity]] Drift
As minor body edits—vision range, muscle efficiency, immunity—become routine, [[Humans]] forget baseline norms.
Art and [[law]] wrestle with defining “unaltered [[Human]].”
