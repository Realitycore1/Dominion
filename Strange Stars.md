I. Physical Definition

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), strange [[Stars]] are classified as stable [[Exotic Matter]] anchors — compact stellar bodies that serve as long-lived quark matter reactors.
They are often found where [[Phaseon]]–[[Aetherion]] coupling in the [[Verseon]] lattice prevents further collapse into a black hole, locking the object into a high-density, high-stability phase.
	•	Core State: Strange quark matter in a degenerate plasma state.
	•	Density: Slightly higher than quark [[Stars]], approaching preon star levels.
	•	Radiation Profile: Predominantly neutrinos and faint [[X-Ray]] emissions; stable over immense timescales.

I. Physical Definition

In speculative astrophysics, a Strange Star is a type of quark star in which quark matter includes strange quarks in addition to the up and down quarks found in normal matter.
Strange quark matter (SQM) is theorized to be more stable than normal nuclear matter, meaning that once a neutron star core converts to SQM, the entire object may transform into a strange star.
The conversion process releases tremendous amounts of energy and could propagate if strange matter comes into contact with ordinary matter.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), strange [[Stars]] are classified as stable [[Exotic Matter]] anchors — compact stellar bodies that serve as long-lived quark matter reactors.
They are often found where [[Phaseon]]–[[Aetherion]] coupling in the [[Verseon]] lattice prevents further collapse into a black hole, locking the object into a high-density, high-stability phase.
	•	Core State: Strange quark matter in a degenerate plasma state.
	•	Density: Slightly higher than quark [[Stars]], approaching preon star levels.
	•	Radiation Profile: Predominantly neutrinos and faint [[X-Ray]] emissions; stable over immense timescales.
