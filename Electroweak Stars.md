I. Physical Definition

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Electroweak [[Stars]] are recognized as phase-transition reactors — [[Natural]] or engineered objects where [[Phaseon]]-mediated state changes continuously convert baryonic matter into high-energy particle streams without full gravitational collapse.
They sit evolutionarily between quark [[Stars]] and preon [[Stars]] in density, but their defining feature is ongoing particle-[[Physics]]-level fusion rather than traditional nuclear or quark degeneracy stability.
	•	Core State: Quark–gluon plasma undergoing electroweak transition.
	•	Energy Output: Far exceeds normal fusion; particle emission dominated by leptons, neutrinos, and high-energy bosons.
	•	Appearance: Compact star with intense neutrino flux, faint EM emission compared to total output.

Registry Role Code: [[VFF]]-ELW-01
	•	Represents a phase-transition stellar engine at the particle-[[Physics]] scale.
	•	Serves as both a [[Natural]] laboratory for force unification and an energy source for civilizations beyond classical fusion tech.
	•	Acts as a mid-stage compact object in the Omniversal stellar life-cycle classification.

I. Physical Definition

In speculative astrophysics, an Electroweak Star is a hypothetical compact stellar object in which the core temperature and density are so high that electroweak force unification occurs.
At these conditions (~10¹⁵ K), quark matter undergoes electroweak burning: up and down quarks convert into strange quarks, and in the process, release immense amounts of energy via W and Z bosons.

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Electroweak [[Stars]] are recognized as phase-transition reactors — [[Natural]] or engineered objects where [[Phaseon]]-mediated state changes continuously convert baryonic matter into high-energy particle streams without full gravitational collapse.
They sit evolutionarily between quark [[Stars]] and preon [[Stars]] in density, but their defining feature is ongoing particle-[[Physics]]-level fusion rather than traditional nuclear or quark degeneracy stability.
	•	Core State: Quark–gluon plasma undergoing electroweak transition.
	•	Energy Output: Far exceeds normal fusion; particle emission dominated by leptons, neutrinos, and high-energy bosons.
	•	Appearance: Compact star with intense neutrino flux, faint EM emission compared to total output.
