# (63) Knowledge of Ecosystems

## Manuscript Source

- Primary source: [[Manuscript]] Draft 2, Part III, Chapters 15-16.
- Manuscript date range: Years 3-17 After Landfall, continuing afterward.

## What Happened

Exploration revealed that Harmina was not a backdrop but a living system. Glassleaf forests changed opacity with light cycles. Needlebacks turned storm valleys into resonant instruments. Lantern swarms filled the sky in coordinated patterns. Hollow runners produced sounds mistaken for human voices. Walking Reefs shifted terrain slowly enough to break maps over years instead of days.

The colonists also encountered folklore seeds: Watchers, Lantern Men, Stone Pilgrims, unexplained tracks, moved equipment, storm sightings, and formations that might be natural or might not.

## What Resulted

- Scientific reports and campfire stories developed side by side.
- Ecologists, cartographers, and weather technicians became trusted interpreters of reality.
- Children became the first Harminan mythmakers because they were less invested in Earth categories.
- The possibility of an intelligent presence remained unconfirmed but narratively persistent.

## Consequences

Harmina's ecology made the planet feel like a homeland with mysteries rather than a hostile map to be conquered. This changed the emotional center of the civilization.
