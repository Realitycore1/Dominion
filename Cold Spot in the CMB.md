•	What it is: A large, unusually cool patch in the cosmic microwave background—temperatures a few×10⁻⁵ below the CMB average—seen in the Eridanus direction. First spotted with WMAP, later confirmed by Planck.
	•	Scale: Roughly 5–10° across with a colder core and a surrounding warm ring—i.e., a feature far larger than typical hot/cold spots.
	•	How unusual: Statistically odd but not impossible in ΛCDM. After accounting for “look-elsewhere” effects, most studies put it at ~[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]–3σ: notable, not decisive.
	•	Leading explanations:
	1.	Line-of-sight supervoid (Eridanus void) + ISW/Rees–Sciama effect: An enormous underdensity slightly cools CMB photons as dark energy changes the potential wells over [[Time]]. Likely explains part of the decrement.
	[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]].	Statistical fluke: A rare but allowed Gaussian fluctuation on large scales.
	[[Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)|Chapter XXX (Munitum Age)]].	Exotics (minority, unneeded): cosmic texture/defect, bubble-collision, non-trivial topology. Interesting, but current data don’t require them.
	•	Why it matters: It’s a clean test of large-scale structure and late-[[Time]] gravity (via ISW), a checkpoint on Gaussianity, and a perennial probe for beyond-ΛCDM [[Physics]].
