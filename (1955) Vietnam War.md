The Vietnam War was a conflict that took place from 1955 to 1975 between North Vietnam, supported by the Soviet Union, and South Vietnam, supported by the United States. The war began as a colonial war between France and the Vietnamese, but quickly escalated into a larger conflict involving multiple regional powers. The United States entered the war in 1959 to support South Vietnam, which was struggling against communist forces. The war was initially successful for the United States, with North Vietnam suffering significant losses. However, as the conflict dragged on, the United States faced increasing resistance from Vietnamese guerrillas and its support for South Vietnam began to wane. 1973, the United States withdrew its troops from Vietnam, and the war continued for two more years until 1975, when North Vietnam finally conquered South Vietnam and reunified the country under communist rule. The Vietnam War was a controversial conflict that led to widespread criticism of the United States' involvement in Southeast Asia. It is remembered as a tragic and divisive event that resulted in millions of casualties, refugees, and environmental damage.
