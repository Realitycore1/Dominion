Apparent, very large associations of [[Quasars]]—identified as overdense “[[Chains]]” or “clumps” in redshift space—typically at z ~ 0.8–[[Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)|Chapter XXIX (Verse Concursus)]]. They’re found with clustering/percolation algorithms (e.g., friends-of-friends) applied to quasar catalogs.

Typical scales & famous cases.
	•	Common LQGs: 100–300 Mpc across.
	•	Claimed extremes (e.g., “Huge-LQG”): effective lengths ≥ 500 Mpc–1 Gpc when measured along their longest axis (often highly elongated).
	•	Memberships range from a few dozen to a few hundred [[Quasars]].

Why they matter.
	•	[[Quasars]] are biased tracers of massive halos, so LQGs probe the cosmic web at early times.
	•	Very large claims test the cosmological principle (homogeneity/isotropy) and ΛCDM predictions for large-scale structure.

How they’re identified (methods).
	•	Friends-of-friends/percolation with a chosen linking length in comoving space.
	•	Validation via Monte Carlo mocks, two-point stats, Minkowski functionals, convex hull/principal-axis (“inertia”) analyses, and comparisons to ΛCDM light-cone simulations with quasar duty cycles.

Physical picture (if real).
	•	Likely strings of filaments and sheets where quasar activity lights up parts of an extended web; not uniform solid objects.
	•	Strong elongation along one or two principal axes is expected; duty cycle and selection make them look patchy.

[[Key]] caveats.
	•	Selection effects: survey depth/masks, redshift errors, and nonuniform completeness spuriously link sources.
	•	Percolation bias: too-large linking lengths stitch chance neighbors into “super-structures.”
	•	A-posteriori statistics: significance can be overestimated after spotting an unusual feature.
	•	Projection: redshift-space distortions can elongate structures along the line of sight.

Current status (consensus).
	•	LQG-scale associations (~100–300 Mpc) are consistent with ΛCDM when accounting for quasar bias and selection.
	•	The largest, Gpc-scale claims remain controversial; many analyses find they can arise from chance alignments + algorithm choices rather than requiring new [[Physics]].
