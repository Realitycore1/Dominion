I. Physical Definition

A Hypernova Star is the progenitor star in the process of undergoing — or having just undergone — a hypernova explosion, an event vastly more energetic than a standard supernova.
Hypernovae typically occur in massive [[Stars]] exceeding 30–50 solar masses, often rotating rapidly and possessing strong magnetic fields, leading to collimated relativistic jets and extreme gamma-ray bursts.

[[Key]] features:
	•	Ultra-Energetic Core Collapse — Releases energies on the order of 10³⁹ joules or higher.
	•	Gamma-Ray Burst Association — Commonly produces long-duration GRBs detectable across cosmological distances.
	•	Relativistic Jet Formation — Bipolar jets accelerate matter [[Close]] to light speed, piercing stellar envelopes.
	•	Compact Object Birth — Often results in the creation of a black hole or highly magnetized neutron star (magnetar).

In the [[VFF]] ([[Verse]] Field Framework), Hypernova [[Stars]] are classified as Cataclysmic Transition Anchors, marking high-energy transformation points in stellar evolution that can reshape entire galactic [[Regions]].
