Phase I — Respiratory Colonization

Initial symptoms mimic:
	•	dry cough
	•	chest pressure
	•	shortness of breath
	•	fever / malaise
	•	reduced oxygen saturation

At this stage it is almost indistinguishable from viral pneumonia.

What’s actually happening:

Microscopic Wetwire-compatible biofilament growth begins along:
	•	alveolar membranes
	•	bronchiolar lining
	•	vascular interfaces

Not enough to be obvious yet.

Just enough to begin anchoring.

⸻

Phase II — Integration / Fibrotic False Healing

This is the nasty part.

The body attempts to heal damaged lung tissue.

Wirelung exploits that healing response.

Instead of normal scar tissue, it induces:
	•	conductive pseudo-fibrosis
	•	wet-organic filament webbing
	•	metallic/proteinaceous micro-lattice formation
	•	increasingly rigid alveolar collapse

So scans may show what appears to be:
	•	fibrosis
	•	fungal mats
	•	unusual inflammatory tissue
	•	calcification-like or metallic artifact anomalies

Doctors think the lungs are healing badly.

But really the tissue is being rewired.

That’s excellent horror.

⸻

Phase III — Degenerative Capacity Loss

Now the patient begins losing actual pulmonary function.

Symptoms escalate into:
	•	severe hypoxia
	•	chronic air hunger
	•	inability to recover exertion
	•	fluid retention
	•	blood oxygen instability
	•	episodes of “mechanical breathing” sensation

At this stage, the patient often feels like:

their lungs are no longer behaving like lungs.

That is exactly the kind of line you want in-world.

⸻

Phase IV — Mutagenic Escalation / Lethal Conversion

This is where Wirelung stops being “just respiratory.”

You mentioned:

accelerated mutagenic properties that cause it to become lethal

That’s the right move.

Once colonization reaches critical density, the infection begins expressing Wetwire’s true nature:
	•	tissue adaptation
	•	foreign structure growth
	•	systemic infiltration

This can cause:
	•	vocal changes
	•	metallic crackles in breathing
	•	subcutaneous wire-like vascular patterning
	•	eye / sinus / throat spread
	•	nervous system involvement
	•	violent terminal conversion in rare cases

Not all victims need to become monsters.

In fact, most shouldn’t.

That keeps it scarier.

Most should simply:

die in ways medicine cannot fully explain.

That’s more believable and more unsettling.
