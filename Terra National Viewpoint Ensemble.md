# Terra National Viewpoint Ensemble

## Ensemble Rule

Each nation receives:

- a governing or institutional perspective;
- a designed child who reaches adulthood before the Exchange;
- a common-person perspective affected by policy;
- a recurring material concern such as food, work, medicine, transit, or shelter;
- at least one choice that contradicts the nation's public mythology.

No nation is represented by one ideology or social class.

## United American Republics

### Existing Leads

- **Nadiya Sayegh:** journalist and archivist.
- **General Clayton Voss:** constitutional restoration leader.
- **Senator Adrienne Solace:** global-renewal federalist and later constrained opposition witness.

### Mara Keene

- Born in Chicago in 2020 after a Wirelung-era employer health plan offered edited embryos to industrial families.
- Changes include respiratory resistance, improved toxin clearance, muscular maintenance, and attention stability.
- Her parents accepted a lifetime maintenance contract they did not fully understand.
- Corporate ownership of her treatment protocols follows her into adulthood.
- Becomes a rail and grid technician because logistics work remains valuable under every government.
- Represents working people who distrust central authority but still depend on national-scale infrastructure.
- Her core conflict is whether she is a citizen with benefits or a company asset with voting rights.

### Ground-Level Lens

- Chicago rail yards, lake freight, industrial neighborhoods, union halls, emergency shelters, and restoration politics.
- Common problems: corruption, factional violence, biometric employment controls, regional legal conflict, and continuity-site inequality.

## Alkebulan Union

### Existing Lead

- **Dr. Imani Okafor:** engineer, ecological recovery specialist, and cold-fusion leader.

### Sade Adebayo

- Born in Ubuntu City in 2038 through a community genome cooperative.
- Changes focus on malaria resistance, heat tolerance, fungal immunity, and efficient water retention.
- The cooperative treats the genome as a public-health commons rather than corporate or dynastic property.
- Becomes an agronomist and seed-network organizer.
- Supports Alkebulan's local council model but criticizes the way regional representation can delay emergency decisions.
- Her core conflict is whether public investment creates a duty to serve where councils assign her.

### Binta Adebayo

- Sade's grandmother and a market trader with farming relatives near Dar es Salaam.
- Distrusts laboratory agriculture but protects local seed diversity.
- Gives the Union a common-person perspective rooted in household economics, markets, and inherited ecological knowledge.

### Ground-Level Lens

- Ubuntu City councils, Dar es Salaam trade, Nairobi research networks, seed banks, port logistics, flexible regional borders, and village agriculture.
- Common problems: infrastructure gaps, foreign debt, climate stress, representation delays, and balancing modern systems with local knowledge.

## Yamama Caliphate

### Existing Lead

- **Abdulrahman bin Ali Al-Saud:** designed royal descendant, military logistician, moderate coalition-builder, and first caliph.

### Fariha al-Hassan

- Respiratory nurse and convoy medic from the Al-Noor bridge corridor.
- Not genetically designed.
- Raises a son while working through checkpoints, bombing campaigns, famine routes, and the formation of Yamama.
- Experiences Abdulrahman's policies through ration changes, military inspections, clinic law, and shelter access.
- Respects his restraint without treating him as a savior.
- Her core conflict is whether unity protects ordinary people or merely gives distant authorities a more sacred vocabulary.

### Ground-Level Lens

- Al-Noor transit, water stations, religious charities, regional courts, convoy routes, military checkpoints, and mixed civil-religious governance.
- Common problems: radicalization, foreign pressure, fragmented authority, food transit, gendered expectations, and conflict between jurisprudence and emergency logistics.

## CRINK / Fourth International

### Existing Lead

- **Dev Mehta:** civil systems engineer who values the bloc because it reliably supplies power, transit, and medicine.

### Kavya Mehta

- Born in Chengdu in 2036 through a state civic-genome cohort.
- Changes include viral resistance, stress regulation, memory retention, and reduced sleep requirements.
- The state funds her health and education but restricts travel and occupation as repayment of public investment.
- Becomes a strategic systems auditor.
- Believes managed systems save lives but increasingly opposes treating citizens as components.
- Her core conflict is whether competence within an authoritarian system can limit its harm or only improve its reach.

### Ground-Level Lens

- Chengdu transit, managed apartment districts, civic scoring, guaranteed pharmacies, ration systems, state schools, and strategic command.
- Common problems: loss of privacy, occupation assignment, ideological conformity, state ownership of enhancements, and the genuine security produced by centralized planning.

## Europolis

### Elise Laurent

- Born near Paris in 2035 through a European public-resilience program.
- Changes include respiratory repair, radiation-damage resistance, neural interface compatibility, and emotional recovery.
- Raised during the European Union's collapse and Europolis consolidation.
- Becomes a continental rail-allocation and civil-continuity planner.
- Believes integrated systems prevent regional abandonment but sees citizenship tiers turn mobility into privilege.
- Her core conflict is whether keeping the continental machine running requires becoming indifferent to the people excluded from it.

### Luc Laurent

- Elise's father, a rail maintenance worker who experiences integration from below.
- Skeptical of Europolis but proud that his daughter can keep trains moving across borders that once stranded families.

### Ground-Level Lens

- Paris family life, refugee rail, continental ration tiers, cultural standardization, public housing, and emergency transit.
- Common problems: corruption, citizenship hierarchy, regional resentment, surveillance, refugee exclusion, and the loss of older national identities.

## Cross-National Convergence

The designed adults meet through the 2077 cold-fusion negotiations:

- Abdulrahman represents Yamama and the Equilibrium Pact.
- Sade supports Imani's ecological and food-production requirements.
- Kavya audits strategic replication risks for CRINK.
- Elise plans continental distribution for Europolis.
- Mara represents independent UAR rail and grid operators.

They recognize that every society gave their bodies a political meaning before they could consent.

Their adult choices diverge:

- Mara fights contractual ownership.
- Sade challenges compulsory public service.
- Abdulrahman limits dynastic and religious ownership.
- Kavya resists state assignment.
- Elise resists algorithmic triage.

The Exchange tests whether any of them can act beyond the purpose for which they were designed.
