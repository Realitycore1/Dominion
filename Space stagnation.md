I. The Great Pause (2030–2060)

Historians call it the Interorbital Plateau.
By 2030, every major power has reusable [[Rockets]] and smallsat fleets. By 2060, no one has left orbit.
Budgets flow, launches continue, but colonization never transitions from rhetoric to settlement.
In the end, [[Humanity]] circles its own debris.

⸻

II. Causes of Stagnation

1. Economic Gravity
	•	Space remains economically adjacent, not essential.
	•	[[Energy plateau]] and [[Crop failures]] force resources toward Earth’s crises.
	•	Politicians keep up appearances with token lunar missions, “symbolic unity projects,” and corporate orbital hotels.

2. Material Bottlenecks
	•	Launch vehicles depend on dwindling helium-3 and rare alloys from terrestrial mines constrained by environmental politics.
	•	Every rocket burns what Earth can’t spare.
	•	Attempts to develop closed-loop manufacturing in orbit stall—too expensive, too few launches.

3. The Liability Problem
	•	Orbital traffic grows exponentially; insurance firms refuse to underwrite new megasatellite networks.
	•	2039–2045 sees the first major Debris Cascades—hundreds of derelict satellites colliding, generating lethal shrapnel belts.
	•	Nations sign emergency “Kessler Protocols,” freezing high-orbit expansion.

4. The Lunar Mirage
	•	AI-driven surveys of the Moon identify promising crater sites rich in volatiles (Aristarchus, Shackleton, Peary).
	•	Missions scout and 3-D map these basins, but every “base” remains robotic.
	•	Earth never sends colonists—only telepresence.
	•	By 2050, the “Moon Program” is a jobs scheme feeding data to bureaucracies rather than exploration.

5. Fragmented Vision
	•	Space [[Federal Agencies]] become propaganda [[Arms]].
	•	Private ventures collapse into state contracts.
	•	The public still sees launches and livestreams—proof of progress—but every flight loops back home.

V. Societal Effects
	1.	Symbolic Exploration [[culture]]
Space becomes mythology again—people watch launches like ancient festivals.
The dream of colonization mutates into religion: “The [[Stars]] are for our souls, not our bodies.”
	2.	Data Colonialism
Instead of mining minerals, nations mine observation data.
Earth becomes the commodity—space is the vantage point.
	3.	Corporate Propaganda
Orbital companies market themselves as saviors of [[Humanity]] while outsourcing component manufacture to famine economies.
Every rocket is a commercial for Earth’s dysfunction.
	4.	Militarization of Orbit
The debris crisis becomes pretext for orbital defense networks.
“Cleanup drones” double as anti-sat weapons.

	5.	Philosophical Hangover
A generation raised on colonization dreams grows cynical.
Writers and artists turn inward, inventing myths of “The Earthborn”—a [[Humanity]] cursed to orbit its failures.
