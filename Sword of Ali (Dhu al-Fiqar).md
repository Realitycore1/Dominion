🧭 Scientific / Archaeological Report

Relic: Dhu al-Fiqar (“The Sword of Ali”)
Discovery Location: Najaf, [[Iraq]], 2039, uncovered in a sealed reliquary beneath the Imam Ali Shrine after bomb damage exposed a [[Forgotten]] chamber.
Summary:
	•	Material: Forged iron double-edged blade, ~90 cm, with a distinct bifurcated tip (legendary hallmark of the sword). Hilt plated with silver, inlaid with Kufic script.
	•	Dating: Metallurgical testing suggests 7th century CE [[Origin]], consistent with early Islamic craftsmanship.
	•	Inscriptions: Faint etchings invoking Allah’s name and the phrase “lā fatā illā ʿAlī, lā sayf illā Dhū al-Fiqār” (“No hero but Ali, no sword but Dhu al-Fiqar”).
	•	Residues: Microscopic traces of blood and desert grit trapped in blade crevices; radiocarbon contamination prevents precise identification, but aligns with oral traditions of early battles.
	•	Archaeological Significance: Strong candidate for the historical sword wielded by Ali ibn Abi Talib at Badr and Uhud.
